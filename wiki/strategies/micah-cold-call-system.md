# Strategy: Micah's Cold Call System (Starbridge Speedrun)
tags: [cold calling, B2B, outbound, SLED, Starbridge, Glen Coco, qualification, booking, commission sales]

## Summary

A documented cold call system built from watching Micah (professional cold caller) run a 7.5-hour speedrun on the Starbridge campaign via the Glen Coco marketplace. He booked 6 meetings in a single session, converting 37.5% of right-ICP conversations — and filled an entire calendar by early afternoon.

Source: YouTube video tdYvRKVr0pk (134 min). Chunks 1-43 = prep + live calling. Chunks 44-56 = warm handoffs + debrief.

## Platform Context: Glen Coco

Glen Coco is a B2B marketplace where companies list sales campaigns with per-meeting bounties. Independent sales reps pick campaigns, cold call on their own, and get paid per qualified booked meeting.

For Starbridge: **$1,000 per qualified meeting** payout to Micah. Starbridge provides the campaign brief, training materials, talk tracks, ICP criteria, and list. Micah provides the calling and books the meetings. Zach (Starbridge director) handles warm handoffs.

This is commission-only, performance-based outbound. No salary, no guarantees. Everything turns on execution.

---

## Phase 1: Research and Prep (1.5 Hours)

Micah spent 1.5 hours before dialing. This is how he used it:

### Step 1 — Read the full campaign brief
Not skimmed. Read. Every product feature, every competitor, every objection handler, every qualification criterion. He took notes while reading.

### Step 2 — Study the competitive landscape
Named competitors: GovSpend, Pursuit, NationGraph. He understood the differentiation before picking up a phone — "GovSpend is great at historical spend. Starbridge is forward-looking, before the RFP hits."

### Step 3 — Internalize the core stat
"80% of RFPs are pre-written for a selected vendor." This became the pivot point of every pitch. He repeated it word-for-word across every call.

### Step 4 — Build the qualification criteria
Before calling, he was clear on what a qualified prospect looks like:
- Company actively sells into SLED (state, local, education)
- Decision maker or influencer (VP / manager level or above)
- SLED is a meaningful part of revenue (not aspirational)

### Step 5 — Internalize the script
He read the Starbridge talk tracks from the brief and blended them with his own style. He did not read a script — he internalized it so he could deliver it naturally.

### Step 6 — Audition call
One live call done before the session officially started. Used it to find the rhythm of the pitch and identify where friction appears.

## Phase 2: The Cold Call Script (Word-for-Word Framework)

Every call follows the same structure. Delivery is natural, not robotic. The words vary slightly. The structure never does.

### Step 1 — Permission Opener
> "Hey [Name]. It's Micah over at Starbridge. I know I'm catching you out of the blue here, but I guess do you mind if I grab half a minute? I'll tell you why I called and you can let me know if it's relevant or not."

Why it works:
- Acknowledges the cold call upfront (disarming, not apologetic)
- "Half a minute" — low commitment, specific
- "You can let me know if it's relevant" — puts them in control, reduces defensiveness
- Almost no one says no to this opener

### Step 2 — Pre-qualifier (before pitching)
> "Just to make sure I don't waste your time — do you guys sell into government or education accounts? Like SLED accounts at all?"

Why it works:
- He qualifies BEFORE pitching, not after
- Frames it as a favor to them ("don't waste your time")
- Binary question: yes/no
- If no → clean exit with zero time wasted on either side
- If yes → proceed

**If no:** "That makes this call a little easier. I'll mark you down so we don't keep bugging you." Done. No pitch, no push, polite exit.

### Step 3 — Thumbnail (10 seconds)
> "Quick thumbnail on us — Starbridge, we are a go-to-market intelligence platform built specifically for folks selling into the public sector."

Why it works:
- One sentence. Positions the product in a market context they already understand.
- Does not try to explain features yet

### Step 4 — Pitch (stat → problem → breakthrough)
> "What we're seeing from a high level is that more than 80% of RFPs are pre-written for a selected vendor. The big question is: is it possible to get in front of every one of your target agencies or schools before you get lost in the stack of generic proposals? And so to cut to it — our breakthrough lies in our AI-driven ability to surface every single intent signal through board meeting minutes, strategic plans, and even grant approvals. And we do it in a way where you can see line by line what your competitors are selling, who they're selling to, and when their contracts expire. Pretty bananas in the space, but super simple."

Structure:
- **Stat** → creates urgency and disrupts their current belief about how the game works
- **Big question** → reframes their problem, makes them feel it
- **Breakthrough** → single clear capability statement, not a feature list
- **Proof** → "line by line" is specific and visual
- **Disarm** → "pretty bananas in the space, but super simple" — takes down the complexity objection before it's raised

### Step 5 — Humble positioning + CTA
> "My timing is probably off and I know you probably have similar tooling in place already, but that's exactly why we've been hosting these 15-minute Zoom screen shares — just to showcase the dashboards and AI in action. And then from there, maybe keep us in the back pocket. Would you be game to loop up, say, tomorrow afternoon?"

Why it works:
- "My timing is probably off" — preemptive acknowledgment; removes the need for the prospect to say it
- "I know you probably have similar tooling" — same move; shows no desperation
- "15-minute screen share" is low-commitment framing, not "full sales demo"
- "Keep us in the back pocket" — reduces pressure; this isn't about closing, it's about being known
- CTA is specific: "tomorrow afternoon" not "sometime this week"

## Phase 3: Booking Mechanics (Once They Say Yes)

Once a prospect agrees to a meeting, Micah runs a tight booking sequence ON THE CALL. He never lets the booking sit as a follow-up.

**Sequence:**
1. "Okay, let me pull up the calendar." (checks availability live)
2. Offers 3-4 time slots
3. "Okay, let me make it easy on you — I'll send you an email calendar invite. That way our schedules are aligned."
4. Sends Calendly invite while prospect is still on the phone
5. "Whenever you see the Calendly link pop up in your inbox, feel free to hit accept — that way I know I didn't fat finger anything."
6. "One thing we do ask — meetings and fires can pop up. What's the best way for you to let us know if something changes?" (gets preferred reschedule channel)
7. "Is there anyone else you want to loop in that typically kicks the tires on these things with you?" (stakeholder expansion check)
8. "Anything else you have for me before I cut you loose here?"

Why the on-call booking matters: follow-up meetings fall off dramatically when the booking requires an email thread. Getting the calendar invite accepted while they're on the phone is the closest thing to a guaranteed show.

## Phase 4: Warm Handoff (Day 2)

Micah doesn't run the demo. His director Zach does. Micah sits in on the Zoom to provide context.

**Zach's handoff structure:**
1. Open with discovery: "Tell me a little about the sales motion today and how you're developing new business."
2. Qualify SLED percentage: "How much of your business would you say is focused on public sector vs. commercial?"
3. Ask what brought them to learn about Starbridge
4. Run the demo customized to their specific market (fire departments, K12, construction, etc.)
5. Ask about evaluation timeline
6. Propose next steps: larger stakeholder demo OR send materials

## Key Principles Micah Articulates

- **"Speed to conversation."** Don't over-think, don't chase one lead manually. If it's not a strong referral, throw it back in the dialer. Volume produces the right conversations.
- **Pre-qualify before pitching.** The pre-qualifier is not just polite — it's what makes the 37.5% conversion rate possible. By the time he's pitching, he already knows it's relevant.
- **Qualify harder on the handoff.** His own debrief: "I should have asked more specifically — are you the one that handles SLED accounts? Are you the decision maker or at least an influencer? Do you have at least five sales folks actively working on the SLED team?" Fuzzier qualification = weaker handoffs = disputed meetings.
- **Bad data ≠ wrong ICP.** He explicitly separates: bad data is a list quality issue, wrong ICP is a targeting issue. They have different fixes. Don't conflate them.
