# Strategy: Brand Social Media from Scratch

## Summary
A complete playbook for launching a brand social media presence from nothing — or fixing and scaling one that has stalled. Built around four expert-driven content types (Storytelling, Rankings/Ratings, Breakdowns, Scenarios), a posting cadence framework, and an understanding of how social content feeds a full marketing funnel including landing pages, lead magnets, pre-call assets, and DM-based sales. The emphasis is on business value over views, and on comments and DMs as active conversion tools.

## When to Use This
- Starting a brand account from zero
- When an existing account posts regularly but doesn't drive leads or sales
- When a team is distracted by hacks, trend-chasing, and scheduling tools instead of content quality
- When a services brand doesn't know how to make content without a photogenic physical product

## The Framework / Steps

### Step 1: Understand the Business Reason (The 7-Minute MBA)
Social media is not an end — it is a channel that replaces or supplements advertising, cold calling, and word of mouth. Content drives awareness; awareness must connect to a funnel:

**Awareness → Nurture → Conversion**

For most businesses, the path from a piece of content to a sale requires intermediate steps. The content drives someone to:
1. A **landing page** — either hard CTA (buy now) or soft CTA (give us your contact info)
2. A **lead magnet** — PDF, checklist, free quote, report — something valuable enough to exchange for an email address
3. An **email list or SMS** — where you educate and nurture until they're ready to buy
4. A **pre-call asset sequence** — content sent to leads before and after sales calls to prime the conversion

Every piece of content you create should be deployable across all of these stages. A video that gets 600 views is not wasted — if it gets placed in the welcome email sequence, the product detail page, and the post-call nurture flow, it works for the entire funnel.

### Step 2: Pick Two or Three Content Types to Start
There are four expert-driven content types that work for any business on short-form video and carousels. Each can be done in video format (talking head, vlog, green screen) or carousel format (no on-camera presence required).

**Type 1 — Storytelling**
Tell the story of your business, or the story of interesting things in your category. Use the hero's journey structure: setup → conflict → failed attempt → persistence → resolution.
- Student perspective: "I'm learning this, here's what I discovered"
- Expert perspective: "I know this craft, here's what others get wrong"
- Examples: Yon Mavan buying a bowling alley and documenting it, Ken Sakata (Front Office) telling the history of hunting jackets to build credibility before launching his own

**Type 2 — Rankings and Ratings**
Put an expert (or a passionate student) on camera rating things in your category. Dentist rating whitening toothpaste, baker rating premium ingredients, roofer rating siding materials, golf coach rating swing techniques.
- Formula: Is X or Y better for Z? Rate these on a scale. Which of these is actually worth the money?
- Works in virtually any industry where people spend money and want expert guidance
- Dress the part — dentist in the dentist chair, baker in baker's coat, mechanic in the garage

**Type 3 — Breakdowns**
Explain things your audience wants or needs to know, or connect your expertise to current events.
- Breaking down your process, your design choices, your category norms
- Example: Graphic designer breaking down why certain packaging works, meta ads consultant breaking down the full awareness funnel
- Give away everything. Most people won't replicate it — they'll hire you instead

**Type 4 — Scenarios**
Put the viewer in a specific situation and show them the solution. The more specific the scenario, the better the targeting.
- "If you're a man over 40 who wants to…"
- "Here's what I'd do if I had 700 square feet and needed to…"
- "My plan to save Country Style and dethrone Tim Hortons"
- Scenario content pre-qualifies the audience — only people who identify with the scenario watch it

### Step 3: Set the Posting Cadence
Pick 2–3 content type combinations (e.g., rankings/ratings in video + breakdowns in carousel). Post two of each per week = 4–6 posts per week. Do this for one full month before evaluating anything.

**Month 1–3:** Four posts per week (minimum), no CTAs in any videos. Focus entirely on building traction. Videos consistently hitting 5K+ views (or 2K+ for narrow niches like construction or B2B services) is the threshold to add calls to action.

**Adding CTAs:** Once traction exists, drive people to lead magnets in the bio. Use ManyChat-style triggers (comment a keyword, get a DM link). Move people from social to email list, SMS, or a booking link.

**Layering on:** Once a content type starts hitting, use the comment questions to generate more content. Someone asks about color correction → your next video hooks with that question and answers it. Let comments build your content roadmap.

### Step 4: Platform and Account Setup
- **Primary platforms:** TikTok and Instagram — free reach from video one, largest addressable markets, content that works on one works on the other
- **Secondary crosspost:** LinkedIn and X if you're willing to actually engage there; YouTube Shorts for awareness only (low conversion value)
- **Account type:** Creator account on TikTok (not brand account — brand accounts are deprioritized in the algorithm). Exception: TikTok Shop requires a business account.
- **Location setting:** Set up accounts while physically in your target country, or use a local phone number and setup. The algorithm distributes based on account origin location.
- **No schedulers:** Every scheduling app — including native ones — demonstrably hurts performance. Upload and post manually every time.
- **No trends:** Unless your team can produce same-day, trends don't work. Skip them entirely.

### Step 5: Set Design and Visual Standards
In 2026, "intentional" is the baseline. Every framing choice, lighting setup, and graphic overlay matters — not because it needs to be expensive, but because the competition is making intentional choices.

**The set:** Build a recurring filming location that's instantly recognizable as yours. Fragrance brands film in front of their collection. Mechanics film in the garage. Dentists in the chair. Designers at their desk with displayed work. A recognizable set removes the need to hook purely on words — the visual format does some of that work.

### Step 6: Work Comments and DMs as Sales Tools
This is the most underused layer. Every person who comments on your content is a warm lead.

**Reactive:** Respond to every comment personally. Don't just acknowledge — answer the question, make a recommendation, turn the conversation into a consultation.

**Proactive:** If someone comments on multiple videos about a topic related to your service (especially if they're in your target geography or market), DM them. "I noticed you've commented on a few of our roofing videos — are you thinking about a project?" This is direct access to warm leads with no advertising cost.

**Proactive DMs on leads:** If you see a CEO or CMO of a target company engaging with your content, slide into the DMs. This has led to major client relationships for consultants and agencies using this approach.

**Sales team in DMs:** If you can't manage DMs personally, put a sales rep there. Speed matters — someone who DMs within minutes of commenting is far more likely to convert than one who waits 24 hours.

## Real Examples
- **Dentist (Cut 30 grad):** First video ever — rating whitening toothpastes in dentist chair. Approaching 1M views.
- **Baker in Vermont (Cut 30 grad):** Ranking premium baking ingredients. Went viral using the same content type.
- **Roofing company (Cut 30 grad):** Rankings content on roof siding. Then proactively DMed commenters from their geographic area and converted social attention to roofing sales calls.
- **Name specialist Scott:** Low-follower account in a narrow niche (brand naming), breaking down Ben Stiller's new soda company name. High top-of-funnel reach from current events angle.
- **Josh (meta ads consultant):** Giving away the complete Facebook ads funnel in a video. People who watch and still want help become leads — proving expertise converts more than gatekeeping it.
- **Ken Sakata / Front Office:** Fashion history storytelling (not his own product) builds credibility that converts when he releases his own items. Expert perspective combined with student framing.
- **Fotic (fragrance):** Filmed in front of his fragrance collection as his recurring set. The collection itself signals credibility before he speaks.

## Pitfalls to Avoid
- **CTA before traction:** Adding "buy now" or "link in bio" before an account has momentum kills distribution. Build the audience first.
- **Scheduling apps:** They hurt organic reach. Post manually every time.
- **Trends:** Require same-day execution. For 99% of brands, not worth attempting.
- **Faceless content:** In 2026, human presence on camera is no longer optional for content that converts. Carousels can be faceless; video should have a person.
- **Setting up accounts in the wrong country:** The algorithm serves content based on where the account was created. An account set up while on vacation in another country will target that country's audience.
- **Guru hacks and platform tricks:** 2-second videos, reposting TikToks to stories, specific audio exploits — all distractions. None of this compounds into business value.

## Key Insight: Content Is Not Ephemeral
Short-form content is widely assumed to die in 24–48 hours. For value-driven content this is false. Instagram Reels now surface in Google search results, seasonal topics resurge each time their industry event recurs (Canton Fair example), and the algorithm retests content against new audiences months later. A video that gets 600 views in week one should be placed in the welcome email, product page, and post-call nurture flow — it is a permanent funnel asset, not a one-day campaign.
