# Strategy: Viral Organic Content Formats — The Complete Taxonomy

## Summary

Orin John's taxonomy of organic content formats that consistently perform across TikTok and Instagram. These are not trends — they are stable formats that have earned repeat engagement across years and categories. The question for any brand or creator is which formats map to their message library and which they are willing to produce consistently.

---

## The 10 Core Formats

### 1. Yapping (People Talking On Camera)
The most effective organic format in 2025. Someone talking directly to camera with conviction.

**Variations:**
| Sub-format | Description |
|---|---|
| **Notebook yap** | Talking through a list written in an open notebook |
| **Object yap** | Handling a product while speaking — demonstrates and explains simultaneously |
| **Car yap** | Filmed in a vehicle; intimacy of the enclosed space, reduced distraction |
| **Walking yap** | Movement-based; energy of forward motion carries the message |
| **Graphic yap** | Overlaid graphics reinforce spoken points; works for data and frameworks |

**Why it works:** Direct eye contact, conversational register, low production barrier. The format is the anti-corporate signal — it reads as genuine.

---

### 2. Rankings and Ratings
Expert opinions applied to a product or service category. A dermatologist ranking sunscreens by actual protection value. A chef ranking grocery store olive oils. A financial planner ranking savings account rates.

**Why it works:** Combines authority with utility. The audience gets a shortcut. The brand or creator gets positioned as the expert who knows the category better than the audience.

**Best for:** Brands with genuine expertise in their category. Credibility amplifies the format.

---

### 3. Definitive Cultural Statements
An assertive, confident claim about a trend or cultural moment. "The algorithm prefers blondes." "Silent luxury is already dead." "GLP-1 is changing the fitness industry."

**Why it works:** Generates engagement without needing to be controversial. The statement is strong enough to provoke response (agreement, disagreement, addition) without attacking anyone. Comments write themselves.

**Best for:** Brands and creators with a distinct POV. Weak when the statement is hedged.

---

### 4. Credible Explainers
An authority figure explaining a topic, with credentials visually present — a doctor in scrubs, a lawyer at their desk, an engineer in a hard hat. The environment signals authority before the first word.

**Why it works:** Audiences are actively searching for "the real version" of professional knowledge — not the watered-down TV version. A specialist explaining exactly what they do professionally taps into an audience that has no alternative. (See [[content-positioning-pyramid]] — the knee doctor problem.)

**Best for:** Brands and creators with genuine domain expertise. Works even with zero followers if the niche is underserved.

---

### 5. Unpopular Opinions
"You shouldn't be paying more for [premium option] vs [budget option]." "The gym doesn't actually matter." "Therapy isn't for everyone."

**Why it works:** States something the audience already suspects but hasn't seen said plainly. The relief of having a suppressed belief named out loud is the engagement driver. Comments flood in with both agreement and pushback — both count.

**Best for:** Brands and creators who have done the research to back the claim. Unpopular opinions without data are just provocations.

---

### 6. Provocative Scenarios
A fictional situation that illustrates a real, emotionally charged point. "Recently divorced couple — one earning $21M, one earning $48K. Here's their financial plan." "A 22-year-old inheriting $500K today..."

**Why it works:** Specificity and stakes. The scenario is concrete enough to be felt, fictional enough to be safe. The audience can project themselves into it.

**Best for:** Finance, insurance, healthcare, legal — high-stakes categories where direct claims are regulated but scenarios are not.

---

### 7. Transformation Content
Before/after narratives. The product, the process, or the person — before and after. Results shown, not described.

**Why it works:** The gap between before and after is the value proposition made visual. Works in any category with a measurable outcome: skincare, fitness, interior design, software, finances.

**Best for:** Any brand where the product produces visible results.

---

### 8. Testimonials
Real customers documenting real experiences. The oldest format in marketing — still effective when authentic.

**Why it works:** Social proof at the point of discovery. The audience sees someone like them, not a brand talking about itself.

**Best for:** Products with genuine results. Testimonials for underwhelming products make the problem worse.

---

### 9. Tutorials
Step-by-step how-to content addressing actual product usage. Not "here's what our product does" — "here's exactly how to use it to get this outcome."

**Why it works:** Utility is shareable. Save rates are highest on tutorials. People return to them. They drive discovery for people searching for the outcome.

**Best for:** Any product with a learning curve, a process, or a result that requires setup.

---

### 10. Reaction Videos
Responding to another piece of content (a news story, a competitor's ad, another creator's take) with commentary. The original content provides the hook; your response provides the value.

**Why it works:** Piggybacks on existing context. The audience already knows the setup — you skip straight to the take. Works especially well for brands with a strong POV on their category.

---

## Format Selection by Goal

| Goal | Best Formats |
|---|---|
| Awareness / reach | Definitive cultural statements, unpopular opinions, yapping |
| Authority / credibility | Credible explainers, rankings, tutorials |
| Conversion / purchase intent | Transformation, testimonials, provocative scenarios |
| Engagement / comments | Unpopular opinions, rankings, reaction videos |
| Saves / shares | Tutorials, rankings, credible explainers |

---

## Industry Content Ideas (Quick Reference)

| Industry | Formats That Work |
|---|---|
| Finance / insurance | Provocative scenarios, unpopular opinions, credible explainers |
| Utilities / energy | Unpopular opinions ("You shouldn't pay more for X"), rankings |
| Furniture / home | Transformation, tutorials (room sizing, small space solutions) |
| Wellness / health | Credible explainers, transformation, walking yaps |
| Fashion / beauty | Definitive cultural statements, rankings, testimonials |
| B2B / SaaS | Credible explainers, notebooks yaps, tutorials |

"No industry is unsexy" — every category has compelling angles when run through the right format.

---

## The Testing Rule

Run four content variations over six weeks. Keep the message constant, change the concept format. Compare performance. Use data to override subjective preference. "They hate money" — brands that refuse to test formats they personally dislike leave performance on the table.
