# Strategy: Marketing Plan Framework 2026

## Summary
A five-exercise workshopping system for building an annual or quarterly marketing plan, developed through live sessions with 40+ brands across industries. The framework moves through diagnosing your current funnel, auditing every possible marketing initiative, mapping content to the funnel, planning asset repurposing, and building a year-long calendar — helping teams prioritize what to keep, what to add, and what to stop.

## When to Use This
- Annual or quarterly planning cycles
- When a brand has hit a revenue ceiling (often around $30–50M) and needs to expand beyond measurable, direct-response tactics
- When a team feels overwhelmed by too many marketing options and can't prioritize
- When leadership approval is blocking new initiatives and you need a framework to justify decisions

## The Framework / Steps

### Exercise 1: Marketing Funnel Audit
Map everything you currently do across three funnel stages:
- **Top of Funnel (Awareness):** What generates attention? Does it actually work?
- **Middle of Funnel (Consideration/Education):** Email automations, educational content, brand affinity content, community. Are you converting attention into interest?
- **Bottom of Funnel (Conversion):** SMS, email, sales reps, Instagram Stories, broadcast channels, retargeting. Most brands underinvest here — they get attention but don't ask for the sale hard enough.

Grade each stage honestly (A–F). Wide-funnel businesses (mass market products) need to minimize drop-off at every stage. Narrow-funnel businesses (B2B, enterprise software) need hyper-targeted reach but the same logic applies.

### Exercise 2: The Normie-to-Fringe / Sales-to-Awareness Brand Options Chart
Plot every possible marketing initiative on two axes:
- **X-axis:** Normie (easy to get approved, low risk) → Fringe (bleeding edge, requires buy-in)
- **Y-axis:** Sales-focused → Awareness-focused

**Normie Sales:** Wholesale, email marketing, Google Ads, Meta Ads, YouTube Ads, cold outbound
**Fringe Sales:** Creators on accounts, TikTok Ads, product seeding, affiliate programs, TikTok Shop, SMS, AppLovin, live selling
**Normie Awareness:** LinkedIn posting, trade shows, organic Instagram/TikTok, influencer
**Fringe Awareness:** Organic YouTube, X, multi-account social strategy, Snap, streaming

After mapping, identify 1–2 new initiatives to add. Most teams — even large ones — can only execute 2 new things well in a 6-month period.

### Exercise 3: Content Funnel Mapping
Apply the marketing funnel to your content specifically:
- Define your **content pillars** (3–5 recurring formats/topics)
- Map each pillar to a funnel stage — does this content drive awareness, education, or conversion?
- Grade each pillar vs. your niche and vs. general quality (A–F)
- Assign owners: who on the team is actually responsible for executing each?

### Exercise 4: Asset Repurposing System
Build a tracking system for existing assets — content from creators/influencers, product photos, UGC, videos. Ask: are they being used on product detail pages? In emails? In paid ads? Many brands create assets and use them once. Repurposing is high-leverage for teams with limited budget.

### Exercise 5: The Year Planning Calendar
Build a matrix with rows for: Culture moments, Industry events, Sales heat map (from Shopify or CRM data), Social performance history, Planned launches, and Missed opportunities from last year.
- Mark each month with what's happening externally (Super Bowl, holidays, fashion weeks, industry shows)
- Color-code sales data: green = exceeded goal, red = missed (note *why*)
- Pre-plan launches around cultural moments
- Capture missed opportunities from Slack/Teams so they don't get missed again

For campaigns: plan both internal (owned channels, email, content team) and external (creators, media, seeding) with roughly 50/50 emphasis.

## Real Examples
- **Airlines and activewear companies** used the funnel audit to discover they were generating plenty of top-of-funnel awareness but failing to close sales
- **Enterprise software firm** (seven-figure contracts) realized their narrow funnel still followed the same logic — they needed ultra-targeted awareness, not just more cold outbound
- **Creator example (the presenter):** Product review videos = top of funnel; marketing education content = middle; brand deals and email list CTAs = bottom

## Pitfalls to Avoid
- **Only focusing on sales-measurable tactics:** Once you hit a revenue ceiling (often $30–50M), purely measurable tactics plateau. Awareness investment is required to scale, even though it can't be measured the same way as Meta ROAS.
- **Adding too many new initiatives:** More than 2 new things at once causes all of them to fail. Prioritize ruthlessly.
- **Ignoring bottom-of-funnel:** Most brands over-invest in awareness and under-invest in closing. If you're getting attention but not converting, fix the bottom before adding more top-of-funnel.
- **Planning without assigning owners:** Great content plans die when no one is named responsible.
- **Not noting why you hit or missed goals:** Without that analysis, you can't replicate wins or avoid repeating mistakes.
