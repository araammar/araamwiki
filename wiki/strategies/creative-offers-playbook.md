# Strategy: Creative Offers Playbook

## Summary
Most creatives lose money not because their work is bad but because they fail to translate their skills into structured, purchasable offers. This playbook covers how to move from "I do all kinds of design" to a specific, productized service with a clear price, deliverable, and timeline — then how to stack those offers into a scalable business. The underlying mindset shift is equally important: creatives have been systematically conditioned by agency culture and counter-commercial art movements to feel guilty about charging what they're worth.

## When to Use This
- You're a freelance creative struggling to get consistent client work or unsure what to charge
- You're transitioning from agency employee to independent operator
- Your portfolio website exists but generates zero inbound leads
- You want to stop doing custom quote-by-quote work and create repeatable service packages
- You're ready to grow beyond one-off projects into a scalable service business

## The Framework / Steps

### Step 1: Overcome the Money Psychology Block
Understand that the "creatives don't talk about money" mindset was manufactured by agency structures designed to keep you underpaid. Large agencies (Omnicom, Saatchi, Havas) silo creatives into narrow roles precisely so the organization captures the value — not you. Platforms like TikTok Shop and Meta Ads now quantify the dollar value of creative work directly. You can see what your video made. Use that data to anchor your self-worth.

### Step 2: Build Your Core Offer
Fill in this sentence: **"I do X for Y at Z."**
- I make landing pages for e-commerce businesses for $1,500 per page
- I do brand identities for tech companies for $5,000 in 2 weeks
- I do brand sprints (5K for icons, illustrations, polished UI screens) in 10 business days

Your offer must have:
- A specific deliverable (not "design services")
- A niche/client type (not "anyone who needs design")
- A price or price range (visible, not "DM for rates")
- A timeline (predictability matters to buyers)

### Step 3: Choose Your Differentiation
You can only win on one or two of three axes — never all three:
- **Better** — superior quality/skill. Only viable if you're objectively at the top. Be honest.
- **Faster** — speed as the value prop. Brand sprints, 48-hour turnarounds, week-long deliveries.
- **Cheaper** — appropriate when building reps and portfolio. Don't stay here forever.

### Step 4: Build a Funnel Around Your Offer
Your website needs a clear journey:
- **Landing page** — clearly displays the offer, price, and timeline
- **VSL (Video Sales Letter)** — an 8–15 minute video embedded on the page that walks through: the problem you solve, your background, how you bridge the gap, what it looks like to buy from you
- **Call to action** — book a call, buy now, or join a waitlist depending on offer complexity
- **Email capture** — collect leads who aren't ready to buy yet; communicate to them until they close

### Step 5: Stack Your Offers
**Offer stacking** means combining skills so the package is worth more than its parts:
- Videographer + Meta ads knowledge + VSL scripting = performance creative service
- Brand strategist + creator skills + production direction = high-premium campaign operator

Build skill stacks deliberately. Every secondary skill you add expands what you can charge and who you can serve. A videographer who learned to run Meta ads can now sell end-to-end video-to-performance packages to service businesses.

### Step 6: Manage Your Pipeline with CRM
Store all leads, past clients, and non-converted inquiries in a CRM. Email them when you launch new offers, have capacity, or want referrals. Most creative freelancers have a goldmine of contacts they never follow up with. The combination of funnel + offer + email list = a real services business, not just project-to-project survival.

## Real Examples
- **Brand identity designer**: "$15,000 brand in 4 weeks, $5,000 strategy-only tier" — clearly listed on Framer site with Figma file preview showing exactly what the deliverable looks like
- **B2B SaaS design studio**: "$10,000 for brand + website, 3–4 weeks, B2B SaaS only" — hyper-niched, removes ambiguity
- **Landing page specialist**: One product only — landing pages. Did not expand beyond that. Became the go-to person for any landing page need
- **Illustration sprint**: "5K for 10 icons, 5 custom illustrations, 2–3 UI screens in 10 days" — productized, time-boxed, specific

## Pitfalls to Avoid
- **Listing "services" instead of an offer** — vague service lists with no prices force potential clients to contact you just to understand what you do, which most won't
- **Pricing by time instead of value** — hourly rates cap your income and commoditize your work
- **Trying to be better, faster, AND cheaper** — you'll get crushed; pick one, maybe two
- **Staying cheap forever** — cheapness as a short-term positioning to build reps is smart; as a long-term strategy, it's a trap
- **Skipping the VSL** — some clients won't read your page but will watch a video; you lose those sales by not having one
- **No CRM** — every lead who didn't buy is future revenue you're leaving on the table
