# Strategy: DTC Allocator of Capital Framework

## Summary

Taylor Holiday's integrated operating strategy for DTC brands in 2026. The core identity shift: stop thinking like a marketer and start thinking like an allocator of capital. Every dollar in the business competes for the highest return — not just ad spend vs. organic, but ads vs. inventory vs. hiring vs. product development. The brands that survive the current era are not the best at marketing; they are the best at capital discipline.

---

## The Identity Shift

**Marketer mentality (fragile):**
- Optimizes within a given budget
- Reports on channel metrics: ROAS, CTR, CPC
- Success = campaign performance
- Question: "What ad works best?"

**Allocator of Capital mentality (durable):**
- Questions whether the budget itself is allocated optimally
- Reports on contribution margin, CAC, LTV, and cash flow
- Success = business outcomes
- Question: "Where does the next dollar generate the highest return — ads, inventory, product, people, or paid-off debt?"

The distinction matters most at the $1–10M stage, when the same person is often responsible for both marketing execution and capital decisions. If that person is thinking like a marketer, they're leaving significant business value on the table.

## Capital Allocation Decision Tree

When you have a dollar to deploy, run it through this sequence:

**1. Does the business have first-order profitability?**
If not (contribution margin is negative on first purchase), fix product economics before adding any growth capital. No amount of scale fixes a broken unit economic.

**2. Is the active customer file growing?**
If not, new customer acquisition has priority over any other use of capital — even if retention looks efficient.

**3. What is the current Creative Demand Score?**
If below 50, creative production bottleneck is capping paid performance. Capital into creative production may return more than capital into ad spend.

**4. What does Four Quarter Accounting show?**
Which quarter is over-indexed? If CAC is at 40% (vs. 25% target), the constraint is acquisition cost — not spend level. If OPEX is at 35%, the constraint is overhead — adding ad spend won't fix it.

**5. What does the Prophit forecast say?**
Board / Budget / Bonus scenarios show where you are relative to expectations. Capital deployment should track toward the Budget scenario; stretch capital goes toward Bonus only when foundation is stable.

## The Contribution Margin North Star

Every capital decision traces back to contribution margin:

```
Net Sales − Cost of Delivery − Ad Spend = Contribution Margin
```

This is the only metric that matters at the business level. Build dashboards where daily CM is visible. Decisions that improve CM are the right decisions; decisions that degrade CM require an explicit long-term rationale (new customer acquisition at a short-term loss is acceptable if the 60-day LTV math supports it).

**First-Order Profitability (2026 standard):**
The goal is to be profitable on the first purchase — not reliant on repurchase to achieve unit economics. Capital is too expensive in 2026 to finance extended payback windows at the 7-figure level.

---

## Survival vs. Growth Mode

Two distinct operating postures:

**Survival Mode (Squeeze the Sponge):**
- Triggered when: CM negative, revenue declining, cash reserves < 3 months
- Actions: Reduce non-performing ad spend, maximize existing customer revenue via email/SMS, right-size OPEX
- Goal: Stop the bleeding, preserve cash, stabilize operations
- Duration: 60–90 days maximum — survival mode is not a strategy

**Growth Mode (Restart the Engine):**
- Triggered when: CM positive, cash reserves stable, active customer file growth metrics in check
- Actions: Invest in new customer acquisition, scale creative production, expand product architecture methodically
- Goal: Grow active customer file faster than churn
- Principle: Accept temporary MER degradation to acquire customers — the sponge must grow

---

## The Benchmarks That Signal Growth Mode Is Safe

Before shifting from survival to growth mode, confirm:
- Contribution margin is positive on first-order
- 30% increase in 60-day customer LTV vs. baseline (brand health signal)
- OPEX < 25% of revenue (lean enough to sustain growth investment)
- Landed margin > 70% (product economics support the four quarters)
- At least 30–40% of traffic is organic (not 100% dependent on paid for demand generation)

---

## What to Steal

The single most actionable shift: **calculate contribution margin daily.** Not monthly. Not in a quarterly finance review. Daily. The team that knows their CM every morning makes different decisions than the team that finds out in the board meeting. Build the dashboard, track the number, make it the culture.
