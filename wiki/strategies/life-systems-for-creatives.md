# Strategy: Life Systems for Creatives

## What This Is For

This strategy is for creatives who feel chaotic — who have ideas they can't track, forget things that matter, can't reliably finish what they start, feel like they're not using their time well, or have tried to build systems but abandoned them. The host frames a full "systems year" as one of the five foundational year-types (alongside money, rebrand, mastery, and obsession) because: "there has to be a point in time where you turn a corner, and it takes a while, and that is the system year."

These are not abstract productivity principles. They are the specific tools, structures, and workflows the host uses and teaches, described with implementation detail.

---

## The Framework

### The 11 Systems

---

#### System 1: The Reference Repository

**What it is:** A database of everything you find, admire, or might use in your creative work — organized so you can actually retrieve it when you need it.

**Why it matters:** Every creative is constantly gathering references but has nowhere to put them that makes them retrievable. A reference library that you can search and filter is one of the highest-leverage long-term investments you can make.

**How to build it:**
- Tool: Notion (preferred) or a spreadsheet
- Structure: Name of asset, type (carousel example, video style, event content, email, product-specific, etc.), link to the asset, status (reference / used / winner), notes
- Sunday night ritual: Take 30 minutes to pull bookmarks from your browser, X, Instagram, TikTok — label them and add them to the database
- Add a type for every category of work you do. The host has separate types for events, emails, carousels, launches, product content, personal brand, expert content

**The compound effect:** "As soon as you start building this library and adding regularly to it, all of a sudden that time goes so much faster." When you need to brief a campaign or apply for a job, you pull up your matrix and filter — "Let's pull up all my event examples. Let's walk through how we could use this" — and you come across as someone most other creatives aren't.

---

#### System 2: The Ideas List

**What it is:** A single, massive document holding every creative idea you've had — with a calendar view attached.

**Why it matters:** Ideas are perishable if not captured. The ideas list is the difference between "I had a great idea once" and being able to recall, schedule, and act on any idea from the last year.

**How to build it:**
- Tool: Notion (or any document with database/table capability)
- Structure: Bullet-list dump of all ideas by type (carousel ideas, video ideas, one-shot ideas, event ideas, etc.) — do not filter or cull, just capture
- Add a calendar/table view: name, date to publish, type (video/ad/carousel), status (idea/edit/record), editor assigned
- The list and calendar together take you "from no organization to some organization"

**Entry-level version:** Open a new doc, add all your text ideas, turn the table into a Notion database, and you have a functional system. Start here before anything more complex.

---

#### System 3: Content Folders on Your Phone

**What it is:** A folder structure in your iPhone Photos that organizes footage by type as you shoot it.

**Why it matters:** For anyone who shoots content day-to-day, the phone camera roll becomes unnavigable quickly. Finding a specific shot takes minutes that add up to hours.

**The four-folder structure:**
- **Me** — all video you are in (for carousels, B-roll, talking-head content)
- **People** — footage of other individuals
- **Places** — establishing shots, facades, environmental/location footage
- **Things** — products, food, packaging, objects

**Travel variation:** Create a single album per trip, or sub-albums with the same People/Places/Things structure.

**The heart hack:** Heart or favorite any shot that could be a hook. This creates an instantly accessible folder of your best material, sorted out from the noise.

**The habit:** Whenever you get in an Uber, take 5 minutes to organize that day's footage into the four folders. "So whenever I'm getting videos of any kind, I can easily know if I can grab more footage of me, or I'm trying to find that packaging, or I need a quick shot of a China trip — it's all there."

---

#### System 4: The Weekly Update

**What it is:** A short email sent at the end of every week to clients, bosses, or collaborators summarizing what happened and what comes next.

**Why it matters:** The weekly update protects you from the single biggest source of professional friction — the other person not knowing what you've done. "Will save you so much heartache, especially if you do freelance or client work."

**The structure (TL;DR at top, detail below):**
1. **TL;DR** — 2-3 highlights in one sentence each
2. **What got accomplished this week** — a checklist of completed items
3. **What's happening next week** — the plan
4. **Blockers** — anything holding you back, anything that didn't go as expected, anything that needs to be escalated ("I still haven't received that doc. Jenny still hasn't gotten this.")

**Why the blockers section is the most important part:** It puts problems on record without requiring a separate conversation. "Whoever client you have knows what you've done, knows what their expectation is next week, knows if they or their team is blocking you from getting things done."

**If you manage a team:** Ask everyone to send you this same structure weekly. Compile their blockers to present to clients or executives. "You're beginning to level all of that up."

---

#### System 5: The Team Tracker

**What it is:** A shared database that tracks every task assigned to every team member, with status, due date, assignee, and link to the deliverable.

**Why it matters:** Without a tracker, creative teams run on verbal agreements and Slack messages that get lost. Every question about "where is X" or "who's doing Y" that gets asked without a tracker is wasted time.

**How to build it (Notion / ClickUp):**
- Database columns: Name of task, assignee, status, due date, link to final deliverable
- For request-based work: add a form on the front end where people submit requests. Managers review and assign. Requesters can see the queue position and due date.
- Use Notion AI to auto-generate weekly summaries: "What's gotten done, what remains, who's assigned to what"

**The host's agency experience:** Managing 4 graphic designers and 3 video team members, with constant complaints about asset turnaround. After implementing the tracker: "Everyone feels better about that process." Key metrics available: 21 assets done this week, 11 for your team, 7 for yours, queue size, ability to reprioritize.

---

#### System 6: Process Docs

**What it is:** A document for every recurring task you want someone else to be able to do — with a Loom video walkthrough, step-by-step breakdown, and FAQ.

**Why it matters:** "Most people assume they can just hire someone and that person will do it all for them. But the more process you have in place, the better off it's going to go."

**How to build one:**
1. Record a Loom (screen-share video, no production value needed) walking through the task as you do it
2. Transcribe the Loom → paste into ChatGPT → ask for a step-by-step breakdown → review and edit
3. Add a checklist of the specific quality standards that must be met (for a carousel: "Core text fits within the square. Photo inside each. Readable at explore page size. Two variants created.")
4. Add a Figma board or folder of existing examples for visual reference
5. As you onboard multiple people, add a FAQ section at the bottom — every question that comes up gets added, so you never answer it twice

"Anytime a new designer comes on, we can add that into it." If your editors or assistants haven't worked out, "it's usually not the editor. It's usually that you don't have enough process and documentation in place."

---

#### System 7: The Calendar (The Systems Year Foundation)

**What it is:** Using a calendar — Google Calendar, Apple Calendar — not just for external commitments but as a personal architecture tool.

**Why it matters:** Most creatives use a calendar reactively (for appointments that come to them). Using it proactively — to design how your time is spent before the week starts — is the difference between a chaotic week and an intentional one.

**Implementation:**
- Pick a planning day: Friday, Saturday, or Sunday
- Every week on that day, block out: mandatory obligations, personal life tasks (laundry, appointments), and your goal-work time blocks
- For each block: estimate how long it takes, put it in the calendar, and block it off
- "Realistically look at what time do I have, what's my extra time, and can I accomplish all these things and where are my trade-offs?"
- Look ahead at weird weeks, vacations, travel — plan how the priority work gets done regardless

"When you start doing that every week and you actually mark it down — this is what I'm supposed to do — and you stick to it cuz it's your systems year and you're acting on improving yourself, you start to see a major shift."

---

#### System 8: The Task Tracking System

**What it is:** A single place to track everything you need to do — with checkboxes.

**Why it matters:** Without a single trusted capture system, tasks live in your head where they create cognitive load and get forgotten. The checkbox provides a dopamine hit that reinforces the behavior.

**Implementation:**
- Pick one tool and only one: Apple Notes, Notion, Evernote, physical notebook — whatever feels easiest
- Checkboxes are non-negotiable: "There's something satisfying and dopamine-providing about checking that box, whether it's on an app or you have a written thing in your house that says it's done"
- Everything that needs to happen gets added here — not into your head

The combination of calendar (when) + task list (what) is the core operating system of a creative who has their life together.

---

#### System 9: The Weekly Communication Habit

**What it is:** A personal or professional journaling practice that tracks what happened, what you learned, and what comes next.

**Why it matters:** Without reflection, weeks blur together and patterns are invisible. With it, you develop a longitudinal view of your own progress that makes the next decision easier.

**Three forms (pick one):**
- **Professional:** Send the weekly update (System 4) to your boss or clients — this doubles as personal reflection
- **Entrepreneurial:** Send a weekly update to your partner: "Here's what we're working on, here's what's coming next"
- **Personal:** Journal: "Here's what I got done this week. Here's my plan for next week."

"Those three things together — calendar, task tracking, and communication — with when acted on for 52 weeks straight, turn you from a chaotic person to an action person."

---

#### System 10: The Signature Series

**What it is:** One repeating creative format that you return to consistently — a fixed structure, visual style, or recurring concept that becomes something people associate with you.

**Why it matters:** One-off creative work builds nothing in the minds of your audience or clients. A signature series builds recognition, expectation, and portfolio depth in a way that scattered output cannot. The host cites his own "Product vs. Brand" review series and a student ("David") who built a "Behind the Brand" format with a consistent visual style and structure. "You can see him going from talking about all these different things to: oh, I found my thing."

**Implementation:**
- Ask: what is the most effective thing I've done? Can I serialize it?
- Define the repeating elements: visual style, structure, length, subject type
- Commit to it: one per week or one per month
- "If I was only to make one thing a week, how can I have that signature, that unique thing to me, and then actually act on it?"

---

#### System 11: The Content Pillar System (with Monthly Measurement)

**What it is:** A structured approach to creative output organized around 3-5 recurring content types (pillars), each measured monthly to track performance and drive improvement decisions.

**Why it matters:** Without measurement, creative decisions are taste-based and arbitrary. With a pillar tracker, every change has a before/after and every decision can be defended.

**Pillars:** A pillar is a concept + a format. "Talking about food" is a concept. "Food" + "carousel" is a pillar. Good creatives and brands have 3-5 pillars at any given time.

**Monthly measurement (per pillar):**
- Views per month
- Average views per post
- Saves
- Shares
- Reach to new vs. existing audiences
- Hook rate

**The decision matrix (monthly):**
- If a pillar's metrics are going up → keep going as-is
- If steady state → choose one thing to improve (hooks, production value, format variation) and test for a month
- If declining → choose to improve one specific variable or replace the pillar entirely

"Within 3 months of being like, 'we're going to change hooks, we're going to evaluate it the entire next month' — your social media will be in extreme better shape." The system makes every creative choice defensible: "This wasn't working, so we changed this thing. This was working, so we kept going."

## Common Mistakes

**Trying to implement all 11 at once.** "Overall, I cannot encourage you enough to just pick one thing." The host's explicit recommendation: choose the single system that would have the most impact on your creative life right now, implement it for several months, and add the next one after it's habit.

**Choosing a tool that feels impressive but doesn't fit how you work.** The task system, the ideas list, the reference repository — all of these are only valuable if you actually use them. "Whatever's easy for you — you have to pick one thing."

**Building the system without the ritual.** A reference repository without a Sunday-night adding ritual becomes stale within weeks. Every system needs a recurring practice time attached to it.

**Treating the process doc as bureaucracy.** Process docs feel like overhead until the first time they save you from re-explaining something you've explained 10 times. They are the infrastructure that makes delegation possible.

**Skipping the systems year and going straight to obsession.** "If you don't have momentum, you don't know what you want to do — you've got to build your systems. You've got to save for wherever you want to go. This is not the year for you." Systems are a prerequisite, not a nice-to-have.

## What Success Looks Like

- You know where everything is: footage, ideas, references, tasks, past work
- Clients and collaborators rarely have to chase you for status updates
- You are able to delegate recurring tasks because you have documented them
- You are tracking whether your creative work is actually improving month over month, with data
- You have a signature thing — one repeating creative format that people associate with you
- "Those three things together — calendar, task tracking, and communication — with when acted on for 52 weeks straight, turn you from a chaotic person to an action person"
