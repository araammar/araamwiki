# Strategy: LinkedIn Brand Playbook

## Summary

LinkedIn is the most underused B2B credibility engine available to consumer brands and creators. This playbook covers the operational brand-side strategies: how to use milestone cheerleading to build buyer credibility, how to assign The Steve Role to get personal-page reach without founder burnout, how to use the founder-as-distributor model for retail growth, how to give employees a sustainable content lens, and when brand accounts are worth running. These are distinct from content tactics — for hook formats and post DNA, see [[linkedin-post-dna]]; for algorithm mechanics, see [[linkedin-content-strategy-2026]].

---

## The Cheerleading Strategy (Chlorophyll Water)

**Case study: Matt, founder of Chlorophyll Water (in Sprouts, Whole Foods)**

The strategy is consistent, high-frequency documentation of every brand milestone — from the very beginning of the brand. Every new retail location. Every store visit with the product in frame. Every distribution win, every buyer meeting, every moment of forward motion.

What cheerleading looks like in practice:
- Post every time the product enters a new retail location
- Document every store visit with the product visible in the post
- Maintain high posting frequency throughout the brand's growth
- Keep the energy personal and founder-driven — not polished brand content

Why it works on LinkedIn specifically:
- B2B buyers (retail buyers, distributors, category managers, foodservice partners) are active on LinkedIn
- Consistent momentum posts compound into a credibility signal — the brand appears healthy, growing, and real
- Each post reinforces the narrative of a brand that is moving. Retail buyers who see it repeatedly develop familiarity before any sales conversation begins
- Social proof for buyers accumulates silently — a brand page full of store wins reads as a track record

The starting principle: cheerleading must begin from day one. Brands that only start posting LinkedIn milestones after they're already well-distributed miss the compounding benefit of having documented the entire journey.

## The Steve Role

**What it is:** A named, assignable function inside every brand — a non-founder employee (marketer, ops lead, community manager, or anyone) who acts as the brand's unofficial LinkedIn correspondent, posting ALL brand content across channels to their personal LinkedIn page.

**What The Steve does:**
1. Curates content from the brand's Instagram, YouTube, email campaigns, influencer deals, retail photos, street team content, collabs — everything
2. Reposts or reworks it for their personal LinkedIn page, often before the brand account even cross-posts it
3. Builds a personal industry network through trade shows, conferences, and direct LinkedIn connections in the brand's sector
4. Becomes the industry's primary source of brand awareness — more informed and more engaging than the brand's own email list or social channels

"People knew Steve before they even knew the brand. They knew about the stories that Steve told and about brand lore in a way that if even if you were on our email list or follow our social media, you probably wouldn't keep up with." — Orin John

**Key distinction — consumer content, not just business content.** The Steve Role is not about posting case studies and professional updates. Steve posts consumer content: influencer deals, product collabs, campaign launches, fun brand moments. This makes the brand feel alive and exciting through a personal account with built-in industry credibility.

**Why a person outperforms a brand page:** Personal profiles get approximately 13x more reach than brand accounts on LinkedIn (see [[linkedin-content-strategy-2026]]). The Steve Role is the structural solution — it moves brand content through a personal account that the algorithm trusts and that industry contacts find relatable.

**Who fills the role:** Any non-founder employee who is actively networked in the brand's industry. If no employee is available or suited for it, the role defaults to the founder. "If there's no one around to do it, then it's probably you." — Orin John

## Founder-as-Distributor (Corell Water)

**Case study: Corell Water founder (CPG / beverage brand)**

The model: a founder — not a marketing team, not a brand account — posts every single retail milestone as a consistent LinkedIn content stream. New store placements. Charity drops. Buyer visits. Distribution wins. Not high-production content — just updates, a few photos, and tags of the relevant people and locations.

**The mechanism that makes it work:**
Tagging buyers, stores, and industry contacts triggers LinkedIn's network-expansion logic. People in professionally adjacent roles — retail buyers, category managers, distributors — are algorithmically surfaced to the content even when a post gets zero likes. Over years of consistent posting, this compounds into exponential retail network growth.

"Doesn't matter how many likes you get, you can get zero likes. If you are building your network and you're connecting with people on there, if you're tagging with people that work in legitimate roles, like buying at whatever store, you're tagging in on these chains, the way that LinkedIn works and shows content means that other people in that industry are going to be aware and are going to find out." — Orin John

**Why vanity metrics are irrelevant here:** LinkedIn's interest graph propagates tagged content to professionally adjacent people — precisely the B2B buyers a CPG or physical goods founder needs. Zero likes on a post does not mean zero reach among the people who matter.

"Him just relentlessly posting multiple times a week, every single business goal and telling that story has probably led to an exponential increase in the retail success of his brand." — Orin John

**Applies to:** CPG and food & beverage brands, physical goods brands, any founder selling into retail or B2B distribution channels.

**Content type:** Text updates and a few photos. Not video. Not produced content. Just consistent milestone documentation with strategic tagging.

## The Lens Framework

**What it is:** A "lens" is a fixed expertise filter through which a professional analyzes any external event in their industry. Every professional already has one — the job is naming it and applying it consistently as a content engine.

**How it works:**
1. Identify your professional lens (art director, meta ads buyer, copywriter, category manager, salesperson, videographer, accountant, analyst, brand strategist)
2. When anything notable happens in your industry — a new campaign launches, a brand makes a move, a product releases, an industry report drops — filter it through your lens
3. Post: "Here's what happened. Here's what I see through my [expertise] lens. Here's why it's notable/good/wrong."
4. This is not about building a personal brand or separate identity — it is demonstrating professional competence applied to real events

**Example in practice:**
"Road launches a new campaign. Hey, this new Road campaign launched. I looked at their two meta ads and this is the ones I think are the best and this is why they did it well. That is a lens that you get to apply." — Orin John

Lens types: art direction, photography, copywriting, meta ads, sales, accounting, analytics, videography, brand strategy, operations — every function has a lens.

**Why the Lens Framework matters for brands:**
- Permanently solves "what do I post?" for employees — industry events are infinite content prompts
- Positions the poster as a practitioner and expert without requiring personal brand ego investment
- Provides a socially acceptable reason to post ("I'm sharing industry learning") that neutralizes the "posting on LinkedIn now?" embarrassment many employees feel
- Generates commentary-style content, which aligns with LinkedIn's preference for substantive, genuine engagement
- The lens approach makes content about the industry, not purely about the individual — lower ego investment, more sustainable long-term

**For brands:** The Lens Framework is how you turn any employee into a content asset. Each team member has a distinct lens. Art directors analyze campaigns. Buyers analyze retail moves. Marketers analyze brand strategy. Encourage each person to apply their lens and the brand gains multiple distinct voices amplifying its industry presence.

## Social-First Onboarding

**The protocol:** Before posting any content, spend 3–5 weeks being social on LinkedIn first.

Specific actions:
- Make 3–5 genuine, substantive comments per week on others' content
- Connect with relevant people in your industry and engage with their content
- Have actual conversations — not AI-generated responses, not "great post," but real reactions and perspectives

**Why it works:** LinkedIn algorithmically tracks how social you are on the platform — how many people you connect with, how many real conversations you have — and uses this as a variable in how far your content distributes when you do start posting. Being social first primes the algorithm. Posting cold into a dormant account produces weaker results than posting into an account that is already socially active.

"How social you are on LinkedIn in terms of how many people you're connecting with that are actually relevant that want to connect with you and how many real conversations you're having on there is a big variable to how successful your content can become." — Orin John

**Non-AI comments only.** AI-generated comments are detectable, add no value, and contribute nothing to the social signal LinkedIn's algorithm is measuring. Every comment in the onboarding phase should be written by a human.

**Applies to:** New accounts, dormant accounts being reactivated, employees starting LinkedIn for the first time, founders launching a personal page alongside an existing brand.

## Brand Account vs. Personal Page

**The default rule:** Personal pages outperform brand accounts by approximately 13x on LinkedIn. Posting from a brand account largely goes nowhere without additional support.

**When brand accounts do work — the Company Cohort Effect exception:**
Brand accounts perform when backed by an organized internal team engagement strategy. The mechanism:
- Individual employees post about the company and tag the brand page
- Colleagues who are listed as working at the company engage with those posts
- The algorithm rewards this collective signal and amplifies the personal posts AND surfaces the brand page
- The brand account becomes the hub that personal posts orbit — not a standalone broadcast channel

"A cohesive strategy of people from the company posting about the company and sharing updates, tagging it, and other people interacting with it actually can push you pretty far in the algorithm." — Orin John

**When personal pages are the only viable option:** Solo founders, early-stage brands with no employees, or brands where team members are not willing or able to participate in a LinkedIn content strategy. In these cases, the founder's personal page IS the brand's LinkedIn presence.

**Practical rule:** Never launch a brand account as the primary content channel without a plan to activate it through personal pages. A brand account with no internal cohort is a broadcast with almost no distribution.

## What to Steal

- **Assign The Steve Role today.** Look at who in your organization is most networked in your industry and most likely to post consistently. That is Steve. Give them the brief: curate all brand content across channels onto your personal LinkedIn page. Consumer content counts.
- **Start cheerleading from day one.** Every retail win, every store entry, every milestone — document it publicly on LinkedIn from the beginning. The compound effect only works if you start before you need it.
- **Zero likes does not mean zero ROI.** The B2B ripple effects of tagged LinkedIn posts happen in the background through LinkedIn's interest graph. Post without waiting for likes.
- **Give every employee a lens.** The lens solves "what do I post?" permanently. Each functional role already has a natural lens — it just needs naming.
- **Warm up before posting.** 3–5 comments per week for several weeks before publishing your first post. The algorithm rewards accounts that are already socially active.
- **Build the Company Cohort Effect.** Make it easy and expected for team members to engage with brand LinkedIn content. This is a free amplification channel.
