# Strategy: Personal Rebrand Playbook

## Summary
Personal transformation and creative reinvention require more than motivation — they require picking the right kind of year to have. This playbook introduces five distinct "year types" that successful creatives move through: the money year, the systems year, the rebrand year, the mastery year, and the obsession year. Each has a specific context where it applies, a set of tactics for executing it, and common failure modes. The central insight is that you can only do one of these well at a time, and trying to do all five simultaneously is why most people never accomplish any of them.

## When to Use This
- You feel stuck and want to make a major change but don't know where to focus
- You've tried and failed to "have a major year" and want to understand why
- You're early in your career and need a roadmap for sequencing big moves
- You want to build new habits, change your perception, or add a new skill — but feel overwhelmed
- You're at an inflection point: between jobs, post-burnout, newly motivated after a trigger event

## The Framework / Steps

### The Five Year Types

**Year 1: The Money Year**
Focus exclusively on understanding and improving your relationship with money — without necessarily making more of it.

*Who it's for:* Anyone who's making a reasonable income (roughly $70K+) but can't figure out where the money goes, can't save, or feels financially stuck.

*Tactics:*
- **Consumables audit**: Stop buying new clothing, home goods, and discretionary items for the year. Try the "only buy secondhand if you must" rule — sell something on Grailed/Depop to fund any secondhand purchase.
- **Subscription purge**: List every subscription. Cut to only what you use weekly. Rotate rather than stack (one streaming service at a time).
- **Vice/alcohol audit**: What's the real monthly spend? This category is typically larger than people acknowledge and delivers the least lasting happiness per dollar.
- **Invest the savings passively**: Put freed-up money into the S&P 500 index. Don't actively manage it. Just let it sit.

*Expected outcome:* A meaningful shift in your relationship to consumption and dopamine-purchasing. Often recovers $500–$1,000/month or more. The goal isn't deprivation — it's clarity about where your money actually goes so future years can be funded by choices, not defaults.

**Year 2: The Systems Year**
Build the organizational infrastructure of your life: calendar management, task tracking, and communication.

*Who it's for:* Anyone who consistently loses track of things, fails to follow through, or has no real structure to their day.

*Three pillars:*
1. **Calendar**: Every Sunday, plan the next 7 days. Block mandatory items first, then creative/productive work. Build in trade-offs explicitly.
2. **Tracking system**: Use any app with checkboxes (Apple Notes, Notion, Evernote). The dopamine of checking things off is real and builds the habit.
3. **Communication**: Send weekly updates — to your boss, your clients, your creative partners, or just yourself in a journal. Establishes accountability loops.

Do all three for 52 consecutive weeks. By the end you will have gone from chaotic to action-oriented. This is the infrastructure year that makes every other year more possible.

**Year 3: The Rebrand Year**
Consciously change how others perceive you — and in the process, become that person.

*Who it's for:* Anyone who wants to be seen differently: more serious, more creative, more confident, more professional, or simply different from who they've been.

*How to execute:*
1. **Archetype exercise**: Write out who you want to become in specific behavioral terms. How do they dress? How do they speak in negotiations? How do they react when challenged? What do they read? What do they consume? Be specific — not "more confident" but "pauses before responding, maintains calm eye contact, doesn't over-explain."
2. **Daily planning**: End of every day, plan tomorrow from the perspective of that archetype. What would this person wear? How would they handle the meeting I have? What would they say?
3. **The pause technique**: Before reacting to anything, take a breath. Think: is this my reflexive response, or is this what my new self would say? Other people don't notice the pause — it reads as confidence.
4. **Repetition**: Identity change requires months of repetition before it becomes natural. Expect it to feel fake before it feels real.

*Key mindset:* People will react to your change with discomfort. Some will be annoyed. That discomfort is proof the change is visible. Don't let other people's resistance to your evolution be the reason you stop.

**Year 4: The Mastery Year**
Commit an entire year to developing one skill at a professional level.

*Who it's for:* Anyone who wants to add a major capability — short-form video, illustration, 3D, design, music, a new language — but keeps dabbling without making real progress.

*How to execute:*
1. **Daily practice, no days off**: Make something every single day, even if it's small. A logo, a scene, a video, a design. The constraint of daily output forces learning faster than any course. This includes Christmas, Thanksgiving, vacation days — plan ahead for those weeks so they don't break the streak.
2. **Time-box the daily session**: Decide when (morning, lunch, evening). Put it on the calendar. If you're working full-time, the window may be small — the creator made short-form videos from ~8:30pm until bedtime every night, after his son went to sleep. The constraint of a tight window forces efficiency.
3. **Ask "how do I do this better than yesterday?"**: This single daily question drives improvement faster than consuming more tutorial content. It orients the session toward refinement, not just production.
4. **Public practice**: Post progress (weekly or monthly minimum) — on LinkedIn, Instagram, or even an account no one follows. Public accountability is a forcing function. It also lets you see improvement over time.
5. **Don't expect money until year's end**: If it comes earlier, great. But don't let the absence of immediate revenue make you quit. The year is the investment; after a year you have something to monetize. "Put in the year. When the year comes, you can start to think about where the money comes from."

**Year 5: The Obsession Year**
Go completely all-in on a specific goal — with the understanding that everything else takes a back seat.

*Who it's for:* People who already have something they want to build — a business, a brand, a creative career — and have enough momentum to justify sacrificing other things for a year. This is NOT for people who don't yet know what they want to do.

*The cultural context:* There's a generation of people who believe you can have it all — massive success with work-life balance, without sacrificing a lot to get a lot. "For 99.9% of cases, that is just absolutely not true." We're also in a culture that makes people feel bad about going all-in. Don't. Every person who has reached mass success has likely had to do this at some point.

*How it works:*
- Every single action of the year must serve the goal. If it doesn't, don't do it.
- Communicate the year to the people in your life so they understand it's finite and intentional — give them a clear picture of what it will look like and how you'll make it up on the other end
- Accept that health, certain friendships, and leisure will be reduced. "It's going to be a long year, it's going to be a hard year, it's probably going to be bad for your health." These are not bugs; they are the nature of the trade.
- Consider bringing your partner INTO the year rather than managing around them — the creator's wife shifted from Netflix evenings to co-building a newsletter together; the obsession year became a shared project
- At the end of the year, you get to ask: "Do I want this to keep going, or am I done?" You can reset with no guilt. You can decide to do another one. Either is fine — because it was always finite.

*The "is this my year" test:* You already have traction. You left a job, you started something, you're seeing early results, and you know you need to go harder to capitalize. You have something worth obsessing over. If you don't know what that is yet, do the rebrand or mastery year first.

### Which Year to Choose
Look honestly at where you are:
- Financial stress with decent income → Money Year
- Chaos, ideas that never execute, missed deadlines → Systems Year
- Want to be seen differently, change your presence → Rebrand Year
- Want a new major skill → Mastery Year
- Already have momentum and a clear vision → Obsession Year

The most common mistake is trying to do all five at once and doing none. Pick one. Commit to it for 12 months. That discipline is itself the transformation.

## Real Examples
- **The creator's mastery year**: Started making one short-form video every day at age 37, working full-time. Mediocre for the first two months. Made one every single day after his son went to sleep. By the end of the year, the skill was built and the trajectory changed entirely.
- **The money year**: Making $150–180K/year with nothing to show for it after a new baby. Spent a year eliminating casual spending, alcohol/vice costs, and rotating subscriptions. Recovered close to $10,000/year. Changed the fundamental relationship with money — from reactive spending to intentional allocation.
- **The obsession year**: Left a job mid-year when creator income exceeded job income. Said yes to every aligned opportunity. Traveled more than usual. Night routine with wife shifted from Netflix to working on a newsletter together. At year's end, evaluated whether to continue or reset.

## Pitfalls to Avoid
- Picking the obsession year before you have clarity on what to obsess over
- Treating the mastery year as a "side thing" — it requires daily non-negotiable commitment
- Expecting the rebrand year to produce results in a month — identity change is 12-month work
- Letting someone else's discomfort with your transformation convince you to stop
- Doing the money year while simultaneously trying to make more money — the money year is about understanding and reducing, not earning more
- Making exceptions to the daily practice in the mastery year — exceptions are where streaks die and habits unravel
