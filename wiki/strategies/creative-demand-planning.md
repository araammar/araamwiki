# Strategy: Creative Demand Planning

## Summary

Taylor Holiday's framework for treating ad creative production as a data-driven supply chain rather than a creative endeavor. 3.5% of ads drive 66% of spend. Most ads are never meaningfully funded. The question is not whether you're making "good" creative — it's whether you're producing enough creative throughput to feed your spend levels. Iteration beats breakthrough. The brands winning on paid in 2026 are producing 150–400+ ads per month and optimizing relentlessly, not waiting for one hero creative to carry the account.

---

## The Core Insight

Most DTC brands approach creative like this:
1. Brief a concept
2. Produce the video
3. Launch it
4. Hope it becomes a winner
5. Repeat when it dies

This is creative gambling. The problem: you're betting on individual pieces of content rather than building a system with predictable output.

**The shift:** Creative demand planning treats ad production like supply chain management. You have a demand signal (how much spend you need to sustain) and a supply requirement (how many ads you need to feed that spend). The system determines production volume, not intuition.

---

## The Creative Demand Score

A 0–100 score that tells you whether you need to produce more or fewer ads than historically necessary.

**Five inputs:**

| Input | What It Measures | High = |
|---|---|---|
| **Zero Spend Rate** | % of active ads receiving no budget allocation | Too many ads being created, most wasted |
| **Ad Concentration** | Spend concentrated in top 5 ads | Fragile — one bad refresh can crater the account |
| **ROAS Degradation** | Whether performance drops after week 1 | Ads burning out fast — need higher production velocity |
| **Spend Degradation** | Whether daily spend drops post-launch | Algorithm not finding audience — creative or targeting problem |
| **Evergreen Share** | % of ads running 30+ consecutive days | High = more durable creative; low = high churn, high production demand |

**Interpreting the score:**
- **Score > 50:** You need fewer ads than historically necessary to maintain efficiency
- **Score < 50:** You must produce more ads just to maintain baseline performance

---

## Why Iteration Beats Breakthrough

The temptation: find one breakthrough creative that runs forever. The reality: breakthrough creatives exist, but building a production system dependent on them is high-variance. Most brands that are "waiting for their hero creative" are just under-producing.

**The math:**
- Top DTC brands run 150–400+ ads monthly
- 3.5% of those ads drive 66% of spend
- That means 5–14 meaningful ads out of 150–400 produced
- You cannot identify the 5–14 winners without producing the other 385

**The implication:** The "creative problem" most brands have is actually a **volume problem.** They're producing 10 ads per month and wondering why none break through. The answer is probability — not quality.

---

## The Creative-to-Paid Pipeline

How high-performing creative production connects to paid media results:

```
Brief → Produce → Launch (organic or paid) → Monitor performance
     ↓
Identify outliers (top 5–10%)
     ↓
Purchase ad rights ($100–400/video for creator content)
     ↓
Feed into paid account continuously — do NOT batch
     ↓
Repeat monthly — ad account always has fresh, pre-validated creative
```

**Do not batch-add creative.** Ad accounts fed a single monthly batch experience fatigue in weeks. Ad accounts continuously refreshed with new assets maintain performance because the algorithm always has new content to test.

## Creative Volume by Stage

| Stage | Ads/Month | How to Achieve |
|---|---|---|
| Early | 10–20 | Internal production + 2–3 creators |
| Growing | 40–80 | UGC affiliate program (20–30 creators) + internal |
| Scale | 150–250 | Full creator affiliate program (100+ creators) + internal team |
| Advanced | 400+ | Large creator program + dedicated production team |

The affiliate creator program is the most capital-efficient way to scale creative volume — creators produce on commission, brands purchase rights to what performs.

---

## What to Steal

**Immediate:** Calculate your Zero Spend Rate. How many of your active ads have received zero spend in the last 30 days? If it's over 50%, you're producing creative that's going nowhere. Either reduce production and improve brief quality, or fix your launch/testing system.

**30 days:** Set a monthly production target based on your spend levels. Rule of thumb: for every $10K/month in ad spend, you need 15–25 fresh ad assets per month. Under-producing for your spend level is the most common creative bottleneck.
