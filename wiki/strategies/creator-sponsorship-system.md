# Strategy: Creator Sponsorship System

## Summary

The creator sponsorship business runs on a media metaphor: you are a bridge, brands are on one side, your audience is on the other side, and they pay to cross your bridge. Building a profitable sponsorship business requires three things: (1) a clearly defined audience that brands want to reach, (2) a systematized deal structure that removes ambiguity from the buying process, and (3) a long-term relationship philosophy that optimizes for renewals, not one-off transactions.

Samir Chaudry (Colin and Samir): "90% of our business is sponsorships. The biggest thing I've learned working with big brands is the experience is one of the primary products you offer."

---

## Framework / Steps

### Step 1: Define Your Audience with Specificity

Before any sponsorship conversation, you must be able to answer: who specifically is in my audience?

Not demographics — identities. Aspiring creators. Professional operators. Business owners who've generated $10M+. The more specific the identity, the easier it is for a brand to understand whether your audience is the audience they want.

A vague audience is not a sellable audience. A clearly defined, verifiable audience (with social proof from named members of that audience) is the foundation of the entire sponsorship business.

Colin and Samir's audience proof in their deck:
- From an aspiring creator: "I started watching this show and it completely changed my business."
- From a professional creator (Marquez Brownley): "Your favorite creator's favorite creator."
- From creator industry (Neil Mohan, CEO of YouTube): "No one knows more about the creator economy than Colin and Samir."

Three audience tiers, three pieces of social proof — each one immediately tells a brand what caliber of audience is present.

### Step 2: Build a Sponsorship Deck (One-Time Investment)

The deck eliminates negotiation friction by systematizing the buy. Once built, every inbound inquiry gets the same document — the time investment goes from a custom pitch per sponsor to a single great document that scales.

**The deck structure that unlocked Colin and Samir's sponsorship business:**

1. **Who we are:** Credentials, years, what we've built. Brief.
2. **Social proof page:** Comments, tweets, press quotes from verified members of your audience tiers. This is the most important page. It tells the brand "we're real" without you having to say it.
3. **What we make:** Show overview, format, distribution channels, frequency, audience size.
4. **Who's in the audience:** Breakdown by identity group with proof for each.
5. **The menu:** What you can buy. Specific, named options. 60-second host read. Instagram story. Newsletter placement. Event sponsorship. No open-ended "what do you want to do?"
6. **Pricing:** Specific. Flat rate. No negotiation required.

This deck is about reducing decisions for the buyer. "Here's how you work with me. You can just say yes or no."

### Step 3: Price Flat, Not CPM

Traditional media pricing is based on CPM (cost per thousand impressions). Creator sponsorships are not traditional media — they are relationship-based endorsements with compounding brand association.

Samir's pricing evolution: he removed CPM-based negotiation entirely and moved to flat-rate pricing. Benefits:
- Eliminates back-and-forth negotiation time
- Signals confidence in your value
- Makes pricing transparent and professional
- Allows you to focus on making a great show instead of haggling

The math: how much money do we need? How many videos will we make? How many need to be sold? What price enables both? Set that as your rate. If the market says no, you're not good enough yet to command that rate — so make better content, not less money.

### Step 4: Ask "What Does Success Mean to You?" — Always

The single most important question in any sponsorship conversation:

**"What does success mean to you?"**

This question accomplishes multiple things:
- Reveals whether the brand's success metric is something you can actually deliver
- Prevents mismatched expectations that kill renewals
- Surfaces creative possibilities (Shopify's answer was "increase the TAM of entrepreneurs" — not drive direct conversions from a link)
- Filters out misaligned partners early (if their answer is "X conversions from your link" and you know you can't guarantee that, the deal won't work)

If the brand's definition of success is direct-response conversion, you are being treated as a Facebook ad. You are not a Facebook ad. Correct the framing:

"You are building long-term association. Sponsoring my show is like introducing a friend to my friend group. If they show up once, no one remembers them. If they show up consistently, people start to trust them and like them. You are playing for long-term brand association, not 30-day attribution."

### Step 5: Sell Packages, Not One-Offs

Never sell a single episode sponsorship to a new advertiser. The association needs repetition to work.

"The first time someone comes and hangs out with your friend group, you kind of get to know each other a little bit. He doesn't really talk to me much. He never comes back. I'm never going to think about that guy again. But if he comes again and again and again — now we're like, oh cool, I like this guy."

Minimum: 3-episode packages. Preferred: multi-month or annual partnerships. Long-term partnerships also enable deeper collaboration — events, consulting, speaking — that create more value for both parties.

### Step 6: Build Toward Partnership, Not Transactions

The most valuable sponsorship relationships transcend the standard "60-second read in an episode" transaction. Colin and Samir's most evolved partnerships (5+ years with Shopify, multi-year with Spotter) include:
- Digital distribution (videos, newsletter, podcast)
- Events and speaking (co-producing the Spotter Summit)
- Consulting (advising on creator economy positioning)

This evolution only happens when you've optimized for the renewal — playing the long game. Every interaction with a sponsor should be designed to make them want to come back.

## The Sponsorship Acquisition Reality

**Cold outreach almost never works.**
Samir's observation: he has rarely seen cold outreach to brands succeed. The path to sponsors is:
1. Make something good enough that brands reach out inbound
2. Warm introductions through shared contacts
3. Agent/management relationships (UTA handles Colin and Samir's brand relationships)
4. Look at who's already sponsoring in your niche — they have a budget and a system for buying creator inventory. Pitch yourself as a complementary addition to their spend.

**The audit approach:**
Write down every brand you see sponsoring podcasts and YouTube channels in your niche. Those brands have already allocated budget for creator partnerships. They're actively looking for more placements. They're lower-friction targets than brands with no creator sponsorship history.

## What to Steal / Application

**Build the deck before you have sponsors.**
Even if you have zero sponsors, creating the deck forces you to answer: who is my audience, what is my social proof, what is my menu, what is my price? Those answers will shape how you build the channel.

**One offer, maximum.**
Orin John's principle applies here: fewer offers with the ability to over-deliver beats many options with confusion. Reduce your menu to the core product, price it clearly, and exceed expectations on every deal.

**"What does success mean to you?" as a pre-episode question too.**
This question isn't just for sponsors. Ask it before every podcast recording. Before every client kickoff. Before every collaboration. Expectation-matching is the antidote to disappointment.
