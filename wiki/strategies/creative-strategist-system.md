# Strategy: The Creative Strategist System

## Summary

Orin John's operational infrastructure for running content strategy at scale — across multiple brands, creator programs, and ad accounts. Three layers: a Notion-based content database, a weekly hook-saving ritual, and a monthly brand checklist. The system makes pattern recognition systematic rather than intuitive, which means output scales without the strategist needing to be in every decision.

---

## Layer 1: The Content Database (Notion)

A master Notion doc that tracks every piece of content across clients and brands.

**Fields per entry:**

| Field | What It Captures |
|---|---|
| **Content type** | Organic video / carousel / AI content / BTS / paid ad |
| **Tags** | Launch status, format type, persona targeted, platform |
| **Link** | Direct URL to the content piece |
| **Performance status** | Performing / underperforming / outlier / not yet run |
| **Usage history** | Used in organic / used in paid / usage rights purchased / not yet used |

**Why the usage history field matters:** High-performing organic content that hasn't been purchased for ad use is money left on the table. The database makes that gap visible.

**Why the performance status field matters:** Builds a portfolio of proven formats over time. Pattern recognition across 500 pieces is different from remembering your last 10 posts.

---

## Layer 2: The Weekly Hook-Saving Ritual

**The practice:** Schedule 30-minute blocks to save and categorize hooks from organic content. Weekly, not monthly.

**What to save:** Any piece of content with unusual performance — unusually high saves, shares, comments, or engagement rate relative to baseline. The outlier is the signal.

**How to categorize:** Tag by message, concept format, hook type, and brand/niche. Make every saved piece queryable.

**Why weekly:** A monthly saving session produces a dump. A weekly session produces a pattern. The difference between a strategist with 50 saved pieces and one with 500 properly tagged pieces is the difference between gut feel and data.

---

## Layer 3: The Monthly Brand Checklist

A structured monthly review of what's working per brand. Documented, not memorized.

**Template:**
```
Brand: [Name]
Month: [Month]

Top performer this month:
- Format: [e.g., price-focused testimonial]
- Headline: [e.g., "Expensive doesn't equal better"]
- Why it worked: [hypothesis]

Format winners:
- Review ads: [performance note]
- Starter packs: [performance note]
- Hero imagery: [performance note]

Creator assignments:
- [Creator A] → [format they're best for]
- [Creator B] → [format they're best for]

Next month priority:
- Test: [format to test]
- Scale: [format to scale]
```

**The compound effect:** After 6 months, the monthly checklist becomes a brand playbook. After 12, it is institutional knowledge that a new team member can read and immediately understand the brand's creative history.

## Persona-Based Segmentation Layer

**The practice:** Use data tools (Outer Signal) to generate data-backed personas for each brand's audience.

**Integration:**
- Segment AI prompts by persona to generate relevant pain points and trending topics
- Tag all ads and content by persona in tracking systems
- Test effectiveness by demographic segment, not just in aggregate

**Why it matters:** "Best performing overall" often masks "best performing for one persona but terrible for another." Persona-level tracking reveals which formats work for which audiences — and which personas are underserved.

---

## Five Human Emotion Frames

Every product and every piece of content can be run through five core human emotion frames to generate angles:

1. **Love** — relationships, family, connection, belonging
2. **Spirituality** — purpose, meaning, identity, values
3. **Money** — financial security, opportunity, fear of loss, status
4. **Physical health** — body, energy, appearance, longevity
5. **Mental health** — stress, anxiety, peace, clarity

"Frame every product through five core human emotions." If you can't find a compelling content angle under any of these five frames, the problem is the frame you're using, not the product.

## What to Steal

Start with the weekly 30-minute hook-saving block. Before building the full database, just the habit of saving and tagging 5 pieces per week — for 12 weeks — gives you 60 tagged references and enough pattern to see what's working. The database catches up to the habit; the habit can't catch up to the database.
