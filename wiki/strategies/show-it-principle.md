# Strategy: The Show It Principle

## Summary
In the distrust economy, the most powerful brand-building move is radical manufacturing transparency. "Show it" means filming the actual production context, naming materials with technical specificity, describing product trade-offs honestly, and demonstrating — not claiming — quality. It is the antidote to a market saturated with empty label language.

The principle applies regardless of where your product is made. Made in Italy? Show the artisans — real ones, not migrants in a safety-stripped facility. Made in China? Don't hide it. Show the actual factory, the actual craft, the actual standards. The origin doesn't matter. The evidence does.

## Framework / Steps

### Step 1: Audit Your Claim-to-Evidence Gap
List every quality or provenance claim your brand currently makes. For each:
- What's the specific evidence behind it?
- Would a skeptical consumer accept the evidence if shown?
- Is there anything about the production reality that contradicts the claim?

If you can't show the evidence, the claim is a liability in the distrust economy — not an asset.

### Step 2: Film the Production Context
The most powerful trust signal is video of actual manufacturing. For each product:
- Show the facility (at an appropriate level of detail)
- Show the hands doing the work
- Show materials at the sourcing stage if possible (the tannery, the wool supplier, the dye house)
- Don't make it a polished ad — make it a genuine window

If your factory is not one you'd be proud to show, that is product and supply chain feedback, not a marketing problem to be managed. The distrust economy will find it eventually.

### Step 3: Write Technical Product Descriptions
AI agents don't read vibes — they read descriptive information. Write product copy that names:
- **Materials:** Specific grade, origin, processing method (not "premium cashmere" — "16-micron Grade A inner Mongolian cashmere, 2-ply")
- **Construction:** How it's assembled, stitched, finished, or treated
- **Weight / density / gauge:** The specific numbers, not just "heavy" or "light"
- **Trade-offs:** What makes this product not for everyone (heavy t-shirt = intense feel; natural fiber = different care requirements)

This serves two purposes: trust-building with human consumers and AI search indexing. AI surfaces brands that can answer "what are the best cashmere sweaters" by matching descriptive content to queries.

### Step 4: Describe the Trade-Offs Explicitly
Don't just describe the upsides. The brands that earn the most loyalty in the distrust economy are the ones that volunteer the downsides:
- "This shirt is one of the most intense things you'll ever wear — here's what that means"
- "This is not a lightweight casual piece"
- "If you're looking for X, this is not the product for you — you want Y"

This is counterintuitive and powerful. It signals you know the product deeply, you respect the consumer's intelligence, and you're confident enough in the overall value to name what it isn't.

### Step 5: Seed the Organic Conversation
The discovery stack is now: AI results → Reddit threads → YouTube videos. Seed the organic conversation on both platforms:
- Engage genuinely in relevant Reddit communities (r/malefashionadvice, product-specific subreddits, etc.)
- Create or seed YouTube content that names and explains your materials and methods
- The goal is organic mentions that get surfaced by AI search when someone asks for a recommendation

Do not pay for Reddit posts. In the distrust economy, being caught doing so destroys what the seeding was trying to build. Genuine product quality is the only sustainable foundation — good products generate organic discussion.

### Step 6: Build the Knowledge Base
On your website, create in-depth informational content:
- "How [product] is made" guides
- Material education pages (what makes Grade A cashmere different from Grade B)
- FAQ pages that address skeptical consumer questions directly
- Behind-the-scenes factory or supplier documentation

This serves the AI search function: when an LLM is asked about cashmere quality, it surfaces brands with thorough, accurate, descriptive knowledge bases. This is the new SEO.

## When to Use
This strategy is highest priority when:
- You are in a category saturated with empty label language (apparel, leather goods, home goods, food)
- Your product actually has a genuine quality story (if it doesn't, fix the product first)
- Competitors are competing on ad spend and marketing language rather than demonstrated product quality
- Consumer trust in your category has been damaged by enshittification

## Examples

**Positive case (Valuables brand):** The author launched a 300 GSM t-shirt with a proprietary finish that made it feel completely different from anything consumers had experienced. He described it specifically in product copy: the weight, the texture, what it would feel like, the upsides and downsides. Consumers said "I've never felt something like this" — because they were set up to experience exactly what they were told to expect.

**Negative case (Tricko streetwear):** Went to Chinese factories and filmed content there — the factories were dark and not impressive. Got criticism ("went to the sweatshop"). But the transparency itself was the right instinct. The lesson isn't "don't show the factory" — it's "improve the factory, then show it."

**Counter-example (any brand hiding provenance):** Luxury brands that have gamed "Made in Italy" while running migrant-labor operations. When raids happen (Dior, Valentino, Armani), the brand damage is catastrophic because the gap between claim and reality is enormous. Transparency would have been painful upfront; discovery is catastrophic.
