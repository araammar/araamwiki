# Strategy: Content Operations Playbook

## Summary
A full guide to building a content-first marketing team at any org size — from solo founders to 20-person teams. The central model is a pod structure: a creative strategist paired with one internal creator and three to four external creators, all producing owned organic, owned paid, and world content simultaneously. Brands that operate like media companies win; the question is how to do it tactically, not just conceptually.

## When to Use This
- When transitioning a traditional marketing org toward content-first execution
- When hiring your first or second social media / content hire
- When a brand wants to become the media company in its niche but doesn't know how to staff or structure it
- When production costs are spiraling and you need a leaner workflow

## The Framework / Steps

### Step 1: Understand the Brand World Architecture
Every marketing org has three zones to build and operate simultaneously:

**Owned Organic:** Your brand social accounts (Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, etc.). This is where your team is creating and posting content directly.

**Owned Paid:** Ad accounts where your content team creates assets — static ads, video ads, UGC-style ads — to run as paid media. Ideally the same people making organic content are making or briefing the ad assets, not a siloed agency.

**Brand World:** The organic ecosystem outside your main account — founder accounts, athlete accounts, affiliate/reseller accounts, external creators you pay on retainer. This content gets pulled into your ad account or sparked with paid spend to extend reach.

Build this flywheel before investing in trade shows, banner ads, sponsorships, or any external marketing. Once this machine is profitable, expand outward.

### Step 2: The Pod Structure
The core unit of a content-first team is the pod — not a single social media manager.

**Pod minimum (2 people):**
- **Creative Strategist / Social Media Manager:** Writes briefs, manages shot lists, runs analytics, does posting scheduling, coordinates between creators and media buyers, runs ideation sessions. This is the brain.
- **Internal Creator:** Films and edits content (often in CapCut), on camera or behind it, executes the strategist's briefs. This is the hands.

**Pod at scale (1 strategist + 3–5 creators):**
The creative strategist can manage multiple creators. Typical pod adds 3–4 external creators sourced from platforms like ManyChat, SuperAffiliate, or Meta Creators, paid per video or on monthly retainer. Tools like Outer Signal can identify influential existing customers to convert into paid creators.

**Pod meeting cadence:**
- Weekly one-on-ones between strategist and each creator (max 5 per week)
- Creative review session before content goes live (hook feedback, framing, etc.)
- Weekly analytics review (week-over-week, month-over-month)
- Research session (new ideas, competitor content, trend links)
- Use any project management tool consistently — Notion, ClickUp, Monday, Asana all work. What matters is having one.

### Step 3: Roles at Scale (20+ Person Teams)
As you grow from one pod to multiple pods, shared resources emerge:

**Multiple pods:** One pod for main brand content. One for founder content (videographer + strategist + editor). One for TikTok-specific output.

**Shared resources across pods:**
- Editors (work across all pods)
- Designers (brief off contract via platforms)
- Producer (handles locations, booking, props as org grows)

**Leads:** An e-commerce repurposing lead ensures all assets from all pods funnel back to product detail pages and email. A channel comms lead ensures marketing is coordinating with retail partners.

**Creative Director:** The hardest role to hire. Sets visual and editorial standards, coaches creative strategists, maintains brand consistency across all pod output. Do not hire a traditional media creative director — they will inflate costs radically. Hire someone with native social media fluency.

### Step 4: What to Actually Produce Each Week
**Goal per pod:** 10 unique assets per week — approximately 5 going into the ad account, 5 on organic. Some organic posts get repurposed into the ad account.

**Priority output types:**
- Content for owned organic feed
- Net new creative for the ad account
- A repeatable signature series (test 4–6 "pilot" episodes of a format, iterate)

**Anchor elements** to build content around:
- A famous collaborator or recurring influencer
- A signature location (warehouse, lab, storefront, founder's home)
- A founder or credible expert on camera
- Events (brand trips, VIP customer nights, trade show piggybacks)

### Step 5: Gear and Production Standards
Reject the traditional production mindset. Workflow speed to publication beats camera quality.

**What works:** iPhone + Osmo gimbal + decent lighting (Amaran 300s or equivalent). Nikon Z8 or Sony FX3 if you genuinely need it.

**What wastes money:** Arri or Red cameras, dedicated sound people, lengthy color grading sessions. These slow the cycle and don't measurably improve social performance.

The standard is: if there are more than 2–3 people on the shoot team, something has gone wrong. Speed from in-camera to published is the competitive advantage.

## Real Examples
- **Red Bull:** The original media company brand — replaced an entire category of TV (action sports, Ridiculousness-style content) with their own Instagram and YouTube.
- **Rapha (cycling):** Long-form documentary films + social carousels + retail clubhouses that screen the documentaries. Media company expressed through physical retail.
- **Flamingo Estate:** Weekly editorial letter from the founder + social media content. Agency-turned-brand that used their existing creative network as the content team.
- **Cheese Store of Beverly Hills:** One post per day, all shot on iPhone by employees. One day per week documenting what happens in the store. A content machine with no dedicated content team.
- **Represent:** Brand YouTube + founder George's YouTube + 24/7 athletic content YouTube + founder personal social + brand main social + Spark ads on dozens of creator fit-check videos. Gold standard at scale.
- **Ladder (fitness app):** Coaches as individual creators — each coach maintains their own content channel that promotes the app. Every brand representative is a creator.
- **Kosas:** Brand social + founder social + brand trips for influencers = cultural moment building at upstart budget.
- **Merit:** Editor deliveries on branded Vespas during Paris Fashion Week — spectacle content at a fraction of what it looks like it costs.

## Pitfalls to Avoid
- **One social media manager model:** A lone SMM cannot brief, shoot, edit, analyze, post, and ideate at the volume required. The pod system splits left brain (strategy) from right brain (creation).
- **Treating content as a cost center:** Content is a revenue system. Build it like a flywheel and tie output directly to ad account performance and sales.
- **Over-investing in production before workflow:** Expensive gear with slow workflows produces worse outcomes than cheap gear with fast publication cycles.
- **Starting a social show without content foundations:** Shows are a tool that works once your org knows how to make good content. Don't skip the foundation.
- **Siloing content from product development:** Product dev and content dev need to be in the same room. Content should document the product roadmap as it happens.
- **Expanding beyond the core flywheel prematurely:** Trade shows, sponsorships, and banner ads are waste until owned organic + owned paid + brand world is a money-printing machine.
