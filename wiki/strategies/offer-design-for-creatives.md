# Strategy: Offer Design for Creatives

## What This Is For

A step-by-step framework for taking any creative skill set and designing, naming, pricing, and positioning a productized offer that generates revenue without constant custom proposal work. This is for designers, videographers, copywriters, brand strategists, creative directors, illustrators, photographers, and anyone whose work is currently described as "I do X, Y, and Z depending on what the client needs" — and who wants to convert that into a specific, purchasable thing.

## The Framework

### Step 1: Identify What You Can Specifically Deliver

Start with the thing you can produce reliably, with consistent quality, on a repeatable timeline. Not everything you can do — one specific thing.

The test: can you describe it in a sentence that ends with a deliverable, a buyer type, a price, and a timeline?

- "Brand identity for B2B SaaS companies, $10,000, 3–4 weeks."
- "Landing pages for e-commerce brands, $1,500 per page."
- "5 icons + 5 illustrations + 2–3 UI screens for tech brands, $5,000 sprint."
- "Video sales letter (VSL) + Meta ad setup for service businesses, $X, Y weeks."

If you cannot fill in all four slots, the offer is not ready.

### Step 2: Choose Your Competitive Position

Every offer must compete on one or two of the following axes. You cannot own all three:

**Better:** Your work is demonstrably higher quality than what's available at comparable or lower prices. If this is your position, premium pricing is justified. Be honest with yourself: most people are not at the top of their category early in their career.

**Faster:** You deliver reliably within a compressed timeline. "Brand in 14 days" or "landing page in 48 hours" are real positions. Speed is extremely underutilized as a competitive angle because most creatives are afraid to commit to a hard deadline.

**Cheaper:** You are willing to work at a lower price point than the market in exchange for the reps, portfolio, and client relationships. This is a valid early-career strategy. It is not a permanent state.

Most creatives starting out will own **faster + cheaper**, with a clear ambition to move toward **better** over time. The transcript's personal example: "I was not making the best websites in the world. But I had a 6-week front-end process that included copywriting. My price was decently low. They knew they could get something done in time from me that hit a pain point."

### Step 3: Productize — Build the Offer Page

The offer page is not a portfolio. It is a sales page for a specific product.

**Required elements:**

1. **Headline:** The problem the buyer is facing or the outcome they want. "Your brand is fuzzy. Let's fix that." "You need content. Here's how you get it in 4 weeks."

2. **The offer in plain language:** What they get, when they get it, what it costs. No ambiguity. "Brand identity (logo, typography, color palette, brand guidelines) in 4 weeks for $15,000."

3. **The competitive position:** Why you over alternatives. Speed? Quality of a specific capability? Niche expertise? State it.

4. **Deliverable preview:** If possible, show a sample of what the output looks like. The transcript example: a Figma file preview linked directly from the offer page. "You can literally click through and see an example of what one of the deliverables will look like."

5. **Social proof:** Past clients, results, brands worked with. This can be minimal early on — one good case study is enough.

6. **CTA:** Book a call, submit an inquiry, or (for lower-priced offers) a direct purchase button. Remove friction from the next step.

**The VSL (Video Sales Letter):** A 10–12 minute video on the offer page that walks through:
- The problems the buyer faces that they cannot solve alone
- Your background and how you came to solve this specific problem
- How you close the gap between their problem and their goal
- What it looks like to buy from you

This is for people who don't want to read. The conversion rate on offer pages with a VSL is higher because it reaches a different processing style. Making a VSL is also a learnable, valuable skill to build for clients.

### Step 4: Build the Two-Tier Structure

If your core offer is priced above $3,000–$5,000, you need a lower-priced entry point. The entry tier:

- Is cheaper (a workshop, a strategy session, a mini audit, a smaller deliverable)
- Allows the buyer to experience your thinking and work at lower risk
- Allows you to validate whether the buyer is a good client before a large engagement
- Creates a natural upsell path: they buy the $1,000 workshop → they see the quality → they purchase the $15,000 full engagement

Example from the transcript: "A $1,000 workshop — something cheaper or smaller that can get people in the door that you can then use to validate if they're the right client for something larger."

The two-tier structure also expands your addressable market. Some buyers are never going to spend $15,000 on a brand identity. Some of those people will spend $1,000 on a brand strategy session. Some of those $1,000 buyers will eventually return for the larger engagement when their business is ready.

### Step 5: Establish Offer Alignment with Content

If you make content, the offer must be directly aligned with the topic and audience of that content. Misalignment is the most common reason creators have large audiences and no revenue.

**Alignment test:** If someone watches 10 of your videos and then visits your offer page, does it feel like a natural next step? Or does it feel like a different person/business?

Strong alignment examples:
- Content about hospitality design + consulting offer for bars and hotels = strong
- Content about B2B marketing + brand strategy offer for B2B SaaS = strong
- Content about financial products + educational financial community = strong

Weak alignment examples:
- Content about restaurant culture + generic video editing services = weak
- Content about AI graphic design + photography services = weak
- Content about content creation + an unrelated business offer = weak

If you have audience but no aligned offer: either build an offer around your content topic, or shift your content to support the offer you already have.

### Step 6: Set Up the Funnel

The funnel is the path from "someone found my work" to "someone bought my offer." Most buyers don't purchase on first contact.

**Basic funnel:**

1. **Discovery:** Someone finds you through social media, referral, or search
2. **Interest:** They visit your site, watch the VSL, or read the offer page
3. **Lead capture:** They sign up for something — an email list, a waitlist, a free resource — even if they don't buy yet
4. **Nurture:** Email sequence, newsletter, or retargeting that keeps them connected to your work over days and weeks
5. **Conversion:** They buy the entry tier or core offer, either through direct purchase or a call

The CRM is where leads are stored and managed. This does not need to be complex early. An email list with a basic automation sequence is enough.

The key principle: most sales happen on follow-up, not on first contact. If you have no lead capture mechanism, you have no follow-up mechanism, and the majority of potential buyers are simply lost.

### Step 7: Price Honestly and Publicly

Show pricing on the offer page. The most common objection to this is "what if it scares people away?" The correct response: buyers who are scared away by your price were never going to buy. Buyers who see your price and contact you are already pre-qualified. Hiding price creates uncertainty, lowers conversion, and wastes time on calls with buyers who were never in your price range.

The heuristic for pricing:
- What does the outcome cost the buyer if they don't solve this problem?
- What would it cost to hire a larger agency for the same output?
- What have you seen similar work command in the market?
- Are you competing on better, faster, or cheaper?

Price to the position you've chosen, not to what feels comfortable. The transcript is explicit: "Never feel bad asking for what you're worth."

### Step 8: Build the Offer Stack Over Time

The initial offer is a single, productized service. Over time, additional skills and offers can be stacked to increase the total value of what a buyer can purchase from you.

**The Eddie progression:**
- Start: videography offer
- Add: Meta ads capability → new offer (video + ad management)
- Add: VSL scripting → new offer (full-service video ad operation: creative + media buying + scripting)
- Result: an offer that generates leads at specific CPAs for service businesses — priced dramatically above the original videography offer

**Stacking principles:**
- Each addition should solve a problem the existing offer exposes (clients who buy your brand identity always struggle with launch content → add a launch content offer)
- The stack should eventually own an outcome, not just a deliverable
- The stack makes you harder to replace and easier to justify premium pricing for

## Common Mistakes

1. **Describing a skill instead of an offer.** "I'm a designer" is not an offer. "Brand identities for tech companies in 2 weeks for $5,000" is an offer.

2. **No pricing on the site.** The most universal friction point. Buyers drop out before contacting when they can't determine price range.

3. **Hiding behind "it depends."** Custom pricing for everything is a signal that you haven't productized. Start with a fixed price for a defined scope. Adjust for complexity, but start from a base.

4. **Misaligning offer and content.** Building an audience about Topic A and selling a service in Topic B. The audience you built is not the buyer you need.

5. **No entry tier for high-ticket offers.** Asking a first-time buyer to spend $15,000 without a lower-risk option first. The entry tier removes the risk and validates the fit.

6. **No VSL.** Some buyers watch, some read. Without a VSL, you are only converting the readers.

7. **No CTA, ever.** The most consistent observation across all creators: they never ask for the sale. Put the ask in stories, newsletters, the content itself. Be explicit: "Looking for a brand or two to make content for."

## What Success Looks Like

An offer that works looks like:
- Buyers can evaluate it without a call (they can read the page, watch the VSL, see the price, and decide whether to proceed)
- You receive qualified inquiries from people who already understand what they're buying and what it costs
- The offer sells repeatedly without custom scoping work
- The entry tier converts buyers into core-offer buyers at a measurable rate
- The funnel runs: leads enter the email list, nurture sequences run, buyers convert on follow-up

The benchmark from the transcript: "One thing I can buy. He does landing pages. I know anytime I need a landing page in Framer that I want to look good, I can go to him." The buyer has no ambiguity. The seller has predictable, recurring demand.
