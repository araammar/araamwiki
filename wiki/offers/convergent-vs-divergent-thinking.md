# Concept: Convergent vs Divergent Thinking (The Brick Exercise)
tags: [offers, Hormozi, creativity, problem-solving, framework, brick-exercise]

## Summary / Definition

A mental-reframe Hormozi inserts before the offer-creation process. The premise: **convergent thinking** (one right answer, school-style) is necessary for math but **divergent thinking** (many possible answers, multiple right ones with varying quality) is what life — and offer-building — actually rewards.

You cannot build a Grand Slam Offer with convergent thinking. You will default to the one obvious solution, which is what everyone else is doing, which is by definition a commodity offer.

---

## The Definitions

### Convergent Problem Solving
- Lots of variables, all known
- Unchanging conditions
- Single answer
- Binary (right or wrong)

> *"Think math."*

**Example from the book:**
- 3 salespeople. Each takes 100 calls/month.
- 4 calls per close.
- Need 110 sales.
- 100/4 = 25 closes per rep
- 110/25 = 4.4 reps needed
- Round up → 5 reps total
- Have 3 → hire 2 more

There is exactly one right answer. Schools train this — it's easy to grade.

### Divergent Problem Solving
- Multiple variables, known AND unknown
- Dynamic conditions
- Multiple answers
- One answer may be "way more right" than others

> *"Life will pay you for your ability to solve using a divergent thought process."*

Almost every meaningful business decision is divergent: how to differentiate, how to price, how to package, how to position, how to hire, how to retain. There is no single correct answer — only better and worse ones, and you discover them through divergent exploration.

## The Brick Exercise

Hormozi prescribes a 120-second exercise to wake up divergent thinking before offer-building.

> *"Right now, I want you to set a timer on your phone for 120 seconds. What you need to do: Think of a brick. Write down as many different uses of a brick as you can possibly think of."*

After the timer, he hits with cheat-code questions:

> *"How big is the brick? A tab of gum, 3-5/8" x 2-1/4" x 8" (Standard), 2 ft x 2 ft x 6ft?"*
>
> *"What is the brick made of? Plastic, Gold, Clay, Wood, Metal?"*
>
> *"How is the brick shaped? Does it have holes in it? Does it have divots for interlocking?"*

The point: most people start with one default mental image and exhaust that image's possible uses. **Varying the brick's properties multiplies the solution space.**

### Hormozi's Brick Use List
- Paper weight
- Door stop
- Building things
- Home for a fish in a fish bowl
- Plant holder (with holes)
- A trophy (painted brick)
- Rustic decoration
- To break windows
- Make a mural (tiny painted bricks)
- A weight for resistance training
- A wedge under uneven platform
- Pen holder (holed brick)
- Children's toy (lego)
- Flotation device (plastic brick)
- Payment for goods (gold brick)
- Stabilizer for leaning something against
- Retainer of value (gold brick)
- Holder for flagpole (holed brick)
- A seat (jumbo brick)

The variety only comes from varying the brick itself — material, shape, size.

## Why This Matters For Offers

Every offer has **building blocks** that can be recombined infinite ways. The divergent thinker sees more recombinations than the convergent thinker.

> *"Every offer has building blocks, the pieces that when combined make an offer irresistible. Our goal is to use a divergent thought process to think of as many easy ways to combine these elements to provide value."*

When constructing the [[5-step-grand-slam-creation]]:
- **Step 2 (List problems)** requires divergent thinking — keep listing past the obvious ones
- **Step 3 (List solutions)** requires divergent thinking — multiple solution paths per problem
- **Step 4 (Delivery vehicles)** explicitly needs divergent thinking — see [[delivery-cube]] for the cheat codes
- **Step 5 (Trim & Stack)** only works if you generated enough variety to trim

A convergent thinker stops after 5 ideas. A divergent thinker generates 50, then trims to the best 8.

## The Cheat Codes For Generating Variety (Applied To Offers)

Hormozi provides explicit cheat codes for the offer step (the equivalent of "vary the brick properties"):

1. **What level of personal attention?** 1-on-1, small group, one-to-many
2. **What level of effort from them?** DIY, DWY (done with you), DFY (done for you)
3. **What environment/medium?** In-person, phone, email, text, Zoom, chat
4. **For recordings: format?** Audio, video, written
5. **What response speed?** 24/7, 9–5, within 5 min, within 1 hour, within 24 hr
6. **10× / 1/10× test:** If they paid 10× the price, what would I provide? If 1/10×, how would I still deliver?

See [[delivery-cube]] for the full breakdown.

## The Convergent Trap In Business

> *"Convergent answers are binary. They are either right or they are wrong."*

This is why business owners get stuck on questions like *"Should I price at $100 or $150?"* The framing forces convergence. The divergent alternative: *"What would I price at if I delivered 10× more value? What if I gave it away free and monetized differently? What if I priced it in a way no competitor can match because they don't have the margin?"*

Each question opens a different solution space. None of them is "right" — but several may be way more right than the binary $100 vs $150 choice.

---

## Application Notes

Use the brick exercise as a literal warmup before offer-design sessions:
1. Set 120 seconds. List brick uses.
2. Read the cheat-code questions (what is it made of, etc.). List more uses.
3. Now switch to your business: 120 seconds. List ways your offer could deliver value. Apply the same cheat-code variation.

The exercise trains the muscle. The muscle builds the offer.

## Connection To Sutherland (Logical vs Psychological)

Convergent thinking gravitates toward logical solutions ("the one right answer"). Divergent thinking opens psychological solutions (multiple non-obvious framings).

See [[logical-vs-psychological-solutions]] — the pair forms one mental shift: stop looking for *the* answer, start exploring *the space of answers*.
