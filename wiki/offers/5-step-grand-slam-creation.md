# Framework: The 5-Step Grand Slam Offer Creation Process
tags: [offers, Hormozi, framework, offer-creation, process]

## Summary / Definition

The complete construction process for a Grand Slam Offer. Five sequential steps that turn a vague service idea into a bundle so valuable it cannot be compared to competitors and so reasonable it cannot be refused.

This is the operational core of $100M Offers. Every concept in the book exists to feed this process.

---

## The Five Steps Overview

| # | Step | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Identify Dream Outcome** | Anchor on what the customer actually wants — the destination, not the vehicle |
| 2 | **List Problems** | Map every obstacle they'll hit, in insane detail |
| 3 | **List Solutions** | Reverse every problem into a solution-oriented headline |
| 4 | **Create Delivery Vehicles** | Brainstorm every possible way to deliver each solution |
| 5 | **Trim & Stack** | Remove the high-cost / low-value vehicles; bundle, name, and price the rest |

---

## Step 1: Identify Dream Outcome

The first move is to articulate the customer's dream outcome — not what you sell, but where they're going.

> *"I wasn't selling my membership anymore. I wasn't selling the plane flight. I was selling the vacation. When you are thinking about your dream outcome, it has to be them arriving at their destination and what they would like to experience."*

### Hormozi's Original Gym Example

Before: trying to sell $99/mo bootcamps. People said *"LA Fitness is $29/mo. This is expensive."* He couldn't even give it away free because $99/mo afterwards was still too much.

After: shifted from selling memberships to selling the **dream outcome**:

> *"Lose 20lbs in 6 weeks."*
> - Big dream outcome (lose 20lbs)
> - Decreased time delay (6 weeks)

The membership is the *vehicle*. The 20lbs is the *destination*. People buy destinations, not vehicles.

### How To Find The Real Dream Outcome

Push past surface desires:
- *"Lose 20 lbs"* → *"Look at myself in the mirror without flinching"* → *"Wear the swimsuit I haven't worn in 5 years to my sister's wedding"*

The deeper you push (without losing specificity), the more powerful the dream outcome becomes. See [[status-as-deep-driver]] for the deepest layer.

## Step 2: List Problems

Once the dream outcome is locked in, list every problem the prospect will encounter on the path to it. **Channel insane detail.** The more problems you list, the more valuable your eventual offer will be.

### The Method

Think in **sequence**: what does the customer have to do first, then next, then after that? At each step, list the friction.

> *"Think about what happens immediately before and immediately after someone uses your product/service. What's the 'next' thing they need help with?"*

### The Four Problem Buckets (Value Equation Mapped)

Each problem fits into one of the four [[value-equation]] drivers, reversed:

1. **Dream Outcome doubt**: *"This won't be financially worth it"*
2. **Likelihood**: *"It won't work for me specifically. I won't be able to stick with it. External factors will get in my way."* (Most service-specific bucket)
3. **Effort & Sacrifice**: *"This will be too hard, confusing. I won't like it. I will suck at it."*
4. **Time Delay**: *"This will take too much time. I'm too busy. It won't be convenient."*

### Hormozi's Weight Loss Problem List

For each sequential task, list problems against each driver:

**Task 1: Buying healthy food, grocery shopping**
1. Buying healthy food is hard, confusing, I won't like it (Effort)
2. Buying healthy food will take too much time (Time)
3. Buying healthy food is expensive (Dream-Outcome / financial)
4. I won't be able to cook healthy food forever. My family will get in my way. If I travel I won't know what to get. (Likelihood + Effort)

**Task 2: Cooking healthy food**
1. Cooking healthy food is hard. I won't like it. I will suck at it. (Effort)
2. Cooking healthy food takes too much time. (Time)
3. Cooking healthy food is expensive. Not worth it. (Dream-Outcome)
4. Won't be able to keep buying healthy food forever. (Likelihood)

**Task 3: Eating healthy food** — etc.

**Task 4: Exercise regularly** — etc.

### The Result

4 tasks × 4 problem types × 2-4 sub-problems each = **32 to 64 problems total**.

> *"No wonder most people don't achieve their goals. Do not get overwhelmed. This is the best news ever. The more problems you think of, the more problems you get to solve."*

Each problem becomes a sales objection in advance — and a slot in your offer to fill.

---

## Step 3: List Solutions

Transform every problem into a solution-oriented headline. Each one becomes either a deliverable in the offer or a reassurance in the pitch.

### The Transformation Method

For each problem:
1. Ask *"What would I need to show someone to solve this problem?"*
2. Reverse the problem statement
3. Add *"how to..."* in front
4. Make the solution language outcome-focused and aspirational

### Hormozi's Worked Example

| Problem | Solution Headline |
|---|---|
| Buying healthy food is hard, confusing, I won't like it | **How to make buying healthy food easy and enjoyable, so that anyone can do it (especially busy moms!)** |
| Takes too much time | **How to buy healthy food quickly** |
| Is expensive | **How to buy healthy food for less than your current grocery bill** |
| Is unsustainable | **How to make buying healthy food take less effort than buying unhealthy food** |
| Family will get in my way | **How to buy healthy food for you and your family at the same time** |
| Undoable if I travel | **How to get healthy food when traveling** |

Same structure repeats for cooking, eating, exercising, etc.

### Critical Note

> *"You will solve every problem."*

You don't have to actually do the work for them — but you must address every perceived obstacle. The anecdote that proves this: the eating-out guide story (see below).

---

### The Eating-Out Guide Anecdote (Why Every Problem Must Be Solved)

When Hormozi sold weight loss, he initially insisted clients prepare all their food at home. He thought eating out broke diets. So he refused the sale of anyone who couldn't commit to home cooking. He lost many sales as a result.

One month he needed rent money. A business exec walked in — wanted to lose weight, but ate lunch out every day. Normally he'd lose this sale.

Instead, he conceded:

> *"I'll make you an eating out guide for when you go to restaurants so you can eat out 100 percent of the time and still hit your goal. How does that sound?"*

She agreed. He closed the sale. Then he built the eating-out guide.

> *"From that point going forward, whenever someone said 'but what about eating out??' I had the solution."*

> *"Don't get romantic about how you want to solve the problem. Find a way to solve every problem a prospect presents with. When you do that, you make an offer that's so good, people just can't say no."*

This is the operating principle of Step 3: **solve every problem**, even ones you find inconvenient.

## Step 4: Create Delivery Vehicles ("The How")

Now think about all the ways you could actually deliver each solution. This is where divergent thinking matters most. See [[convergent-vs-divergent-thinking]] and [[delivery-cube]].

### The Group-By-Attention-Level Method

For each solution, brainstorm vehicles at three attention levels:

#### One-on-One Vehicles (Example: Buying Healthy Food)
1. In-person grocery shopping — take clients to the store, teach them how to shop
2. Personalized grocery list — teach them to make their own list
3. Full-service shopping — buy food for them, done for them
4. In-person orientation — teach them what to get
5. Text support while shopping — help if they get stuck
6. Phone call while grocery shopping — planned call for direction

#### Small Group Vehicles
1. In-person group grocery shopping
2. Personalized grocery list workshop
3. Group food purchase + delivery
4. In-person small-group orientation

#### One-to-Many Vehicles
1. Live virtual grocery tour (live-streamed)
2. Recorded grocery tour (reusable)
3. DIY grocery calculator tool
4. Pre-determined weekly lists per plan
5. Grocery buddy system (paired customers)
6. Pre-made InstaCart carts for delivery

> *"As you see, the list can really go on and on here. This is just to illustrate the many ways to solve a single problem."*

### The Product Delivery Cheat Codes

If brainstorming gets stuck, vary along these dimensions:

1. **Level of personal attention** — 1-on-1, small group, one-to-many
2. **Level of effort from them** — DIY, DWY (done-with-you), DFY (done-for-you)
3. **Live medium** — in-person, phone, email, text, Zoom, chat
4. **Recording format** — audio, video, written
5. **Reply speed** — 24/7, 9–5, within 5 min, within 1 hour, within 24 hr
6. **10× / 1/10× test** — if customer paid 10× more, what would you add? If 1/10×, how would you still deliver?

These are the brick-exercise variations applied to offers. See [[delivery-cube]] for the full mapping.

### The Critical Rule

> *"It's important that you solve every problem. I can't tell you the amount of times one single item becomes the reason someone doesn't buy."*

Hormozi adds:

> *"Note: You must resolve every obstacle a buyer believes they will have to convert the highest amount of people. That's not to say that if you don't, you won't sell people. Not at all. But you won't sell as many people as you otherwise could have."*

## Step 5: Trim & Stack

Now you have a gigantic list of possible solutions and delivery vehicles. Two sub-steps to make this a coherent offer.

### Step 5a: Trim

Run each delivery vehicle through the cost-to-you / value-to-customer matrix:

1. **High cost, low value** — REMOVE FIRST
2. **Low cost, low value** — REMOVE SECOND
3. **Low cost, high value** — KEEP (these are the magic items)
4. **High cost, high value** — KEEP ONLY WHEN WORTH IT (be selective)

**Value to customer** test: run each item against the [[value-equation]]:
- Will they financially value it?
- Does it make them believe they'll be more likely to succeed?
- Does it make them feel they can do it with less effort and sacrifice?
- Does it help them accomplish the goal in less time?

### The Continuum Question

> *"Just take one step back at a time until you arrive at something that has a time commitment or cost you are willing to live with."*

If a delivery vehicle is valuable but expensive (e.g., flying out and living with the client), ask: *what's a lesser version of this that delivers similar perceived value?* Iterate down the cost without losing the value.

See [[sales-fulfillment-continuum]] for the full continuum logic.

### Step 5b: Stack

Bundle the surviving deliverables into named, priced components.

Each component should:
1. **Solve a specific problem** (link back to the problem list)
2. **Be named with a benefit-oriented title** (see [[magic-naming-formula]])
3. **Have an explicit dollar value ascribed to it**
4. **Read like a complete sub-offer of its own**

### Hormozi's Gym Bundle (Verbatim Example)

The full stacked offer for a gym:

**Buying food** → *Foolproof Bargain Grocery System* — saves hundreds per month, takes less time than current shopping — **$1,000 value**
- 1-on-1 Nutrition Orientation
- Recorded grocery tour
- DIY Grocery Calculator
- Weekly grocery list per plan
- Bargain grocery shopping training
- Grocery Buddy System
- Pre-made InstaCart carts for delivery
- Weekly check-in via text

**Cooking** → *Ready in 5min Busy Parent Cooking Guide* — anyone can eat healthy even with no time — **$600 value (200 hours back per year = 4 weeks of work)**
- 1-on-1 Nutrition Orientation
- Meal Prep Instructions
- DIY Meal Prep Calculator
- Weekly meal prep instructions
- Meal prep buddy system
- Healthy snacks under 5min guide
- Weekly tag-for-feedback post

**Eating** → *Personalized Lick Your Fingers Good Meal Plan* — easier to follow than what you used to "cheat" with, costs less — **$500 value**
- 1-on-1 Nutrition Orientation
- Personalized Meal Plan
- 5min Morning shake guide
- 5min Budget Lunches
- 5min Budget Dinners
- Family-size meals
- Daily picture of meals
- 1-on-1 feedback meeting (and upsell)

**Exercise** → *Fat Burning Workouts Proven To Burn More Fat Than Doing It Alone* — adjusted to your needs so you never go too fast, plateau, or risk injury — **$699 value**

**Traveling** → *The Ultimate Tone Up While You Travel Eating & Workout Blueprint* — amazing workouts with no equipment, no guilt — **$199 value**

**Sticking with it** → *The "Never Fall Off" Accountability System* — works without your permission — **$1,000 value**

**Social life** → *The "Live It Up While Slimming Down" Eating Out System* — eat out and live without feeling like the "odd man out" — **$349 value**

**Total Value: $4,351 — All for only $599.**

> *Author Note: Most of our facilities now sell this bundle for longer periods of time for $2,400 to $5,200. Wild.*

### What The Bundle Achieves

> *"The bundle does three core things:*
> *1. Solves all the perceived problems (not just some)*
> *2. Gives you the conviction that what you're selling is one of a kind (very important)*
> *3. Makes it impossible to compare or confuse your business or offering with the one down the street."*
