# Framework: The M-A-G-I-C Naming Formula
tags: [offers, Hormozi, naming, copywriting, framework, enhancement, offer-fatigue]

## Summary / Definition

> *"Like the tree that falls in the forest that no one hears, having a Grand Slam Offer will not make you money if no one finds out about it. The goal must be that upon hearing about your offer, your ideal prospects are interested enough to take action. Naming it properly is an integral part of this process."*

— Alex Hormozi, *$100M Offers*

The M-A-G-I-C formula is Hormozi's framework for generating offer names that hook attention. It also doubles as the **offer-fatigue refresh system** — change the wrapper without changing the offer to keep lead flow consistent forever.

> **Implicit-egotism effect: we are generally drawn to the things and people that most resemble us.**
>
> (Chapter epigraph — explains why specific avatar-aware naming converts so much better.)

---

## The Framework

**M-A-G-I-C**:

| Letter | Component | Marketing Theory | What It Does |
|---|---|---|---|
| **M** | **Make a Magnetic "Reason Why"** | Attention | Catches the eye and answers *"why this promotion?"* |
| **A** | **Announce Your Avatar** | Discrimination | Calls out who this is for (and isn't) |
| **G** | **Give Them A Goal** | Purpose | Articulates the dream outcome |
| **I** | **Indicate a Time Interval** | Timeline | Sets a duration so prospects can visualize the path |
| **C** | **Complete With A Container Word** | Method | Bundles everything into a system that can't be commoditized |

You don't need all five. Three to five is the sweet spot. *"It's a balance between brevity and specificity."*

---

## M — Make A Magnetic "Reason Why"

Start the name with a word or phrase that tells people the "reason why" you're running this promotion.

> *"I like to tell people to think like a fraternity party planner. When I was in college, we had a party once because a guy got his wisdom teeth removed."*

The "reason why" can be **literally anything**, so long as you believe it. Even a joke works (like the wisdom-teeth party).

The reason should answer:
- *Why are they making this great offer?*
- *Why should I respond to this offer?* / *What's in it for me?*

### Reason-Why Examples
Free, 88% off, Giveaway; Spring, Summer, Back To School; Grand Opening; New Management; New Building; Anniversary; Halloween; New Year.

---

## A — Announce Your Avatar

Call out your ideal avatar: who you're looking for, who you're not.

> *"You want to be as specific as possible, but no more. When in a local area, the more local you can make your headline, the more it will convert."*

> *"So don't do a city, try and go to the sub market, or hyper local area. Not Baltimore but Towson, MD. Not Chicago, but Hinsdale, Etc."*

### Avatar Examples
- Bee Cave Dentists
- Rolling Hills Moms
- Brick & Mortar Businesses
- Salon Owners
- Retired Athletes
- Brooklyn Busy Executives

The implicit-egotism effect runs in the background: people are drawn to language that names them specifically. Generic copy slides off; targeted copy sticks.

This is also the naming-side of [[niche-pricing-power]] — narrower avatar = stronger hook.

## G — Give Them A Goal

Articulate your prospect's dream outcome. Single word or phrase. Can be an event, feeling, experience, or outcome. The more specific and tangible, the better.

### Goal Examples

- Celebrity Smile
- 1st Place
- Never Out Of Breath
- Perfect Product
- Grand Slam Offer
- Little Black Dress
- Double Your Profit
- First Client
- High Ticket
- 7 Figure
- 100k

This is the [[value-equation]] Dream Outcome surfaced in the name itself. See also [[status-as-deep-driver]] for picking the goal language with maximum pull.

## I — Indicate A Time Interval

Tell people the duration to expect — gives an example of how long the result will take.

### Compliance Caveat

> *"If you're making any sort of quantifiable claim (like income gain or weight loss) most platforms will not approve this type of messaging with a stated duration to achievement because it implies a guarantee."*

Don't pair a quantifiable outcome with a duration on platforms that flag it.

**Wrong (for ads):** "$10,000 in 10 days"

**Right (for ads):** "Make Your First Sale in 10 Days"

The first implies a guarantee. The second is process-focused.

### Time Interval Examples
- 4 Hour
- 21 Day
- 6 Week
- 3 Month

Or generic: Minutes, Hours, Days, Weeks, Months.

## C — Complete With A Container Word

The container word denotes that this offer is a **bundle of things put together** — a system that can't be held up to a commoditized alternative.

### Container Word Library

Challenge • Blueprint • Bootcamp • Intensive • Incubator • Masterclass • Program • Detox • Experience • Summit • Accelerator • Fast Track • Shortcut • Sprint • Launch • Slingshot • Catapult • Explosion • System • Getaway • Meetup • Transformation • Mastermind • Game Plan • Deep Dive • Workshop • Comeback • Rebirth • Attack • Assault • Reset • Solution • Hack • Cheatcode • Liftoff

This is the de-commoditization word. When you call it a "Bootcamp" or "Blueprint," it stops looking like a one-line service and starts feeling like a unique, structured experience.

## The Offer Fatigue Variation Order (Critical)

This is one of the most important operational principles in the book.

> *"As you market offers, you will need to create variations over time as the tastes of the market change over time."*

**The order in which to change things to keep lead flow consistent:**

1. **Change the creative** (images, videos in your ads)
2. **Change the body copy** in your ads
3. **Change the headline** — the "wrapper" of your offer
4. *Free 6-Week Lean Challenge → Free 6-Week Tone Challenge*
5. *Holiday Hangover → New Year New You*
6. **Change the duration** of your offer
7. **Change the enhancer** of your offer (free/discount component)
8. **Change the monetization structure**, series of offers, price points (covered in Vol II)

> *"I follow this variation framework because most of the time it's the first handful of items that need to be changed. Typically, they need to be changed again and again without touching anything on the bottom of the list."*

### The Underlying Logic

When ads fatigue, you don't change your business — you change the wrapper.
1. Same business, different video. Run again. Stops working?
2. Same business, different copy. Run again. Stops working?
3. Same business, different headline name. Run again.

> *"Then, and only then, would you change the wrapper."*

> *"The lower on the list you go, the more operationally heavy it is, so really be sure you have exhausted the earlier 'lighter' ways of varying your offer."*

### The Don't-Change-What's-Working Principle

> *"Once you've monetized an offer, rarely should you change it. Just rinse and repeat over and over and over again. This can be hard because we are entrepreneurs and love change. Change here usually just creates inefficiency and operational drag, costing you money. No bueno."*

> *"So use your entrepreneurial ADD on the 'wrapper' first — the 'look and feel' of the offer (copy, creative, headlines). Then change the seasonality of the offer. Then change the duration. If you're still stuck, change what you are giving away for free or discount. Change the entire machine behind it only as a last resort and for a darn good reason."*

## How To Get Initial Traction

> *"Try the offer structure and headline that you think has the highest likelihood of working. Then stick with it. And if they don't convert at first, don't worry. You'll get better."*

> *"Oftentimes, if you are using these types of models, many of them will work. In that case, stick with the one that gives you the highest return. You can also rotate between offers if it doesn't create lots of operational drag for your type of business."*

> *"This is the ultimate position of power. You have multiple 'aces in the hole' that you can play at any time, which keeps your marketing converting at an even higher level."*

## Local Business Marketing — Why The Variation Matters So Much

Local marketing is both easier and harder than national.

> *"It's easier to get to work, but harder to keep working or scale."*

**Why easier:** In local markets, there's trust in the familiar. Selling in-person at higher prices is inherently easier — you convert a much higher percentage of leads.

**Why harder:** Offers fatigue rapidly because there's only a limited radius the local business can serve. The TAM (Total Addressable Market) is the immediate radius. The smaller the radius, the faster offers fatigue.

> *"Learning to rapidly variate my offers, headlines, and creative when I had my local businesses was a cornerstone skill that made my expansion to national level advertising much easier for me."*

> *"So if you are in a local market, just remember you aren't going to change the value stack of your offer. You are just going to change the way it looks to the marketplace in your marketing."*

## Quick-Start Worksheet

When naming any new offer:

1. **Pick a Reason Why** (1 word or phrase) — *Free, Spring, Grand Opening, 88% Off*
2. **Identify Avatar** (1-2 words) — *Lakeway Moms, B2B Founders, Salon Owners*
3. **State the Goal** (specific dream outcome) — *Celebrity Smile, First Client, 7 Figures*
4. **Add Time Interval** (be careful with quantifiable claims on ads) — *21 Days, 6 Week, 4 Hour*
5. **Pick a Container Word** — *Blueprint, Bootcamp, Intensive, Sprint, System*
6. **Try rhyme or alliteration** — *don't force it*
7. **Test 2-3 names** against each other
8. **Note the winner. Test new names against that as the control.**

## The Power Of "Free" And Discount Reasons

Compliance permitting, "Free" and percentage-off framings are some of the most magnetic reasons-why. They answer *"why should I respond?"* instantly. Pair them with everything else (avatar + goal + interval + container) for the full effect.
