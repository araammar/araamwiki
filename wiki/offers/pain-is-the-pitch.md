# Concept: Pain Is The Pitch
tags: [offers, Hormozi, sales, copywriting, persuasion, pain-points]

## Summary / Definition

Hormozi's sales-team mantra. The principle that **how clearly you articulate the prospect's pain back to them determines whether they buy** — not how well you describe your solution. Articulation comes first; persuasion follows.

> *"The pain is the pitch."*

> *"If you can articulate the pain a prospect is feeling accurately, they will almost always buy what you are offering. A prospect must have a painful problem for us to solve and charge money for our solution."*

— Alex Hormozi, *$100M Offers*

---

## The Underlying Principle

> *"The point of good writing is for the reader to understand. The point of good persuasion is for the prospect to feel understood."*

Selling isn't about being more eloquent than the prospect. It's about reflecting their internal experience back to them with such precision that they think *"this person finally gets it."* Once that happens, the rest is almost automatic.

---

## Why It Works — The Mechanism

A prospect's purchasing decision flows through these stages:
1. **Do they recognize they have a problem?** (Often the hardest hurdle — many prospects haven't named it.)
2. **Does my solution match the problem they recognize?** (Relevance.)
3. **Is the discomfort of buying lower than the discomfort of staying the same?** (Trade-off.)

Articulating pain accurately compresses all three:
- It names the problem clearly, often more clearly than the prospect ever has
- It signals you understand them deeply enough to be trusted with a solution
- It re-amplifies the discomfort of *not* buying, increasing urgency

---

## Connection To The Value Equation

The four drivers (see [[value-equation]]) live in the prospect's perception. If they don't perceive the pain, they don't perceive value in solving it.

> *"The degree of the pain will be proportional to the price you will be able to charge."*

When prospects hear the solution to their pain — and inversely what their life looks like without the pain — they should be drawn to your solution.

This is why Hormozi lists **Massive Pain** as **Indicator #1** in [[starving-crowd-market-selection]]. Without pain, every other piece falls apart.

## The Brooke Castillo Example (Relationship Coaching)

Brooke Castillo, who runs an enormous life-coaching business, was building a 90-Day Relationship course offer. She used the same problems-solutions list framework Hormozi teaches (see [[5-step-grand-slam-creation]]).

> *"Don't be fancy. Just get all the problems down then turn them into solutions."*

Listing pain in obsessive detail is how the pitch gets built. Each pain becomes a bullet. Each bullet becomes a piece of the offer. Each piece of the offer becomes proof that you understood.

## Connection To Starbridge — The 80% Stat

The cold-call success of [[starbridge-product-analysis]] is **pain-is-the-pitch in action**. Micah's opener walks through the prospect's current situation:

> *"You're spending all your time responding to RFPs and submitting bids — but 80% of those RFPs are pre-written for a selected vendor before they're even published."*

Three things happen in one sentence:
1. **Pain articulated** — your current effort is partly wasted
2. **A specific stat** — credibility transfer (see [[facts-over-adjectives]])
3. **Inversion of the dream outcome** — your life if you skip the rigged 80% game and get in before it's decided

This is exactly the mechanism the book describes — articulate the pain with surgical precision before pitching anything.

See [[starbridge-colandrum-parallel]] for the explicit note that Colandrum needs its version of this stat.

## The Discomfort-As-Trigger Framing

> *"Once the discomfort of staying the same surpasses the discomfort of change, they take the leap."*

This is the entire job of the pain articulation step. Your prospect's status quo has invisible costs they've stopped noticing. Re-illuminate them. The buying decision is the moment their current pain exceeds the friction of acting.

Phrased differently: people don't buy at the peak of dream outcome desire. They buy at the peak of dissatisfaction with their current state.

---

## Pain-First Sales Sequence

Putting it together as a sequence:

1. **Pre-qualify** — confirm they fall in your ICP / have the pain
2. **Articulate the pain** — describe their current experience back to them, more vividly than they would themselves
3. **Show the inverse** — what life looks like without this pain
4. **Present the bridge** — your offer
5. **Reverse the risk** — guarantee
6. **Ask for the decision**

This is the bone structure of every successful cold call in [[micah-cold-call-system]] and every successful pitch in $100M Offers.

## Pain-Articulation Templates

Templates Hormozi uses or implies:

- "*Right now you're probably [current pain state] — and the worst part isn't [obvious cost], it's [hidden secondary cost the prospect hadn't fully realized].*"
- "*Most people in your position [common but wrong response]. What they don't realize is [reframe that turns the response into part of the problem].*"
- "*Imagine if 30 days from now, [reverse of pain state] — no more [specific friction], just [tangible better state].*"

The plastic surgeon ad copy from the book is a textbook example: *"Tired of wasting countless hours in the gym . . . tired of trying diets that just don't work?"* — that's effort + time-delay + likelihood pain all in one sentence.

---

## Why Logical Solutions Fail (And Pain Articulation Wins)

A logical solution to a problem typically has been tried — that's why the problem still exists. The remaining lever is psychological. See [[logical-vs-psychological-solutions]].

Articulating pain is a psychological move, not a logical one. The prospect already *knew* they had the pain. You're not informing them — you're making the pain emotionally present, which moves them across the action threshold.

---

## Application — Building A Pain Articulation Library

Per Hormozi's offer-creation method (see [[5-step-grand-slam-creation]]):

1. Pick your dream outcome
2. List every problem the prospect encounters **before, during, and after** working with you
3. List them in **insane detail** — the more granular, the better
4. Group them by the four Value Equation drivers (Dream Outcome doubt / Likelihood / Time / Effort)
5. Each item becomes a pain articulation line you can use in copy, scripts, ads, demos
6. The longer the list, the more touchpoints your pitch lands

This is the raw material for both your offer (the solutions to each pain) **and** your pitch (the language that articulates each pain).
