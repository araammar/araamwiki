# Concept: Niche Pricing Power
tags: [offers, Hormozi, niche, pricing, positioning, Dan Kennedy]

## Summary / Definition

The narrower the niche, the higher the price you can charge for the same underlying product. Hormozi (via Dan Kennedy) demonstrates this with a single example that shows a **100×** price increase from identical core content — just by progressively tightening the audience.

> **"Riches Are In The Niches."**

---

## The $19 → $2,000 Stair-Step (Dan Kennedy's Time Management Example)

A generic course on **Time Management** has no compelling unique angle. The market values it at: **$19**.

Now apply progressive niching:

| Level | Product | Price | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Time Management | **$19** | Generic. Forgettable. Anyone could write this. |
| 1 | Time Management for Sales Professionals | **$99** | Sales people are specific. One extra deal could justify the cost. |
| 2 | Time Management for B2B Outbound Sales Reps | **$499** | Higher-ticket sales context. A single deal nets ~$500+ commission. Easy to justify. **25× the original.** |
| 3 | Time Management for B2B Outbound Power Tools & Gardening Sales Reps | **$1,000–$2,000** | A power tools sales rep thinks: *"This is made exactly for me."* Happily pays. **~100× the original.** |

The actual content can be identical to the generic $19 course. But:
- The sales messaging speaks directly to them
- They feel understood
- They get more real value from the same content because they apply it
- They have stronger conviction it will work

This is identical to Hormozi's fitness example: **a generic weight-loss program priced at $19 vs. a fitness program designed and marketed only to shift-nurses priced at $1,997** — even though the core program is essentially the same (eat less, move more).

---

## Why It Works — The Mechanism

Niching down activates **all four drivers of the Value Equation** simultaneously (see [[value-equation]]):

1. **Dream Outcome** — the prospect can vividly see their *specific* dream (closing more outbound power-tools sales, looking great after night shifts as a nurse). Specificity sharpens the picture.
2. **Perceived Likelihood of Achievement** — *"this was built for someone like me"* → certainty
3. **Time Delay** — case studies show their exact peer hitting the result in X weeks
4. **Effort & Sacrifice** — *"the examples will already be relevant, I don't have to translate"*

The "wantrepreneur of one" feeling collapses every objection.

---

## The Magic Sentence

Hormozi distills the niching identity sentence as:

> *"I solve **this type of problem** for **this specific type of person** in this **unique counter-intuitive way** that **reverses their deepest fear**."*

Each variable can be tightened to ratchet up the niching power.

---

## How Hormozi Did It (Gym Launch)

Before this realization, Hormozi was doing acquisition for "anyone who will pay me" — typical broad consulting positioning.

After internalizing Kennedy's lesson, he niched all the way down:

> *"In my instance, I decided on a microgym owner with ~100 members, a signed lease, at least one employee, and wanted to help clients lose weight."*

How specific:
- **Type of business**: microgym (not gym chain)
- **Size**: ~100 members
- **Status**: signed lease (not pre-launch)
- **Team**: at least one employee
- **Goal of their clients**: weight loss

That's *brutally* specific compared to "small business owners" — and Gym Launch turned down anyone who wasn't that avatar. No personal trainers. No online coaches.

**Could he have helped them?** Of course. The majority of Hormozi's portfolio is non-gym companies. But:
> *"In order to maintain product focus, and high converting messaging, knowing exactly who the product was for was a game changer. It helped us know exactly who we were speaking to at all times. And exactly whose problems we were solving."*

This was the leverage point that took Gym Launch from $500k/year to $28M/yr off less than $1M in ad spend.

## The Common Trap — "I'll Lose Customers"

The natural fear: "If I niche down, I'll exclude potential buyers and make less money."

The reality: niching gets you **more** customers, at **higher prices**, with **lower** cost-per-acquisition because your messaging finally resonates.

> *"You want to be 'the guy' who services 'this type of person' or solves 'this type of problem.'"*

The exclusion is the feature. Anyone outside the niche doesn't buy, but anyone *inside* the niche feels seen — and pays a premium for being seen.

---

## When To Broaden (The Caveat)

Hormozi explicitly notes: for most readers under $10M/year, niching makes more money.

After $10M, it depends on TAM (Total Addressable Market). A business can only grow to meet its total addressable market.

For context: many companies expanded to $30M+/year serving a single niche — chiropractors, gyms, plumbers, solar, roofers, salon owners. If you're at $1M–$3M thinking you've capped, you haven't. You need to be better, not broader.

Getting to $10M/year is already a top 0.4% achievement (1 in 250 businesses). So **99.6% of business owners** should be niching down, not up.

---

## Decision Framework

When tempted to be broader, ask:
1. **Can I describe my customer in one specific sentence?** (Avatar test)
2. **Does my marketing copy specifically address them?** (Resonance test)
3. **Could a competitor's broader copy beat mine on their narrow niche?** (Specificity advantage test)
4. **Am I at $10M+ already?** (TAM ceiling test — only here does broadening usually help)

---

## The Bigger Pricing Implication

This is why Hormozi's pricing arguments only fully cash out **after** niching. You cannot reasonably charge $1,997 for a generic weight-loss program — that's not a fair value-to-price ratio in the market. But you absolutely can for "Shift-Nurse Sleep-Reset & Energy Protocol" — because the value is specifically delivered.

> *"Reason: you can literally charge 100x more for the exact same product."*

Niching is the highest-leverage pricing lever in the book.
