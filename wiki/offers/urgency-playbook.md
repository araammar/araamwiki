# Framework: The Urgency Playbook
tags: [offers, Hormozi, urgency, FOMO, deadlines, enhancement]

## Summary / Definition

> *"Scarcity is a function of quantity. Urgency is a function of time."*

— Alex Hormozi, *$100M Offers*

Urgency limits **when** people can sign up, not **how many** can. Scarcity and urgency are frequently used together, but they're distinct levers.

Hormozi's central urgency claim: **"Deadlines drive decisions."**

---

## The Four Urgency Methods

1. **Cohort-Based Rolling Urgency**
2. **Rolling Seasonal Urgency**
3. **Pricing or Bonus-Based Urgency**
4. **Exploding Opportunity**

Each is suitable for different business models. All four employ urgency ethically — no fake countdown timers, no manufactured fear.

---

## Method 1: Cohort-Based Rolling Urgency

If you start clients on a regular cadence (weekly, biweekly, monthly, quarterly), you can use the cadence itself as urgency.

### The Standard Pitch
> *"If you sign up today, I can get you in with our next group that kicks off on Monday, otherwise you'll have to wait until our next kickoff date."*

### The Juiced-Up Version
> *"I actually had a client who signed up a few weeks ago drop out, so I have an opening for our next cohort that kicks off on Monday. If you are pretty sure you're gonna do this sooner or later, might as well get in on it now so you can start reaping the rewards sooner rather than paying the same and waiting."*

### Why It Works
> *"Those two tweaks above have pushed so many sales over the edge by just reminding a potential customer that if they sign up, they will be starting on Monday, and if they do not, they will have to wait a week."*

The less frequently you kick off, the more powerful the urgency. Starting clients every other week (or once a month) creates a real, recurring deadline.

### Handling The "What If I Lose Sales?" Fear

> *"Just like guarantees, there is always a fear that you will make less money by employing this strategy. We are afraid that we will lose sales we would have otherwise made. Every experienced marketer on the planet will tell you — it is a fear, and it is unfounded."*

The data point Hormozi cites:
> *"The biggest sales on a week long campaign or launch happen in the last 4 hours of the last day (up to 50-60%). That means that the last 3% of the time allotted creates 50-60% of the sales . . . that's completely illogical, but also unmistakably human."*

So like a guarantee, you make more money from people who decide to take action than you lose from people who actually miss out — because those people were never going to buy.

### What If Someone Wants In But Just Missed The Cohort?

Two options:

**Option 1 (Hormozi's least preferred):** Offer them a speedy personalized onboarding as a "bonus" for signing up today, and still get them in.

**Option 2 (Hormozi's preferred):** Explain that since the next group starts in a while, they'll have:
- More time to review materials
- Time to talk to their employees (B2B) or family members (B2C)
- A more extended payment plan you can only make available because the start date is so far out
- An advantage most clients do not get

> *"In the end, remember, you always have the advantage because you are the one who calls the shots."*

## Method 2: Rolling Seasonal Urgency

In a digital setting, real countdown deadlines work — but they must be *real*. If they aren't, you lose credibility and look like every other wannabe marketer.

### The Mechanic

Hormozi loves having the dates of his promotion visible everywhere — landing pages, ads, copy.

> *"You can always fire up another ad campaign and a new landing page with new dates and be right as rain. You will see your conversions go through the roof, and it takes maybe five minutes of editing — well worth the time investment."*

### The Year-Long Rolling Calendar

Same underlying offer. New seasonal wrapper each month.

| Month | Promotion Name |
|---|---|
| January | New Year Promotion ends Jan 30! |
| February | Valentine's Lovers Promo Ends Feb 30! |
| March | Sexy By Spring Special Ends March 31! |
| April | Fools in Love April Promo Ends April 30! |

> *"The actual promotion may be the same, but naming it something different 'by season' gives you a 'real' differentiator that gives you a start and a finish. Deadlines drive decisions. By simply having these, you can point to them and let human beings push themselves over the edge so as not to miss out."*

### Pro Tip: Local Businesses

> *"This is my number one strategy for local businesses. They must vary their marketing more frequently than national advertisers. Putting a new wrapper with a date on the same core service gives you urgency and novelty that will consistently outperform the 'same old' campaigns."*

See [[magic-naming-formula]] for how to vary the wrapper while keeping the core offer unchanged.

## Method 3: Pricing or Bonus-Based Urgency

Using your actual offer or promotion/pricing structure as the thing that could be missed.

> *"Yes, let's get you started today so you can take advantage of the discount you came in for. I'm not sure how long we will be running it as we change them every 4 weeks or so, and this is one of the better ones we have run in a while."*

What's being made urgent isn't the *service* (a roofer will still install your roof later) — it's the **promotion**, **discount**, or **bonus**. This lets businesses that sell year-round still use urgency.

### Variations
- Pricing promotion expires
- Discount expires
- Added bonuses (free install, free onboarding, extra workshop valued at $X) expire
- These can be swapped around your core offer to create urgency without compromising integrity

### Pro Tip: Clean Your Pipeline With Every Price Change

> *"If you ever really are planning on raising your prices (hopefully soon if you are reading this book!) then you can always clean out your pipeline by letting people know 'The price is going up! So get in now!' Never raise your prices without letting people know."*

> *"It shows a position of strength and will give you a nice little influx of cash from the people in the pipeline who were on the fence."*

This is a beautifully practical operating tactic: every planned price hike doubles as a sales acceleration event.

## Method 4: Exploding Opportunity

On occasion, you'll expose a prospect to an arbitrage opportunity. The opportunity itself has a ticking clock — every second of delay means missed gains.

### Hormozi's Example
> *"If I was explaining an arbitrage opportunity between buying products on eBay and selling them on Amazon, this market inefficiency would over time correct itself. The sooner someone acts the better it will be for them."*

### Where Exploding Opportunities Appear
- Crypto trading opportunities
- New advertising platforms before competitors flood in
- Stock buying windows
- Job offers (where pay/bonuses decrease the longer you wait)
- Early-mover advantages in any emerging market

### How To Frame It

The frame: *"This won't always be possible. The window is closing. Acting now captures gains that won't be available later."*

> *"All of these examples show opportunities that decay with time, so if you find yourself in front of an opportunity like this, make sure to emphasize it!"*

---

## When To Use Each Method

| Method | Best For | Cadence |
|---|---|---|
| **Cohort-Based** | Service businesses, courses, programs | Weekly to quarterly |
| **Rolling Seasonal** | Year-round retail, local businesses, repeat-promotion businesses | Monthly |
| **Pricing/Bonus** | Service businesses that sell year-round | Anytime |
| **Exploding Opportunity** | Anything with a real time-decay component | One-off |

## Combining Scarcity And Urgency

The most powerful enhancement layer is scarcity + urgency stacked:

> *"Only 5 spots available for our March cohort — starting March 1. Lock yours by Feb 25."*

- Scarcity (only 5 spots)
- Urgency (deadline Feb 25)
- Real cohort cadence
- Real start date

Either alone moves the needle. Together they compound. See [[scarcity-playbook]] for the supply side.

## The Operating Mantra

> *"Adding a deadline and incorporating one or multiple forms of urgency will get more people to take action than would otherwise. I have employed all four of these methods with great effectiveness. I suggest you do the same."*

---

## Application Checklist

For any offer or promotion, ask:
1. Is there a natural cohort cadence I can lean into?
2. Is there a seasonal hook I can wrap around the same offer?
3. Is there a price increase or bonus expiration I can make explicit?
4. Is there a time-decaying opportunity I should be highlighting?

The right answer is usually *yes* to at least one. Use it.
