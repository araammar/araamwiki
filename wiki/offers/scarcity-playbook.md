# Framework: The Scarcity Playbook
tags: [offers, Hormozi, scarcity, FOMO, supply-demand, enhancement]

## Summary / Definition

> *"Scarcity is one of the most powerful and least understood forces to unlock unlimited pricing power. If you want to learn how to sell air for millions of dollars, then pay attention."*

— Alex Hormozi, *$100M Offers*

Scarcity is a **function of quantity**. By limiting the supply (or perceived supply) of your offer, you increase demand and price simultaneously — without changing the underlying product.

The psychological lever: **fear of loss is stronger than desire for gain**. Humans are far more motivated to take action to hoard a scarce resource than they are to act on something that could merely help them.

---

## How Scarcity Works (The Mechanism)

When supply is fixed or limited:
- Each unit appears more rare
- Each unit feels more valuable by association
- The unsatisfied prospects' desire intensifies (see [[delicate-dance-of-desire]])
- The action threshold drops (people buy faster to avoid missing out)
- Social proof compounds when others see the scarcity confirmed (sold out)

---

## Who Already Profits From Scarcity (Implied Demand)

> *"The reason an authority (like a doctor), a celebrity (like Oprah), or a celebrity authority (like Dr. Oz or Dr. Phil) can charge egregious rates is because of implied demand. People assume that there is a lot of demand for their time, and, therefore, not a big supply of it. As a result, it must be expensive."*

Authority + celebrity = implied scarcity. You can engineer the same implied scarcity in your business without being a celebrity.

---

## The Three Types Of Scarcity

1. **Limited Supply of Seats/Slots** (in general or over X period of time)
2. **Limited Supply of Bonuses**
3. **Never Available Again**

Each type can be applied to either physical products or services.

---

## Scarcity For Physical Products

### The Mechanic
Limited releases. *"This month, we are releasing 100 boxes of mint chocolate cookie flavored protein bars."*

You can limit by:
- Flavor
- Color
- Design
- Size
- Quantity per period

### The Two Critical Rules

**Rule 1: Always sell out.** It's better to sell out consistently than over-order and fail to create scarcity. Once a month is the sweet spot for most companies that do this regularly.

**Rule 2: Announce that you sold out.** This is what makes the tactic compound.
- Even people who were on the fence see the sell-out
- Social proof: other people thought it was worth it
- The choice has been made for them — they now desire it *more* because they can't have it
- Next time you offer it, they'll be far more likely to buy

> *"This method stacks in effectiveness if it is done repeatedly over time (just not too often). Once a month seems to be the sweet spot."*

### The Chanel Example

> *"Chanel, a brand that has maintained insane margins and pricing for over a century, is a master of scarcity. They send only 1-2 of each piece to each store so every store has a different selection and every item is the last or second to last item in stock. This allows them to price far above market and turn buying impulses into purchases."*

Every Chanel shopper sees nearly-empty inventory. Every piece feels like the last one. The brand never has a "stocked shelf" feeling — it's always artificially constrained.

## Scarcity For Services

Trickier than products because services are theoretically infinitely replicable (you can always take on one more client). But Hormozi gives three repeatable methods.

### Method 1: Total Business Cap

*"Only accepting X clients. Period."*

You publicly cap the total number of clients you'll ever take. Example: *"My agency only services 25 customers total."*

What happens:
- Waiting list forms
- When a slot opens, prospects jump in immediately
- Price resistance disappears (they're grateful to get a slot at any price)
- You can periodically increase capacity by 10-20%, then re-cap
- Works best for highest tiers or service levels

Over time, you can increase prices and squeeze the lower-performing accounts out, bringing in more profitable accounts.

### Method 2: Growth Rate Cap

*"Only accepting X clients per week."*

> *"We only accept 5 new clients per week and we already have the first 3 spots taken. I have 6 more calls this week, so you can take the spot or one of my next calls and you can wait until we reopen."*

Hormozi has used this since the beginning of his business:
> *"I always knew what my capacity was per week, and simply chose to let our prospects know how many openings we had left. This banks on the fact that you can only handle a certain amount of new clients anyways, on a regular basis, so you might as well let them know it."*

### Method 3: Cohort Cap

*"Only accepting X clients per class or cohort."*

> *"We take on 100 clients 4 times a year. We open the doors then close them."*

This has the operational bonus of helping you build a choreographed onboarding experience. Great for scaling.

## Pro Tip: Limited Access For Higher Ticket Services

> *"These scarcity tactics work especially well for higher ticket upsells. If you want to create one off workshops, trainings, events, seminars, consulting, etc. These are things that by their nature take time and provide more access. Pairing them with clear scarcity or fixed amounts, seats, or spots will rapidly drive up the demand."*

> *"But always remember: have less spots available than you think you can sell . . . so that when you want to do it again in the future, everyone will remember you sold out . . . fast. This is a compounding strategy that increases in effectiveness over time. One of the few in the marketing arsenal."*

## Honest Scarcity (The Most Ethical Form)

> *"The easiest scarcity strategy is honesty."*

You probably can't handle 1,000 clients tomorrow. But how many *can* you handle? 5? 10? 25? Define that number, then advertise it.

Simply letting people know:
- *"We're three-fourths of the way to capacity this week"* — moves people over the edge
- *"We're 81% to capacity in our total business"* — drives signups

The closer you get to your arbitrary fullness, the faster the spots disappear (social proof + scarcity compounding). **But only you get to draw the line where "full" is.**

> *"Scarcity also implies within it, social proof. If you are 81% to capacity then a decent amount of people made the decision to work with you."*

## Scarcity For Free Lead Magnets

A free checklist with no scarcity is "neat." A free checklist with weekly download caps becomes coveted.

> *"If I told you I have it set so that every week the page only allows twenty new people to download it, you'd be far more likely to go see if you can grab it. And even more so if when you try it, you see that it has already run out for the week. Result? You join a list that notifies you the next time twenty more checklists become available for download."*

By controlling supply:
- Free becomes desirable
- People who get access actually consume it (because they earned it)
- A waiting-list audience builds

## Extreme Scarcity (Pro Tip)

> *"If you don't hate money, sell a very limited supply of 1-on-1 access. You can do that via any of the mediums described in 'Delivery Cube.' Direct message access. Email Access. Phone access. Voice memo access. Zoom access. Etc."*

> *"I promise you this — if you want to immediately make a lot of money, create a very exclusive service level based on access to you (yes, unscalable), that you cap at a tiny number. Price it very high. Then, tell people. You will make more money than you thought possible."*

> *"These also tend to be some of the best clients. And limit your delivery to something that you don't hate. For me, I hate emails and messages but don't mind zoom calls. Make it work for your working style. The cream of the crop (the 1% of 1% will adjust and take action)."*

## "Once You're Out, You Can Never Come Back" (Pro Tip)

> *"You can create scarcity by also capping your service level and saying that if they leave then can never return. This type of scarcity makes people think extra hard about leaving."*

Hormozi started doing this with his gyms early on. Then he was in a mastermind that employed it. Then he started using it in his higher level Gym Lords offering.

**Works best with small groups.** As groups become much bigger, the tactic loses some teeth (he speaks from experience).

## The Arnold Schwarzenegger Fundraiser (Scarcity Doctrine In Action)

May 2019. Arnold's home. After School All Stars fundraiser.

George (a major luxury watch dealer and major donor) gave the charity CEO Ben the advice that became operating doctrine:

> *"When demand increases, cut supply."*

The result: Ticket price went from $15,000 to $25,000. Fewer tickets sold. Total raised: **$5,400,000 from 100 people** — most successful fundraiser in the charity's history.

Each auction item was sold as "one-of-a-kind — if you miss it, you'll never have a chance again." Arnold even added bonuses when bidding got high enough so that both bidders could win — the charity captured both donations.

> *"People want what they can't have. People want what other people want. People want things only a select few have access to."*

This is scarcity, urgency, and bonuses fused into one mechanism — driving premium pricing on items that wouldn't have sold for a fraction at a different venue.

## The Operating Principle

> *"Employ one or multiple methods of scarcity in your business. You will drive a faster purchasing decision from your prospects, and at higher prices. Just let them know your limits and let psychology do the rest."*

---

## When To Use Each Method

| Method | Best For |
|---|---|
| Total Business Cap | Premium service tiers, high-touch consulting, agencies |
| Growth Rate Cap | Recurring service businesses with steady inflow |
| Cohort Cap | Course/program businesses, structured onboarding |
| Limited Product Releases | E-commerce, supplements, physical goods |
| Lead Magnet Scarcity | Top-of-funnel offers that have grown too easy to access |
| Extreme 1-on-1 Scarcity | Already established experts looking to maximize per-customer profit |
| Never Come Back | Small mastermind / community offerings |
