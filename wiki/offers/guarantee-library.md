# Framework: The Guarantee Library
tags: [offers, Hormozi, guarantees, risk-reversal, Jason Fladlien, enhancement]

## Summary / Definition

> *"The single greatest objection for any product or service being sold is . . . drum roll . . . risk."*

— Alex Hormozi, *$100M Offers*

The guarantee is how you remove risk. Hormozi cites Jason Fladlien's claim that the conversion on an offer can move **2-4×** simply by changing the quality of the guarantee. It's that important.

> *"You will want to spend a disproportionate amount of time figuring out how you want to reverse it."*

This page is the canonical library of guarantee types and templates from the book.

---

## The 4 Types Of Guarantees

1. **Unconditional** — pay first, trial, refund if unhappy
2. **Conditional** — refund/extra service IF certain conditions are met
3. **Anti-Guarantee** — explicitly "all sales are final" with a strong reason why
4. **Implied Guarantee** — performance-based pricing (revshare, profit share, etc.)

> *"You must always hit your guarantee hard, even if you don't have one. Say it boldly and give the reason why."*

---

## The Refund-Math Reality Check

Many business owners avoid bold guarantees out of fear of refund spike. The math:

> *"If you close 130 percent as many people, and your refund percentage doubles from 5 percent to 10 percent, you've still made 1.23x the money, or 23 percent more, and that all goes to the bottom line."*

Worked out:
- **Old:** 100 sales, 5 refunds (5%) = 95 Net Sales
- **Guarantee Offer:** 130 sales, 13 refunds (10%) = 117 Net Sales
- 117 ÷ 95 = **1.23× (23% increase)**

> *"Don't be emotional, just do the math."*

For a guarantee to *not* be worth it, the increase in sales would have to be 100% offset by the increase in refunds — i.e., a 5% absolute increase in sales would need to be offset by a 5% absolute increase in refunds (a doubling of the refund rate, which is unlikely).

## The Bad-Customer Warning

> *"While guarantees can be effective sellers, people who buy because of guarantees can become very shitty customers."*

A person who only buys because of a guarantee may not be willing to do the work to succeed. The solution: tie your guarantee to the actions they need to do to succeed (a conditional guarantee — see below).

### High-Cost Services Warning (Pro Tip)
> *"If you have a tremendous amount of cost associated with your product or service, you will likely want to employ a conditional guarantee or an ANTI guarantee, as you will have to eat the cost of the refund AND the cost of fulfilling."*

---

## The Conditional-Statement Structure

> *"What makes a guarantee have power is a conditional statement: **If you do not get X result in Y time period, we will Z.**"*

> *"Without the 'or what' portion of the guarantee, it sounds weak and diluted."*

**Bad example:** "We will get you 20 clients guaranteed."

**Better:** "You will get 20 clients in your first 30 days, or we give you your money back + your advertising dollars spent with us."

The teeth come from the consequence to *you* if they don't succeed.

## TYPE 1: UNCONDITIONAL GUARANTEES

The strongest form. Basically a paid trial — they pay first, then decide if they like it.

> *"These get a LOT more people to buy, but you will have some people refund, especially as consumer culture continues to shift towards entitlement and zero accountability."*

### Variant 1A: "No Questions Asked" Refund Guarantee

What the client gets (options):
- A) Full refund
- B) 50% refund
- C) Refund of their ad spend and any ancillary costs incurred
- D) You pay for a competitor's program instead
- E) You return their money plus an additional $1,000

**Hormozi's take:** Simple. Risky. *"You put yourself in a situation where if someone does not achieve the results, whether because of your fault or not, you will still be held accountable. Obviously this is a strong, but unoriginal guarantee."*

### Jason Fladlien's Unconditional Guarantee Wording (Verbatim)

Hormozi includes Fladlien's actual pitch verbatim:

> *"I'm not asking you to decide yes or no today . . . I'm asking you to make a fully informed decision, that is all. The only way you can make a fully informed decision is on the inside, not the outside. So you get on the inside and see if everything we say on this webinar is true and valuable to you. Then, if it is, that's when you decide to keep it. If it's not for you, no hard feelings. You will then, after signing up at URL, be able to make a fully informed decision that this isn't for you. But you can't make this decision right now for the same reason you don't buy a house without first looking at the inside of it. And know this . . . whether it's 29 min or 29 days from now . . . if you ain't happy, I ain't happy. For any reason whatsoever, if you want your money back you can get it because I only want to keep your money if you're happy. All you have to do is go to support@xyz.com and tell us 'gimme my money' and you got it, and in short order — our response times to any support request average 61min over a 24/7 time period. You can only make such a guarantee when you're confident that what you have is the real deal and I'm fairly confident that when you sign up at URL you're getting exactly what you need to BENEFIT."*

Note the elements: reframe as "fully informed decision" → trust-building → easy mechanic → reverse risk → confidence assertion.

### Variant 1B: Satisfaction-Based Refund Guarantee

What the client gets: If at any time they're not satisfied with the level of service, they can request a refund at any time.

**Hormozi's take (verbatim — this was his actual weight-loss guarantee):**

> *"Besides being an irresistible offer, I guaranteed satisfaction. I used the strength of my guarantee to close a lot of deals."*

> *"Do you think I'd still be in business if I gave a crazy guarantee like that and wasn't good at what I did? Now I'm not guaranteeing you're going to hit this goal in six weeks, after all, because I can't eat the food for you. But I am guaranteeing that you will get $500 worth of value and service from us to support you. If you don't feel like we gave you that level of service, I'll write you a check the day you tell me we suck."*

### The Best-Case/Worst-Case Close (Verbatim)

> *"Best case you get the body of your dreams and we give you all your money towards staying with us to hit your long-term goal. Worst case you tell me I suck, I write you a check, and you get six weeks of free training. Both options are risk free. But, the only thing guaranteed not to help you is walking out of here today."*

> *"That line made me a lot of money. I had two people take me up on it out of 4,000 sales in three and a half years."*

The "best case / worst case both work for you" framing eliminates the prospect's only escape hatch: indecision.

### Pro Tip: Name Your Guarantee Something Cool

> *"If you are going to give a guarantee, spice it up. Instead of using 'satisfaction' or some other 'vanilla' word, describe it more strongly."*

- Generic (bad): 30 Day Money Back Satisfaction Guarantee
- Creative #1 (good): *"In 30 days, if you wouldn't jump into shark infested waters to get our product back, we will return every dollar you paid."*
- Creative #2 (great): *"You'll get our famous 'Club a Baby Seal Guarantee' — After 30 days of using our services, if you wouldn't club a baby seal to stay on as a customer, you don't have to pay a penny."*

### Pro Tip: Unconditional vs Conditional Based On Business Type

> *"Bigger broader guarantees work better with lower ticket B2C businesses (many people just won't bother taking the time). The higher the ticket, and the more business oriented it is, the more you want to steer towards specific guarantees. That may or may not include refunds, and may or may not have conditions."*

## TYPE 2: CONDITIONAL GUARANTEES

Include "terms and conditions." You can get very creative here. In general, you want these to be **"better than money back"** guarantees, because the customer made an investment and you want to match it with an equal or higher perceived commitment.

> *"In a perfect world, 100% of your customers would qualify for a conditional guarantee, but will have achieved their result, and therefore will not want to take it."*

> *"And just fyi — if given the option of getting a refund or getting the outcome they were promised, the vast majority of people will take the outcome."*

### Variant 2A: Outsized Refund Guarantee

What the client gets: Double or triple their money back, or a no-strings $X,XXX payment (far more than what they paid).

**Hormozi's take:** For high-margin products. Add a *consumption condition* (they must do certain things to qualify).

### Jason Fladlien's $25,000 Store Guarantee

> *"A world class affiliate marketer Jason Fladlien (who did $27M in a single day) recently used an amazing guarantee for a course he sold. He said 'if you buy this course and spend $X on advertising your ecommerce store using the methods herein, and don't make money, I will buy your store from you for $25,000 no questions asked.'"*

> *"He claimed that an additional $3M in sales came from this crazy guarantee on a $2997 course. What's more, he only gave 10 of these $25,000 refunds out. So the refund generated $2.75M in extra sales."*

This is the gold standard for outsized conditional guarantees: dramatic, specific, real downside on the seller — with consumption conditions ensuring only people who actually did the work qualify.

### Variant 2B: Service Guarantee (Hormozi's Personal Favorite)

What the client gets: You keep working for them free of charge until X is achieved.

**Hormozi's take (verbatim):**

> *"This is probably my personal favorite guarantee of all time. It essentially guarantees they will achieve their goal, but it eliminates the element of time. You are never at risk for losing the money. The guarantee is around the outcome."*

Make it conditional on key success actions: setting up a web page, attending calls, showing up to workouts, weighing in, reporting data.

> *"Since I have been advising businesses to use this particular guarantee, I have yet to have a single person say a client took them up on it."*

Why: if a client does everything you asked and still hasn't hit the result, one of two things happens:
1. You happily keep working with them (their commitment is obvious)
2. It gets dropped (they're satisfied or close enough)

### Variant 2C: Modified Service Guarantee

What the client gets: You give them another Y-long period of free service. Y should be at least 2× the original duration.

**Hormozi's take:** Like the service guarantee but time-bounded. You're on the hook for a finite period instead of "forever." A reasonable starting point before the all-out version.

### Variant 2D: Credit-Based Guarantee

What the client gets: Money back as credit toward any service you offer.

**Hormozi's take:** Best for upsells. The customer already likes what they have — they apply credit to that. Maintains goodwill.

### Variant 2E: Personal Service Guarantee

What the client gets: You work with them 1-on-1, free of charge, until they reach X.

**Hormozi's take:** *"Absolutely one of the strongest guarantees in existence. It's like a service guarantee on crack."*

Add conditions: must respond within 24 hours, must use products you tell them to, must execute XYZ. Only then do you continue 1-on-1.

> *"This is especially powerful as you scale and become more edified as a business owner. Can you imagine one of my sales people saying, 'Alex will personally work with you until your offer converts'? Right. It would work. It would also be a nightmare."*

So he'd add stipulations: *"Provided you've already spent $10,000 on your existing offer using our structure, the offer you ran was for lead generation, and it was a free offer. These are things that would make it unlikely they would not succeed. If for some reason they hadn't with those stipulations in place, I could probably fix their problem in ten minutes just looking at it."*

### Variant 2F: Hotel + Airfare Perks Guarantee

What the client gets: If you don't receive value, we reimburse your product AND your hotel + airfare.

**Hormozi's take:** Technically a "refund of ancillary costs." Loves it for workshops and in-person experiences. Adds ~$1,000 to the guarantee but feels more tangible than just adding $1,000 cash.

### Variant 2G: Wage-Payment Guarantee

What the client gets: You offer to pay their hourly rate if they don't find your call/session valuable.

**Hormozi's take:** Another original ancillary-cost guarantee. *"If someone ever actually asks for the wage payment, just ask them for their tax return and divide it by 1,960 (number of working hours at 40hrs/wk for a year). But no one asking for a refund will actually do that, so you will never actually have to give one of these out. Like ever."*

### Variant 2H: Release Of Service Guarantee

What the client gets: You let them out of their contract free of charge.

**Hormozi's take:** Voids a commitment or cancellation fee. *"If you are in a business that does not enforce your contracts, then you have nothing to lose by adding the guarantee."*

### Variant 2I: Delayed Second Payment Guarantee

What the client gets: You won't bill again until they make/get their first outcome. (E.g., "lose your first 5 pounds . . . make your first sale . . . get your website live")

**Hormozi's take:** Especially good if you have a systematized process for first results. Focuses both the client AND your team on activation. *"I've successfully used this guarantee loads of times."*

### Variant 2J: First Outcome Guarantee

What the client gets: You continue paying their ancillary costs (ad spend, hotel, etc.) until they reach their first outcome. Example: *"If you don't make your first sale in 14 days, we will pay for your ad spend until you do."*

**Hormozi's take:** Like delayed second payment but centered on ancillary cost. Keeps everyone focused on getting that first dollar over the bridge.

## TYPE 3: ANTI-GUARANTEES ("All Sales Are Final")

You explicitly state no refunds. **You must come up with a creative reason why.**

> *"Typically, you'll want to show a massive exposure or vulnerability on your part that a consumer could immediately understand and think 'Yes, that makes sense.'"*

### When To Use
For consumable items, or items that massively diminish in value once given.

**Examples:**
- A line of code to improve checkout (once seen, can be used without paying)
- Opening messages for picking up girls
- Opening sentences for cold prospects
- Things that are very valuable but easy to steal once seen

### The Mechanic

> *"This can enhance the persuasiveness of the sale and the value of the product or service. It essentially implies that the client is going to use it and see an immense benefit thereby exposing the business to vulnerability. It acts as a damaging admission."*

The frame: *"We have an 'all sales are final' policy, but that is because our product is so exclusive and so powerful that once used it cannot be unused."*

> *"Since it is so standard to have some sort of guarantee, not having one is attention-worthy."*

### Anti-Guarantee For Lead Generation Pitches

> *"We are going to show you our proprietary process that we are using right now to generate leads in our business. Our funnels, ads, and metrics. We're going to be exposing the inner workings of our business, as a result, all sales are final."*

(Note: strong reason why is required. Make one up that sounds compelling. The more real-feeling the exposure, the more effective.)

### Anti-Guarantee For High-Touch Services

> *"If you're the type of customer who needs a guarantee before taking a jump, then you are not the type of person we want to work with. We want motivated self-starters who can follow instructions and are not looking for a way out before they even begin. If you are not serious, don't buy it. But if you are, boy are you going to make a killing."*

This filters customers AND reframes refusing a guarantee as a strength signal.

## TYPE 4: IMPLIED GUARANTEES (Performance-Based)

> *"Performance, Revshare, and Profit-shares aren't guarantees 'per se', but for all intents and purposes, they are."*

The implicit guarantee: **if you don't make money, you don't have to pay me.**

### Performance Models
- $X per sale / $X per show
- $X per pound lost

### Revshare
- 10% of top line revenue
- 20% profit share
- 25% of revenue growth from baseline

### Profit-Share
- X% of profit
- X% of gross profit

### Ratchets
- 10% if over X
- 20% if over Y
- 30% if over Z

### Bonuses / Triggers
- I get X when Y occurs

**Hormozi's take:**

> *"In my opinion, this is one of, if not THE most desirable setup. First because it makes you accountable to your clients' results. Second it weeds out low performers. Perfect alignment between client and service provider fosters collaboration and a long-term relationship."*

> *"The drawbacks are tracking and collection. So if you can find a way around that . . . you've hit a gold mine."*

This is exactly what Hormozi teaches his agencies via his software: switch from retainer to performance, wrap into the Grand Slam Offer.

> *"I've seen countless agencies go from $20k/mo to $200k+/mo in a matter of a few months."*

### Hybrid: Performance + Minimum

> *"You can also pair a revshare or performance setup with a minimum. It would be like saying 'we get the greater of $1000 or 10% of revenue generated.'"*

Or: $1000/mo for the first 3 months, then 100% performance. Ideal for setups that take time to ramp.

These work best when:
- You have quantifiable outcomes
- You have transparency/control over measurement
- You trust you'll get paid when you perform

## Stacking Guarantees

> *"An experienced salesman understands that, like bonuses, you can actually stack guarantees."*

### Example Stack 1
- 30-day unconditional no-questions-asked guarantee
- PLUS conditional triple-your-money-back 90-day guarantee

### Example Stack 2 (Sequential Conditional)
> *"You'll make $10,000 by 60 days, $30,000 by 90 days as long as you do thing 1, 2, and 3."*

This future-paces the prospect into an outcome they now believe is far more likely. *"Doing this shows the prospect you are serious about getting them results and convinced that they will achieve what they want. This shifts the burden of risk back from them onto us . . . a very powerful strategy."*

## Creating Your Own Winning Guarantee

> *"Reversing risk is the number one way to increase the conversion of an offer. Experienced marketers spend as much time crafting their guarantees as the deliverables themselves. It's that important."*

The construction method:

1. **Identify a client's biggest fears, pain, and perceived obstacles.**
2. *"What do they not want to have happen if they pay you? What are they most afraid of?"*
3. **Reverse their fears into a guarantee.**
4. **Think of the time, emotion, and outside costs associated with any program or service.**
5. **The more specific and creative the guarantee, the better.**

---

## The Critical Caveat

> *"Guarantees are enhancers. They can enhance the magnetism or attraction of any offer, but they cannot make a business. If a guarantee is used to cover up a poor sales team or a poor product, it will backfire into lots of refunds. No bueno."*

You must actually deliver. The guarantee removes risk for the buyer — but the seller's product still has to work.

---

## Hormozi's Recommended Starting Point

> *"Start selling service-based guarantees or setting up performance partnerships. This will make all sales final (so no fear from refunds). Most importantly, it will commit you to your customers' results and keep you honest. From there, either keep that guarantee and scale (perfectly fine), or move up the food chain to less restrictive guarantees to increase volume."*

The progression:
1. Start with **implied guarantees** (performance/revshare) — no refund risk, perfectly aligned
2. As volume becomes the goal, evolve toward **conditional guarantees**
3. For lower-ticket B2C at scale, **unconditional** can produce the biggest absolute lift
