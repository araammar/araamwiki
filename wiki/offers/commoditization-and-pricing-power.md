# Concept: Commoditization & The Virtuous Cycle of Price
tags: [offers, Hormozi, pricing, commoditization, premium-pricing, value]

## Summary / Definition

Two halves of one idea: (1) **the commodity problem** — what happens when you let yourself be compared, and (2) **the virtuous cycle of price** — the moral, strategic, and operational case for charging premium prices. Together they form the pricing philosophy of $100M Offers.

---

## Part 1: The Commodity Problem

### Definition

> *"A commodity, as I define it, is a product available from many places. For that reason, it's prone to purchases based on 'price' instead of 'value.' If all products are 'equal,' then the cheapest one is the most valuable by default."*

If a prospect compares your product to another and thinks *"these are pretty much the same, I'll buy the cheaper one,"* they have **commoditized you**. Hormozi calls this one of the worst experiences a value-driven entrepreneur can have.

### Commoditized vs. Differentiated

> Commoditized = Price-Driven Purchases (race to the bottom)
> Differentiated = Value-Driven Purchases (sell in a category of one with no comparison)

### Why It's Devastating

Commodities are valued at the point of **market efficiency**. The marketplace drives prices down through competition until margins are just enough to keep the lights on. The owner becomes "a slave to their business" — waiting anxiously for things to "turn around," and by the time they realize the lie, they are too deep to pivot.

### The Typical Pricing Process (And Why It Kills)

Most owners price like this:
1. Look at marketplace
2. See what everyone else offers
3. Take the average
4. Go slightly below to remain "competitive"
5. Provide what their competitors offer + "a little more"
6. End up at a value proposition of "more for less"

The dirty secret: **those competitors they're copying are dead broke.** So why copy them?

Over time in an efficient marketplace, more competitors enter offering "a little more for a little less" until eventually no one can provide any more for any less. Market reaches perfect efficiency. Owners make just enough to keep going. Bottom 10–20% get washed out. Fresh blood enters and repeats the process. Around and around.

### The Grand Slam Offer Solves This

A Grand Slam Offer forces the prospect to stop and **think differently to assess the value of your differentiated product**. This establishes you as your own category, which means it's too difficult to compare prices, which **recalibrates the prospect's value-meter**.

## Part 2: The Virtuous Cycle of Price

### Definition

Hormozi's framework that explains why charging a premium is not only smart but **moral** — it's the only choice that lets you provide the most value.

> *"The forces of the marketplace will grate on your belief system. You must stay strong and ignore them!"*

### When You Decrease Your Price (The Death Spiral)

When you lower prices, you:
- **Decrease your clients' emotional investment** — it didn't cost them much
- **Decrease perceived value** — it can't be that good if it's cheap
- **Decrease your clients' results** — they don't value your service, aren't invested
- **Attract the worst clients** — never satisfied until your service is free
- **Destroy any margin** — no money to hire the best, pamper clients, invest in growth, scale, etc.

> *"In essence, your world sucks. And to make matters worse, your service probably sucks because you are squeezing blood from the proverbial stone."*

You fall in line with the armies of average businesses racing to the bottom. *"I've lived that life. It's terrible. If you love your customers and your employees, please stop short-changing them when there is a better way."*

### When You Raise Your Prices (The Virtuous Cycle)

When you raise prices, you:
- **Increase emotional investment** — they paid real money, they care
- **Increase perceived value** — must be good if it costs this much
- **Increase results** — they value it and follow through
- **Attract the best clients** — easiest to satisfy, cost less to fulfill, perceive the most value
- **Multiply margin** — money to invest in systems, smart people, customer experience, scale, and your own personal bank account growing month over month even after reinvestment

This allows you to enjoy the process long-term and help more people as you grow, rather than burning out and shriveling into obscurity.

## The Wine Tasting Study (Higher Price = Higher *Perceived* Value)

In a blind taste test, researchers asked consumers to rate three wines: low-priced, medium-priced, expensive. They rated them in order of price — most expensive = "best," cheapest = "cheap wine."

**The catch:** all three wines were identical.

> *"The tasters reported a wide discrepancy between the 'high priced' wine and the 'cheap' wine. This has deep implications for the direct relationship between value and price."*

**Implication:** Raising your prices can directly enhance the value you provide. Same product. Higher price = higher experienced value. People want to buy expensive things — they just need a reason.

## The "Category Of One" Mechanic

> *"The goal isn't just to be slightly above the market price — the goal is to be so much higher that a consumer thinks to themselves, 'This is so much more expensive, there must be something entirely different going on here.'"*

> *"That is how you create a category of one. In this new perceived marketplace, you are a monopoly and can make monopoly profits. That is the point."*

---

## The Stinging-Investment Principle (Pay Attention = Get Results)

> *"If you offer a service where a customer must do something in order to achieve the result . . . the more invested they are, the more likely they are to achieve the positive result. Therefore, it follows that if you care about your customers, you should get them as invested as humanly possible. Ideally, this means pricing your services or product in such a way that it stings a little when they buy."*

> **"Those who pay the most, pay the most attention."**

If your customers are more adherent and achieve better results with your service than your competition, **you are in a very real way providing more value than anyone else.**

---

## The Moral Argument

Hormozi explicitly frames premium pricing as a **moral choice**:

- Premium pricing → margin → ability to actually serve clients well → better outcomes for them → genuinely providing the most value
- Cheap pricing → no margin → can't afford to truly serve → mediocre outcomes → you took their money for a worse experience

Charging too little is morally questionable. Charging high prices that deliver disproportionate value is the kindest thing you can do.

## Hormozi's Premium-Price Experience (Gym Launch)

**Market context when he entered:**
- Low-price competitors: full-service marketing at **$500/mo**
- Single high-price competitor: **$5,000**

**What Hormozi did:**
- Came in at **$16,000** for a 16-week done-with-you intensive
- That was **3× the highest** competitor and **32× the lowest**
- Then upsold 35% into a **3-year, $42,000/year** agreement

**Why it worked:**
- The conviction was unshakable because the product delivered
- A Gym Launch gym averaged the following after 11 months in the program (158 gyms surveyed):
  - Top Line Revenue: **+$19,932/mo (+$239,000/yr)**
  - Recurring Revenue: **+$13,339/mo (+$160,068/yr)**
  - Bottom Line Profit: from $2,943/mo → **$8,940/mo (3.1× increase)**
  - Client Growth: +67
  - Churn: 10.7% → 6.8%
  - Retail Sales: +$4,400/mo
  - Avg Member Price: $129 → $167

He was selling this to grown men as a kid in his twenties. *"This was possible because my conviction was stronger than their skepticism."*

## Dan Kennedy's Core Pricing Doctrine

Two Dan Kennedy quotes Hormozi cites:

> *"Charge as high a price as you can say out loud without cracking a smile."*

> *"There is no strategic benefit to being the second cheapest in the marketplace, but there is for being the most expensive."*

## Outwork Your Self-Doubt

> *"To have the 'gonads' to charge big ticket prices, you must outwork your self doubt. You must be so confident in your delivery, because you have done it so many times, that you know that this person will succeed."*

> *"Experience is what gives you the conviction to ask for someone's entire year's salary as payment. You must believe so deeply in your solution that when you look at yourself in the mirror at night, alone, your conviction remains unshakable."*

This is non-negotiable. The pricing model doesn't work without the conviction underneath.

## The Core Takeaway

> *"99 percent of businesses need to raise their prices to grow, not lower them. Profit is oxygen. It fuels the fire of growth. You need it if you want to reach more people and make a bigger impact."*
