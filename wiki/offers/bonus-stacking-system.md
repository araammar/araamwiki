# Framework: The Bonus Stacking System
tags: [offers, Hormozi, bonuses, stacking, Jason Fladlien, enhancement]

## Summary / Definition

> *"A single offer is less valuable than the same offer broken into its component parts and stacked as bonuses."*

— Alex Hormozi, *$100M Offers*

Bonus stacking is the practice of taking the components of your core offer (or peripheral items) and presenting them as separately-named, separately-priced *bonuses* on top of the main offer. Each bonus widens the [[price-to-value-discrepancy]], increasing perceived value without lowering price.

> *"Every second of air time costs money and must be justified with ROI."* (Why infomercials have used this technique for decades.)

Hormozi attributes his renewed appreciation for bonuses to **Jason Fladlien**.

---

## The "But Wait, There's More" Mechanic

Classic infomercials sold one knife for $38.95, then added:
- 37 other knives
- Sharpeners
- Pans
- Guarantees

> *"They establish the price, then they expand upon it until you feel it's such a good deal it would be stupid to pass it up."*

The mechanic:
1. Anchor the price to the core offer
2. Add a bonus → discrepancy widens
3. Add another bonus → discrepancy widens more
4. Continue until the rubber band in the prospect's mind snaps

> *"We are now going to present that 'stack' of deliverables that we assembled earlier in a way that makes them irresistible."*

This connects directly to [[5-step-grand-slam-creation]] — the trim & stack output becomes the bonus stack.

---

## Why Bonuses Beat Discounts (Pro Tip)

> *"Whenever trying to close a deal, never discount the main offer. It teaches your customers that your prices are negotiable (which is terrible). Adding bonuses to increase value to close the deal is far superior to cutting prices. It puts you in a position of strength and goodwill rather than weakness."*

Discounting destroys trust, anchors lower future prices, and trains customers to ask. Bonuses do the opposite — they expand value while preserving (and reinforcing) the price.

## Bonus Presentation: 1-on-1 vs Group Selling

### 1-on-1 Selling

Ask for the sale **first**, before offering bonuses.

If they say yes → after signup, reveal additional bonuses. *Creates a wow experience and reinforces the decision to buy.*

If they say no → **present a bonus that matches their perceived obstacle**, then ask again. Don't feel weird asking again.

> *"You simply agree with the prospect, add the bonus, and ask if this consolation was 'Fair enough.' People have a hard time rejecting reciprocity, so adding a bonus to accommodate, then another, then another, and people will feel almost obligated to buy from you."*

This is the offer-doubling pattern: each "no" gets met with a new bonus targeted at the specific objection that produced the no.

### Group Selling

> *"Group selling is beyond the scope of this book."*

But the basic principle remains: you reveal bonuses in sequence to widen the perceived discrepancy as you go.

## The 11 Rules Of Bonuses

Hormozi's explicit checklist for crafting bonuses:

1. **Always offer them** (use the bundle you built in [[5-step-grand-slam-creation]])
2. **Give them a special name** that has a benefit in the title
3. **Tell them:**
   - How it relates to their issue
   - What it is
   - How you discovered it, or what you had to do to create it
   - How it will specifically improve their lives (Faster, easier, less effort/sacrifice — the value equation)
4. **Provide proof** (stat, past client, personal experience) showing the thing is valuable
5. **Paint a vivid mental image** of their life after using it, already enjoying the benefits
6. **Ascribe a price tag** and justify it
7. **Tools & checklists > additional trainings** (lower effort & time = higher value — see [[value-equation]])
8. **Each bonus addresses a specific concern/obstacle** in the prospect's mind about why they can't or won't succeed
9. **Bonuses can also be what they'll logically need next** — solve their next problem before they encounter it
10. **The value of the bonuses should eclipse the value of the core offer**
11. **You can stack scarcity and urgency *on the bonuses themselves*** — see below

## Why Bonus Value Should Eclipse The Core Offer

> *"Psychologically as you continue to add offers, it continues to expand the price to value discrepancy. It also, subconsciously communicates that the core offer must be valuable because if these are the bonuses, the main thing has to be more valuable than the bonuses right? (No, but you can use this psychological bias to make your offer seem wildly compelling)."*

The reasoning trick the prospect's mind plays on itself:
- Bonuses are normally "extra stuff"
- If the bonuses are this valuable, the *main* product must be even more valuable
- Therefore the price-to-value ratio is incredible

Whether that's logically true doesn't matter. The perception is what closes the sale.

## Bonuses With Scarcity

**Version 1 (exclusive access):**
> *"Only people who sign up for XYZ program will have access to my Bonus #1, 2, 3 that are never for sale or available anywhere else other than through this program."*

**Version 2 (limited supply of slots):**
> *"I have 3 tickets left to my $5,000 virtual event, if you buy this program you can get one of the last 3 tickets as a bonus."*

These don't constrain by time — they constrain by program membership or by available units.

---

## Bonuses With Urgency

> *"If you buy today, I will add in XYZ bonus that normally costs $1,000, for free. And I'll do that because I want to reward action takers."*

This is time-bound. They lose the bonus if they don't buy today.

> *"Minor difference, but worth noting."*

Scarcity = quantity constraint. Urgency = time constraint. Either can be applied to bonuses.

## Advanced Level: Other People's Products & Services (OPP)

> *"You can get other businesses to give you their services and products as a part of your bonuses in exchange for exposure to your clients for free. This is free marketing for them, and high value products for you at no cost."*

The win-win:
- They give you their service/product as a bonus
- You give them exposure to high-quality prospects (your customers)
- No direct competition required — just adjacency

### The Pain Clinic Example (Worked Out)

If Hormozi owned a pain clinic, he might assemble:

- Massage therapist: 1-2 free massages
- Chiropractor: 2 free adjustments ($100 value)
- Low-inflammation food company: discount on products ($50 savings)
- Braces & orthotics: discount ($150 savings)
- Local health club: free personal training session + free month pool membership ($100 value)
- Pharmacy: discount on pharmaceutical drugs ($100/mo savings)
- Repeat with multiple service providers (10 chiros × 1 free adjustment each = 10 free adjustments in the bundle)

> *"Now if my offer was $400, then the value of these free bonuses ALONE is worth more than the $400."*

### Going Further — Get Paid Affiliate Commissions

> *"As if that weren't already awesome enough, if you really want to be a Jedi, negotiate a group discount and a commission to yourself."*

Hormozi did this exactly with Prestige Labs (his supplement company):
- Gym owner clients who use Prestige sponsored athletes get **30% discount** on products
- The sponsored athlete gets **40% of all sales** netted after the applied discount
- Everyone wins: clients save 30%, athletes get paid for giving discounts, Prestige gets customers

### Stacking The Math

Continuing the pain clinic example:
- Chiro gives you $100 per person who comes into their office
- Food company gives free food
- Orthotics company gives $100 per referral
- Health club gives free membership OR $50/referral
- Pharmacy gives $100/referral

Now: $400 offer has the potential to generate **$350+ extra in pure profit** from the affiliate commissions of the bonuses.

> *"The other businesses will pay you and you don't have to do anything but refer customers to them that you have already spent the money to acquire."*

### Going Maximally Jedi

> *"If you really want to get crazy, come up with a grand slam offer with these partner businesses by using the same concepts in the book so that each of the bonuses themselves becomes even more valuable than a simple commoditized service."*

Build Grand Slam bonus offers with each adjacent business. The bonuses themselves become differentiated, premium-priced, irresistible deals.

## What Goes In The Core Offer vs Bonuses?

> *"Short answer: Wow Factor — in other words — something you wouldn't want someone to miss."*

The decision rule: when you're providing so much stuff that valuable nuggets get lost, pull the most distinct ones out and highlight them as bonuses. This is especially true for:
- Things that are **short in length but high in quality** (checklists, infographics)
- Things that can almost stand on their own
- Things someone might not pay for separately but perceive as enormously valuable as bonuses

> *"Someone might not feel justified paying lots of money for a product launch map (for example) but as a bonus would be perceived as very valuable."*

## The Bonus Asset Library (Long-Term Play)

> *"The longer you are in business the more of these bonus assets you will have at your disposal. All of these things are valuable. Put them in a vault and keep them in your back pocket to sprinkle into an offer to get the deal closed."*

Hormozi's hierarchy of best bonus assets:
- **Information products** — high perceived value, low cost, zero operational effort beyond a login
- **Tickets to virtual experiences or events** — high perceived value, low marginal cost
- **A higher level of service that has a fixed cost** (e.g., VIP service for a month, which also doubles as an upsell to keep them at that level)

Habits to build:
- Record every workshop, webinar, event, interview
- Build checklists, tools, swipe files, scripts, templates
- Create anything that takes lots of time/effort to make once but is easy to give away repeatedly
- Proactively negotiate group discounts and referral commissions with adjacent businesses

---

## The Summary Checklist For Building Bonuses

1. **Create assets** — checklists, tools, swipe files, scripts, templates, recordings of every event
2. **Record everything** — every workshop, webinar, event, interview
3. **Negotiate with adjacent businesses** — group discounts + referral commissions for "next natural thing" your customers will need

> *"What's the next natural thing they might want? Go to those businesses, get a deal for them they could never get for themselves (because you are negotiating with the purchasing power of all your customers at once, very powerful)."*

---

## Why Bonuses Are The Most Productive Enhancement

Bonuses expand the price-to-value discrepancy without:
- Lowering price (which would destroy your positioning)
- Raising cost (because most bonuses are zero or low marginal cost)
- Compromising the core offer (which stays unchanged)

They also massively increase the perception of value, which directly closes more deals.
