# Concept: Reverse-Engineer The Buying Process
tags: [offers, Hormozi, Lost Chapter, buyer-journey, content, sales-process]

## Summary / Definition

Step 4b of the Lost Chapter's avatar refinement process — and arguably the most operationally valuable single insight in the supplement.

> *"Look at what caused these better customers to buy. Reverse-engineer the buying process your best customers went through. Then, make it happen on purpose."*

— Alex Hormozi, *$100M Offers: The Lost Chapter*

Instead of guessing what marketing and sales should look like, you study how your *actual best customers* moved through their decision — then deliberately recreate that exact journey for every new prospect.

---

## The 78% Insight (Gym Launch's Defining Discovery)

When Hormozi ran the Lost Chapter's 4-step process on Gym Launch's customer base, the buying-process bucket revealed something specific:

> *"After looking at the data, we found that 78% of our top customers had consumed AT LEAST TWO pieces of long form content before purchasing from us."*

The implication was immediate:
> *"This means that if we got on the phone with someone who had not done that, our chances of selling them were lower."*

Two pieces of long-form content was the threshold. Below it, conversion dropped. At or above it, conversion climbed.

This wasn't a guess. It wasn't a best practice from a podcast. It was a pattern in their own data.

---

## How They Operationalized It

Three concrete moves:

### 1. Inject Two Long-Form Pieces Into Every Lead's Journey
> *"From this point onwards, we injected two long form high value content pieces to each lead as a part of their buyer journey."*

Every lead — regardless of source — got pushed through two pieces of long-form content before sales engagement. The threshold became the floor, not an optional ingredient.

### 2. Increase Total Content Output
> *"We increased our total output of content."*

If two pieces had to be consumed before the sale, the catalog needed to be deep enough to give every prospect relevant pieces. Volume of high-quality content became a strategic input, not a marketing-team afterthought.

### 3. The "All Time Greatest Hits" Library For Sales
> *"On top of that, we created a list of our 'all time greatest hits' of content to arm the sales team. They then hand select two to three pieces they think could help the prospect."*

Salespeople didn't just push the same two pieces to everyone. They had a curated catalog and picked 2-3 pieces *specific to each prospect's situation* — based on what came up in early conversations.

> *"By doing that, they forced them to go through the same buying process that caused our best customers to buy."*

The phrase "forced them to go through the same buying process" is exact: they engineered the prospect's journey to match the path the top 20% naturally took.

---

## The Critical Caveat — Value, Not Disguised Pitches

> *"They were not disguised sales pitches, they were genuinely value-in-advance content. (Like this, hopefully)."*

This distinction matters enormously. If the long-form content is a thinly-veiled pitch, the mechanism breaks. The mechanism works because:
- The prospect actually gets something valuable
- They learn something genuinely useful about their situation
- They form an opinion about Hormozi's expertise without being sold to
- Trust accumulates over the two pieces
- By the time the sale happens, the prospect has already done their internal due diligence

This is the same principle as [[show-me-you-know-me]] (SMYKM) — value-in-advance creates conversion compounding.

---

## Why This Is Different From "Content Marketing"

Standard content marketing thinking:
- Post stuff
- Get views
- Build awareness
- Hope it converts

The Lost Chapter's reverse-engineered approach:
- **Identify the specific content patterns of your best customers' buying journeys**
- **Manufacture those exact patterns for every new prospect**
- **Treat content as a sales-process requirement, not a top-of-funnel awareness play**

It inverts the relationship between content and sales. Content stops being "what we publish" and becomes "the prerequisite buying process."

## How To Run This For Your Business

### Step 1: Survey
Question to add to the customer survey (from [[lost-chapter-avatar-refinement]]):
- *"Before purchasing, what specific pieces of content did you consume from us?"*
- *"What other content did you consume from anyone else in the space?"*
- *"How many pieces did you consume?"*
- *"In what order?"*
- *"Was there a specific piece that pushed you over the edge?"*

### Step 2: Find The Pattern
Across the top 20% of customers, look for:
- **Common piece count threshold** (Hormozi's was 2)
- **Common content format** (long-form articles? video? podcast? webinar?)
- **Common piece sequence** (was there a typical order?)
- **Specific "tipping point" piece** (the one that closed the loop)

### Step 3: Operationalize
- Build out the catalog of content needed
- Inject required pieces into every lead's journey automatically
- Train sales to recommend 2-3 specific pieces per prospect from the catalog
- Track whether each prospect consumed the required content before the sales call

### Step 4: Refine
- Track conversion rates: prospects who consumed N pieces vs. prospects who didn't
- Identify which specific pieces correlate most with conversion
- Promote those pieces, deprecate the underperformers
- Re-survey annually

---

## What This Tells You About Sales Calls

If a prospect hasn't consumed the required pieces:
- Conversion probability is lower than baseline
- Either delay the call until they have, or
- Send them the pieces with a follow-up booking, or
- Run the call but expect a longer cycle

This is also the logic behind [[micah-cold-call-system]]'s pre-qualifier and the pre-pitch sequence: confirm the prospect is in the right state before pitching. The Lost Chapter is the strategic version of the same instinct.

---

## Why It Feels Like Cheating

> *"It feels like cheating because it's so obvious. But here's the great part — no one does it."*

Most businesses:
- Treat content and sales as separate departments
- Treat lead consumption as something you "hope" happens
- Have no idea what content their best customers consumed
- Send every prospect down the same generic funnel

The Lost Chapter approach:
- Treats content as a sales-process input
- Measures what actually correlated with high-value conversion
- Manufactures the same conditions for every new prospect
- Sales team has the catalog and the discretion to pick the right pieces

Competitors who don't do this lose to those who do — because their conversion rates can't compete with a process designed around what actually works.

If you wanted to apply this to Colandrum:

1. **Survey existing high-value clients** about what content they consumed before purchasing
2. **Look for the threshold** — is it 1 piece? 2? A specific format?
3. **Identify the tipping-point piece** — what was the "this is for me" moment?
4. **Build the catalog** to support the threshold
5. **Inject required content into the lead-nurture sequence** before sales engagement
