# Framework: The Delivery Cube
tags: [offers, Hormozi, delivery, framework, cheat-codes, product-design]

## Summary / Definition

A three-dimensional brainstorming aid for generating delivery-vehicle variations during Step 4 of the [[5-step-grand-slam-creation]] process. The "brick-variation cheat codes" applied to offers.

The three axes:

1. **Attention level** — How personalized is the delivery?
2. **Effort distribution** — Who does the work?
3. **Medium & timing** — How is the value transmitted?

Each combination produces a different delivery vehicle with different cost, scale, and value characteristics.

---

## Why "Cube"?

Hormozi presents the framework as a series of dials. Treating them as the axes of a cube gives you a visual map of every possible delivery vehicle for any solution. Most businesses sit at one corner of the cube (typically 1-on-1, done-for-you, in-person) and never explore the other 26 corners. Each unexplored corner is a new offer.

---

## Axis 1: Level Of Personal Attention

| Level | What It Means | Cost To You | Perceived Value |
|---|---|---|---|
| **One-on-One** | Direct individual attention to each customer | Highest | Highest (perceived likelihood spike) |
| **Small Group** | A handful of customers at once | Medium | Medium (peer learning + social proof) |
| **One-to-Many** | Same content delivered to unlimited audience | Lowest | Variable — depends on perceived likelihood |

The key tension: one-on-one feels most valuable but doesn't scale. One-to-many scales but feels generic. The art is engineering one-to-many that *feels* like one-on-one — via personalization, branching content, peer cohorts, calculators, etc.

---

## Axis 2: Effort Distribution

| Mode | What It Means | Customer Effort | Your Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **DIY (Do It Yourself)** | Customer figures it out on their own with your materials | Highest | Lowest |
| **DWY (Done With You)** | You teach them how to do it, they execute | Medium | Medium |
| **DFY (Done For You)** | You do it all for them | Lowest | Highest |

The classic price ladder:
- DIY = $X
- DWY = ~3-5× DIY
- DFY = ~10-20× DIY (or more)

Why DFY is most expensive: it removes Effort & Sacrifice (a [[value-equation]] driver) and increases Perceived Likelihood (they trust the expert more than themselves).

> *"This is why 'done for you services' are almost always more expensive than 'do-it-yourself' because the person doesn't have all the effort and sacrifice. There is also a component of 'perceived likelihood of achievement' difference as well. People believe that if an expert does it, then they will be more likely to achieve the outcome than if they try on their own."*

---

## Axis 3: Medium & Timing

If delivering live:
- In-person
- Phone support
- Email support
- Text support
- Zoom support
- Chat support

If delivering recorded:
- Audio
- Video
- Written

Reply speed (for service businesses):
- 24/7
- 9–5
- Within 5 minutes
- Within 1 hour
- Within 24 hours

Faster reply = higher perceived value (Fast Beats Free principle — see [[all-hormozi-pro-tips]]). Slower reply = lower cost.

## The 10× / 1/10× Test (The Imagination Stretcher)

> *"If my customers paid me 10x my price (or $100,000) what would I provide?"*

Forces you to imagine the absolute best possible delivery, regardless of cost. This surfaces high-value ideas you can then look for cheaper ways to deliver.

> *"If they paid me 1/10th the price and I had to make my product more valuable than it already is, how would I do that? How could I still make them successful for 1/10th price?"*

Forces you to think about low-cost / high-leverage solutions like templates, AI tools, communities, automated coaching, etc.

**Both directions surface delivery vehicles you wouldn't otherwise consider.**

## Worked Example: "Buying Healthy Food" (Hormozi's Walk-Through)

For a single solution ("Help client buy healthy food"), here are 16 vehicles generated by varying the cube:

**One-on-One:**
1. In-person grocery shopping (DFY-adjacent, in-person)
2. Personalized grocery list (DWY, written)
3. Full-service shopping (DFY, in-person)
4. In-person orientation (DWY, in-person)
5. Text support while shopping (DWY, text)
6. Phone call while shopping (DWY, phone)

**Small Group:**
1. In-person group grocery shopping (DWY, in-person)
2. Group grocery list workshop (DWY, in-person)
3. Group food purchase + delivery (DFY, in-person)
4. Small group orientation offsite (DWY, in-person)

**One-to-Many:**
1. Live virtual grocery tour (DWY, Zoom)
2. Recorded grocery tour (DIY, video)
3. DIY grocery calculator (DIY, tool)
4. Predetermined weekly lists per plan (DIY, written)
5. Grocery buddy system (DIY, peer-pairing)
6. Pre-made InstaCart carts for one-click delivery (DFY-adjacent, automated)

Same underlying solution. 16 different delivery vehicles. Each one a different cost-to-you / value-to-customer tradeoff.

The [[5-step-grand-slam-creation]] Step 5 (Trim & Stack) then evaluates each vehicle and keeps only the high-value-per-cost ones for the final bundle.

## The Strategic Sweet Spot — High Value, Low Cost

> *"If there's one type of delivery vehicle to focus on, it's creating high value, 'one to many' solutions. These will be the ones that typically have the biggest discrepancy between cost and value."*

The magic corner of the cube:
- **One-to-many** (low cost to deliver at scale)
- **DIY or DWY** (low ongoing cost per customer)
- **Recorded / asynchronous** (no real-time cost)
- **High perceived value via personalization, status, or completeness**

### Hormozi's Excel Sheet Example

Before his first gym, he had an online training business. He built a small Excel sheet that:
- Took someone's goals as input
- Auto-generated 100+ meals perfectly matched to their macros and calories
- Generated grocery lists with exact amounts
- Generated bulk-prep instructions

**Build time: ~100 hours**. Sale time per customer: ~15 minutes. He sold these as "truly personalized eating plans for very expensive prices."

**High value. Low ongoing cost.** This is the corner of the cube most businesses never explore.

> *"These types of solutions require a high, one-time cost of creation, but infinitely low additional effort after. (Fyi - This is exactly why software becomes so valuable)."*

The meal plans went on to be used by 4,000+ gyms and hundreds of thousands of people. Two weeks of building → years of returns.

## When To Use The High-Cost Corner

> *"That doesn't mean you don't ever want to do something in a small group or one-on-one model. After all, I do 1-on-1 with all of my portfolio company CEOs that we help scale past $30m+. You just want to make sure you save those high cost items for big value adds only."*

Use 1-on-1, in-person, DFY when:
- The price the customer pays massively exceeds the cost to deliver (e.g., $100K+ engagements)
- The relationship has long-term equity or strategic value
- The personalization is genuinely required (not just preferred)

Otherwise, find the lower-cost vehicle that produces the same outcome.

---

## The Combinatorial Math

3 attention levels × 3 effort modes × ~7 mediums × ~5 reply speeds = **~315 distinct delivery vehicles** for any given solution.

Most businesses use 1–3 of them. The Grand Slam Offer aggressively explores all 315.

---

## Application Notes

Before finalizing any offer:

1. **Pick one solution from your problems-solutions list**
2. **Generate at least 10 delivery vehicles using the cube axes**
3. **Score each by cost-to-you and value-to-customer**
4. **Keep the top 2-3 vehicles per solution for the bundle**
5. **Repeat for every solution**
6. **The resulting bundle is your Grand Slam Offer's core**

The cube isn't a one-time exercise. Revisit it whenever you want to scale, lower cost, or differentiate from a competitor copying your current vehicle.
