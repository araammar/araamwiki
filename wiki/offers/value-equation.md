# Concept: The Value Equation
tags: [offers, Hormozi, value, pricing, psychology, framework]

## Summary / Definition

The Value Equation is the formula Hormozi developed to quantify what creates value in any offer. He claims he has never seen it displayed elsewhere. Once you see it, you cannot unsee it — it becomes a permanent filter for evaluating every product, service, and pitch.

> **Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement) ÷ (Perceived Time Delay × Perceived Effort & Sacrifice)**

Two drivers on top (you want to **increase**), two on the bottom (you want to **decrease**).

---

## The Four Drivers

### 1. Dream Outcome (Increase)
The expression of the feelings and experiences the prospect has envisioned in their mind. The gap between their current reality and their dreams. Our goal is to accurately depict that dream back to them so they feel understood, then explain how our vehicle gets them there.

The dream outcome is the easy part. **It's the "getting there" where value is enhanced or detracted.**

What people generally want:
- To be perceived as beautiful
- To be respected
- To be perceived as powerful
- To be loved
- To increase their status

These are all powerful drivers. But multiple vehicles satisfy the same desire. The desire "to be perceived as beautiful" is touched by makeup, anti-aging creams, supplements, shapewear, plastic surgery, fitness. They all channel the same desire.

**The dream outcome is most prominently used when comparing the relative value between two different desires being satisfied.** The dream that most directly increases a prospect's status is the one they value most. See [[status-as-deep-driver]] for the deeper layer underneath every desire.

### 2. Perceived Likelihood of Achievement (Increase)
The last variable Hormozi added — "I just felt like something was missing with only the other three."

**"People pay for certainty."**

> "How likely do I believe it is that I will achieve the result I am looking for if I make this purchase?"

Example: How much would you pay to be a plastic surgeon's 10,000th patient vs. their first? A lot more for the 10,000th. The service is technically the same. Only the perceived likelihood of getting what you want changes. (You might even ask the first-time surgeon to pay *you*.)

To increase perceived likelihood, use: messaging, proof, what you include or exclude in your offer, and guarantees. See [[guarantee-library]].

### 3. Perceived Time Delay (Decrease)
The time between the client buying and receiving the promised benefit. The shorter, the more valuable.

**Two elements:**
- **Long-term outcome** — what they're really buying (the dream)
- **Short-term experience** — what makes them stay long enough to get it

> *"The thing people buy is the long-term value, aka their 'dream outcome.' But the thing that makes them stay long enough to get it is the short-term experience."*

Tie as many short-term wins as possible into the journey. Get an emotional buy-in close to the purchase moment. This is the **Fast Wins** principle (see [[all-hormozi-pro-tips]]).

**Example (Gym Launch):** Adding $239,000/yr to a gym takes 11 months. But within the first 7 days, Hormozi gets the gym to get ads live and close a $2,000 sale. The fast emotional win reinforces the decision and gets them committed for the long haul.

**Example (Bikini body sale):** The 12-month transformation is the dream. But along the way: higher sex drive, more energy, new community. They buy the dream — they stay for the benefits they discover along the way.

### 4. Perceived Effort & Sacrifice (Decrease)
What it "costs" the prospect in ancillary costs along the way. Tangible (time, money) and intangible (discomfort, embarrassment, learning curve).

This is the biggest single driver of why expensive things often beat cheap things in the same category.

**Example: Fitness vs. liposuction.**
- Same dream outcome (slimmer body)
- Fitness: high effort, high time, high uncertainty → low value
- Liposuction: low effort, fast, certain → high value → people pay $25,000

**This is why "done for you" services are always priced higher than "do it yourself."** Less effort. Plus, the prospect believes they're more likely to succeed if an expert does it (a perceived-likelihood boost too).

## Why It's A Division, Not Addition

Hormozi designed it as division deliberately. **If you can drive the bottom side of the equation to zero, the value becomes infinite** (technically undefined, but functionally infinite).

> "If you can reduce your prospects' true time delay to receiving value to zero (aka they realize your immediate dream outcome), and your effort and sacrifice is zero, you have an infinitely valuable product."

The hypothetical: a prospect runs their credit card → their dream outcome instantly becomes reality. That is the limit every entrepreneur should strive toward.

---

## "Get The Bottom To Zero" — The Strategic Insight

> *"In the beginning of my career, I focused all my attention on dream outcomes and the perception of achievement. That's where beginner marketers make bigger and bigger claims. It's easy, and it's lazy. But as time has gone on, I have realized that these larger-than-life claims are the easiest to establish (and therefore less unique)."*

The top side of the equation is where amateurs compete. Anyone can make a promise.

The bottom side — making things **immediate, seamless, and effortless** — is where the best companies in the world compete:
- **Apple**: made the iPhone effortless vs. other phones of its era
- **Amazon**: one-click purchase + same-day delivery
- **Netflix**: instant, effortless TV consumption

> "The older I get, the more I have shifted my focus to 'the hard stuff' — decreasing the bottom side of the equation."

**Takeaway:** if you're stuck competing on dream outcomes with rivals, stop. Win by removing time delay and effort instead.

## Perception Is Reality

It is not about how much you actually increase likelihood of success, decrease time delay, or decrease effort. **It is about how much the prospect *perceives* those things changing.**

> *"Many times, they will have no idea. The Grand Slam Offer only becomes valuable once the prospect perceives the increase in likelihood of achievement, perceives the decrease in time delay, and perceives the decrease in effort and sacrifice."*

This is the link to [[logical-vs-psychological-solutions]].

**London Tunnel Example:** The biggest rider-satisfaction increase wasn't from faster trains. It was from a simple **dotted-line map** showing when the next train was coming and how long they had to wait. Cost: a few million dollars. Alternative (actually making trains faster): billions of dollars. Same perceived value increase. The dot map decreased the *perceived* time delay and the *perceived* effort/sacrifice of waiting.

This is what marketing actually does. We communicate the value drivers with clarity so the prospect perceives the increase.

## The Fast-Beats-Free Pro Tip

> *"The only thing that beats 'free' is 'fast.' People will pay for speed."*

Examples:
- **MVD vs. DMV**: pay $50, skip the line
- **FedEx vs. USPS**: "when it absolutely positively has to be there overnight"
- **Spotify vs. slow free music**
- **Uber vs. walking**

Many people will always pay for speed. If you find yourself competing against free, **double down on speed.**

## Quantifying The Equation — The Binary Comparison

Hormozi suggests scoring each driver as 0 or 1 (1 = value achieved, 0 = missing). Sum the four for a relative value rating.

### Example: Same Dream Outcome, Different Vehicles

**Dream Outcome:** "Relaxation, decreased anxiety, feelings of well-being"

| Driver | Meditation | Xanax |
|---|---|---|
| Dream Outcome | 1 | 1 |
| Perceived Likelihood | 0 (skeptical) | 1 (certain) |
| Time Delay | 0 (slow) | 1 (immediate) |
| Effort & Sacrifice | 0 (hard) | 1 (effortless) |
| **Score** | **1/4** | **4/4** |

> *"And that is why Xanax is a multi-billion dollar product while I know of almost no multi-billion dollar meditation businesses."*

(Hormozi adds: not arguing meditation isn't better. Just observing that *perceived* value is what people pay for.)

### Example: Industry Sizes Confirm The Equation

The supplement industry ($123B, Grandview Research) is **twice the size** of the health club industry ($62B, IHRSA). Both accomplish "being healthy," "losing weight," "looking good." Same dream. But supplements have lower costs (less time, less effort). Hence: valued more.

People pay $200 for supplements faster than $29/mo for a gym membership. Taking a pill is faster and easier than going to the gym every day.

## The Father-Son Sales Call (Putting The Equation Into A Pitch)

Hormozi was selling $42,000/year Gym Lords memberships. His dad called terrified. The conversation became a perfect Value Equation walkthrough:

**Dad's questions, mapped to the equation:**
- "What will I make?" → **Dream Outcome**
- "How will I know it's going to happen?" → **Perceived Likelihood of Achievement**
- "How long will it take?" → **Time Delay**
- "What is expected of me?" → **Effort & Sacrifice**

Hormozi's answers:
- $239,000 extra in 11 months (Dream + Timeline established)
- Average from real surveys of 158 gyms (Likelihood proven)
- 15 hours/week (Effort sized)
- Pay nothing up front, just pay as you make money using the system (Effort + risk eliminated)

Dad's response: "Oh, well then, yeah, I would do it."

> *"And that's why they do it, too."*

When you can answer all four questions clearly, the sale becomes almost automatic.

## The Reality Check: You Can Be Right Or You Can Be Rich

> *"You can either sit there and make 'complain' posts about how people 'ought' to be a certain way. Or you can take advantage of the way people are and capitalize. This book is for those people who want to be victors, not victims of circumstances."*

> *"You can either be right or you can be rich. This book is for getting rich."*

Knowing what people *value* vs. what is *good for them* is key. You can monetize what people value to deliver what they actually need.
