# Framework: The Lost Chapter — Avatar Refinement
tags: [offers, Hormozi, Lost Chapter, avatar, segmentation, Vista, VAM, ICP, survey]

## Summary / Definition

The "Lost Chapter" of $100M Offers is a focused supplement on **avatar refinement via data-driven customer analysis**. The previous market-selection work (see [[starving-crowd-market-selection]]) and niching work (see [[niche-pricing-power]]) is upstream of this chapter. This is the next level of specificity: among the avatars you already serve, which ones are actually worth keeping — and how do you find more like them?

> *"Earlier, I talked about picking the right market. It's an important strategic business decision. Choosing the perfect avatar is a subset of that larger decision. This is where we become more nuanced about exactly who we serve, and more importantly, who we do not."*

— Alex Hormozi, *$100M Offers: The Lost Chapter*

The chapter is roughly 1,900 words and was released separately from the main book.

## The Vista PE Method

Vista's process when considering acquiring a company:

1. **Analyze the company's current customers.**
2. **Score them by value** — who stays the longest and pays the most? Highest scores to the most valuable. Lowest to the least.
3. **Look for "a vein of underserved valuable customers"** — if it exists, buy the company.
4. **Once owned, cut channels that bring low-value customers.**
5. **Double down on channels that bring the best ones.**

> *"That's it. More of the high profit customers. Fewer low profit customers. Rinse. Repeat."*

### Why It Works — Pareto On Steroids

> *"It was Pareto's principle (80/20) on steroids. Twenty percent of customers bring in eighty percent of revenue. If you replace the eighty percent with those high spenders, you grow the business 5x. No small feat, especially in billion dollar companies."*

The compounding logic:
- Top 20% drive 80% of revenue (Pareto baseline)
- Replace the bottom 80% with more top-20%-type customers
- Total revenue can grow ~5×
- Costs stay roughly the same
- Margin expansion is enormous

This is now a pillar of **Acquisition.com's Value Acceleration Method (VAM)**.

## The 4-Step Avatar Refinement Process

The operational installation Hormozi extracted from Vista's approach.

### Step 1: Survey Your Customers

Set up a form with the questions below. Send it. Or — for higher engagement — go over it with them live at an event or on a call. Make sure they show they completed it to receive some benefit.

**Question Buckets (for business services customers):**

**a) Demographics**
- Age
- Gender
- Political affiliation
- Geographic location
- Digital location
- Single/Divorced
- Partnered in business or solopreneur

**b) Business Stats — Before & Current**
- Revenue
- Profit
- # of employees
- Churn
- Pricing
- Products
- Customer lifetime value
- # of customers
- Niche
- How long in business

**c) Aspirations**
- What was their goal upon purchasing your services/products?
- What problem were they trying to solve?

**d) Buying Process**
- What's the single biggest reason they bought?
- Was there a trigger event that caused them to buy?
- Did they consume any specific piece of content?
- Was there a specific testimonial they consumed?
- How many pieces did they consume?
- When did they first hear about you vs. when they bought?
- Where did they first see you?
- Did someone refer them?

The Buying Process bucket is critical — it's what makes Step 4b possible.

### Step 2: Find Your Biggest Spenders

> *"Sort the replies by the customers you like the most, spent the most, and stayed the longest. Focus on the top 20%. Ignore the rest."*

Three filters: enjoyment + spend + tenure. The customers who hit all three are your gold mine.

### Step 3: See What They Have In Common

> *"This takes reading through all the answers and using your brain. I know. Thinking is hard. The good news is — your competitors won't do it — easy advantage."*

Goal: come up with **the fewest qualifiers they all have in common.** Usually 3 to 5 qualifiers.

This is where divergent thinking (see [[convergent-vs-divergent-thinking]]) pays off. The pattern isn't always obvious. It might be "Right-leaning + Married + Male + Gym Owner + US-Based" — and that combination might not surface if you only think demographically.

### Step 4: Execute

Two execution moves, both essential.

**4a: Speak Your New Avatar**

> *"Be upfront about your customer requirements. Get all advertising to speak directly to them. You will repel the bad customers and attract the good ones. Stop selling anyone who does not meet your ideal customer requirements. Seriously, stop it. Then, increase effort on the channels these people come through."*

The repel-bad/attract-good dynamic is identical to the niching mechanic in [[niche-pricing-power]] — narrowing the avatar increases conversion *and* pricing power simultaneously.

**4b: Re-Engineer The Sales Process**

> *"Look at what caused these better customers to buy. Reverse-engineer the buying process your best customers went through. Then, make it happen on purpose."*

This is the unique contribution of the Lost Chapter — and the most operationally powerful part. See [[reverse-engineer-buying-process]] for the full mechanic and the 78% long-form content insight from Gym Launch.

## The Gym Launch Implementation (Full Verbatim Findings)

Hormozi ran the 4 steps on Gym Launch's customer base. Here's what surfaced.

### Demographics Of The Top 20%
- Right-leaning / conservative
- Married
- 25–45 years old
- Male
- Gym Owner
- US-based

### Business Requirements Of The Top 20%
- Signed Lease
- 1+ Employees minimum
- $10,000+/month revenue minimum when starting
- Min. 30 existing clients

### Aspirations Of The Top 20%
- $1M+ gym
- Not work so much
- Open more locations

### Buying Reasons Of The Top 20%
- Not enough leads
- "Bad market"
- Bad pricing
- Can't find good employees

### Buying Process Of The Top 20%
- **78% had consumed AT LEAST TWO pieces of long-form content before purchasing**

This last data point became the foundation of the buyer-journey overhaul. See [[reverse-engineer-buying-process]].

---

## What Hormozi Did With The Findings

### Action 1: Avatar-Targeted Marketing
> *"We focused on the audiences that had the highest concentration of these types of gym owners. We spelled out our requirements in our ads and pages. We talked only about the specific problems and aspirations of our best customers, rather than all customers."*

Notice the explicit nature: they spelled out the requirements in the ads. They weren't trying to hide qualifications behind clever copy — they were broadcasting them. The bad customers self-selected out. The good ones self-selected in.

### Action 2: Buyer Journey Re-Engineering
> *"From this point onwards, we injected two long form high value content pieces to each lead as a part of their buyer journey."*

Why two pieces? Because that's the threshold the data revealed.

The "all time greatest hits" content list was given to the sales team. They'd hand-select 2-3 pieces they thought could help the specific prospect.

> *"By doing that, they forced them to go through the same buying process that caused our best customers to buy."*

Critical note from Hormozi:
> *"They were not disguised sales pitches, they were genuinely value-in-advance content. (Like this, hopefully)."*

## Why Competitors Don't Do This

> *"Many competitors try to recreate our buyer journey. However, they don't fully commit. They panic, then cut out steps to get more volume. This is often a mistake."*

> *"Everytime we removed qualification steps, our lead volume increased, but we made less money."*

The hidden cost of avoiding the chapter: more volume of low-quality leads chokes the sales team, lowers conversion, and lowers margin.

See [[quality-over-quantity-acquisition]] for the full counter-argument and the 70× profit comparison.

## The "Merging Marketing And Sales" Insight

Hormozi explicitly merged the two departments into **one acquisition department** to enforce the avatar discipline.

> *"Marketing stopped complaining that the sales team wasn't closing. Sales team stopped complaining that they wanted more leads. Everyone came together to focus on what mattered: closing lots of valuable deals."*

The organizational point: if marketing and sales are separate departments with separate KPIs, marketing optimizes for *more leads* and sales optimizes for *more closes*. Both metrics can go up while the business gets worse — because lead quality crashes.

A single acquisition team can optimize for the right metric: profitable deals.

## The 3-Action Immediate Checklist

Hormozi closes the chapter with three actions:

1. **Survey your existing customers** (Step 1)
2. **Use the data to decide which characteristics are leading indicators of high value** (Steps 2 and 3)
3. **Change your messaging in advertising and re-engineer your sales process for them specifically** (Steps 4a and 4b)

> *"Over the long haul, this increases the quality and quantity of your lead flow. It also increases your average customer value as you are weeding out all the less-than-qualified potential customers up front."*

## The Closing Reframe

> *"Growing a business comes down to selling more customers or making them worth more. This chapter accomplishes both. You get more clients because your marketing becomes more tailored. And, you make your clients worth more by exclusively selling the highest value people."*

> *"It feels like cheating because it's so obvious. But here's the great part — no one does it. Getting this step right will act as a force multiplier on the remaining contents in this book."*

The Lost Chapter is positioned as a **force multiplier** on the main book. Every concept in $100M Offers gets more powerful when applied to a precisely-defined avatar.

## Application Notes

Run the process every 6-12 months. Customer composition changes:
- New segments may emerge as your offer evolves
- Old segments may decay as the market shifts
- Buyer-journey patterns may change (TikTok content might replace blog posts, for example)

The discipline isn't a one-time exercise. It's an ongoing audit.
