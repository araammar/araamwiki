## Origin Stories

### The Christmas Eve $1,036 Story (December 24, 2016)

The opening scene of the book. Hormozi at a movie theater on Christmas Eve, heart racing, having just been told by his payment processor that they were holding $120,000 of his sales for six months as "precautionary." His salesman was owed $22,000 in commission for sales that month.

He wired the $22,000 to his salesman. Bank balance: **$1,036**. He screenshotted it because he knew he'd tell the story one day.

Earlier that month, his "partner" had drained the $46,000 from their joint account (down to $300). His mother was in critical condition from a near-fatal accident. He'd totaled his car in a 60mph head-on collision. He'd earned a DUI.

His only saving grace: a new "challenge offer" at a gym, collecting all the cash up front as a "turnaround fee."

Two days after the bank call, he was scheduled to launch six new gyms simultaneously. Between airfare, hotels, rental cars, gas, and ad spend × 6, he was about to spend **$3,300/day of money he didn't have** on a $100,000-limit credit card.

> *"-$3,300 . . . I now officially have nothing*
> *-$3,300 . . . I now have officially less than nothing*
> *-$3,300 . . . I have $10,000 less than nothing*
> *-$3,300 . . . This one decision is going to ruin my future forever."*

January 2017: he made **$100,117**. Just enough.

End of year: $1,500,000+/mo. 12 months later: $4,400,000/mo. 24 months later: crossed $120,000,000 in sales. Donated $2,000,000 to fund equal opportunity in low-income areas. Met Arnold Schwarzenegger (lifelong hero), became board members of his charity After School All Stars.

Twelve months after that: a portfolio of seven 8-figure and multi-8-figure companies (photography, publishing, fitness, consulting, beauty) doing **~$1,600,000 per week**.

> *"All of this was because of a girl who believed in me, a credit card, and a Grand Slam Offer."*

### The Hot Dog Stand Professor Parable

A marketing professor asked: *"If you were going to open a hot dog stand, and you could only have one advantage over your competitors . . . which would it be?"*

Students: "Location! Quality! Low prices! Best taste!"

Professor: *"A starving crowd."*

> *"You could have the worst hot dogs, terrible prices, and be in a terrible location, but if you're the only hot dog stand in town and the local college football game breaks out, you're going to sell out."*

## Value & Psychology Stories

### Russell Brunson's Wife Collette (Status As Driver)

Russell Brunson's wife Collette initially rejected the idea that she was driven by status. She claimed she wasn't — she'd never want a Lamborghini, she preferred her minivan.

Deeper conversation revealed:
- Driving a Lamborghini would **decrease her status** among her mom friends
- Driving a minivan would **increase her status** (signaled good motherhood)

> *"So it's not about the money, it's about the status."*

Both the Lamborghini and the minivan are status purchases. Different groups. Different signals. Same underlying mechanism.

→ Concept: [[status-as-deep-driver]]

### London Tunnel Dotted-Map

Logical solution to commuter dissatisfaction: faster trains. Cost: billions.

Psychological solution actually deployed: **a dotted-line map showing when the next train arrives**. Cost: a few million dollars. Bigger satisfaction lift.

The problem wasn't train speed. It was the *uncertainty of waiting*. Solving the perception of time delay was the actual fix.

→ Concepts: [[logical-vs-psychological-solutions]], [[value-equation]] (Perception is Reality)

### Elevator Mirror

### The Eating-Out Guide Anecdote

When Hormozi sold weight loss, he insisted clients prepare all food at home. He found it too difficult to help clients lose weight when they ate out. So he refused sales of anyone who couldn't commit. **He lost many sales.**

One month he needed rent money. A business exec walked in. Wanted weight loss. Ate lunch out every day. Normally he'd lose this sale.

Instead: *"I'll make you an eating out guide for when you go to restaurants so you can eat out 100 percent of the time and still hit your goal. How does that sound?"* She agreed. He closed.

He built the guide. From that point on, whenever anyone said *"but what about eating out??"* he had the solution.

> *"Don't get romantic about how you want to solve the problem. Find a way to solve every problem a prospect presents with. When you do that, you make an offer that's so good, people just can't say no."*

### Arnold Schwarzenegger After School All Stars Fundraiser

May 2019. Arnold's home. $25,000-per-ticket fundraiser. 100-person invite list. Lamborghinis, Bugattis, A-List actors.

Hormozi met George — luxury watch dealer who'd built an ultra-high-end jewelry and watch business ($100K to $2M+ status symbols). Donated $700,000 in merchandise as auction prizes.

George's doctrine: **"When demand increases, cut supply."**

Result: night raised **$5,400,000 from 100 people** ($54,000/head average) — most successful in the charity's history.

Each auction item sold as "one-of-a-kind — if you miss it, you'll never have a chance again." Arnold added bonuses when bidding got high enough so both bidders won.

> *"People want what they can't have. People want what other people want. People want things only a select few have access to."*

> *"An item that wouldn't sell at a different venue for $10,000 sold for $100,000."*
