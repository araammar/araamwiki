Every "Pro Tip" boxed insight from $100M Offers, indexed and searchable in one place. Hormozi uses these throughout the book as the high-value operational shortcuts — they don't always fit cleanly into a chapter's main argument, but they're often the most actionable bits.

## Sales & Persuasion

### The Two Goals Of Writing vs Persuasion
> *"The point of good writing is for the reader to understand. The point of good persuasion is for the prospect to feel understood."*

— Hormozi (Starving Crowd chapter)

Used in: [[pain-is-the-pitch]]

---

### Fast Wins For Long-Term Retention
> *"Always try and incorporate short-term, immediate wins for a client. Be creative. They just need to know they are on the right path and that they made the right decision trusting you and your business."*

— Hormozi (Value Equation chapter)

Used in: [[value-equation]] (Time Delay driver)

Implementation: Within the first 7 days of a client signing up, engineer one big emotional win. For Gym Launch: getting their ads live and closing their first $2,000 sale. This reinforces the buying decision and builds momentum.

---

### Fast Beats Free
> *"The only thing that beats 'free' is 'fast.' People will pay for speed. Many companies have entered free spaces and done exceedingly well with a 'speed first' strategy."*

Examples:
- MVD vs DMV — pay $50 to skip the line
- FedEx vs USPS — "when it absolutely positively has to be there overnight"
- Spotify vs Slow Free Music
- Uber vs Walking

> *"Many will always be willing to pay (price) for the (value) of speed. So if you find yourself in a market competing against free, double down on speed."*

Used in: [[value-equation]]

---

### Frame Benefits As Status From Others' Viewpoint
> *"When writing copy, you can make it that much more powerful by talking about how other people will perceive the prospect's achievement. Connect the dots for them."*

> *"Example: If you buy this golf club, your drive will increase by 40 yards. Your golf buddies' jaws will drop when they see your ball soar 40 yards past theirs . . . they'll ask you what's changed . . . only you will know."*

Used in: [[status-as-deep-driver]]

---

### Logical vs Psychological Solutions (Rory Sutherland Lens)
> *"As business owners and entrepreneurs I increasingly approach problems to find psychological solutions, rather than logical ones. Because if there were a logical solution, it probably would have already been solved."*

> *"Any fool can sell a product by offering it for a discount, it takes great marketing to sell the same product for a premium."* (Sutherland)

Used in: [[logical-vs-psychological-solutions]]

## Pricing

### The Stinging-Investment Principle
> *"Those who pay the most, pay the most attention."*

Used in: [[commoditization-and-pricing-power]]

If your product requires effort from the customer for the result to land, get them as invested as possible. Pricing that "stings a little when they buy" forces their attention and adherence.

---

### Dan Kennedy's Two Pricing Doctrines
> *"Charge as high a price as you can say out loud without cracking a smile."*

> *"There is no strategic benefit to being the second cheapest in the marketplace, but there is for being the most expensive."*

Used in: [[commoditization-and-pricing-power]]

---

### Clean Your Pipeline With Every Price Change
> *"If you ever really are planning on raising your prices (hopefully soon if you are reading this book!) then you can always clean out your pipeline by letting people know 'The price is going up! So get in now!' Never raise your prices without letting people know. It shows a position of strength and will give you a nice little influx of cash from the people in the pipeline who were on the fence."*

Used in: [[urgency-playbook]] (Pricing-Based Urgency)

---

## Bonuses

### Add Bonuses Instead Of Discounting On Core Offers
> *"Whenever trying to close a deal, never discount the main offer. It teaches your customers that your prices are negotiable (which is terrible). Adding bonuses to increase value to close the deal is far superior to cutting prices. It puts you in a position of strength and goodwill rather than weakness."*

Used in: [[bonus-stacking-system]]

---

### Tools & Checklists > Trainings
> *"Tools & checklists are better than additional trainings (as the effort & time are lower with the former, so the value is higher. The value equation still reigns supreme)."*

Used in: [[bonus-stacking-system]] (Rule 7)

---

## Scarcity

### Provide Limited Access For Higher Ticket Services
> *"These scarcity tactics work especially well for higher ticket upsells. If you want to create one off workshops, trainings, events, seminars, consulting, etc. These are things that by their nature take time and provide more access. Pairing them with clear scarcity or fixed amounts, seats, or spots will rapidly drive up the demand."*

> *"But always remember have less spots available than you think you can sell . . . so that when you want to do it again in the future, everyone will remember you sold out . . . fast. This is a compounding strategy that increases in effectiveness over time. One of the few in the marketing arsenal."*

Used in: [[scarcity-playbook]]

---

### Extreme Scarcity — Sell Limited 1-on-1 Access
> *"If you don't hate money, sell a very limited supply of 1-on-1 access. You can do that via any of the mediums described in 'Delivery Cube.' Direct message access. Email Access. Phone access. Voice memo access. Zoom access. Etc."*

> *"I promise you this — if you want to immediately make a lot of money, create a very exclusive service level based on access to you (yes, unscalable), that you cap at a tiny number. Price it very high. Then, tell people. You will make more money than you thought possible. These also tend to be some of the best clients."*

> *"And limit your delivery to something that you don't hate. For me, I hate emails and messages but don't mind zoom calls. Make it work for your working style. The cream of the crop (the 1% of 1% will adjust and take action)."*

Used in: [[scarcity-playbook]], [[delivery-cube]]

---

### Once You're Out, You Can Never Come Back
> *"You can create scarcity by also capping your service level and saying that if they leave then can never return. This type of scarcity makes people think extra hard about leaving."*

Hormozi started doing this with his gyms early on, then in a mastermind, then in his Gym Lords offering.

> *"This works best with small groups. As groups become much bigger, the tactic loses some teeth (speaking from experience)."*

Used in: [[scarcity-playbook]]

## Guarantees

### Name Your Guarantee Something Cool
> *"If you are going to give a guarantee, spice it up. Instead of using 'satisfaction' or some other 'vanilla' word, describe it more strongly."*

- Generic (bad): 30 Day Money Back Satisfaction Guarantee
- Good: *"In 30 days, if you wouldn't jump into shark infested waters to get our product back, we will return every dollar you paid."*
- Great: *"You'll get our famous 'Club a Baby Seal Guarantee' — After 30 days of using our services, if you wouldn't club a baby seal to stay on as a customer, you don't have to pay a penny."*

Used in: [[guarantee-library]]

---

### Unconditional vs Conditional Based On Business Type
> *"Bigger broader guarantees work better with lower ticket B2C businesses (many people just won't bother taking the time). The higher the ticket, and the more business oriented it is, the more you want to steer towards specific guarantees. That may or may not include refunds, and may or may not have conditions."*

Used in: [[guarantee-library]]

---

### High Cost Services Warning
> *"If you have a tremendous amount of cost associated with your product or service, you will likely want to employ a conditional guarantee or an ANTI guarantee, as you will have to eat the cost of the refund AND the cost of fulfilling."*

Used in: [[guarantee-library]]

---

## Naming

### Find Time To Rhyme
> *"Good rhymes stick in people's minds. Rhyme your program name to win the game."*

> *"Google 'rhyming dictionary' for an easy shortcut. Note — Don't try and force it. It's not a requirement, it's just a 'nice-to-have'."*

Examples: Six-Pack Fast Track, 5-Day Book Print Sprint, Marriage Thrive Deep Dive, 12-Week 2-Putt Shortcut, 12-Month No-Debt Reset, Celebrity Butt Shortcut.

Used in: [[magic-naming-formula]]

---

### Alliteration
> *"Alliteration is when you make all (or most) of the words start with the same letter or sound. An alternative approach to rhyming is to use alliteration when naming your program. This is easier for most people than rhyming. Again, you do not need to rhyme or alliterate. Don't force it."*

Examples: Make Money Masterclass, Change Your Life Challenge, Big Booty Bootcamp, Debt Detox, Real Estate Reset, Life Coach Liftoff.

Used in: [[magic-naming-formula]]

---

### Name Sub-Items And Bonuses
> *"Use the magic headline formula for each item in your stack and bundle. It will automatically enhance the value of your offerings simply by naming in a way that resonates with your prospects."*

Used in: [[magic-naming-formula]], [[bonus-stacking-system]], [[5-step-grand-slam-creation]]

## Lost Chapter Pro Tips

### Your Competitors Won't Do The Work
> *"This takes reading through all the answers and using your brain. I know. Thinking is hard. The good news is — your competitors won't do it — easy advantage."*

(Lost Chapter, Step 3 of avatar refinement)

Used in: [[lost-chapter-avatar-refinement]]

The structural advantage of any tedious-but-valuable work: nobody else does it. Just by doing it, you've already outrun most of the market.

---

### Stop Selling The Wrong Customers (Even When You Need The Money)
> *"Stop selling anyone who does not meet your ideal customer requirements. Seriously, stop it."*

Used in: [[lost-chapter-avatar-refinement]], [[quality-over-quantity-acquisition]]

The hardest implementation step. Disqualifying a paying customer feels wrong in the moment. The math is on your side over the long haul.

---

### The $5,000-for-$45,000 Reframe
> *"I would rather pay $5000 to acquire $45,000 than pay $1000 to acquire $5000 (even though it costs five times as much)."*

Used in: [[quality-over-quantity-acquisition]]

ROI is a multiplier, not a cost. Higher CAC for higher-value customers is almost always the right trade.

---

### Knowing The Ideal Buyer Journey Forces Patience
> *"Knowing your ideal buyer journey forces patience. You see the business holistically, rather than as a widget to be sold to as many people as possible."*

Used in: [[reverse-engineer-buying-process]], [[quality-over-quantity-acquisition]]

The patience point is about temporal scope: short-term thinking optimizes for fast closes; long-term thinking optimizes for the right closes.

---

### 8× Your Price, Keep Your Costs The Same
> *"For a moment, I want you to imagine 8x your price, and keeping your costs the same. How much more profit would you be making? Probably a lot. That's the difference."*

Used in: [[quality-over-quantity-acquisition]]

The thought experiment that makes margin compounding viscerally obvious.

---

### Merging Marketing And Sales Solves The Volume Tension
> *"Marketing stopped complaining that the sales team wasn't closing. Sales team stopped complaining that they wanted more leads. Everyone came together to focus on what mattered: closing lots of valuable deals."*

Used in: [[quality-over-quantity-acquisition]]

Organizational design follows incentive design. One acquisition department with one KPI (profitable deals) outperforms two departments with two KPIs.

---

### The Lost Chapter As Force Multiplier
> *"Getting this step right will act as a force multiplier on the remaining contents in this book."*

Used in: [[lost-chapter-avatar-refinement]]

Hormozi explicitly positions avatar refinement as upstream of all the offer-creation work. A 10× offer to the wrong avatar still underperforms a 5× offer to the right one.

---

### Why Wouldn't You Make The Right Long Term Call?
> *"Unless you're planning on quitting business, why wouldn't you make the right long term call?"*

Used in: [[quality-over-quantity-acquisition]]

The rhetorical question that ends the patience debate. If you're in this for the long haul, the math is clear. If you're not, none of this applies anyway.
