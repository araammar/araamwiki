# Caret Model — Six Messaging Angles (Phase 4)

## Strategic Context

The entire category (Apollo, Clay, Instantly, Belkins, CIENCE, ProspectEcho) is locked into "more data, more automation, more pipeline" messaging. All six angles below attack a different enemy and target a different emotional state of the agency-owner buyer. Zero overlap between them.

---

## The Six Angles

---

### ANGLE 1 — THE DIAGNOSIS

**The Enemy:** Contact lists. The entire "buy a list, blast a sequence, hope for replies" paradigm.

**The Buyer State:** Skeptical and exhausted. They've bought lists before. They want evidence on the page, not more promises.

**The Core Claim:** Every lead arrives with a documented conversion problem and the evidence to prove it, so you're not pitching a stranger — you're delivering a diagnosis.

**Headline Variants:**
1. We don't sell leads. We sell diagnoses.
2. Every prospect arrives with a documented problem and the screenshot to prove it.
3. A list tells you who. A diagnosis tells you why now.
4. Stop pitching strangers. Start delivering diagnoses.
5. The only prospecting tool that hands you the proof, not just the contact.

**Subheadline:** Local businesses already running Google or Meta ads, audited for the specific conversion failure that's killing their ROAS — delivered with a ready-to-send hook.

**CTA:** "See a sample diagnosis"

**Tone:** Clinical, confident, slightly contemptuous of the alternative.

**Best Channel:** Landing page hero, LinkedIn ads, LinkedIn organic

**Kill Criteria:** Landing page conversion under 1.5% after 4 weeks of paid traffic

---

### ANGLE 2 — THEY'RE ALREADY PAYING (YOU JUST DON'T KNOW IT YET)

**The Enemy:** Pitching businesses that have no budget.

**The Buyer State:** Money-focused, ROI-driven, doing back-of-the-envelope math.

**The Core Claim:** A business spending $3,000 a month on Google Ads has already answered the budget objection — your job is just to show up with a better offer than the one they're paying for.

**Headline Variants:**
1. They're already spending $3,000 a month. The only question is who gets it.
2. Stop pitching budgets. Pitch the businesses that already have one.
3. Active ad spend is the only buy signal that pays the bill.
4. Find businesses bleeding money on Google Ads. Be the one who stops the bleeding.
5. Their credit card is already on file. Yours just isn't on it yet.

**Subheadline:** Every lead is a local business actively running Google or Meta ads — proof of budget, proof of intent, proof they buy this kind of help.

**CTA:** "Show me who's spending right now in [my city]"

**Tone:** Confident, slightly mercenary, transactional. Sales-floor energy.

**Best Channel:** Cold outreach, Meta/Facebook ads, X/Twitter

**Kill Criteria:** Cold email reply rate below 4% across 2,000+ sends

---

### ANGLE 3 — OUTREACH WITH DIGNITY

**The Enemy:** Cold outreach as currently practiced — the begging, the "just bumping this," the apology-laced templates.

**The Buyer State:** Demoralized. Burned out. Slightly ashamed of what they've been doing.

**The Core Claim:** When you reach out with a documented problem the prospect didn't know they had, you stop being a salesperson and start being useful — and useful people don't have to beg.

**Headline Variants:**
1. Cold outreach without the part where you feel like you're begging.
2. You're not interrupting them. You're telling them their site is broken.
3. Stop apologizing for the email. Lead with the evidence.
4. The version of outreach that doesn't cost you your self-respect.
5. Evidence is permission. Begging is what you do without it.

**Subheadline:** A lead with a documented problem isn't a cold email — it's a favor. We hand you both.

**CTA:** "See what an outreach hook looks like when you actually have something to say"

**Tone:** Empathetic, knowing, quietly angry on the buyer's behalf. Manifesto energy.

**Best Channel:** Long-form LinkedIn posts, manifesto landing page, X threads, YouTube pre-roll

---

### ANGLE 4 — THE LEAKY FUNNEL VOYEUR

**The Enemy:** The fiction that local businesses have their act together.

**The Buyer State:** Curious, contemptuous, entertained. The same emotional state that makes people watch ad teardown videos.

**The Core Claim:** Every day, local businesses pour ad budget into landing pages that load in 8 seconds, have no booking form, and score 12/100 on mobile — and the agencies who can see it have a permanent supply of warm pitches.

**Headline Variants:**
1. The day spa in your zip code is paying for traffic that bounces before it books. Want their name?
2. Real broken funnels, paid for in real ad dollars, audited every morning.
3. Look at what the businesses near you are doing to their own conversion rates.
4. We watch local businesses bleed. Then we tell you which ones.
5. A daily feed of local businesses paying Google to send traffic to a broken site.

**Subheadline:** Active advertisers, real audit data, fresh every day — the ones you'd be embarrassed to know existed if you weren't the one fixing them.

**CTA:** "Show me today's leaks in [city]"

**Tone:** Conspiratorial, slightly gossipy, voyeuristic.

**Best Channel:** Meta/Instagram carousels, TikTok/Reels, weekly "Worst Funnels" content piece

---

### ANGLE 5 — BURN THE ROLODEX

**The Enemy:** Apollo, ZoomInfo, Clay, and the entire contact-database orthodoxy.

**The Buyer State:** Contemptuous of the status quo, looking for a reason to feel righteous about switching.

**The Core Claim:** A contact database is a phone book. We're not selling you a phone book; we're selling you a reason to call.

**Headline Variants:**
1. Apollo gives you 210 million phone book entries. We give you a reason to call one.
2. Burn the rolodex. The data isn't the moat anymore — the diagnosis is.
3. They sell contacts. We sell context. There's a $79/mo difference.
4. The contact list is dead. Long live the documented problem.
5. Stop renting a database. Start buying reasons.

**Subheadline:** Evidence-first prospecting for agencies who are done paying $499 a month for emails that bounce.

**CTA:** "Compare a list to a diagnosis"

**Tone:** Confident, cocky, slightly aggressive. Category-disruptor energy.

**Best Channel:** Google Search on competitor brand terms, comparison landing pages, X/Twitter

**Risk:** Naming Apollo/ZoomInfo directly invites legal review. Deploy only after 50+ customer logos.

---

### ANGLE 6 — THE HOOK COMES PRE-WRITTEN

**The Enemy:** The blank page. The freelancer's daily ritual of staring at a cold-email draft with no good first line.

**The Buyer State:** Practical, time-starved, copy-anxious.

**The Core Claim:** Every lead arrives with the opening line already written — pulled from the actual audit, in plain English, ready to paste.

**Headline Variants:**
1. The first line of your cold email, written for you, before you ever see the lead.
2. Stop writing 30 openers a day. We hand you 30 a day.
3. Every lead ships with a hook. You ship the email.
4. The audit writes the opener. You write the close.
5. Personalized cold email at the speed of copy-paste.

**Subheadline:** A documented problem plus a ready-to-send opening line attached to every prospect — your sequences, your voice, your sending domain, no more blank pages.

**CTA:** "See three real hooks from today's leads"

**Tone:** Pragmatic, utility-focused, productivity-first.

**Best Channel:** Product Hunt launch, indie-hacker and freelancer communities, Google Search on "cold email personalization"

## Priority Ranking

### Test These Two First

**#1: THE DIAGNOSIS** — owns the largest piece of unclaimed whitespace and maps directly to the product's core mechanic. Test on landing page and LinkedIn.

**#2: THEY'RE ALREADY PAYING** — exploits the sharpest insight from the product mechanics. Also the angle Caret itself uses when doing cold outreach. Test on cold email and Meta ads.

### Why Not Lead With Outreach With Dignity?

Most emotionally resonant but most fragile. Doesn't differentiate on cold traffic. Better for retention and warm conversion than cold acquisition.

### Why Not Lead With Burn the Rolodex?

Generates great content and Twitter engagement, but picks fights with $1B+ incumbents. Phase-two angle, after 50+ customer logos.

---

## The Combination Play

**The Diagnosis** owns the homepage hero, the LinkedIn presence, and the way Caret gets talked about in third-party reviews.

**Outreach With Dignity** takes over once the buyer is in a sequence — the post-signup onboarding email, in-product copy on the lead-detail page, case study language.

**They're Already Paying** runs underneath both as the conversion-rate-optimizer on cold channels — the headline on the Meta ad that drives traffic to the Diagnosis landing page, the subject line on the cold email.

---

## Words Banned From All Copy

- "intelligent," "AI-powered" (unless you specifically explain what the AI does)
- "seamless" (poisoned by Seamless.AI's 1.4/5 Trustpilot)
- "intuitive," "powerful," "robust"
- "verified" (say what you verified and when)
- "qualified leads" (say what makes them qualified)
- "scale your outreach" (synonymous with spam)
