# Reference: Recommended Books for Branding & Marketing

Books organized by category. This list is curated for modern creatives, marketers, brand strategists, and entrepreneurs — prioritizing books that shape how you think, not just what you know. Specifically selected to avoid the common "top marketing books" lists that skew heavily toward Seth Godin and obvious business bestsellers.

---

## Branding, Marketing & Design

**Like Art** — Glenn O'Brien
A collection from O'Brien's column in Art Forum, written by a noted advertiser. Takes a sophisticated, nuanced, and at times ridiculous look at the world of selling things — similar in spirit to analytical breakdowns of why campaigns work or don't. Written during a classic era when copy mattered enormously and the industry was changing rapidly. One of the best windows into advertising thought that's available.

**Emotion by Design** — Former CMO of Nike
Breaks down the left-brain side of building major brand concepts — the actual frameworks used to put a brand like Nike together. Useful for understanding how big brands think about identity, not just execution.

**Capslock** — Ruben Pater
Extremely dense — read 10 pages at a time, skip around, open to any section. Examines graphic design's impact on capitalism: currency, credit cards, modern UX, the design of cities, tourism, and advertising. Probably the most comprehensive look at how everything around us is designed to sell ideas or things. Builds the lens with which you view the commercial world. Highly recommended.

**Made to Stick** — Chip Heath & Dan Heath
The bible for messaging. How to make ideas that people care about. If you're a marketer, product developer, or anyone who needs to communicate that something is different — this teaches positioning and angle at a foundational level. Recommended by multiple CEOs.

## Context: Oral Histories & Industry Turning Points

**Powerhouse** — James Andrew Miller
Oral history of CAA (Creative Artists Agency). Multiple voices from the same era, each telling their version of the story of how CAA changed Hollywood talent management and how the influencer generation is now replacing the talent system described in this book. If you work in or around the creator economy, this is essential context.

**These Guys Have All the Fun** — James Andrew Miller
Oral history of ESPN — how one channel changed cable television, bullied an entire industry because it had sports, and built a generation of personalities from Bristol, Connecticut. Now watching cable's decline in reverse. Read alongside Powerhouse to understand how media power shifts from one era to the next.

**Grantland Volume 7** (or any volume)
The last pinnacle of serious sports journalism — writing by people who cared about craft and fandom, before the industry became click-optimized slop. Good for understanding what well-written storytelling looks and feels like. Also useful for seeing what journalism at its best looks like if your content consumption is dominated by fragmented, algorithm-optimized writing.

**Fear and Loathing on the Campaign Trail '72** — Hunter S. Thompson
Thompson covering the McGovern/Nixon election in his signature overdone style. Most useful as perspective: US politics was just as crazy, divided, and hyped in the '70s. The difference is the quality of the writing. For creatives who feel overwhelmed by sensationalist news, this is a calibration book — things have always been chaotic, and even chaos can be written about with nuance.

## Mindset

**The War of Art** — Steven Pressfield
Gives the enemy a name: "The Resistance" — the force that prevents you from completing the work you want to make. Written by someone who is simultaneously a best-selling author of historical fiction and someone who dealt deeply with procrastination throughout his career. Read this alongside Gates of Fire (also Pressfield) for the duality of a man who writes about conquering opponents while fighting his own resistance every day.

**Gates of Fire** — Steven Pressfield
Historical fiction. The story that 300 is loosely based on. Included here not as a self-help book but as a companion to The War of Art — the best context for understanding how someone at the top of their craft thinks about mastery and discipline. Also just a genuinely excellent book.

**The Obstacle Is the Way** — Ryan Holiday
Every time you find yourself thinking "I'll do anything but that thing" — the answer is to do that thing. Stoic philosophy applied to modern decision-making. Useful for creatives who avoid selling, avoid putting their face on camera, avoid any specific high-leverage thing. The obstacle is always the answer.

**The Courage to Be Disliked** — Ichiro Kishimi & Fumitake Koga
Based on Adlerian psychology. Structured as a dialogue. The core insight: being genuinely good at things — managing people, leading creative work, creating content — requires being okay with not being liked all the time. Essential for anyone who identifies as a people-pleaser or who avoids strong opinions to stay comfortable.

**The 4-Hour Work Week** — Tim Ferriss
Read for the ideas about scalable business design, outsourcing, and lifestyle architecture — not to literally work four hours. The specific business-design concepts (how to think about time spent, what can be systematized, how to build something that isn't entirely dependent on your active presence) are still valid. Ignore the promise of the title.

**Meaning at Work** — Various
Examines the role work plays in human life and how to find meaning in it — relevant for creatives who feel caught between wanting a creative life and being very good at a business function. Useful at any moment of career confusion or professional transition.

## Fashion

**House of Errors** — (Founder memoir/photo book)
Five years of House of Errors from its origin — scraping together, overordering, paying Instagram archive pages to post just to get first sales, to building a significant brand. Comes free with some orders. More realistic view of building a fashion brand than any "make $100K on your first drop" YouTube content.

**An Atlas of Modern Clothing**
Nomenclature compendium — walks through clothing types (safari jacket, trench, blazer, etc.) and labels every component (epaulettes, patch pockets, flat collars, Prussian collars). Gives you the vocabulary to talk to designers, manufacturers, and pattern makers without pointing at things. Rare in print; check textile industry shows.

**Textileedia**
Walks through fabric types (chino, cavalry twill, fleece weights), compares fabrics for different applications, covers printing styles, dying methods, tanning techniques. Bridges the gap between "I send mood board images" and "I understand what I'm actually making."

## Money & Business

**$100M Offers** — Alex Hormozi
Teaches you how to structure what you sell. If you freelance or sell creative services, this transforms how you think about packaging — from selling time to selling outcomes with stacked value. Covers the sales psychology behind guarantees, bonuses, and offer structure that gets people to say yes without negotiating price.

**Sponsor Magnet** — Justin Moore
Specifically for creators trying to get brand deals. Covers how to reach out, how to prepare a media kit, how to frame value to brands, and how to actually close sponsorships when you don't have a manager. Also useful for anyone selling services to marketing departments.

**Becoming Trader Joe's**
Every decision at Trader Joe's — packaging design, pricing structure, store layout, compensation model — exists for a critical reason. A case study in positioning through culture. Recommended for any marketer who wants to understand how operational choices create brand identity.

**Super Pumped** — Mike Isaac
The Travis Kalanick/Uber story. Officially a cautionary tale about ego-driven leadership; functionally treated by many as a blueprint for aggressive company building. The interesting read is in the gap between those two interpretations.

**Traction** (EOS / Entrepreneurial Operating System) — Gino Wickman
The framework for running a business with 10+ people. Covers goal-setting, meeting structure, accountability, and communication between leadership and teams. Creative people who grow to 20–30-person agencies or brands often don't know structured frameworks exist — this is the one worth learning.

**Exponential Organizations** — Salim Ismail
Describes businesses that grow disproportionately beyond the sum of their people — the hockey stick that can explode at any point. Core concept worth understanding even if you don't read the whole book: what makes a business capable of non-linear growth?

## World Building & Fiction

**Blood Meridian** — Cormac McCarthy
Dark. Set in the Texas-Mexico border in the 1850s. McCarthy immerses you in a world through word choice and narrative structure — every decision creates an atmosphere before you've consciously noticed it. Read this for how to build worlds through writing, which translates directly to brand world-building. See his broader canon for a consistent example of this approach applied across multiple settings.

**Zorville / The Adderall Diaries** — American literary fiction
Modern, American, emotional — books that capture a particular version of American life with enough specificity to feel cinematic. Useful for thinking about how you'd write or interpret your own experience in narrative form.

**The Three-Body Problem** — Liu Cixin
Chinese science fiction translated into English. The most useful thing about it: in Western sci-fi, the hero is an individual. In this book, humanity is the protagonist. For anyone who works with China or wants to understand a fundamentally different way of framing collective action and scale. Also shows what happens when you read outside a single cultural tradition.

## How to Read More of These

The most sustainable reading habit: 30 minutes before sleep, devices off, no screens. One book per month is realistic for most people with full schedules. The goal isn't volume — it's building a lens that helps you think differently than the people around you who are consuming the same algorithm-curated content as everyone else.

## Current Read (Bonus)

**Gray Mirror** — (author Curtis Yarvin / Mencius Moldbug)
A book about critical thinking that "positions a lot of common liberal perceptions of the past and presents counterpoints to them." Recommended not for the conclusions but for studying how contrarian arguments are constructed: "the way with which they argue, the way with which they talk, how they present the alternatives, and the way people's minds work and approach it is just a really fascinating thing." The host's explicit framing: reading contrarian material is preparation for an era where ChatGPT and Claude summarize consensus — your edge is the ability to position contrarian statements and think in ways that AI summarizes poorly. For strategists who want to study argument structure and positioning mechanics from outside the marketing canon.
