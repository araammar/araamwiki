# Concept: CM1 / CM2 / CM3 Contribution Margin Stack

## Summary / Definition
A three-tier P&L structure used in CPG businesses to measure profitability at each level of cost removal. Dom Iacovone uses CM1 as his single most important weekly metric.

**The Stack:**
- **Net Revenue** = Gross revenue minus trade spend (discounts, rebates, deductions — the "gross-to-net waterfall")
- **CM1** = Net Revenue − Cost of Goods − Inbound Freight
- **CM2** = CM1 − Outbound Freight
- **CM3** = CM2 − SG&A, Marketing, and all other overhead

CM1 is the "first line of profitability" — the most real-time indicator of business health. CM2 and CM3 have too much lag for weekly tracking; those get trued up monthly.

## Why It Matters
Most founders lump everything into one P&L number and don't know where margin is being lost. The CM stack tells you exactly where profitability degrades at each cost layer.

Critically: **the gross-to-net waterfall sits above the P&L entirely** — trade spend is taken out before the money even hits the bank, so it doesn't appear on a standard P&L. This is where most margin is silently lost.

Dom: *"Gross to net is the biggest place where you leak. That's your trade spend, your discounts, your rebates — all the stuff that comes out before it hits your P&L. And that's where most margin is lost. Because it doesn't hit a P&L, most people forget about it."*

## How It Works / The Mechanism

**Weekly tracking:** Net Revenue + CM1 only.
- CM1 fluctuates by channel (D2C has higher CM1 than Sam's Club, for example)
- Monday finance meeting: track these two numbers against monthly target
- Everything else (CM2, CM3) is reviewed at end-of-month true-up

**CM1 target for CPG health:**
- North of 18% EBITDA at the bottom line is a healthy target for a CPG business
- If CM1 is degrading, it's the first signal something is wrong with pricing, product mix, or channel distribution

**The SGNA benchmark:**
Dom's SGNA target: **4% of revenue.** Built up from 6-8% during the build phase, compressed to 4% as the business scaled. This is considered exceptional; AI is pushing it lower.

**P&L structure top-to-bottom:**
1. Gross Revenue
2. − Trade Spend (rebates, discounts, deductions) → **Net Revenue**
3. − COGS + Inbound Freight → **CM1**
4. − Outbound Freight → **CM2**
5. − SG&A + Marketing + Other → **CM3 / EBITDA**

## What to Steal / Application
- Track CM1 weekly. Don't wait for month-end financials to know if you're on track.
- Hire someone specifically to manage the gross-to-net waterfall — Dom has a dedicated ex-P&G executive for this role who does nothing but manage trade spend, anti-cannibalization, and price pack architecture across channels.
- At 10% blended trade spend on $100M revenue = $10M leaving before the P&L starts. On a 10–20x multiple that's $100M–$200M of exit value destroyed.
- SGNA over 8% is a warning sign. Under 6% is strong. 4% is world-class.
