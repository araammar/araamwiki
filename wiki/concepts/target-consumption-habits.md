# Concept: Target Consumption Habits — The Underrated Marketing Insight

## Summary / Definition

Most content strategy focuses on what to say and how to say it. Target consumption habits is a prior question: *when, where, and in what context does your specific audience actually consume content?* The answer to this question changes production decisions, format selection, platform choice, and scheduling in ways that raw demographic data never could. Understanding consumption context — lunchtime TV-mode YouTube, late-night couch scrolling, commute audio, work-break Instagram — is what separates content that fits into a real person's day from content that exists in a vacuum.

---

## The Core Insight

Orin's framing: "Consumption habits — target consumption habits — is the most underrated marketing concept that people don't think about."

The example that crystallizes it: **young men watching YouTube on TV at lunch.**

This is a specific consumption behavior with specific implications:
- The audience is not casually scrolling; they are in a lean-back, full-attention mode
- They are eating, which means audio is active (not muted like in public)
- The content that fits this moment is longer, more like a show episode, more visual
- The creator who understands this makes 20-minute art direction videos showcasing brand portfolios — "giving you 20 minutes of a brand's greatest hits while an entertaining, credible person talks to you about why it matters"
- This is indistinguishable from watching a TV documentary at lunch

The creators Orin observes who capture this consumption moment: Represent guys, Marcus, Minted. Their content functions as "lunchtime guy consumption content" — aspirational lifestyle viewing for a 20-something male watching men he wants to become live their lives for 20 minutes.

## Platform-Specific Consumption Contexts

### YouTube (TV Mode)
- **Primary consumption moment:** Lunch, evenings, weekends — lean-back mode on TV or large screen
- **Audience behavior:** Full attention, long session time, will watch 20–45 minute videos
- **Age skew:** YouTube's youngest audiences are younger than TikTok or Instagram audiences for similar creator types
- **Format fit:** Visual-heavy content, documentation, "experiencing something through the creator," educational but entertaining
- **Creator proof:** Orin regularly gets DMs from people watching his YouTube content on their television — "I'm on the thing in your house. You're eating lunch and I'm on the TV."

### Instagram / TikTok (Mobile Scroll Mode)
- **Primary consumption moment:** Commute, break time, morning, evening — active scroll
- **Audience behavior:** Low attention tolerance, 3-second hook requirement, 60-second ceiling for most
- **Demographic note:** Most VP/C-level executives are on Instagram casually — this is where B2B content actually reaches decision-makers, not LinkedIn
- **Format fit:** Punchy takes, visual hooks, green screen with compelling graphics, quick value delivery
- **Decision-maker use:** VPs and C-suite browse Instagram casually and DM content to their Slack channels or teams → this is the B2B Slack-share mechanic in action

### LinkedIn (Professional Intent Mode)
- **Primary consumption moment:** Between meetings, morning routines, professional development mode
- **Audience behavior:** Higher intent, lower entertainment tolerance, looking for practical information
- **Unique dynamic:** Not where executives are consuming casually — they are already in professional mode
- **Format fit:** Depth-first analysis, frameworks, specific case studies, credentialed expertise
- **The limitation:** Top-tier executives browse Instagram for marketing ideas; they go to LinkedIn to signal their own expertise

## Gender and Age Consumption Differences

Orin's observation on thumbnail and format differentiation:

**Male business content (YouTube):**
- Simple, minimal thumbnails: face + one or two words, clean background
- Lunchtime lean-back viewing
- Aspirational lifestyle documentation performs well

**Female content (YouTube):**
- Busy, multi-element thumbnails work better — "the ones with all the different stuff in the background, everything calling them out"
- Examples: Grace Beverly and pop culture female creators
- The complexity signals richness of content rather than cognitive overload

**Age-based consumption differentiation:**
- 20–27 year olds on YouTube: primary marketing education audience; will watch full episodes
- Same age group on TikTok: shorter form, entertainment-forward
- Executive/senior demographic (35+): Instagram casual scroll, LinkedIn professional mode

## Day-in-the-Life vs. Lunchtime Content

Orin's take on what's working vs. what's declining:

**Declining:** Traditional day-in-the-life (vlog) content
- "I think building in public for anything, especially if you're a fashion brand, is actually a negative signaler"
- "No one cares what I did in a day. They're there for the value."
- Only works for well-established brands and creators who have already earned episodic consumption

**Working:** Business + location hybrid (HGTV-meets-business)
- "I started a coffee shop" / "I'm opening a burger joint" content
- Combines the discovery mechanics of home improvement content with the business narrative
- Physical transformation as the visual hook

**Working:** Lunchtime lifestyle-aspirational (Represent, Minted model)
- Not technically day-in-the-life
- Gets into the "soul of what their customer wants to see"
- Functions as fitness/lifestyle inspiration that happens to be attached to a brand

## How to Apply This in Practice

**Step 1: Map your target audience's day**
When does the specific person you're trying to reach consume content? What device are they on? Are they in active scroll mode or lean-back mode? What's their emotional state at that moment?

**Step 2: Match format to consumption moment**
- Lean-back / TV / lunch → longer, visually rich, episodic content
- Commute / scroll / break → short, immediate hook, value in 60 seconds
- Professional / intent-driven → detailed framework, specific case study

**Step 3: Understand the platform's default consumption mode for your niche**
- Beauty on TikTok: casual scroll, entertainment expectation, tutorial or reaction format
- Marketing on YouTube: educational viewing session, 20+ minutes acceptable
- B2B on Instagram: casual executive discovery, Slack-share as the goal action

**Step 4: Build content around the consumption moment, not the format**
The question is not "should I make a vlog?" — it's "does a vlog fit into how my audience actually spends their Tuesday lunch?"

## The B2B Instagram Insight

The most counterintuitive finding Orin shares: for B2B creator content reaching VP and C-suite decision-makers, **Instagram is the highest-leverage platform** — not LinkedIn.

The reason: most executives are on Instagram in a casual browsing mode. They are discovering content the same way a consumer discovers it. When marketing content appears that's relevant to their professional responsibilities, they do what users always do: **they share it with their team in a Slack message or a DM.**

"Most executives of major companies — VP and C-level — are on Instagram casually browsing, and they are DMing to a group chat or to a Slack channel their teams being like 'why aren't we doing this?' — this is a common user action that happens all the time."

This is why marketing-niche videos with 3,000 views can generate multiple serious inquiries from major companies. The distribution is not algorithmic; it's social sharing from a decision-maker who discovered the content in leisure mode.
