# Concept: Enshittification

## Definition
Enshittification (also "insipidification") is the process by which products and brands gradually degrade in quality while prices rise, driven by the combination of mass production at scale, obfuscated supply chains, and the democratization of brand-starting that floods markets with underdeveloped goods. It is the era in which everything we buy increasingly sucks — and we know it, but feel trapped in it.

## Why It Matters in Marketing
Enshittification creates both a problem and an opportunity. The problem: consumer trust is collapsing, and every brand operates in a [[distrust-economy]] where buyers assume they're being deceived. The opportunity: any brand that genuinely shows what it is — its materials, its manufacturing, its honest trade-offs — stands out dramatically because the bar for transparency has fallen so low.

For marketers: the brands that survive and thrive in this era will be the ones that replace opacity with radical honesty, and replace inflated margin with genuine craft.

## The Five Mechanisms

### 1. Provenance Fraud
"Made in Italy" no longer means what it once did. The legal loophole: a product only needs to have its *last real procedure* completed in Italy to carry the label — it can be assembled and produced anywhere else. Italian factories hired migrants from China, Vietnam, and Burma to do the work, removed safety guards to stay within the minimum regulation, and the result is still "made in Italy." Dior, Valentino, Armani, and Loro Piana have all faced raids or charges related to this practice. The US is stricter — the majority of materials and production must happen domestically; Shinola had to remove "Made in Detroit" from its products. Italy has a "100% Made in Italy" designation that essentially no one uses. See [[provenance-fraud]] for the full legal mechanics.

### 2. Mass Production Killing Scarcity
Rolex produces over 1 million watches per year. There are more than 1 million Porsche 911s on the road — more than the adult residents of San Francisco (this excludes Cayennes, Macans, and other dilutive models). Objects that once signified rarity are now mass-produced psychological experiences. The luxury signal has been hollowed out: buying a Rolex in 2026 is a purely "psychologically influenced choice," not a function decision or an access-to-rarity signal.

### 3. The Everyone-Starts-a-Brand Problem
Democratized manufacturing and internet advertising have enabled millions of people to launch brands without deep product knowledge. Dropshippers, influencer brands, and fast e-commerce operations compete on ad performance, not product quality. This accelerated through Amazon marketplace economics — consumers searching for a product encounter the real brand, a lookalike, a knockoff, and Chinese variants, all competing on the arbitrage of demand at lowest cost. The result: the market fills with things that look good in ads and disappoint on arrival. Influencers promoting drop-shipped candy or generic clothing are not learning weights, finishes, fit patterns, or food science — they're learning Meta Ads.

### 4. Material Obfuscation and the Half-Education Trap
"100% cashmere" tells you almost nothing. Micron count, hair length, yarn spinning method — all determine whether a sweater is the $95 Zara version or the $12,000 Loro Piana. The internet taught consumers about GSM weight in t-shirts, but not about finishes — which can matter more than weight. A brand (Valuables) ran a 300 GSM shirt with a proprietary finish that made it feel completely different, and consumers had no language for what they were experiencing. The half-educated consumer is the most exploitable: they learned just enough to be misled by technically accurate but contextually empty labels.

**Polyester Smut:** A specific sub-pattern — brands like Comfy sell cheap synthetic materials (blankets, loungewear) engineered to feel maximally soft at the point of purchase while offering none of the long-term durability, breathability, or health profile of natural materials. Positioned slightly above mass market, slightly below luxury, the product is cheap material made to feel premium. Consumers may literally be inhaling synthetic particles while thinking they're treating themselves.

### 5. Material Arbitrage in Luxury
Prada sells bags at leather-equivalent prices while using nylon — a fraction of the material cost. Bottega Veneta actually buys exclusive-access leathers from tanneries that won't sell to most brands — the leather Bottega gets is physically unavailable to other buyers. The gap between honest luxury and marketing luxury has widened enormously. The brands that operate actual luxury (exclusive materials, genuine craft constraint) are in the minority.

## The China Quality Spectrum (Misunderstood)
China is not one thing. Two factory brothers: one runs a small artisan operation producing work that stands equal to any Portuguese or Italian factory — the author has personally sourced from these for projects. His brother's factory produces for Shein: not dramatically abusive, but chaotic, poorly managed, hot, staffed by people without craft expertise, producing crappy work. Both factories exist within miles of each other. The problem was consumers assumed a ceiling on China that didn't exist, and assumed a floor on the rest of the world that didn't exist. Both assumptions were wrong. Givenchy and Rimowa are made in Chinese factories the author has visited. The issue isn't country of origin — it's which factory, what standards, what craft culture.

## The Design Dupe Economy
A separate branch of enshittification: legal copying of designs. In the US, furniture is classified as a functional object, not a creative work — copyright protection is weak and designs (especially older ones) can be freely copied as long as they're not represented as the original. The EU protects designs for 70 years from the designer's death. Brands like Interior Icons systematically exploit US law to reproduce iconic designs (Togo sofa, Ultra Fragola mirror, Eames-style chairs) at fraction of price. The counterfeit/replica distinction: a counterfeit *pretends* to be the original (illegal); a replica copies the design without claiming to be the original (largely legal in the US). See [[design-dupe-economy]] for the full framework.

Real-time brand examples:
- **Blue Elephant** — dupe of Gentle Monster sunglasses; sued by Gentle Monster; captures the margin that Gentle Monster overcharges
- **Alohas** — makes accessible variants of Miu Miu and New Balance silhouettes at reasonable prices; built ecosystem around this position
- **Keens** — similar de-middleman positioning

The thesis: wherever a brand is massively overcharging, a "margin recapture" brand will emerge and grow. This is structural, not a trend.

## The Discovery Stack Shift
Product discovery is migrating from Google to AI (ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini AI summaries). AI systems surface Reddit and YouTube content heavily — in ChatGPT's own framing, Reddit and YouTube build results, not traditional PR or backlinks. Brands that seeded authentic discussion on those platforms gain AI visibility; brands that relied on paid search and programmatic ad spend are losing ground. Reddit itself is now heavily gamed — regular people are taking payments to post there — making it simultaneously the most SEO-powerful and least trustworthy network. See [[ai-seo-strategy]] and [[distrust-economy]].

## The Counter-Strategies

**The Show It Principle:** Whether made in Italy or China, show it. Film the artisans. If the factory is dark, show that honestly and document the improvement journey. If the cashmere is genuinely exceptional, explain the micron count, the hair length, the spinning process. If the product has an unusual finish, name it and describe what it does. Consumers can't feel what they haven't bought yet — the written and filmed description is the only bridge. See [[show-it-principle]].

**Expert-First Brand Building:** Before the internet lowered barriers, brand founders came with domain expertise — the example: Experiment beauty brand, founded by two organic chemists with genuine product innovation for Gen Z. In a world where product differentiation shows up in AI results and drives repeat purchase, domain expertise is the sustainable moat. The Meta Ads guru who taught you to hit $10K/month cannot teach you weights, finishes, cashmere grades, or tannery relationships. The brands that escape enshittification will care about product over economics.

**The De-Middleman Move:** Brands like Alohas and Blue Elephant are not enshittified — they're the opposite. They capture margin from overpriced originals, produce good product, and grow. This is a structural opportunity wherever legacy margin bloat exists.

## Examples in This Vault
- [[Supreme]] — built its brand on genuine subculture curation, now faces the enshittification risk of corporate ownership
- [[Balenciaga]] — uses spectacle to maintain mystique even as luxury broadly degrades
- [[RalphLauren]] — heritage brand that risks enshittification through mass licensing and scale
- [[Japanese Branding Philosophy]] — Japan's proximity to cheap manufacturing from China forced the development of craft-first brand strategies as the antidote
- [[David]] — anti-enshittification brand: product-surplus model, no compromise on formula, grew through genuine consumer surplus

## Key Takeaways
- Consumers in the distrust economy already assume they're being deceived — transparency is a differentiator, not a vulnerability
- "Show it": film the manufacturing, describe the materials with specificity, be honest about trade-offs. Don't let consumers feel ripped off after purchase.
- Half-education is the most dangerous consumer state — they know enough to ask about GSM but not enough to ask about finishes. Educate them fully or someone else will exploit the gap.
- The China quality spectrum is wider than most realize in both directions. Origin is not quality — factory culture is.
- The brands that will win the next generation are product-obsessed ones that care more about craft than margins.
- The counterfeit/replica distinction is legally important and commercially growing. Wherever a brand overcharges by 10x+, a replica/dupe brand will capture that margin.
- AI search is now a distribution channel — get into Reddit and YouTube conversations organically, because that's what AI surfaces.
