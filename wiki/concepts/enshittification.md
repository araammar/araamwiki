# Concept: Enshittification

## Definition
Enshittification (also "insipidification") is the process by which products and brands gradually degrade in quality while prices rise, driven by the combination of mass production at scale, obfuscated supply chains, and the democratization of brand-starting that floods markets with underdeveloped goods. It is the era in which everything we buy increasingly sucks — and we know it, but feel trapped in it.

## Why It Matters in Marketing
Enshittification creates both a problem and an opportunity. The problem: consumer trust is collapsing, and every brand operates in a "distrust economy" where buyers assume they're being deceived. The opportunity: any brand that genuinely shows what it is — its materials, its manufacturing, its honest trade-offs — stands out dramatically because the bar for transparency has fallen so low.

For marketers: the brands that survive and thrive in this era will be the ones that replace opacity with radical honesty, and replace inflated margin with genuine craft.

## How It Works

**The Luxury Lie:**
"Made in Italy" no longer means what it once did. Italian factories hired migrants to legally produce goods that carry the Italian provenance label (legally valid if the final procedure is done in Italy). Dior, Valentino, Armani have all faced raids or charges related to their supply chains. Loro Piana, often held up as a quiet luxury ideal, has been implicated in similar migrant labor exploitation. The brand story and the production reality have diverged.

**Mass Production Killing Scarcity:**
Rolex produces over 1 million watches per year. Porsche 911s number over 1 million on the road — more than the adult residents of San Francisco. Objects that once signified rarity are now mass-produced psychological experiences. The luxury signal has been hollowed out.

**The Everyone-Starts-a-Brand Problem:**
Democratized manufacturing and internet advertising have enabled millions of people to launch brands without deep product knowledge. Dropshippers, influencer brands, and fast e-commerce operations compete on ad performance, not product quality. The result: the market fills with things that look good in ads and are disappointing on arrival.

**Material Obfuscation:**
"100% cashmere" tells you almost nothing. The microns, the length of the hairs, the spinning process — all determine whether a cashmere sweater is a $95 Zara product or a $12,000 Loro Piana item, with no obvious signal to the consumer. GSM weights of t-shirts became popular consumer knowledge, but finishes — which may matter more — are invisible. The information asymmetry allows bad actors to profit from consumers who learned just enough to be misled.

**Material Arbitrage in Luxury:**
Prada sells bags at leather-equivalent prices while using nylon — a fraction of the material cost. Bottega Veneta actually buys exclusive-access leathers from tanneries that won't sell to most brands. The gap between honest luxury and marketing luxury has widened enormously.

**The Discovery Stack Shift:**
Product discovery is migrating from Google to AI (ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini). AI systems surface Reddit and YouTube content heavily. Brands that have seeded authentic discussion on those platforms gain AI visibility; brands that relied only on SEO and paid search are losing ground.

## Examples in This Vault
- [[Supreme]] — built its brand on genuine subculture curation, now faces the enshittification risk of corporate ownership
- [[Balenciaga]] — uses spectacle to maintain mystique even as luxury broadly degrades
- [[RalphLauren]] — heritage brand that risks enshittification through mass licensing and scale
- [[Japanese Branding Philosophy]] — Japan's proximity to cheap manufacturing from China forced the development of craft-first brand strategies as the antidote

## Key Takeaways
- Consumers in the distrust economy already assume they're being deceived — transparency is a differentiator, not a vulnerability
- "Show it": if your manufacturing is good, film it; if your materials are exceptional, describe them specifically; if you're honest about trade-offs, say so explicitly
- The brands that will win the next generation are product-obsessed ones that care more about craft than margins
- The distinction between counterfeit (pretends to be original) and replica (copies design legally without claiming to be the original) matters — both exist in gray zones consumers increasingly navigate with full awareness
- AI search is now a distribution channel — get into Reddit and YouTube conversations organically, because that's what AI surfaces
