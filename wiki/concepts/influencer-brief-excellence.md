# Concept: Influencer Brief Excellence — The Creative Brief as Campaign Infrastructure

## Summary / Definition

Most influencer campaigns fail not because of creator selection, but because of brief quality. A weak brief produces generic content that performs like an ad, not like an organic post. An excellent brief gives the creator exactly enough direction to integrate the brand authentically, leaves room for the creator's own voice and aesthetic, and includes creative elevation elements that raise production value without adding friction. The creative brief is the single highest-leverage document in an influencer campaign.

---

## What Makes a Great Brief

### 1. Visual References First
The most important brief element is not copy — it is references. Show the creator exactly what you want the content to look, feel, and perform like. Not a mood board for your aesthetic preferences; actual examples of content that has worked in similar contexts.

The reference is the brief's thesis statement: "make something like this, but yours."

### 2. Personalization Layer
A generic brief sent to 50 creators produces 50 generic videos. The best briefs are personalized to how the specific creator operates.

- Incorporate the creator's best-performing content type
- Reference their existing content style explicitly ("we know you do get-ready-with-me routines — do this as a GRWM")
- Show that you've watched their content and understand their audience

The Reformation model: "Go remake your best video ever, but you're wearing Reformation." The brief is a single sentence. The personalization is complete.

### 3. The "Send Us Your Vision" Opt-Out
The most counterproductive brief behavior is boxing in a creator so tightly that their authentic voice disappears. Excellent brief design includes explicit permission to push back:

"If you want to say no to the brief, okay — just send us something back that you're stoked on."

This creates two good outcomes: either the creator executes the brief (because they're comfortable with it) or they come back with a genuine creative interpretation that's probably better anyway.

### 4. Campaign Tie-In
The brief should connect to the larger campaign arc. An influencer video that's thematically isolated from the rest of the campaign misses the amplification that comes from campaign coherence. When influencer content echoes the same themes as the brand's organic content, paid ads, and retail activations, the cumulative impression is compound — the consumer sees the same idea from multiple vectors.

### 5. Width Without Looseness
A great brief is wide enough to preserve creator authenticity but specific enough to protect the brand's core message. The balance:
- **Protect:** what must be in the video (claim, product integration, key message)
- **Liberate:** how it is presented, the creative angle, the format, the creator's own jokes and hooks
- **Suggest:** elevation ideas the creator can accept or decline

## The Elevation Layer

The most underutilized brief element is what Orin calls "elevation" — the question of how to upgrade a working creative format before sending it out again.

Example scenario: a male UGC creator made a get-ready-with-me routine in their apartment that performed well as an ad.

**Flat approach:** replicate with a female creator, an older creator, a younger creator (demographic diversification only)

**Elevation approach:**
- Can you do it in public? On a subway? In a car?
- Can we give you a small extra budget to shoot in a luxury hotel room?
- Can we add a reflective jacket, a mirror vanity, a specific prop that elevates the visual?

The elevation question is: **for $400, how much can we improve this asset?**

One brand sent their top 10–20 creators an Osmos device alongside the product. The creator gets professional-grade equipment; the brand gets professional-grade content. The creative brief included "shoot with this."

## The Prop Box System

For ongoing influencer programs, Orin recommends building a permanent prop box: a curated list of sub-$100 items that reliably elevate video production value when sent to creators.

Examples:
- Vintage camcorder (unboxing B-roll, nostalgic aesthetic)
- Reflective jacket
- Mirror / vanity element
- Printer (for personalized branded inserts)
- Silly string (visual chaos = visual interest)
- Branded props that match the campaign concept

The prop box serves a dual purpose:
1. Elevates video production without requiring the creator to own or rent equipment
2. Creates visual consistency across a multi-creator campaign — all videos have a related prop element

## Gel Blaster Case Study (Cavell Agency)

One of Orin's clearest examples of brief excellence in action:

Context: 70-creator campaign for Gel Blaster, with a smaller creator making video game recreation content.

The brief element that changed the video:
- "Can you add video game sound effects?"
- "Can you add target overlays on the video? Here are the PNGs for the overlays."

The creator added these elements. The video went from ~200K views to ~2M views.

Orin's assessment: "When you need people that are in the weeds — it really comes down to your rep and the experience they have and how they treat influencers."

The lesson is not that this specific idea was brilliant — it's that the agency was obsessive enough about brief detail to think through the video at the execution level, not just the concept level.

## The Biggest Waste in Influencer Marketing

From Orin's direct experience:

**Bad pattern:** Large agencies go in, spend $12,000–$75,000 per creator, send briefs without the right CTAs, produce "bad posts," and report on outcomes while the brand has no visibility into whether the brief was the problem.

**Good pattern (small agencies):** Relentless attention to brief detail. Actual feedback on creator videos. Obsession with creator success, not just deal execution.

"The difference between 'just pay 75,000 bucks to the biggest person you can' and obsessing about the briefs — that's the difference between offense and results."

## UGC Brief Elevation (vs. Influencer Brief)

For UGC (non-creator, organic-style content for ad use), brief design follows the same principles but with an added emphasis on demographic expansion:

- Start with a winning male format → replicate for female
- Iterate across age groups: older / younger
- Iterate across demographics: different backgrounds, skin tones, body types
- Available market of the ad expands with each demographic variation

The brief for a UGC program should specify not just creative direction but the **diversity matrix** — which demos have been covered, which haven't.

## What to Steal / Application

**For brands running influencer campaigns:**
1. Lead every brief with three reference videos that capture what success looks like
2. Personalize at least one element of the brief to each creator's actual work
3. Include an explicit opt-out: "if this doesn't feel right for you, send us your take"
4. Add a $400–500 elevation budget per creator (prop, location upgrade, equipment)
5. Build a prop box of sub-$100 items that can be sent with any product shipment
6. Ask yourself: "What's the one execution detail that turns 200K views into 2M?" — then put it in the brief

**For agencies:**
- Brief obsession is a competitive moat; it's not common
- Small agencies with better briefs beat large agencies with bigger budgets
- The rep metric that matters: do creators enjoy working with you enough to do their best work?
