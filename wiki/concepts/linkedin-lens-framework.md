# Concept: The Lens Framework

## Summary
A "lens" is a fixed expertise filter applied consistently to everything that happens in your industry. It permanently solves the "what do I post?" problem for employees and brand contributors on LinkedIn by turning external events — campaign launches, brand moves, product releases, industry news — into infinite, pre-justified content prompts. The lens approach makes posting about the industry rather than about yourself, which removes ego friction and creates consistent professional positioning over time.

## What a Lens Is

Every professional already has a lens — they just haven't named it or consciously applied it to LinkedIn.

A lens is your specific area of professional expertise applied as a filter to anything notable that happens in your space. It is not a topic list. It is a perspective.

Examples from Orin John:
- **Art direction lens**: When any brand releases a campaign, you analyze the art direction — what they did well, what they missed, why certain creative choices work
- **Meta ads lens**: When a brand makes a move, you pull their actual ads and evaluate the copy, creative, targeting logic
- **Copywriter's lens**: You read launch headlines and email subject lines through a copywriting practitioner's eye and break down what works
- **Videographer's lens**: You watch branded content and evaluate production choices, pacing, shot selection
- **Salesperson's lens**: You observe a brand's customer experience or pitch flow and decode the sales mechanics
- **Accountant's lens / Analyst's lens**: You look at funding news, pricing changes, or market moves through a financial or data lens

The lens is not "I talk about marketing." It is "When anything marketing-related happens, here is the specific angle I bring to it." The specificity is the value.

## How It Works in Practice

The mechanism is simple and repeatable:

1. **Something happens in your industry** — a brand launches a campaign, a competitor makes a move, a new tool drops, industry news breaks
2. **Filter it through your lens** — ask what this event looks like from your specific area of expertise
3. **Post the observation** — "Here's what happened. Here's what I see through my [expertise] lens. Here's why it's notable / good / worth watching."

Real example from Orin: "Road launches a new campaign. Hey, this new Road campaign launched. I looked at their two meta ads and these are the ones I think are the best and this is why they did it well. That is a lens that you get to apply."

The content structure is: external event + your expertise filter + your specific take. That's it.

## Why It Solves the Posting Problem Permanently

Industry events never stop happening. Campaigns launch, brands pivot, products release, market conditions shift, cultural moments occur. If your lens is defined, every one of these events is automatically a content opportunity.

You never have to start from "what should I post about?" You start from "what happened this week in my industry?" and your lens tells you exactly how to engage with it.

This is the structural fix to the most common LinkedIn barrier: not cringe-aversion, not time, but the blank-page problem of having no system for deciding what's worth saying.

## Why the Lens Approach Works Socially

The lens framing solves a social problem as much as a content problem.

Posting about yourself on LinkedIn triggers an internalized social risk: "People are going to think I'm becoming a LinkedIn person." The lens approach sidesteps this entirely because you're not posting about yourself — you're posting about your industry.

The framing, as Orin describes it: "I'm sharing the things I'm learning in my industry. I'm trying to connect with more people so I can better be better at my job and better promote the brand that I work for."

This is socially acceptable, professionally defensible, and genuinely useful to your network. The "posting on LinkedIn now?" embarrassment that stops most employees never activates because the lens content has obvious justification built into it.

## Why It Creates Consistent Positioning

The compounding effect of a consistent lens is positioning.

When you apply the same expertise filter to enough events over enough time — weeks and months of "here's what I see as a meta ads buyer / copywriter / art director / analyst" — your network develops a clear association between you and that expertise. You become the person in your industry who thinks rigorously about [your lens area].

This is professional positioning without self-promotion. It is demonstrated competence through repeated public application of your knowledge to real events. It is more credible and more specific than a bio that claims expertise without showing it.

## The Company Cohort Effect and the Lens

When multiple employees at the same company post through their individual lenses, a secondary effect activates: the internal team forms an engagement pod that is organic, algorithmically blessed, and free.

If the art director, the copywriter, the strategist, and the media buyer all post lens content and engage with each other's posts, LinkedIn's algorithm reads this as strong organizational signal. Internal engagement on posts that tag the brand page amplifies reach significantly. A team of five people each posting once a week through their lens, commenting genuinely on each other's content, outperforms most brand accounts.

## The Social-First Onboarding Protocol

Before posting anything, Orin's recommended protocol for warming up the LinkedIn algorithm:

**3 to 5 genuine comments per week on other people's content before your first post.**

Not AI-generated responses. Not emoji reactions. Actual substantive comments that engage with the content. LinkedIn tracks social behavior as a variable in how widely it distributes your eventual posts — building that signal before you start posting means your first posts benefit from algorithmic credibility you've already earned.

The protocol:
1. Connect with relevant people in your industry
2. For 2 to 4 weeks: comment 3 to 5 times per week on content you genuinely find interesting
3. Begin posting lens content after social behavior is established
4. Continue commenting and engaging alongside posting — the social activity compounds reach indefinitely

## Posting Cadence With a Lens

The lens resolves the frequency question too. The recommended entry cadence:

- **Month 1 minimum**: One contribution post per month (what you worked on, what you learned)
- **With a lens added**: One lens post per week becomes achievable and sustainable without burnout
- **Long-term rhythm**: Contribution posts monthly + lens posts weekly + cheerleading posts as needed (see [[the-steve-role]] for the cheerleading layer)

The lens is what makes weekly posting sustainable. Without a lens, weekly posting requires inventing new topics constantly. With a lens, weekly posting just requires paying attention to your industry — which professionals do automatically.

## What to Steal
Name your lens today. Write one sentence: "When anything happens in [your industry], I look at it through the lens of [your specific expertise]." Then post it once. From that point forward, every piece of industry news is a content brief. The lens converts external events into professional positioning without requiring you to create anything from scratch or talk about yourself. One lens post per week for a year builds a professional reputation that no amount of resume-polishing can replicate.
