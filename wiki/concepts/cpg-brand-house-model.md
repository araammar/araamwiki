# Concept: CPG Brand House Model

## Summary / Definition
Operating multiple distinct CPG brands under a single holding company structure, where each brand is a legally separate entity but shares distribution relationships, financial infrastructure, operator expertise, and manufacturing leverage. Dom Iacovone is the American CEO of the Quality Group — a $1.7B holding company with RAW Nutrition, Bum Energy, 3D Energy, Cadence, Revive MD, Relive Health, Onside, and Eterna as portfolio companies.

The brand house model allows each brand to be unique and independent while benefiting from the operational scale and relationship capital of the parent.

## Why It Matters
Running multiple brands isn't just about diversification — it's a flywheel. Distribution relationships built with one brand open doors for the next. A retail buyer who trusts you with Bum Energy will take a meeting for Onside. Manufacturing scale reduces COGS across the portfolio. Executive talent hired for RAW can advise 3D Energy.

Dom: *"The strategy is always the best in CPG — once you get to the Walmarts, the Targets, the Costcos, it's very easy to shift vertically and say, 'Hey, we have this. Hey, we have this.'"*

## How It Works / The Mechanism

**Portfolio structure (Quality Group / Dom's companies):**

Under Quality Group (institutional PE via CVC):
- **RAW Nutrition** — sports nutrition (protein, pre-workout, creatine)
- **Bum Energy** — lifestyle energy drink (positioned away from fitness)
- **ESN** — European equivalent of RAW (German parent brand)
- **MORE Nutrition** — also under Quality Group Europe

Dom's personal portfolio (separate from Quality Group, separate entities):
- **3D Energy** — blue-collar/value energy drink (~$1.50/can)
- **Cadence** — sports nutrition
- **Revive MD** — vitamins / health supplements
- **Relive Health** — franchise health/hormone optimization clinics
- **Onside** — men's personal care (body/face/hair)
- **Eterna** — premium stem cell / regenerative medicine clinic

**The isolation principle:**
Every brand is a separate company. This is critical for:
- Exit optionality: can sell each brand independently
- Liability isolation: a brand problem doesn't contaminate the whole portfolio
- Team clarity: each brand has its own CEO/GM who owns that P&L
- Investor clarity: each brand has its own financials, its own story, its own multiple

**The distribution flywheel:**
Once you're in Target and Walmart as Brand A, you have credibility and relationships to bring Brand B to the same buyers. You're not starting from zero — you're leveraging an established trust account.

Dom used Bum Energy's retail relationships to get 3D Energy into Dollar General, Dollar Tree, QuickTrip, and HEB. He used his Vitamin Shoppe relationship from RAW to get 3D into specialty retail.

**The athlete/influencer flywheel:**
An influencer signed to RAW can be introduced to Bum Energy. An athlete who's already building personal brand on behalf of one brand becomes a distribution multiplier for another. Dom can offer a single talent relationship two or three brand deals simultaneously — creating leverage for him and income for the talent.

**The anti-cannibalization mandate:**
Running multiple brands in the same category requires strict non-cannibalization planning:
- Bum Energy: lifestyle/mass energy drink ($3.50, premium functional)
- 3D Energy: blue-collar/value energy drink ($1.50, basic)
- These two target completely different segments and price points; they don't compete

Separation of teams is the operational solution: each brand has its own team with its own focus. Dom explicitly points to the cautionary tale of brands that launch an energy drink alongside their powder business and neglect the powder business until they've lost category leadership. C4 lost the #1 pre-workout position on Amazon to RAW because Nutrabolt was focused on C4 Energy.

**The flywheel for services (Revive → Relive):**
Revive MD (vitamins) builds brand trust and loyalty with consumers aged 18–35. When those consumers turn 35 and need hormone replacement therapy and concierge health services, they go to Relive — because Revive has been their trusted brand for 15 years.

Dom won't sell Revive separately from Relive. They compound together.

**The multiplier reality:**
- Supplement/CPG brand multiples: varies by scale and channel mix
- Franchise multiples: ~30x EBITDA (Orange Theory founder validated Relive's financials and understood the economics)
- Beauty/personal care multiples: extremely high (Onside — a natural strategic acquisition target for Estee Lauder, Unilever, P&G)
- Regenerative medicine (Eterna): emerging category, potentially massive with Florida's regulatory advantage

## What to Steal / Application
- **Keep every brand as a separate legal entity.** Shared infrastructure is fine; shared equity is not.
- **Design non-competing positioning for every brand in your portfolio.** Bum vs. 3D is a masterclass in this.
- **Leverage distribution relationships across brands.** Every new brand you launch should benefit from the buyer relationships of the brands before it.
- **Design for exit at the brand level, not the holding company level.** Buyers want one clean story, not a bundle.
- **The services/franchise model alongside CPG is underexplored.** A product brand and a service brand serving the same consumer lifecycle is a compounding trust flywheel.
