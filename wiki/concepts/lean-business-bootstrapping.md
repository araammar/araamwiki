# Concept: Lean Business Bootstrapping

## Summary / Definition
Running a business with no debt, minimal overhead, and a distribution model (vs. salary model) from day one — so that all available cash is reinvested into growth and the P&L is maximally clean for future fundraising or exit. Dom Iacovone and Matt Jansen scaled RAW Nutrition to nearly $200M/year without taking on any debt and without taking salaries — only distributions when the business could afford them.

This philosophy was taught to Dom by his mother, who ran Relive Health's operations and who learned operational discipline working from cashier to SVP of Operations at BJ's Wholesale Club.

## Why It Matters
Founders who "live through the company" — running personal expenses, inflating salaries, taking distributions constantly — damage their exit value and make it harder to raise capital. More importantly, lean operations force the discipline that creates durable businesses.

Dom's mom's lesson: *"She taught me from a very early age how to run a lean business to make real money. And that to me is the most important thing."*

## How It Works / The Mechanism

**The distribution vs. salary model:**
In the early stages, Dom and Matt took zero salary. They took distributions — meaning when the company had enough cash flow to sustain several months of operations, they took a small amount out.

As the business grew, distributions grew. This approach:
- Keeps the P&L clean (no inflated owner salaries inflating SGNA)
- Keeps cash in the business for reinvestment
- Avoids personal tax structure complexity
- Leaves maximum EBITDA visible to future buyers

*"Salaries and all that shit really shouldn't come to fruition until... We really didn't start taking salaries until we sold."*

**The 20% EBITDA reinvestment principle:**
Dom's philosophy on cash management: maintain 20% EBITDA. Everything above 20% should be reinvested into the business to fuel growth. If a business has 40–50% EBITDA and isn't growing, the founder is taking too much out rather than compounding the asset.

*"If your company has 40-50% EBITDA and it's not growing, then you need to just keep reinvesting that back in to make it grow."*

Once the growth plateau is reached and reinvestment no longer produces returns, then you start taking cash out.

**SGNA as a lean indicator:**
Dom's SGNA target: 4% of revenue. At the build phase it was 6–8%. AI is pushing it toward 4% and lower. This is significantly below industry average — made possible by:
- Lean hiring (hire for hunger under $100M, no bloat)
- AI tools replacing administrative overhead
- Operational discipline on every overhead category

**Bootstrapping RAW to $200M:**
No venture capital. No bank debt. No investors. Just reinvested cash flow and operational discipline. This is the exception in CPG, not the rule — most brands raise angel/VC capital in the first $5–10M.

The benefit: when Dom did sell, he sold from a position of strength, not from a fundraising-forced timeline. The deal terms were on his terms.

**The warehouse lesson:**
Dom discovered that RAW's South Florida warehouse location was costing them 7 figures annually in excess inbound freight costs (every truck had to drive to the bottom corner of the country and then back). A heat map analysis of logistics routes revealed the problem. Moving the warehouse next to the manufacturer (eliminating all inbound freight) and eventually centralizing the warehouse in the Midwest (reducing all outbound freight) are expected to produce multiple 7-figure savings annually.

This is what lean operations looks like at scale: relentless attention to cost structure even when the business is profitable and growing.

## What to Steal / Application
- **Take distributions, not salary, as long as the business can sustain itself.** Treat it as deferred compensation that compounds in the business.
- **Run no debt if possible.** Debt restricts your exit options and adds a fixed cost to a business that needs variable cost structure.
- **SGNA under 6%** is strong. Under 4% is exceptional. The delta between 6% and 4% on $100M revenue is $2M/year in profit — that's $20–40M in exit value on a 10–20x multiple.
- **Track logistics costs with the same rigor as COGS.** Warehouse location, inbound freight, and outbound freight are massive levers that most founders ignore.
- **"Run it lean when you're building, not when you're winning."** Early-stage lean creates the discipline that persists even when cash is easy.
