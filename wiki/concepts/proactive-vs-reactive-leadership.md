# Concept: Proactive vs. Reactive Leadership

## Summary / Definition
Dom Iacovone's single most important hiring and organizational filter: **no reactive people in the company.** Every team member — from the CFO to the warehouse — must operate proactively (anticipating problems before they occur) rather than reactively (responding to problems after they've already happened).

Reactive identification of problems is, by definition, too late.

## Why It Matters
The difference between a proactive and reactive organization is the difference between catching a fire before it starts and fighting it after it's burning. In a multi-brand holding company where no single leader can be in every room, reactive orgs get destroyed by problems that a proactive org would never let develop.

Dom: *"When reactivity is the number one way you identify problems, that's too late every time."*

## How It Works / The Mechanism

**The proactive filter in hiring:**
Dom hires people he describes as "entrepreneurs inside companies." They are:
- Constantly scanning for problems coming down the pipe
- Bringing solutions, not just problem reports
- Making decisions within their domain without waiting for direction
- Acting as the CEO of their team — setting direction for those below them

The opposite: people who wait to be told what to do, only raise issues when they've become crises, and look to leadership for answers rather than generating their own.

**Proactive applied to finance:**
Dom: *"Finance sees everything first."* His Monday morning finance meetings are designed to surface leading indicators — not lagging outcomes. CM1 being slightly off target is visible Monday. If you don't have this cadence, you find out at month-end when there's nothing you can do.

**Proactive applied to operations:**
The ops/SNOP meeting (Sales and Operations Planning) is a mid-week, high-level overview of everything happening in the company. It exists so the leadership team can see problems 2–4 weeks out, not the week they hit.

**Proactive applied to logistics:**
Discovering that South Florida was the worst possible warehouse location for a national brand wasn't obvious until someone looked at the freight heat map and analyzed the problem systematically. A reactive team would have continued paying excess freight costs indefinitely. A proactive operations person identified the structural inefficiency and fixed it for a 7-figure annual saving.

**The proactive team as the multi-company enabler:**
Dom runs 10+ companies simultaneously because each company's leadership team is proactive. They don't bring him fires — they bring him summaries. He can check in weekly because problems are being anticipated and handled before they escalate.

**The healthy argument culture:**
Proactive leadership cultures have debates. Dom explicitly values C-suite disagreement: *"I think a healthy argument between a C-suite is a very valuable thing. If everyone's just not talking or following blindly, it's no good for anybody."*

The opposite of proactive isn't just reactive — it's also passive. Yes-men who nod at everything the founder says are not proactive. They're not thinking about the problem — they're managing the founder's comfort.

## What to Steal / Application
- **Make "proactive" a filter in every interview.** Ask for examples of problems they identified and solved before they were assigned to fix them.
- **Build cadences that surface leading indicators** (weekly finance meetings, ops reviews, stagegate product meetings). These are the organizational infrastructure of proactivity.
- **When you catch a proactive person doing the right thing, amplify it publicly.** This models the behavior for the rest of the team.
- **Tolerate mistakes from proactive people** — they're learning through action, which is the right kind of error. Penalize reactive behavior more than proactive mistakes.
- **The CEO's role: protect space for proactivity.** If you're making all the decisions, your team has no reason to think ahead.
