# Concept: Cohort Business Model

## Summary / Definition

A cohort-based course is a time-limited, recurring educational offer run on a quarterly cadence. It is Dan Koe's preferred business model for creator-entrepreneurs, specifically for solo or small operators, because it creates real scarcity, sustainable intensity rhythms, and direct income without the ongoing operational overhead of a permanent community.

**Core mechanics:**
- 8-week program, run once per quarter (4x per year)
- Enrollment opens for a specific window, then closes
- Live calls baked in during the cohort
- Income is chunked and launch-dependent, not subscription-drip

## Why It Matters

Most creator monetization advice pushes two models: the always-on community (subscription) or the evergreen course (passive income). Both have structural problems for solo operators that the cohort model avoids.

**Problems with always-on communities:** Recurring revenue = recurring work. The community becomes more active and valuable as it grows, which means it demands more of the creator's time exactly when they'd want to be doing less. At a large enough scale, you're running a community management operation, not a creative business.

**Problems with evergreen courses:** Lower conversion rates (no urgency), lower engagement (students don't feel accountable), and a passive-income fantasy that rarely materializes at meaningful scale.

The cohort model solves both by building genuine scarcity into the offer architecture.

## The Key Advantages

**Real scarcity, not manufactured scarcity**
Cohorts open and close on actual dates. You enroll or you don't get in. There is no ethical version of "only 3 spots left!" — the scarcity is structural and true. This encourages enrollment without requiring manipulative framing.

**Seasonal intensity rhythms**
Running a cohort 4 times per year bakes predictable seasons of intense activity into your calendar. You know when you'll be launching hard, when you'll be delivering, and when you'll have creative recovery time between cohorts. This is a fundamentally better operating rhythm for a creator than the "always-on" grind.

**Community quality and leverage**
Because cohort participants are all in the same temporal window, they form a real peer group. The live calls create genuine accountability. The time-bound container increases the perceived value and the actual transformation rate.

**Forced launch discipline**
If the cohort model is your primary income source, you have to launch well every quarter. This creates a recurring forcing function for marketing excellence — you can't coast on an evergreen funnel that's slowly dying.

## The Community Pricing Ladder (Dan Koe's Case Study)

For the Modern Mastery community, Koe used a deliberate tiered pricing approach to build from 0 members to 3,000:

1. **$7/month** — get the first 150 people in; prove the concept, generate early testimonials
2. **$14.99/month** — price increase creates urgency for the existing audience to join before the window closes
3. **$20/month** — next tier; grandfather existing members at their original rate
4. **$27/month** — final tier; regular marketing uses "join before the price increases again"

**The grandfather rate as retention mechanic:** Members who cancel lose their original rate — they re-enter at the current (higher) price. This dramatically reduces churn. Canceling becomes costly, not just inconvenient.

**The $5 first-month trial:** At $27/month, Koe ran periodic promotions (tied to YouTube videos and newsletters) offering the first month for $5. People join at zero commitment, experience the value, and convert to full price. The low trial removes the barrier to entry without devaluing the ongoing subscription.

**Key lesson:** Set the starting price low enough to generate momentum and testimonials. Do NOT start high and try to lower it — you can't bring the price down without upsetting existing members. Build up.

## What Breaks Community Models

Recurring revenue means recurring work. The trap that catches many creators:

- Community grows → demands more active management → creator's time is consumed → quality of core creative work suffers
- The northstar check: "Is the quality of my primary work (writing, content, etc.) suffering because of this?" If yes, the community has grown past the sustainable operating point for a solo creator.

Koe's diagnostic: his northstar was always writing quality. When the community started pulling him away from writing, he knew something needed to be restructured.

## What to Steal / Application

- If you're building an educational offer as a solo creator, start with the cohort model rather than the evergreen course or always-on community.
- Launch the first cohort at a lower price than you think is right. Get people in. Get testimonials. Raise the price for the next cohort.
- Build quarterly launches into your content calendar. Use each launch period as a marketing intensity burst.
- Grandfather existing members to build long-term retention that doesn't depend on the community getting more active.
- Monitor your northstar: what is the core creative work that everything else serves? When the business model starts degrading that work, restructure.
