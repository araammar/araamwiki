# Concept: Unique Process as AI Moat

## Summary / Definition

Your manually-developed process for how you do your work is the primary asset that makes AI useful — and the primary moat against AI commoditization.

Anyone can ask AI to "write a viral YouTube script." The output is average because AI is drawing from all available knowledge to produce something generic. But if you have developed your own distinct process for creating YouTube scripts over years of manual practice, you can feed that process into AI — and get output that reflects your unique method, not the average of the internet.

The core insight: **AI amplifies your process. It cannot create a process you don't have.** People who rely on AI before developing their own process will always produce generic output and will never learn to adapt when AI's default answer isn't enough.

## Why It Matters

The AI era creates two classes of operators:
1. **Doers** — people who use AI to execute tasks they could not do themselves, producing generic output
2. **Orchestrators** — people who have developed their own process manually, encoded it into AI via detailed prompts/SOPs, and now use AI to execute that process at scale

The orchestrator gets 10x leverage from AI. The doer gets marginal convenience from AI. The gap between them is: manual practice first.

This also explains why being able to "do things the manual way without AI" will be more valuable in the AI era, not less. If you can do it manually, you can teach AI to do it your way. If you can only do it with AI, you're permanently dependent on AI's generic defaults.

## The Mechanism: Prompt as Employee

Dan Koe's framing: "A prompt is the new employee."

A well-constructed prompt encodes:
- The task to be performed
- The principles and standards that govern quality
- The voice, style, and constraints specific to your work
- What *not* to do (as important as what to do)
- Examples of what excellent output looks like

Once you have prompts like this — built from your own manually-developed process — you are orchestrating. The prompts run the tasks. You run the prompts.

**The development path for a prompt:**
1. Do the task manually until you're excellent at it
2. Start with a basic prompt; the output is mediocre
3. Get more specific; add your principles
4. Add examples; add what not to do
5. Gradually the prompt grows until it almost matches your manual output
6. Continue refining — it will only get better as models improve

Koe's own case: a ~2,000-word set of instructions for how to write tweets. Started as a short prompt. Over time, became a comprehensive guide that produces tweets that almost match his style.

## The Expert Extraction Method

You don't have to develop every process manually yourself. A shortcut:

1. Find someone who has mastered the task you need done (landing pages, ad copy, scripts, whatever)
2. Gather examples of their best work
3. Paste into AI with the prompt: "Break down exactly why this works. What are the psychological tactics? The core principles? Create a mega guide on how to replicate this step by step."
4. AI produces a comprehensive guide
5. Turn that guide into a prompt that interviews you for all the context it needs, then produces the output

Result: you've captured 5 years of expertise without the 5 years. You can now use AI to execute at that expert's level for your specific context. You didn't copy their work — you extracted the principles behind their work and encoded them into a system.

## Why Generic AI Output Fails

"Anyone can go into AI right now and just ask 'write me a viral YouTube script' and that may get them some kind of result, but they didn't earn that knowledge or experience."

The problem: there is no one right way to create excellent YouTube content. Alex Hormozi's process is different from Ali Abdal's process is different from a gaming YouTuber's process. All are valid. None is the generic default. AI's generic default is a blend that matches none of them perfectly.

If you have your own earned process, you can encode it and AI produces something that matches your process specifically — which is far more valuable than the generic blend.

## Related to AI Arbitrage Window

The people who develop their unique process during the current AI arbitrage window (2026-2028) — who go hard on building manually before automating — will be the orchestrators. Those who skip the manual phase will be permanently operating on AI's generic defaults, which is a ceiling, not a floor.

## What to Steal / Application

- Before reaching for AI to do any task, ask: "Do I understand this task well enough to teach it to someone else?" If no, do it manually until you do.
- After you've developed your manual skill, start building your prompt. Treat it as a living document that gets refined over time.
- Use the expert extraction method to accelerate learning in domains adjacent to your core skill — get the principles without the years.
- The prompt library is a genuine business asset. As it grows, it represents accumulated knowledge that compounds.
- Test prompts rigorously. If AI gives you the default mediocre answer, the prompt needs to be more specific — add more constraints, add examples, add what not to do.
