# Concept: The Creative Toolkit — Bookmark Organization as Competitive Infrastructure

## Summary

Most creators bookmark content and never touch it again. Orin John has spent years building what he calls his "creative toolkit" — a master Notion database that turns every saved reference into a queryable, categorized, actionable library. The result is that when a campaign brief lands on his desk, he can surface dozens of proven references in seconds instead of spending hours re-finding things he's already seen. The toolkit is the operational backbone that makes fast, high-quality creative strategy possible at scale.

## The System

Every Sunday, Orin processes everything he's bookmarked during the week — Instagram saves, Twitter bookmarks, Safari links — and logs them into a master Notion doc with the following fields:

**What is this for?** A selector tag: campaign, launch, tool, organic, ads, strategy. This is the primary filter that makes the database useful rather than just big.

**Notes:** Context on why this was saved and what's interesting about it.

**Link:** The direct reference.

**Tried / Not Tried:** A binary column indicating whether he's actually executed on this reference or it's still experimental. This allows him to separate proven winners from things he wants to test — critical when briefing clients or building a new campaign.

The database is now years old, which means it contains hundreds of references per category. When building a launch strategy, he filters by "launch content" and instantly has a library of real examples to pull from.

## Why This Beats Standard Bookmarking

Standard bookmarking (Instagram saves, browser favorites) has no retrieval layer. You save something, it disappears into a list, you never find it again. The Notion system adds: category filtering, status tracking (tried vs untried), and notes that preserve the original context of why you saved something. It transforms passive saving into active reference building.

The "tried it / haven't tried it" column is the underrated key — it's the difference between having references and having a tested playbook. A client facing a launch can be told "here are 40 references for launch content, and here are the 12 I've personally run and know work."

## The Feed Engineering Layer

Separate from the bookmark system, Orin runs a deliberate feed engineering practice using multiple devices and accounts:

- Two phones, multiple accounts per phone
- Each account follows only creators and brands in a specific niche (beauty, fashion, personal brand, etc.)
- Scroll time is time-blocked: dedicated 30-minute sessions, 2-3x per week per niche
- One session per week reserved for personal consumption — other gurus, different niches, personal library building
- Scroll speed is deliberately varied: faster scrolling trains the algorithm toward shorter content, slower and more complete viewing pulls longer content

The goal is to build feeds that mirror a target customer's actual feed — what influencers do they follow, what brands appear, what content dominates. This is competitive intelligence disguised as scrolling.

## The Relevancy Problem It Solves

Orin left his executive role (12-14 person team, $100M revenue targets) and discovered within six months that his content had lost its edge. He wasn't in the mix anymore, so he couldn't speak with authority about things he was no longer experiencing. He went back to working part-time specifically to stay current — not for the income, but for the content sharpness. The toolkit and feed engineering system are how he maintains that sharpness without needing to be in a full-time role.

## What to Steal

Start the Notion bookmark doc this Sunday. Every bookmark you process goes in with a category tag and a one-line note on why you saved it. Add the tried/untried column from day one — it's what makes the database a strategy tool rather than a junk drawer. Set up at least one dedicated niche-research account on TikTok and Instagram; follow nothing else on it. Block 30 minutes twice a week to scroll only that account.
