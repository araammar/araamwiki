# Concept: DSD vs. Direct-to-Retail

## Summary / Definition
Two fundamentally different models for getting a CPG (especially beverage) product onto retail shelves. **Direct-to-Retail (DTR):** the brand sells directly to the retailer's distribution center; the retailer's own employees stock the shelves. **Direct Store Distribution (DSD):** the brand sells to independent distributors who own trucks and fill the shelves themselves — multiple times per day if needed.

Dom Iacovone explains this distinction in the context of Bum Energy's nationwide Walmart/Target rollout.

## Why It Matters
The difference in shelf velocity between DSD and direct is enormous. DSD distributors are incentivized to fill shelves because they own the inventory and sell it to the retailer. DTR relies on a Walmart employee who is not incentivized to restock your product four times a day.

For high-velocity beverage brands, direct-to-retail creates a structural bottleneck: the shelf goes empty and stays empty, suppressing reported velocity — which then threatens your placement.

## How It Works / The Mechanism

**Direct-to-Retail (DTR):**
- Brand sells to retailer's DC (distribution center)
- Retailer ships to individual stores
- Retailer employee stocks the shelf
- Brand gets a "pog" — a designated 3-product spot on the shelf
- Pro: speed to market, no distributor margin to give up
- Con: shelf replenishment is slow, shelf maintenance is zero, no ability to place extra displays

**DSD (Direct Store Distribution):**
- Brand sells to a DSD (e.g., a Pepsi house, Anheuser-Busch house, Coca-Cola house)
- DSD owns the trucks and delivers directly to individual stores
- DSD employees fill shelves multiple times per day
- DSD can place **free-standing displays, end caps, cold coolers near checkout** — massively expanding shelf presence beyond the pog
- Pro: 2–6x more in-store placements per location, maintained shelves, DSD incentivized to maximize velocity
- Con: lower margin for the brand (DSD takes a cut), harder to get a DSD partnership without an established brand

**The velocity math:**
Switching from DTR to DSD can produce 200–500% more shelf placements per store. For a high-velocity product, this translates directly to proportionally higher sales.

**The chicken-and-egg problem:**
- You need a strong brand for a DSD to say yes (they buy the inventory and bear the risk)
- You need DSD to build the brand past a certain velocity ceiling
- Dom's solution: launch DTR first to prove velocity, then pitch DSDs

**The Bum Energy story:**
1. Launched Bum Energy directly into Walmart DTR first
2. Proved velocity with the DTR model
3. Transitioned to DSD (nationwide rollout)
4. Now doing a "heartbeat" chart — sells out fast when DSD truck arrives, then sits empty until the next delivery
5. Solution: build a 100-person field team by end of 2026 to sit with DSDs, hold them accountable, and educate them on the brand's value

**DSD incentive management:**
DSDs carry multiple brands on their trucks. For them to prioritize your brand:
- Build strong relationships with DSD owners/managers
- Take them to dinner, show up at their operations
- Run contests (who sells the most gets a trip, prizes, etc.)
- For non-alcoholic beverages: all incentives are legally permissible. Alcoholic beverages have restrictions.

**The instacart problem (DTR failure mode):**
Dom describes Walmart DTR: Walmart employee stocks shelf at 7am. Instacart comes through and clears the entire shelf. Shelf sits empty the rest of the day. No one restocks it. Velocity appears flat or falling — which is actually demand exceeding supply with no replenishment system. DSD solves this.

## What to Steal / Application
- **Start DTR to get into the door.** You need some velocity data to attract a DSD partner.
- **Move to DSD as fast as your velocity justifies it.** The shelf presence multiplier is too powerful to ignore.
- **Build a field team once you're DSD.** DSDs need to be managed and incentivized — they won't prioritize you by default.
- **For non-alcoholic beverages: use every incentive available.** Sales contests, trips, cash bonuses for top DSDs. It's legal and it works.
- **Model the margin trade-off.** DSD takes margin, but the velocity increase usually more than compensates.
