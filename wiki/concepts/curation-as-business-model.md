# Concept: Curation as Business Model

## Summary / Definition
For people with genuine taste and domain expertise, building a curated store — physical or online — is often a more viable and better-matched business than building an original brand. Curation as a business model means acquiring, assembling, and presenting inventory from multiple sources under a cohesive aesthetic or cultural point of view. The curator does not manufacture; they filter. The taste *is* the product.

This concept is distinct from curation-as-marketing (The Row's Instagram curation strategy, hotel soap introductions) and is specifically about building a business around the act of selecting and presenting things for others.

## Why It Matters
The internet has pushed a default narrative onto creative people: "You have taste and an audience — go build a brand." But brand-building requires a skill set most creators don't have: manufacturing relationships, inventory management, cash flow, product development, returns, fulfillment. What many creators are actually good at — and what the internet has democratized the ability to monetize — is having a trustworthy point of view about *what is worth buying*.

The shift: taste has become a monetizable asset in its own right, separate from production capability. The assumption that the next step after "audience" is "brand" skips over a potentially better-fit business model: owning the role of trusted curator.

## How It Works / The Mechanism

**What curation-first businesses look like:**
- They stock and sell products from multiple makers/brands under a unified aesthetic vision
- The store's identity — not any single product's brand — is the primary value proposition
- Customers come for the filter, not for any individual item

**Why the model is viable:**
- You don't need to manufacture; you need to source well
- Margins can be healthy if you have genuine taste that others trust
- Physical and online expressions are both viable; the best examples often have both
- The creator's audience provides a built-in qualified customer base that most new retail businesses would spend years and significant capital to acquire

**The new infrastructure making it easier:**
- The classic curation gatekeepers (luxury hotel concierges, department store buyers, travel magazines) have lost their monopoly on taste transmission
- TikTok, Instagram, and YouTube creators now distribute taste recommendations at scale with higher credibility and specificity than institutional gatekeepers
- A creator with 50K engaged followers in a specific niche (horse culture, Japanese gardening, Mediterranean interior aesthetics) has a more qualified audience than a department store has for any single category

## Real-World Examples

**Good Hood (London)** — Multi-brand store with an "incredible curation" that survives and thrives on the taste of its buyers, not on manufacturing anything.

**March (Copenhagen)** — Curated lifestyle/home store; the selection itself is the differentiator.

**Murtha (Paris)** — Similar model; editorial selection of objects.

**Canoe (Portland)** — Curated objects, mostly designed goods and functional art; the store's taste is the brand.

**Manhattan Saddlery (New York City)** — The only horse-specific retail shop in Manhattan; thriving niche business serving a luxury consumer, built entirely on curation and access to a specific category of goods.

**Flamingo Estate** — Sells curated kits from Japan, gardening objects, lifestyle items. The brand is not primarily a manufacturer — it is a lifestyle curator selling the bottled fantasy of a specific kind of life.

**Milwaukee (London)** — Japanese gardening store selling well-made tools and classes. The experience of learning is sold alongside the objects.

## The Creator Application
For creators building in lifestyle-adjacent niches (fashion, interiors, food, travel, equestrian, gardening, fragrance, etc.), the curation model offers:

1. **Lower capital requirement** than brand-building (no product development, tooling, or manufacturing risk)
2. **Better fit** for people whose skill is aesthetic judgment, not operations
3. **Existing audience** that already trusts their taste — the hardest thing for any new retailer to build is already present
4. **Recurring discovery engine** — content naturally generates visits and sales without requiring ad spend

**The constraint to honest with:** Curation businesses require genuinely good taste and curatorial discipline. The model fails when the curation is lazy, trend-following, or not genuinely differentiated from what customers could find themselves. The curator must have a real point of view.

## What to Steal / Application
- If you have an audience in a niche with luxury or aspirational purchasing behavior, ask whether you're better positioned to curate than to manufacture
- The "I should start a brand" reflex should be stress-tested against the curation alternative, especially for creators whose primary skill is taste and recommendation
- The best curation businesses have a clear cultural thesis — not "we sell nice things" but "we sell the specific objects that belong in the life of someone who [does / believes / values X]"
- Physical retail is not dead for this model — niche curatorial stores in dense urban markets (London, NYC, Copenhagen, Paris, Portland) are thriving because the taste-filtering function is genuinely valuable and not replicable by Amazon
