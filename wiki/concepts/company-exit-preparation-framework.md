# Concept: Company Exit Preparation Framework

## Summary / Definition
The operational and financial preparation a founder needs to do before running an M&A process. Dom Iacovone's acquisition by the Quality Group/CVC took a full year — largely because of cleanup required upfront. He now knows what he'd do differently.

## Why It Matters
Most first-time founders don't prepare for an exit until they're in the process. By then, it's too late to clean up the financial mess, install the team, or build the 2+ years of audited financials that institutional buyers expect. Every problem discovered during due diligence chips away at your valuation — and you can't hide anything once the process starts.

Dom: *"When you start to sell a company, it's called running a process. If you do all these things first, when you start to run your process, you don't have to go back and do all that stuff. It's already done."*

## How It Works / The Mechanism

**Step 1 — Decide which game you're playing**
Two fundamentally different business designs:
- **Cash flow king:** Take distributions, run personal expenses through the company, maximize personal lifestyle. This is incompatible with selling for full value.
- **Exit-ready:** Keep the company clean, pay yourself a market salary (or distributions only after business is self-sustaining), maximize EBITDA and CM1 on the P&L.

You can't switch between these mid-stream and expect full credit. Buyers see through personal expense add-backs. They discount messy financials.

**Step 2 — Audit-ready financials**
- Start 2+ years before you plan to sell
- Get at minimum **2 years of audited financials**
- Run a **Quality of Earnings (QoE)** — a third-party financial analysis that validates your EBITDA and identifies add-backs and risk items before the buyer's team does
- The buyer will do their own QoE during due diligence. You want zero surprises.

**Step 3 — Team independence**
The single most common red flag for buyers: **a company that doesn't work without the founder.**

If you are the company — if your relationships, knowledge, and energy are the engine — the buyer faces massive key-man risk. This dramatically reduces your valuation (or makes you unsellable at all).

Fix: Build a C-suite of proactive operators (not order-takers) who can run the business without your involvement. The test: could you disappear for 3 months and the company still hit its targets?

Dom: *"When you're going to sell, you need to have a team in place so the company doesn't rely on you. That's a massive, massive red flag."*

**Step 4 — Running the process**
1. Hire an investment bank (I-bank runs the process)
2. I-bank prepares you before the formal process: are financials clean? Are there holes?
3. Pitch to 15–20 strategic/PE buyers
4. Receive LOIs (Letters of Intent)
5. Select your partner — not necessarily highest bid (see below)
6. Enter exclusivity: buyer digs into the data room, trying to find problems to chip the valuation
7. Close

**Step 5 — Choosing the right partner**
Dom did not take the highest bid. He chose Quality Group because of their operational knowledge and their ability to help him hit the next level. This is the key insight: if you're rolling equity (not selling 100%), your partner's operational value compounds your second bite.

*"Unless you're selling the whole company, this is going to be a marriage. You're interviewing them as much as they're interviewing you."*

**Step 6 — Earnout structure**
In CPG and retail, there's often a 12–24 month lag between work done and revenue recognized (retail doors don't load in until next year). An earnout captures this gap:
- Sell X% at current trailing 12-month value
- Layer in earnouts tied to future performance milestones
- Keep equity rolling for the second bite

The second bite of the apple is often bigger than the first — especially if you chose the right partner.

**Salary note:**
Dom took no salary before the exit — distributions only (when cash allowed). Post-acquisition he moved to a CEO-market salary. For founders planning to sell: distributions are cleaner for the P&L than inflated salaries. Document everything.

## What to Steal / Application
- **Start preparing 2–3 years before you want to sell.** Not 6 months.
- **If you want to sell, stop running personal expenses through the company immediately.**
- **Get a QoE done on yourself** before running a formal process. Know what a buyer will find before they find it.
- **Build a team that owns their domain** — the company must survive without you for buyers to believe in it.
- **Don't pick the highest bid if there's a meaningful second bite.** Strategic > pure financial if the partner has operational knowledge.
- **Earnouts are not a concession** — they're a mechanism to capture value you've already earned but can't show on trailing financials.
