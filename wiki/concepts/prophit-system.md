# Concept: The Prophit System

## Summary

Taylor Holiday's proprietary financial forecasting infrastructure at Common Thread Collective. Forecasts over $3B in DTC revenue within 4% accuracy across 1,000+ brands. Core insight: **the forecast belongs in the marketing department, not finance.** The marketing calendar drives the financial model — not the other way around. Turns ad production "from gambling into math."

---

## The Four Data Models

The Prophit System is powered by four interconnected data models:

| Model | What It Captures |
|---|---|
| **Spending Power** | How much the business can deploy in paid media given its financial position |
| **Retention** | Historical customer cohort behavior — repurchase rates, LTV curves by acquisition cohort |
| **Event Effect** | How calendar events (Black Friday, product launches, promos) impact revenue beyond baseline |
| **Creative Demand** | How many ads a brand must produce to sustain performance at current spend levels |

Each model feeds into a unified forecast. The system uses Statlas (CTC's proprietary admin platform) to match channel-specific media strategies against marketing calendars.

---

## The Three Forecast Scenarios (Board / Budget / Bonus)

Rather than a single forecast number, Prophit produces three scenarios:

| Scenario | Description | Audience |
|---|---|---|
| **Board** | Conservative / floor outcome | External stakeholders, investors |
| **Budget** | Most likely (50th percentile) | Operational planning, team decisions |
| **Bonus** | Stretch goal (75–80th percentile) | Team incentive targets |

This removes the politics from forecasting. Leadership doesn't get a single number they can manipulate — they get a range with defined confidence levels.

## Creative Demand Planning Layer

3.5% of ads drive 66% of spend. This means:
- Most ads are never meaningfully funded
- The question isn't whether an ad is "good" — it's whether you have enough creative throughput to feed spend
- Brands at scale run 150–400+ ads per month

**Creative Demand Score (0–100):**
Five inputs produce the score:

1. **Zero Spend Rate** — % of active ads receiving no budget
2. **Ad Concentration** — Spend concentrated in top 5 ads (high = fragile)
3. **ROAS Degradation** — Whether performance drops after week 1
4. **Spend Degradation** — Whether daily spend drops post-launch
5. **Evergreen Share** — % of ads running 30+ consecutive days

- Score > 50: You need fewer ads than historically necessary
- Score < 50: You must produce more ads just to maintain baseline performance

## Why Marketing Owns the Forecast

Most brands create financial forecasts in the finance department and marketing plans in the marketing department — with different teams, different assumptions, and no handoff. The friction this creates is invisible but expensive.

The Prophit System argues: **the events on the marketing calendar are the primary driver of revenue variance.** A Q4 marketing calendar without a finance model attached to it isn't a plan — it's a wish list. A finance model without the marketing calendar is guesswork.

One "Prophit Engineer" diagnoses performance from contribution margin down to specific campaign adjustments — a single point of accountability for the numbers.

---

## What to Steal

1. Build three forecasts (conservative / most likely / stretch) instead of one number
2. Attach every major marketing event to a revenue expectation
3. Track Creative Demand Score — if your score is below 50, your production bottleneck is your performance ceiling
4. The 60-day LTV window (30% customer value increase in 60 days; 100% in a year) is the Prophit System's signal that a brand is building something durable, not just transacting
