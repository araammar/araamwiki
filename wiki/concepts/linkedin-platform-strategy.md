# Concept: LinkedIn Platform Strategy

## Summary

LinkedIn is not a fame platform or a job board. It is personal and brand marketing infrastructure — and it is underused precisely because most people look at the surface-level cringe and opt out. That opt-out is the opportunity. For creators, LinkedIn functions as a bottom-of-funnel closing layer for business inquiries. For brands, it is a B2B credibility engine. For employees, it is career leverage infrastructure. The strategic unlock is treating LinkedIn in its own vacuum: it operates by different mechanics than Instagram or TikTok, and strategies from those platforms do not transfer directly.

---

## The Cursed Opportunity

LinkedIn's negatives are well-documented and widely felt:
- Immediate cringe on login — professional posturing, performative vulnerability, AI-generated responses
- Higher posting anxiety than any other platform (bosses, co-workers, and clients all see what you post)
- AI slop proliferating fast — generic button-press comments are everywhere
- Relentless DM spam from sales reps means many people actively avoid logging in

The insight Orin John calls "the cursed opportunity": most professionals look at these negatives and opt out entirely, which radically reduces competition for those who lean in. The cringe is not evidence that LinkedIn doesn't work — it is evidence that the field is open.

Rule: LinkedIn must be treated in its own vacuum. Engagement behaviors, content formats, and growth mechanics from Instagram or TikTok do not map directly onto LinkedIn. The platform operates differently and must be approached on its own terms.

## Video Metrics: Plays vs. Impressions

LinkedIn introduced a video carousel module on desktop that displays 3+ videos continuously across the feed as users scroll. A video appearing in that carousel counts as an **impression** whether it plays or not.

The result: creators see impression counts in the hundreds of thousands or millions that are largely phantom — the video appeared on screen but was never watched.

**The only metric that matters: PLAYS.** Plays require someone to actually click and watch. Play counts are significantly lower than impression counts, which causes most creators to misread their own performance — either overestimating reach (if they trust impressions) or underestimating it (if they expect TikTok-style play volume).

LinkedIn video consumption also behaves differently from other platforms:
- People watch LinkedIn video at work — it "feels like doing work," which changes the consumption context
- LinkedIn content is not inherently shareable the way TikTok or Instagram content is — people don't forward LinkedIn videos as social objects
- Despite this, plays on LinkedIn represent high-quality, intentional traffic — the engagement is real, just smaller in volume

## LinkedIn as a Bottom-of-Funnel Closer

LinkedIn's primary value for creators in business and marketing niches is not top-of-funnel reach. It is closing people who already know you from other platforms.

The mechanism:
1. A potential client, sponsor, or partner discovers you on Instagram or TikTok
2. They want to hire you or make a business inquiry but feel awkward DMing on a personal social app
3. They find your LinkedIn, connect, and reach out from their work account — professional context removes the social friction
4. The deal closes on LinkedIn even though the relationship started elsewhere

"You get this bottom-of-funnel traffic of people who have seen you on these other platforms but who finish the deal inside LinkedIn." — Orin John

Why professional context matters: a connection request with a note from a work account is a low-friction, socially acceptable business inquiry. The same outreach over Instagram DM carries different social weight. LinkedIn provides the professional wrapper that makes the first move comfortable.

Strategic implication: Creators do not need to build a native LinkedIn audience or post LinkedIn-native content to benefit from this. Cross-posting existing content from Instagram or TikTok is sufficient — the content establishes presence and credibility; LinkedIn handles the closing.

## The Three Personas

LinkedIn strategy differs materially depending on your role. Orin John closes with three distinct use cases:

**Persona 1: The Creator**
Goal: inbound business inquiries and bottom-of-funnel conversion.
Strategy: Cross-post existing content. Maintain visible presence. Do not worry about building a separate LinkedIn audience — the value is closing people who already follow you elsewhere.
"If you're a creator, I feel like you have to get on there for bottom of funnel and be cross-posting your content and just have people be aware they're there because you're going to get more business inquiries that way."

**Persona 2: The Brand (Owner or Marketer)**
Goal: B2B relationships, retail and buyer credibility, industry awareness, consumer brand love.
Strategy: Find or assign The Steve Role — a person who curates all brand content across channels onto their personal LinkedIn page. If no one is available for this role, the founder defaults into it. Post consumer content (not just business content) on personal pages as much as possible.

**Persona 3: The Employee**
Goal: professional visibility, career advancement, industry network, future optionality.
Strategy: Use two frameworks — Contribution Posts (what you worked on, what you learned) and Lens Posts (industry events filtered through your expertise). Be genuinely social on the platform before and alongside posting.
Especially critical for: rising from junior to senior roles, senior professionals maintaining future options, anyone in B2B sales.

## Company Cohort Effect

When multiple employees at the same company post about the company, tag the brand page, and interact with each other's content, LinkedIn's algorithm treats this as a strong collective signal and amplifies all of them.

The mechanism:
1. An employee posts and tags the company's brand page — this can trigger a notification to company admins
2. If colleagues who are listed as working at that company engage with the post, the algorithm rewards it
3. A structured internal content strategy — multiple team members posting, tagging, and reacting to brand content — compounds reach significantly without paid spend

"A lot of the companies that do well on there is because there's a high engagement level between their team members and the actual content that they're producing as a brand." — Orin John

This is the legitimate, organic, algorithmically blessed version of an engagement pod. It also resolves the brand account exception: brand pages perform when they are the hub that personal posts orbit and link to — not when they broadcast as standalone channels.

Practical implication: Make it easy and expected for employees to engage with brand content on LinkedIn. The internal team is a free amplification channel most brands ignore entirely.

## What to Steal

- **Treat LinkedIn as infrastructure, not a fame platform.** You don't need to go viral. You need to be findable and credible when the right person searches for you.
- **Track plays, not impressions.** If you're measuring LinkedIn video performance by impressions, you are measuring the wrong thing.
- **Cross-post as the minimum viable strategy.** For creators already active on Instagram or TikTok, moving that content to LinkedIn is low effort with disproportionate business ROI.
- **The cursed opportunity rule:** the cringe drives out competition. Opt in while everyone else opts out.
- **Identify your persona** (Creator / Brand / Employee) and apply the matching strategy. Do not use a creator strategy if you're a brand, and vice versa.
- **Activate the Company Cohort Effect.** Build a simple internal protocol where team members engage with brand LinkedIn content. It costs nothing and amplifies everything.
