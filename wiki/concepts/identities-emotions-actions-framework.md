# Concept: Identities-Emotions-Actions Framework (IEA)

## Summary / Definition

A pre-production planning tool for content creators and brand communicators. Before creating any piece of content — or when launching any new channel, show, or campaign — map three columns: (1) Identities (who is this for?), (2) Emotions (what do I want them to feel?), and (3) Actions (what do I want them to do?). The answers operate as a filter for every production and packaging decision that follows.

Samir Chaudry uses this framework with his team at Colin and Samir as a foundational alignment tool, particularly when launching new shows or reviewing packaging direction.

---

## Why It Matters

Most content failures are not execution failures — they are clarity failures. A video can be well-produced and technically excellent but still fail because the creator never clearly defined who it was for, what emotion it was designed to create, or what the viewer was supposed to do next.

The IEA framework forces this clarity before investment. It also resolves the "who is this for?" question that most creators avoid because it requires commitment. You cannot execute compelling packaging until you know the answers to these three columns.

---

## How It Works / The Mechanism

**Column 1: Identities**

Who specifically are you trying to reach? Not a demographic — an identity. People who define themselves as something.

For Colin and Samir:
- Aspiring creators (earlier stage, want to start a YouTube channel)
- Professional creators (already have a channel, want to grow)
- Creator industry (brands, agencies, managers, platforms working in creator economy)

These aren't interchangeable. The packaging, framing, and emotional register that works for an aspiring creator is different from what works for a professional creator. The identity question forces this distinction.

The corollary: if the answer is too broad ("everyone" or "marketers and operators and creators"), you may have a coherence problem. The more specific the identity, the more powerfully the content resonates. Samir's advice: "Getting more specific is better."

The third rule of YouTube ("give them more") depends on having a core identity group — because the people who keep coming back are those who deeply feel like the content is for them.

**Column 2: Emotions**

What do you want them to feel? Not what information do you want to give them — what emotional state do you want to create?

For Colin and Samir:
- Educated
- Empowered
- Less alone (sense of community)

The emotion question is often the most neglected in business content, where the instinct is to think about information transfer. But people come back to shows and channels based on how those shows make them feel, not just what they learn. The emotional contract is what creates loyalty.

Designing toward an emotion also affects packaging. If the emotion is "empowered," the title language and thumbnail expression point in a specific direction. If the emotion is "entertained," entirely different choices follow.

**Column 3: Actions**

What do you want them to do as a result of consuming the content? This is the action — not necessarily a purchase, but a specific behavior.

For Colin and Samir: "Press publish." They wanted viewers to feel educated and empowered enough to go publish their own ideas.

The action defines what a successful piece of content actually looks like beyond the view count. It links content to outcomes and prevents the trap of optimizing for metrics that don't serve the actual business or creative goal.

---

## Applying the Framework to Packaging

The IEA framework most directly serves packaging. Once you've answered the three columns:

- The identity tells you who the thumbnail and title should signal to. A packaging concept that appeals to one identity group may actively repel another. This is why Samir advises against testing thumbnails that attract off-target audiences just for the view count — a longevity/health thumbnail will pull a Huberman audience, not a creator/business audience.
- The emotion tells you the register of the packaging — aspirational, funny, alarming, empowering, reassuring.
- The action tells you whether the packaging is pointing toward the intended outcome or away from it.

---

## What to Steal / Application

**Use it as a pre-launch filter:**
Before starting any new show, channel, series, or campaign — write the three columns. Don't film until the answers are clear.

**Use it as a packaging audit:**
Look at existing title/thumbnail combinations and ask: does this packaging attract the right identity, create the right emotion, and point toward the right action? If any column is misaligned, the packaging needs work.

**Use it for sequencing decisions:**
When choosing what episode to release next, run the IEA filter. If one episode is great content but attracts a different identity group than your core, it may fragment community even if it gets views.

**The business version:**
This framework maps directly to any brand's marketing positioning work. Identity = ICP. Emotions = brand feeling. Actions = conversion behavior. It's a lightweight version of full audience segmentation that any individual creator can execute in 20 minutes.
