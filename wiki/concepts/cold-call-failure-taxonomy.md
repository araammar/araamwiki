# Concept: Cold Call Failure Taxonomy
tags: [cold calling, B2B, outbound, failure analysis, objection handling, qualification, Starbridge]

## Summary / Definition

A documented map of every failure type encountered in Micah's 7.5-hour cold call speedrun — with the exact handling for each. Not all "failures" are equal. Some are data problems, some are targeting problems, some are disqualifications (which are actually wins — they save everyone's time). Understanding the difference is what separates efficient callers from ones who grind without improving.

Source: YouTube video tdYvRKVr0pk (Starbridge campaign, Glen Coco marketplace).

---

## Why It Matters

In Micah's session: 770 dials → 50 pickups → 33 conversations → only 16 right-ICP conversations → 6 bookings.

That means 754 dials were some form of "failure." His 37.5% conversion rate exists because of how he handled those 754 — each one resolved quickly with a clear protocol, no wasted time.

He also explicitly distinguishes: **"Bad data is a list quality issue. Wrong ICP is a targeting issue. Don't conflate them — they have different fixes."**

---

## The 8 Failure Types

### 1. Wrong Number / Misdial
**What it looks like:** Pick up, wrong company or wrong person.
**Example:** "You have the wrong number." / "I don't think I'm at the Pete store." 
**Handling:** Quick apology. "Oh, shoot. Sorry about that." Hang up. Move on. Takes 10 seconds.
**CRM action:** Remove/correct the number. Don't mark as DNC.
**Root cause:** Bad list data. Fix at the list level, not the call level.

---

### 2. Voicemail
**What it looks like:** Rings through, goes to voicemail.
**Handling:** Two options — leave a message or hang up and let the auto-dialer recycle. Micah typically hangs up and recycles: "Speed to conversation. Unless it's a high-intent referral, I'll throw them back in the dialer."
**When to leave a voicemail:** Strong referral or mutual connection where a message adds context.
**Note:** Voicemail is not a failure, it's a volume reality. Expect it on the majority of dials.

---

### 3. Gatekeeper Block
**What it looks like:** Receptionist or admin picks up, target person unavailable.
**Example (from transcript):**
- "He should know. It's Micah."
- "I don't have him available, but I can send him a message."
- "That's okay. All right, appreciate it."
**Handling:** Offer to leave a message, but usually decline and recycle into the dialer. Don't burn time building a relationship with the gatekeeper on the first call.
**When to leave a gatekeeper message:** If you've called 3+ times — then leave a message for accountability.
**CRM action:** Log as "gatekeeper, not reached," throw back in queue.

---

### 4. Not ICP — Clean Disqualification
**What it looks like:** Prospect picks up, confirms they don't sell to SLED accounts.
**Example:** "No, not at the moment. We don't really target government or education."
**Handling:** "That makes this call a little easier. I'll mark you down so we don't keep bugging you." End the call. Immediate and clean. No pitch. No push.
**Why this is actually a win:** He asks the pre-qualifying question BEFORE pitching. A clean DQ at this stage costs 20 seconds. A pitch to the wrong person costs 3 minutes and ends in frustration.
**CRM action:** Mark as "not relevant persona — SLED not a priority." DNC or remove from ICP list.
**Key phrase:** "That makes this call easier." Reframes the DQ as a gift to both parties.

---

### 5. Adjacent ICP — Fuzzy Disqualification
**What it looks like:** Company has some SLED connection but not in the right way for Starbridge to help.
**Examples:**
- **Burke (dental equipment company):** "We have contracts with all 46 dental universities in North America, but they use us — we don't bid for them. We don't need to identify accounts because if you're a dental school, you're already a customer." → Not a fit.
- **Joanna (construction):** "We work with some government but through private clients, not directly as a SLED vendor." → Not a fit.
- **Leah (small firm, K12/higher ed):** Had SLED exposure as a sub-contractor via architect prime. Fit was marginal — booked anyway for "apples to apples" comparison.

**Handling:** Listen carefully. Don't force the pitch if the underlying business model doesn't match. "Gotcha. So you guys work with them but they're not really the ones you're bidding to. Yeah, that's — makes this one a little easier." Move on.
**Key skill:** distinguishing "we work with government entities" from "we actively bid on and win government contracts." They sound the same but they're not.
**CRM action:** Mark as "adjacent ICP — not a fit" with a note on why.

---

### 6. Existing Tool Objection
**What it looks like:** Prospect already uses a competitor — GovSpend, Pursuit, NationGraph.
**Example (Leah):** "We already have a contract with that, and I'm not sure when we renew. I think it's sometime in January."
**Handling:**
1. Acknowledge without attacking: "Yeah, definitely don't want to rip and replace, especially if it's working well for you."
2. Differentiate briefly: "We're kind of the new wave of AI capability on top of what you might already have."
3. Low-pressure ask: "Would it be a ridiculous idea to do an apples-to-apples comparison — and then from there, maybe keep us in the back pocket for renewal time?"
4. If they agree → book for "renewal timing" frame, not "switch now."
**Why this works:** Takes all pressure off. The meeting is framed as information gathering, not a sales close. Leah booked a meeting even though she had an existing contract.
**Key phrase:** "Keep us in the back pocket for renewal."

---

### 7. Wrong Internal Contact — Not the Decision Maker
**What it looks like:** The person who picks up is not the one who handles SLED accounts or vendor decisions.
**Example (Jamie):** "I I don't make those decisions. That's my director of sales." / "I service portion — Amanda would be the one to look at something like this."
**Handling:** Don't push. Ask for the right path: "Do you mind if I send an email to the address you provide? What was your director's name?" Send a note to the team email. Let Amanda find it.
**Alternative handling:** Ask for a referral. "Would it be okay if I just referenced your name when I reach out to her?"
**Key skill:** Know when to cut bait and route the outreach correctly vs. trying to close a person who can't say yes.
**CRM action:** Update contact to reflect the right decision maker. Tag for follow-up via email.

---

### 8. DNC Request
**What it looks like:** Prospect explicitly asks to be removed from call lists.
**Example (Chad):** "If it's a sales call, it's probably not relevant. I'm not in the market for anything. Take me off the list. Thanks."
**Handling:** "Interesting. Do not call. Requested DNC." Immediate mark. No pushback. No "just one more thing."
**CRM action:** DNC — do not contact. Full stop. Attempting to re-contact after a DNC request is both illegal (in many jurisdictions) and destructive to list hygiene.
**Key principle:** Honor the DNC instantly. Every second you spend arguing after a DNC request is a second wasted and a reputation damaged.

## The Stat Breakdown (End of Session)

- Total "not closable": 58% of pickups
- Bad data (wrong person, wrong number, disconnected): ~24%
- Wrong ICP (not SLED): ~34%
- Right ICP but no close: ~5-6 calls
- Micah's note: "One in four are just the wrong person altogether. It's workable. When we got in front of the right person, they would convert."

---

## What This Means for List Quality

Micah's post-session take: Starbridge likely scraped companies that have any contracts with government entities — which creates ICP fuzziness. The list has people who work adjacent to SLED but don't bid for it directly.

Better list = tighter ICP = lower DQ rate = higher conversion rate from same number of dials.

**The lesson:** Bad data rate and wrong ICP rate are both controllable, but they're controlled at different stages:
- **Bad data** → fix at the data sourcing/verification stage
- **Wrong ICP** → fix at the targeting definition stage (tighter qualification criteria before dialing)
