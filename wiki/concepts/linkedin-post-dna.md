# Concept: LinkedIn Post DNA — 100-Post Analysis

**Creators studied:** Justin Welsh, Lara Acosta, Jasmin Alic, Matt Barker, Codie Sanchez, Sahil Bloom, Gary Vaynerchuk, Timothy Armoo, Dan Go, Ethan Mollick, Tom Fishburne, Leila Hormozi, Joel Klettke, Katelyn Bourgoin, Jay Clouse, Dickie Bush, Guillaume Moubeche

---

## The Master Rule

Every post that performs gives the reader something to CLAIM — a belief, an identity, a phrase, a permission.

People don't share content they agree with. They share content that makes them look like who they want to be. The post is the vehicle. The reader's self-image is the destination.

---

## The Hook Rule

LinkedIn truncates at ~210 characters. That is your hook window — everything before "see more."

It must create tension, promise specific value, or force a reframe in two lines max. Never open with context, biography, or warm-up. Start with the sharpest point.

---

## Hook Taxonomy (13 Types)

### 1. Problem Reframe
"Most X don't have a Y problem. They have a Z problem."
Absolves the reader before offering anything. Resets their mental frame. Highest hook-to-comment conversion in the dataset.
> "Most entrepreneurs don't have a strategy problem. They've got a what-do-I-do-next problem." — Justin Welsh

### 2. Permission Declaration
"I'll say it so you don't have to." Hands the reader a suppressed belief and gives them public cover to claim it. Comments flood in because people want to signal they hold the belief.
> "Being rich is awesome. I'm going to say it so you don't have to." — Codie Sanchez

### 3. False Belief Reframe
"You're not afraid of X. You're afraid of Y." Diagnoses the real feeling underneath the stated feeling. Two sentences. No explanation needed.
> "You're not afraid of failure. You're afraid of being seen failing." — Sahil Bloom

### 4. Stakes Escalation
Open with something small and recoverable → elevate to life-defining consequence. Forces the reader to care before they've agreed to.
> "You can mess up small choices and recover. But the big ones — those define your life." — Justin Welsh

### 5. Named Framework
Give an idea a proper noun. Creates a citable concept people reference in comments. Start with the label, not the explanation.
> "The Pygmalion Razor." — Sahil Bloom

### 6. Anaphora List
Repeat the same opening phrase across 4-6 short lines, each adding a new person to the in-group. Every reader scans looking for themselves. When they find it, they share it.
**#1 performer in the entire 100-post dataset: 3,396 reactions | 319 comments | 158 reposts**
> "People who were laid off. People who took a career break. People who changed industries. People who graduated during COVID. The best candidates aren't always the most linear."

### 7. Business Identity Reframe
"You're not in the X business. You're in the Y business. X is the asset. Y is the vehicle." Forces the reader to recategorize who they are in one sentence.
> "You're not in the content business. You're in the attention business."

### 8. Age-Bracket Targeting
"If you're in your [20s / first year / fifth year], read this." Instant self-selection filter. Triggers identity match before first idea lands.
> "If you're in your 20s, read this." — Codie Sanchez

### 9. Journey Arc
Two hyper-specific data points separated by a time gap. No emotional language. The gap IS the story. Best performing narrative hook in the dataset.
> "At 17, I laid out 2,000 chairs to get a press pass. Last week I was invited to speak on Richard Branson's private island." — Timothy Armoo (205 comments | 22 reposts)

### 10. Anti-Shortcut Declaration
State the hack doesn't exist. Validate effort as the only path. Resonates because the reader already suspects it — saying it out loud is the release valve.
> "There is no hack for it. Stop looking for the shortcut. The shortcut is the long way around." — Codie Sanchez (487 comments | 136 reposts)

### 11. Platform Insider
"[Platform] is doing X." Signals proximity to information others don't have. Works only when followed by specific, verifiable claims.
> "LinkedIn is cleaning up. They're targeting pods, automated comments, and comment-for-ebook tactics."

### 12. Single Open Question
The entire post IS the question. One to three words. No setup. No payoff. Widest possible response surface. Violates every "add value" instinct and consistently outperforms.
> "Agree?" — Codie Sanchez (159 + 232 comments across two uses)

### 13. Third-Person Proxy
"I have a friend who..." Vulnerability without full exposure. Reader knows it might be the writer. That ambiguity is the tension.
> "I have a friend who disappeared for years. Then re-emerged better, richer, happier than ever." — Sahil Bloom

## Body Moves

**Tricolon Escalation** — Three items, each bigger than the last. Third always reframes the first two. No connectives.
> "Audience watches. Network connects. Community shows up." — Jasmin Alic

**Hyper-Specific Number** — Exact, unrounded number to establish authority. The oddness signals it's real.
> "192,138 followers" not "190K" — Matt Barker

**Analogy Pair** — Map unfamiliar judgment to something emotionally safe. Transfers the moral frame instantly.
> "Making fun of someone for trying to build a business is like making fun of someone for trying to get in shape." — Justin Welsh

**Stop/Start Directive** — Two-part command. Each half one sentence. No explanation needed.
> "Stop performing success. Start building the depth that makes success inevitable."

**Specificity Rule** — Name the Slack message, the conference room, the dollar amount, the exact days. Abstract lessons don't land. Specific moments do.

## Closing Move Taxonomy (7 Types)

### Definitional Reframe ← Most shareable device in the dataset
Last line redefines a word the reader thought they understood. Doesn't summarize — upgrades.
> "Consistency isn't a strategy. It's a personality trait." — Jasmin Alic

### Paradox Close ← Highest repost rate of any device
Two truths that seem to contradict but are both correct. Compresses wisdom into a quotable phrase.
> "The shortcut is the long way around." — Codie Sanchez (136 reposts)

### Contrast Pair
Two items. One is what people chase. One is what works. No connectives, no explanation.
> "Usefulness compounds. Virality fades." — Matt Barker

### Character Sketch
"The most [dangerous / underrated / powerful] person in the room is the one who..." Invites reader to self-identify.
> "The most dangerous person in the room is the one who already knows everything." — Sahil Bloom

### Superlative Declaration
"X is the only sustainable form of Y." Ends a debate the reader didn't know they were having. Highly shareable.
> "Competence is the only sustainable form of confidence." — Leila Hormozi

### Philosophical Elevation
Zooms from the specific post topic out to a universal truth. Turns a business point into a life principle.

### Open Question Close
The last line IS the question. Not "What do you think?" — a question so specific it can only be answered with a real answer.

## Engagement Engines (3)

### Comment-Driver
**Goal:** maximize comments
**Use hooks:** permission_declaration, single_open_question, anaphora_list, third_person_proxy
**Close with:** open_question_close or definitional_reframe
**Rule:** Never explicitly ask for comments. Invite response through the position, not the request. March 2026 Authenticity Update penalizes explicit engagement asks.

### Repost-Driver
**Goal:** maximize reposts and saves
**Use:** paradox_close, named_framework, tricolon_escalation, anti_shortcut_declaration
**Rule:** The closer must work as a standalone quotable sentence. Saves and reposts are now the durability signals — they resurface posts days after publication.

### Reactions-Driver
**Goal:** maximize reactions
**Use:** first-person tool observation, journey_arc, stakes_escalation
**Rule:** Casual, specific, authoritative. A qualified person reporting what they found.

## What Kills Posts

- Explicit engagement asks ("Comment below", "Drop a like") — penalized by March 2026 Authenticity Update
- External links in post body — reduce reach up to 68%. Links go in first comment.
- Opening with context, biography, or "I've been thinking about..."
- Generic advice with no specific detail
- Rounded numbers — "190K followers" vs "192,138 followers"
- Walls of text — max 2-3 lines per paragraph
- Single image posts — underperform text-only by 30% in 2026 (reversal from 2024)
- Company page posting — personal profiles get 13x more reach
