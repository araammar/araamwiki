# Concept: The Content Goal Framework — Shares, Saves, and Comments as Distinct Design Targets

## Summary / Definition

Most creators design content to get views. This is a category error. Views are a byproduct of achieving a specific audience action — and the action you're designing for determines every creative decision: the topic, the format, the take, the CTA, the level of polarization, and the distribution channel. Orin John's framework assigns a primary goal to each piece of content before production starts, and builds the content around that goal rather than retrofitting a goal after the fact.

---

## The Three Goals

### Goal 1: Shares (in Work Slack / Group Chat)
**Target content type:** B2B, professional, expert knowledge, marketing, operations  
**Design principle:** Make something so useful that a professional is compelled to share it with their team  
**The test question:** "Is this good enough that someone will send this in their work Slack?"  
**Why it works:** B2B audiences don't comment publicly — they share privately. A video that gets shared in a work Slack channel reaches an audience that would never find it through the algorithm. One share into a 30-person team = 30 qualified impressions with zero additional reach cost.

**Practical implication:** The content needs to be expert-level specific and immediately actionable. Generic marketing content doesn't get Slacked. Specific, insight-dense content does.

### Goal 2: Comments (Argument-Starting Takes)
**Target content type:** Consumer-facing content, opinion content, brand comparisons, cultural analysis  
**Design principle:** Make a take people have to respond to — either to agree loudly or to argue  
**The test question:** "Will this start a conversation or a fight?"  
**Why it works:** Comment velocity is one of the strongest engagement signals. Content with high comment counts gets algorithmic amplification. The fastest way to generate comments is to have an opinion.

The generational context: "We've been basically educated to not have an opinion. If you said something was bad, oh, you can't say that's bad. If you say something is good, they're just a shill. Everyone's in this weird middle ground. So it actually stands out if you have an opinion."

Orin's own CTA: "Tell me why I'm brutally wrong in the comments." This explicitly licenses disagreement, which unlocks the comment section.

**Format implication:** Content structured as "this is good / this is bad / here's what this says about you" naturally generates comment activity because it makes a claim that invites response.

### Goal 3: Saves (Reference Content)
**Target content type:** Brand deals, B2B SaaS, complex purchase decisions, professional resources  
**Design principle:** Create something so useful as a reference that people bookmark it to act on later  
**The test question:** "Will someone save this to come back to when they need to solve this problem?"  
**Why it works:** Saves signal purchase intent at a latency — the person isn't buying now, but they're indicating this is in their consideration set. For products or services with a longer purchase cycle, saves are the most valuable engagement metric.

Orin's benchmark for sponsored content: **500 combined saves and shares as the success threshold**. He guarantees this to brands: "I will get hundreds of brands to save or share this — pay me nothing if we don't get 500 combined of those two things."

**The Paperless Pro model:** A video framed as "a guide to the types of brand events worth doing, and here's how you'd use Paperless Pro for each" — it functions as a reference guide. Someone saves it because they're planning events but aren't buying today. Three months later, when they need event software, they have Paperless Pro bookmarked.

## Why CTA Language Doesn't Drive Comments

The standard "leave a comment below" CTA does not work for generating comments. The comment section is not a transactional space — it's a social and argumentative one.

What works instead: design the *content body* to provoke a response. The take has to be strong enough that silence feels like submission. Not asking for comments — earning them by making a claim that demands engagement.

This is why listicle content ("Here are five ways to improve your marketing") underperforms opinion content ("This is why your marketing strategy is wrong") — the former is easily consumed without responding; the latter creates cognitive dissonance that requires resolution through agreement or argument.

## The B2B Goal Asymmetry

B2B content exists on a different axis than consumer content:

- **B2B views are often a negative signal** for business outcomes — high views on marketing content tends to come from the wrong audience (aspirational non-buyers rather than decision-makers)
- **Low-view B2B content can massively outperform** on actual business outcomes: brand deals, clients, inbound inquiry
- Three videos Orin identifies as "flopped" (low views) that drove the most serious inquiries: HR and creator management content, trade show experience content, AI quiz-brand content — all too specific and niche for mass audiences, all perfect for the specific decision-makers who needed that exact knowledge

**Principle:** Don't be blinded by views. Optimize for the goal that aligns with your monetization path. Consumer content gets views; it does not get brand deals.

## Content Goal Assignment in Practice

Before production starts:
1. Identify the primary audience segment for this piece
2. Assign one goal: share, save, or comment
3. Design the topic, format, and take to optimize for that goal
4. Use the goal's test question as the quality gate before posting

One goal per video. Multiple goals dilute focus and typically achieve none of them.

## Platform/Format Implications by Goal

| Goal | Best Platform | Best Format | Success Metric |
|------|--------------|-------------|----------------|
| Shares (Slack) | Instagram, LinkedIn | Green screen / talking head with data | Share count; Slack mentions |
| Comments (Argument) | TikTok, Instagram | Opinion take / ranking video | Comment count; comment velocity |
| Saves (Reference) | Instagram, YouTube | Guide / framework / step-by-step | Save count; save-to-view ratio |
