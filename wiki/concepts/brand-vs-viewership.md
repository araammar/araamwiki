# Concept: Brand vs. Viewership

## Summary / Definition

High viewership and a strong brand are not the same thing. In the current attention economy, viewership is easy to manufacture. Brand — a consistent identity that people trust, recognize, and feel something toward — is the asset that compounds and lasts. Creators and media companies have gotten dangerously over-optimized for viewership metrics while neglecting the brand that makes those views matter.

Samir Chaudry: "We have gotten into a world where brand and viewership are confused. Just because you have high viewership does not mean you have a brand. And a brand I think matters a lot more right now than high viewership."

---

## Why It Matters

The proliferation of short-form content, viral moments, and algorithmic recommendation has made generating views easier than ever. A single viral Reel or Short can deliver millions of views to a creator with no established identity. But those views do nothing for long-term brand equity.

The collapse of the brand/viewership distinction is why creators plateau or burn out: they chase the next viral moment instead of building the asset that makes every future piece of content land harder. Brand compounds. Viewership doesn't.

---

## How It Works / The Mechanism

**The Three Types of Views**

Not all views are equal. Colin and Samir identified three categories:

**1. Forgettable Views**
Watched passively, retained nothing, forgotten immediately. Classic bathroom scroll behavior. You consume something, can't name it the next day. TikTok passive consumption generates mostly forgettable views. These inflate view counts but build no relationship, no brand awareness, no loyalty.

**2. Regrettable Views**
Watched and felt vaguely bad afterward. Extended short-form feed scrolling often produces this. You're not sure what you watched but you feel like you wasted time. Regrettable views are actively harmful to the brand serving them — associating a creator or brand with the feeling of wasted time is the opposite of brand equity.

**3. Memorable Views**
Watched, retained, and referenced later. At the end of the year, these are the shows and videos you can name. "I watched Colin and Samir and it changed how I think about YouTube." "I watched that documentary and it stuck with me." Memorable views build genuine brand equity. They are the views that drive long-term loyalty, referrals, sponsorship pricing power, and community.

**The Case Study: 44 Million Empty Views**

Colin and Samir made a YouTube Short about the three oldest videos on YouTube. It did 44 million views. The obvious follow-up was to make another top-three YouTube list.

They didn't. Their diagnosis: "These are empty views. This is empty calories. This is not really providing much value. It's kind of fun to watch. It's entertainment, but it's not us. It's not the brand we want to build. We don't want to be known for these top lists. We want to be known for deep conversation about YouTube, the business of YouTube, creativity."

44 million views of non-brand-aligned content would have pointed the channel (and its algorithm signal) in the wrong direction. They sacrificed the viewership for the brand.

**What Brand Actually Is**

Brand is:
- A set of values that a viewer can anticipate
- Trust — a consistent experience every time they engage
- An emotional feeling that forms when someone sees the logo or name
- Reputation — what people think of when they hear your name or see your show

"A brand is when I see the Open Residency logo, I can immediately imagine what it looks like. I know what the experience is and I have an emotional feeling towards it."

Viewership is easily manufactured. Reputation cannot be bought.

---

## The Economic Implication

Brand drives sponsorship pricing power. Samir's observation: if you consistently produce memorable views over time, the rate curve is a hockey stick. Premium brands pay premium rates. If your audience perceives your brand as premium, advertisers will too.

"If you have memorable views over time, those numbers — it is literally a hockey stick as far as what people will pay."

Brand also governs what you can say no to. Samir and Colin have said no to almost every sponsorship opportunity. The ones they've taken are brand-aligned. The willingness to say no to money is what preserves brand integrity — and brand integrity is what commands better money over time.

---

## Sponsorship Brand Alignment

Brand discipline extends to who sponsors you. A sponsor is "a friend you're bringing to the party." How you introduce them reflects on your brand. Accepting misaligned sponsors for short-term revenue is a brand devaluation trade.

The test: "Is this a cool friend to bring to the party in a way that people can understand — yes or no?" If the answer requires explanation, the answer is probably no.

## What to Steal / Application

**Audit your content for view type, not just view count:**
What percentage of your views are forgettable vs. memorable? Are you building toward memorable content consistently, or chasing the viral moment?

**Make decisions from brand, not from viewership:**
When a piece of content performs well but doesn't reflect who you want to be — don't double down on it. Samir's 44M views lesson: high viewership in the wrong direction is worse than low viewership in the right direction.

**Define your brand before your content calendar:**
What do you want to be known for? What emotion do you want viewers to associate with your name? The [[identities-emotions-actions-framework]] is the tool for this.

**Think in memorable minutes, not views:**
Replace "how many views did we get?" with "how many memorable minutes did we create?" Events, in-person experiences, and deep long-form content generate more memorable minutes per dollar than viral short-form. See [[memorable-minutes-framework]].
