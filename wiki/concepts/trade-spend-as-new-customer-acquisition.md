# Concept: Trade Spend as New Customer Acquisition Tool

## Summary / Definition
The strategic reframe of retail trade spend (BOGOs, end cap fees, rollbacks, discounts) — not as a cost of doing business, but as a new customer acquisition mechanism with measurable ROI. When executed correctly, trade spend is a deliberate, timed investment in expanding the active customer base. When executed incorrectly, it becomes an uncontrolled margin drain that turns the brand into a discount brand.

Dom Iacovone's rule: *"The only time you discount is for new customer acquisition."*

## Why It Matters
Most CPG brands run promotions because they have to — the retailer demands it, or they're trying to hit velocity hurdle rates. Brands that treat trade spend as a cost inevitably over-discount because they don't have a framework for when to stop.

Brands that treat trade spend as CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost) can measure it, time it, and turn it off — protecting margin between acquisition spikes.

## How It Works / The Mechanism

**The core framework:**

Trade spend should NOT be: a consistent percentage every month, a reflexive response to retailer pressure, or a tool to prop up failing products.

Trade spend SHOULD be: deployed in strategic bursts to acquire new customers, then withdrawn so new customers buy at full margin.

**The peaks-and-valleys model:**
1. Run a high-value promotion (BOGO, pallet deal at Sam's)
2. Track velocity uplift: did sales increase beyond baseline?
3. After promotion ends, monitor if new baseline is higher than pre-promotion baseline
4. If yes: you acquired new customers. If no: you just discounted existing customers.

Example: Publix average order basket = $30/month. Run a BOGO. After the BOGO period, if monthly Publix revenue goes from $100K to $120K, you won — you added ~666 net new monthly customers.

**The BOGO principle:**
Prefer product discounts (BOGO) over monetary discounts (30% off). Why:
- BOGO gives the consumer more product rather than less money
- Harder for consumers to "stack" the discount as a behavior habit
- Avoids training the consumer to expect a % off the price
- Better for brand equity: the product price stays intact; you're just giving more

*"I like to do it in product instead of monetary. So a BOGO. And I only like to do it for new customers."*

**The discount brand trap:**
Brands that run continuous discounts train consumers to wait for the sale. The brand's full price becomes the "error state" and the discount becomes expected. This is extremely hard to reverse.

Dom: *"If you're doing them all the time, what do you have now? You have a discount brand."*

**Timing your promotions:**
High-value windows to deploy trade spend:
- New year / new me (January) — highest intent window for health/fitness
- Black Friday / Cyber Week — high commerce intent, price-motivated buyers
- Product launches / line extensions — use a BOGO to drive trial of new SKUs
- Seasonal moments relevant to your category

Dom: *"The beautiful part is that trade spend percentage doesn't need to be the same every month. It shouldn't be. That's bad. You want big peaks and valleys."*

**The 60-day vitamin rule:**
For Revive (supplements), Dom's specific acquisition thesis: it takes 60 days to feel the effects of a vitamin. If you can get a customer to use the product for 60 days (by giving them a 2-month supply via BOGO), they'll feel the results and come back at full price. The acquisition cost is one free unit; the LTV is years of recurring purchases.

**Post-promo attribution (retail version):**
Unlike DTC, retail doesn't have clean attribution. Dom's workaround: measure baseline velocity before the promo, then measure post-promo baseline. The delta is your proxy for net new customer acquisition.

## What to Steal / Application
- **Reframe every promo with the question: is this for new customers or existing ones?** Only the former is justified as CAC.
- **Build a P&L by retailer** to see which promos actually moved the needle.
- **Pick 2–3 moments per year** for major trade spend. Make them big and strategic. Stay at full price the rest of the year.
- **Use BOGO over % off wherever possible.** Product generosity feels different than price discounting.
- **Stop promos that don't show baseline uplift.** If velocity returns to the same baseline after every promo, you're just discounting existing customers.
