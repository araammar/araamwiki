# Concept: Scent Emotion Marketing

## Summary / Definition
The strategic use of scent — both in product formulation and in how the scent is named and described — to create emotional association rather than merely describing a smell. Dom Iacovone learned this framework from Georgia, an ex-Estee Lauder executive who joined Onside's board, while building his men's personal care brand.

The insight: **fragrance brands aren't selling a smell — they're selling a memory, an experience, an aspiration.** Cologne commercials don't show the bottle. They show someone on a yacht in Italy.

## Why It Matters
In the fragrance and personal care category, the purchase frequency is low (cologne once a year, body lotion once a month). This means every touchpoint has to do heavy emotional lifting. The scent name on the label is not a descriptor — it is a destination.

When done right, scent names + packaging + visual identity trigger a felt experience before the consumer even smells the product.

## How It Works / The Mechanism

**The neurological mechanism:**
Scent is uniquely tied to memory. Unlike other senses, olfactory signals bypass the thalamus and connect directly to the amygdala and hippocampus — the brain's emotional and memory centers. This is why:
- Smelling something can instantly transport you to a specific moment
- You can have an emotional reaction to a scent before you consciously identify what it is
- Scent creates stronger brand loyalty than taste, sight, or sound

Georgia's advice: *"Scents really drive people back to a place or an experience in the past. Something reminds them of something. So playing into the emotion of scent is something that most people don't do when building out a brand."*

**The naming principle:**
Onside's scent names are experiential, not literal:
- **Santal Leather** — not "cedar wood + leather"
- **Eucalyptus Mint** — evocative combo, not a generic description
- **Coconut Noir** — sounds luxurious, not tropical

Stupid/generic names kill the emotional potential:
- "Ocean Breeze" — too literal, no aspiration
- "Fresh Scent" — a placeholder, not a destination
- "Clean Cotton" — functional, zero emotion

**The fragrance commercial formula:**
Every successful cologne commercial follows the same template: someone jumping in the ocean in Italy, someone on a yacht, someone in a beautiful European setting. The product is barely shown.

Why: the commercial is selling the experience the scent represents, not the scent itself. This works because the human brain connects the visual experience to the sensory memory and transposes both onto the product.

**Application to naming/copy:**
The packaging's scent name description must do the same thing in 3–4 words. "Coconut Noir" implies: warmth, darkness, sophistication, exoticism. The consumer imagines a scenario before they open the bottle.

**Meticulous scent selection:**
Georgia's guidance: be obsessively selective about the actual scent. A fragrance brand's equity is the scent — not the bottle, not the celebrity. The scent that creates memory wins long-term.

Dom: *"If you find a cologne, you're staying on that cologne for a while."* This is the ultimate loyalty mechanism in the category. Once a scent becomes associated with identity, it becomes a moat.

**The "signature scent" strategy:**
Onside is building a signature scent with Jason Tatum (coconut noir) launching end of year. This creates:
- A permanent association between the athlete's identity and the scent
- A collectible/cultural object beyond a functional product
- A story anchored in specific ingredients and emotional territory

## What to Steal / Application
- **Name your scents like experiences, not ingredients.** "Tuscan Amber" > "warm amber." "Arctic Smoke" > "menthol cedar."
- **Design a coherent emotional world for your fragrance line** — every scent should feel like it belongs to the same experiential universe.
- **In ad creative, sell the experience, not the product.** What does this scent make you feel? Film that.
- **Invest disproportionately in scent selection** in fragrance products — this is your actual moat, not the packaging.
- **Use a signature scent to anchor athlete/celebrity partnerships** — personalizes the product and deepens the association.
