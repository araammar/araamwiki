# Concept: Relative Zero

## Summary / Definition

Relative zero is the view count or performance metric that a creator's brain treats as "zero" — as if nothing happened — even when that number would be a massive achievement for a different creator. It is the psychological phenomenon where a creator's benchmark for "success" inflates with their channel size, making each specific milestone feel worse over time rather than better. A creator doing 80K views on a video feels like they "failed" when their norm is 300K views; a larger creator feels the same feeling at 500K views.

Samir Chaudry: "I noticed a couple weeks ago we had an episode that did 80,000 views. I mean, everyone on our team was like, man, like it's not going well. 80,000 views. That number for us is... I call that number relative zero. You look at something and you're like 'zero people watched this' but actually 80,000 people watched it."

---

## Why It Matters

Relative zero is one of the most psychologically destructive forces for long-tenured creators. It creates a permanent dissatisfaction trap: no performance metric can feel "good" because the baseline for "acceptable" keeps rising with the channel. The emotional relationship to the work becomes mediated entirely by whether the number exceeded the relative zero threshold — not by whether the work was good, whether it reached the right people, or whether it created genuine impact.

Over 15 years, this is why great creators burn out: not from the work itself, but from the psychological torture of a moving benchmark that can never be permanently satisfied.

---

## How It Works / The Mechanism

**The inflation mechanism:**
When a creator is starting out, 1,000 views feels like a victory. As the channel grows, 10,000 views becomes the baseline. Then 100,000. Then 500,000. At each stage, the previous milestone has become "nothing" in the creator's mental model. The relative zero keeps inflating.

This is not irrational — it's partly algorithmic. If your channel normally signals to YouTube that your content is worth showing to 500K people, a video that tops out at 80K sends a negative signal. The creator's brain is reading that signal correctly in terms of channel mechanics. The problem is letting the algorithmic signal dictate the emotional relationship to the work.

**The objectification problem:**
Relative zero objectifies work — turns it into a number, strips it of its actual value. Samir on an episode about Chinese micro-dramas that "tanked" on YouTube with less than expected views: it simultaneously did 1.4 million downloads on Spotify, one of their best-performing podcast episodes ever. The YouTube metric said "failure." The actual audience response said something different. Relative zero only sees one number.

**The public pressure dimension on YouTube:**
Unlike Spotify (where low performance is private), YouTube comments are public. When a video underperforms, the YouTube comments often say things like "Colin and Samir fell off" or "next video better hit, boys." This public commentary amplifies the relative zero feeling — not only do you feel it internally, but the audience performs it back at you.

**The platform identity amplification:**
If you've positioned yourself as an expert in the platform, underperforming on that platform stings more. Samir is positioned as "the guy who understands YouTube." A video that underperforms on YouTube hits his identity in a unique way — not just "my video underperformed" but "my work on the thing I'm supposed to understand underperformed."

---

## The Platform/Distribution Split as Antidote

One partial solution: measuring success across all distribution channels rather than any single platform. The Chinese micro-dramas episode is the clearest example. YouTube said underperformance. Spotify said massive success. The work was excellent; it reached the wrong audience on one platform.

This argues for creators to have multiple distribution channels and to aggregate the measurement of impact rather than letting one platform's metric determine the verdict on the work.

---

## What to Steal / Application

**Name your relative zero:**
What's the view count that your brain treats as "zero"? Write it down explicitly. Naming it as a psychological artifact (rather than an objective fact) is the first step to managing it.

**Distinguish metric from value:**
An 80,000-view video reached 80,000 people. Imagine a room of 80,000 people. That is not zero. The question is not "did it hit my internal benchmark?" — it is "did it reach the right people? Did it say the right thing? Was it worth making?"

**Multi-platform measurement:**
If you publish across YouTube, Spotify, newsletter, and Instagram, don't let any single platform's metric be the sole verdict on a piece of content. Aggregate. Some content over-indexes on Spotify; some over-indexes on YouTube. The work's total impact is the real metric.

**Separation of craft and metrics:**
Samir describes working to "navigate that emotion" of relative zero. The separation he's describing is between pride in the craft (did I make something good?) and the metric (did it perform?). These are not the same. Consistent craft, over time, is what builds the brand. Individual metric performance is highly variable.
