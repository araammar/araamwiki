# Concept: CEO Delegation Framework

## Summary / Definition
Dom Iacovone's operating philosophy for running multiple companies simultaneously: define the mission, hire proactive operators, let them lead, measure only the outcomes, and be available to course-correct — not to manage.

The central thesis: **a founder in the room makes everyone worse.** When the CEO is involved in everything, teams stop thinking, stop taking initiative, and stop making decisions. Everyone becomes a yes-person. The org becomes a full-time job for the CEO instead of a self-operating machine.

Dom: *"One of the issues with founders is they fuck their own team by trying to stay involved in everything."*

## Why It Matters
Most founders holding companies back are doing it to themselves. Their involvement becomes the ceiling. The path to running 7, 10, or 15 companies is learning to define the destination and get out of the way.

## How It Works / The Mechanism

**The CEO's actual job:**
1. Define the mission — what the brand exists to accomplish
2. Identify the 4 SGM initiatives for the year (see [[strategic-growth-model]])
3. Give the team the tools they need (budget, talent, clarity)
4. Track headline KPIs weekly (net revenue + CM1)
5. Be available to course-correct when something goes wrong
6. Keep your own schedule as light as possible — you need space to jump on fires

The CEO should **not**: manage the team's day-to-day, attend every meeting, weigh in on execution decisions, or hold on to any function.

**The 80/20 mistake rule:**
If the team is doing the right things 80% of the time, Dom is satisfied. The 20% margin is for mistakes — which is how teams learn. The only rule: don't make the same mistake twice.

*"You learn way more from fucking up than you do from not being able to do it at all."*

**Proactive vs. reactive:**
Dom wants zero reactive people in his organization. Reactive = identifying problems after they've already happened. Proactive = seeing problems coming and preventing them.

The C-suite should mirror the founder's entrepreneurial energy — they're just doing it for the company instead of their own company.

Dom's test for a good C-suite hire: do they ask questions, push back, argue? Healthy debates between the C-suite are valuable. Silent agreement is dangerous.

**Multi-company application:**
- Each company has its own CEO/GM who sets their own operating rhythm with their team
- Dom does not manage these CEOs' teams — he manages the CEOs
- Each CEO has their own SGM that Dom reviews in a weekly 1:1
- On Fridays (work from home): touch base with every portfolio company head
- If something goes wrong: Dom steps in to course-correct, not manage

**The key man trap:**
A company that can only operate with the founder present cannot be sold, scaled, or delegated. Build so that you can walk away for 3 months and the business still hits its targets.

**Hire for hunger vs. skill:**
- Under $100M: hire for hunger — people who will figure it out, who treat the company like their own
- Over $100M: hire for skill — you need ex-P&G, ex-Unilever operators who've solved these problems before

**First interview question:**
*"Where do you want to be in 5 years?"* — Dom asks this to filter out people holding the role as a placeholder. He won't train someone only to watch them leave when a better opportunity comes.

## What to Steal / Application
- **Gut-check yourself: are you attending meetings you don't need to be in?** If yes, those meetings will outlive your attendance — start stepping back.
- **"Elevate, delegate, get out the way, be there if they need you."** That's the formula.
- **Track outcomes, not activity.** Don't monitor what your team is doing. Monitor whether the KPIs are moving.
- **Let your team make small, non-fatal mistakes.** Your interventions are the ceiling on their development.
- **The overrated advice:** "You need to know everything happening in your company at all times." Dom calls this "absolute bullshit." Know what the results are. Let the team own the execution.
