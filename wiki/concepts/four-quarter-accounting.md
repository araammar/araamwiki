# Concept: Four Quarter Accounting

## Summary

Taylor Holiday's diagnostic framework for reading a DTC P&L. Break your revenue into four equal 25% buckets. The benchmark isn't a rule — it's a reference point that immediately shows you which quarter is bleeding profitability. Instead of saying "we're not profitable," you can say: "Our CAC is running at 40% instead of 25% — that's the problem."

---

## The Four Quarters

Idealized benchmark: each quarter = 25% of net revenue.

| Quarter | What It Is | What "Broken" Looks Like |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)** | Total paid media spend to acquire new customers | Running 35–40%+ — brand is buying revenue it can't afford |
| **2. Cost of Delivery (COD)** | COGS + fulfillment + shipping + payment processing | Running 40%+ — product margins are too thin at this price point |
| **3. Operating Expenses (OPEX)** | Fixed overhead: salaries, rent, tools, agency fees | Running 30%+ — team/infrastructure is too large for the revenue base |
| **4. Profit** | What remains | Negative or near-zero — no resilience, no reinvestment capacity |

---

## How to Apply It

**Step 1:** Calculate each quarter as a % of net revenue for the current period.

**Step 2:** Identify which quarter is over-indexed.

**Step 3:** Make decisions in that specific quarter — not everywhere at once.

**The diagnostic power:** Without this structure, "we need to cut costs" is noise. With it, you know whether to reduce ad spend, renegotiate supplier terms, or right-size the team. Different problems, completely different solutions.

---

## The Growth Formula

Four Quarter Accounting connects to Taylor Holiday's core growth formula:

```
(V × CR × CM) - VC = Profit
```

- **V** = Visitors
- **CR** = Conversion Rate
- **CM** = Cash Multiplier (60-day LTV ratio)
- **VC** = Variable Costs (COD + CAC)

The framework shows exactly which lever to pull when profit disappears.

---

## First-Order Profitability (2026 Emphasis)

The emerging standard: brands must be profitable on the **first purchase**, not relying on repurchase to achieve unit economics.

This is "the new law" for sustainable DTC in 2026 because:
- Capital is expensive — you can't float unprofitable acquisition waiting for LTV to materialize
- Rising CACs mean the old "lose money to acquire, win on retention" model requires more capital than most 7-figure brands have
- If your first-order contribution margin is negative, your entire model depends on retention that may not happen

---

## The Mistake This Prevents

Brands focus on top-line revenue metrics (MER, ROAS) because they're easy to measure and feel like performance. Four Quarter Accounting forces the question no one wants to ask: **after paying to deliver the product, are we actually keeping money?**

A brand with 4.0 ROAS can be losing money if COD is 50% and OPEX is 30%. The ratio says success; the P&L says disaster.
