# Concept: Franchise Business Model (Services)

## Summary / Definition
Dom Iacovone's Relive Health franchise arc — from a solo 600-square-foot hormone optimization clinic in Stuart, Florida to a licensed franchise with 40+ locations open and 250 licenses sold — illustrates the economics and mechanics of scaling a service business through franchising vs. company-owned expansion.

## Why It Matters
The franchise model compounds operational leverage in a way that direct expansion cannot: franchisees pay to open your concept, bear the capital risk, and operate the units, while you collect licensing fees + royalties and maintain brand standards. At scale, franchise multiples (~30x EBITDA) are dramatically higher than most CPG multiples.

The Relive model also demonstrates the **services-to-product flywheel**: a product brand (Revive vitamins) and a services brand (Relive clinics) serving the same consumer lifecycle creates a compounding trust relationship that is nearly impossible to replicate.

## How It Works / The Mechanism

**The origin story:**
- 2017: Dom opens first clinic in Stuart, FL. 600 sq ft, bet his entire life on it.
- Timing: early Facebook ads for healthcare/medicine — no one was doing it
- First location: ~$200K/month revenue at ~25% EBITDA from a 600 sq ft space
- Second location: Palm Beach, also successful
- First licensing: gave two friends from school the license to use the name for $1,500/month (no royalty structure — too early for formal franchise legality)

**The founder of Orange Theory moment:**
The Orange Theory founder (Dave Long) walked into Dom's Palm Beach location, liked the concept, had his finance team audit the numbers. They confirmed: $200K/month, 25% EBITDA, sub-$100K build-out cost. Compared to Orange Theory: $1M+ to open, $200K/year in net profit. Orange Theory's unit economics are dramatically worse.

*"He's like, 'There's no fucking way this place makes this much money.'"*

The deal: Orange Theory founder invested (small cash) for 30% equity in exchange for helping sell 100 franchise licenses within a year. Result: 250 licenses sold, 40+ open, 6 more opening in the next month.

**The franchise economics:**
- Build-out cost: sub-$100K (was; likely higher now with more build-out standards)
- Revenue per unit: $200K+/month
- EBITDA per unit: ~25%
- Franchise multiple: ~30x EBITDA (per Dom)
- Compare to: Orange Theory with $1M+ investment and ~$200K/year net

**The management structure:**
Dom's mom runs Relive Health's franchise operations. She went from cashier to SVP of Operations at BJ's Wholesale Club over her career — her operational DNA is exactly what franchise management requires: systems, standards, accountability across many locations.

**Why services scale differently than CPG:**
- CPG revenue ceiling often involves inventory, logistics, retailer relationships
- Franchise/services revenue is powered by operator networks
- Franchisees are motivated owners, not employees
- The parent company's job is brand standards, training, proprietary service protocols, marketing support
- Exit: a PE buyer or strategic (like a large healthcare company) would acquire Relive and the 250+ licensed territories represent enormous expansion capital already raised

**The "don't sell Revive without Relive" decision:**
Dom will not sell Revive MD independently from Relive Health. The two compound:
- Revive MD builds brand loyalty with consumers 18–35 (vitamins, supplements)
- When those consumers need HRT, hormone optimization, advanced regenerative therapies at 35+, Relive is already their trusted brand
- The consumer doesn't have to choose who to trust — Revive/Relive have already earned it

This is a rare example of a **consumer lifecycle brand strategy**: you own the customer from a $40 vitamin subscription to a $5,000/year health membership.

## What to Steal / Application
- **The services franchise model is dramatically underexplored by product founders.** The capital-light, high-multiple nature makes it worth exploring for anyone with a repeatable services concept.
- **Validate in a terrible location first.** Dom opened in Stuart, FL — a small market that shouldn't have worked. If it works there, it works anywhere.
- **Find a "Dave Long"** — an operational franchisor who will trade equity/cash for the ability to sell your licenses.
- **Couple a product brand to your service brand** if they serve the same consumer lifecycle. The trust flywheel is a structural moat.
- **Never split the flywheel brands.** If selling Relive, sell Revive with it. The combined value is greater than the sum of parts.
