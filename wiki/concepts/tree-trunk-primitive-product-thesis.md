# Concept: Tree Trunk Primitive Product Thesis

## Summary / Definition
A product selection framework from Silicon Valley systems thinking: always try to go as far "up the stack" as possible when choosing what to build. Prefer innovations that are "tree trunk primitives" — fundamental to what billions of people are already doing — over "leaf innovations" that are only valuable at the fringe of a niche.

Michael Brandt (HVMN) applied this CS/engineering mental model directly to supplement product selection: ketones are a metabolite every human body already makes and uses. That's a tree trunk. A new flavor of carbohydrate gel sachet is a leaf.

Named from the phrase: *"You're trying to introduce something that's a primitive — something that is fundamentally useful. You don't want to introduce something that is only valuable at the fringes."*

## Why It Matters
- Solves one of the hardest early-stage questions: is this product a real business or a niche hobby?
- Tree trunk products have inherently larger TAMs and stronger defensibility because they solve for a universal function, not a preference
- Leaf innovations can be commercially successful but are fragile: trend-dependent, copyable, limited ceiling
- Applies across industries: a new productivity app architecture (tree trunk) vs. a new productivity app theme (leaf)

## How It Works / The Mechanism
The framework asks two diagnostic questions:

**Question 1: How universal is the underlying function?**
- Tree trunk: the function is something 8 billion humans do continuously (metabolism, sleep, hydration, communication)
- Leaf: the function is a behavior only a subset of people have adopted (keto dieting, ultra-marathoning, cold plunge routines)

**Question 2: Does the product serve the function or just dress it up?**
- Tree trunk: the product makes the fundamental function work *better* in a new way (ketones as accessible fuel, caffeine as accessible stimulant)
- Leaf: the product is a variation or flavoring of an existing solution in the category

**The stack metaphor (CS framing):**
- Infrastructure layer (OS, database) → always tree trunk, highest value, hardest to displace
- Application layer → tree trunk or leaf depending on user base
- Feature layer → almost always leaf

In product/CPG terms: metabolic fuels → protein → specific supplements → specific product forms → flavors. Move as high up the stack as your innovation allows.

## What to Steal / Application
- **For product founders:** Before committing to a product, run the tree trunk test: "Is the underlying function something 8 billion people do, or something 800,000 people choose to do?" Both can be valid businesses, but only the tree trunk gets to claim the macro-trend tailwind.
- **For brand positioning:** Tree trunk products should be positioned against the universal function, not against competitors. Ketone-IQ isn't positioned against other supplements — it's positioned against the metabolic need every brain has.
- **For investors and fundraising:** The tree trunk framing is a powerful slide in a pitch deck — it explains why the TAM isn't niche even if the early customer looks niche (elite athletes, military)
- **Related to [[three-lever-product-decision-framework]]:** Both frameworks diagnose product opportunity, but three-lever focuses on competitive positioning, tree trunk focuses on TAM and universality
