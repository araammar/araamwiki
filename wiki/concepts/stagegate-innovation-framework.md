# Concept: Stagegate Innovation Framework

## Summary / Definition
A structured go/no-go evaluation process for all new product ideas before they are developed and launched. Dom Iacovone runs a dedicated "stagegate innovation meeting" weekly — any new product or line extension must pass through this gate before receiving resources.

The framework prevents the "new shiny object" problem: founders see a trend, rush a product, and discover it's either not profitable, not differentiated, or cannibalizes existing business.

## Why It Matters
In CPG, a product that fails in retail is often worse than not launching at all. A failed product:
- Consumes manufacturing, marketing, and logistics resources
- Can get you delisted or reduce your shelf presence with a retailer
- Damages brand credibility with buyers
- Burns CM1

The stagegate forces the question "should we make this?" before "how do we make this?"

## How It Works / The Mechanism

**The three stagegate criteria (Dom's version):**

**1. Can we innovate on this product?**
Is there genuine differentiation? Is the product better than what already exists? If there's no real innovation, competing against established products in a saturated category is nearly impossible.

Innovation can be:
- Superior formulation (better ingredients, better efficacy)
- Superior unit economics (making a cheaper product at the same quality)
- Unserved market segment (a gap no one is targeting)

*"If there's no innovation or there's no reason for the product to be made, it's going to be pretty tough to compete."*

**2. Is it margin-creative?**
Will this product make money at a price point the market will bear?

The formulation trap: Dom's early partner Matt would create world-class formulas that were priced out of the category. The product might be technically superior but if the MSRP is 40% above every competitor, consumers won't buy it — regardless of quality.

The test: map the competitive shelf landscape. What are competitors charging? Can this product be profitable at a competitive price point?

**3. Is the TAM (Total Addressable Market) big enough?**
Is there a large enough audience that warrants this product? A technically excellent, profitable product in a niche with 50,000 people in it may not be worth the operational complexity.

**The weekly meeting format:**
The stagegate meeting is one of Dom's three core weekly meeting types (alongside Monday finance and ops/SNOP). Any new product concept gets pitched in this meeting. Ideas that don't pass all three criteria get killed here — before any development spend.

## What to Steal / Application
- **Run a stagegate before any product launch, line extension, or format pivot.** No exceptions.
- **The three questions are the whole framework:** Can we innovate? Is it margin-creative? Is the TAM big enough?
- **Map competitor pricing before any development conversation.** Know the price ceiling for your category.
- **The "kill it in the meeting" discipline is the valuable part.** Most brands skip this and build products that should never have been built.
- **This applies beyond CPG:** any business with multiple potential initiatives (product lines, service offerings, content series) benefits from a formal kill/go process.
