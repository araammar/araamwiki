# Concept: Subscription AOV Architecture

tags: [DTC, subscription, AOV, retention, habitualization, pricing, unit-economics]

## Summary / Definition
A subscription tier design that uses a 3-month prepaid option to simultaneously increase AOV, improve logistics unit economics, drive habitualization, and improve LTV — while adding non-discount value (access to experts, experiences) to make the upgrade irresistible rather than just cheap.

Pioneered at I Am Ath by Danny Yeung: launched 3-month subscription, saw 50%+ of purchasers immediately select the 3-month tier, with global AOV jumping to $280 and "Beckham Stack" customers paying $500 upfront.

## Why It Matters
Most subscription brands default to a 1-month recurring model. The 3-month architecture solves the core retention problem for products that require sustained use to deliver results — and does so through structural design, not just marketing.

## How It Works / The Mechanism

### The Three-Tier Pricing Stack
```
One-time purchase:     $112 (US)
1-month subscription:  $89/month
3-month subscription:  $78/month → $235 paid upfront
Beckham Stack (3-mo):  $500 paid upfront
```

### Why the 3-Month Tier Wins (Multi-Stakeholder Logic)

**For the customer:**
- Lower per-month cost (savings signal)
- Access to expert advisory board Zoom sessions quarterly
- Ample runway for the product to actually work (90-day results data: 95% felt better energy)

**For the brand:**
- AOV increase: 1-month vs. 3-month = 2.6x revenue per transaction
- Logistics savings: single delivery vs. three deliveries per period
- Revenue recognition: as a public company (Prenetics/Nasdaq), recognizing 3 months of revenue upfront is structurally valuable
- Retention effect: 90-day commitment creates a daily habit loop before the renewal decision arrives

### The Habitualization Mechanic
Products that require sustained use face a "doesn't work" objection that is actually a "didn't use it long enough" problem. The 3-month subscription forces the usage window needed to deliver results.
- 30–90 days: the range where most users see noticeable change
- Routine formation: once something is in a daily routine for 30–60 days, churn psychology shifts from "should I stop?" to "I use this every day"
- The 3-month window is the minimum viable habit formation period for supplements (and likely most CPG health/wellness products)

### Non-Discount Value Stacking
The key innovation beyond the pricing structure: I Am Ath layered in *access* as the upgrade incentive, not just discount.

3-month subscribers get:
- Quarterly Zoom masterclasses with 9 scientific advisory board members
- Live Q&A with Dr. Don (Mayo Clinic), Bobby Rich (Beckham's trainer), and others
- "Golden ticket" experiences: 2 lucky winners flew to Dubai for backstage access to Aryna Sabalenka at Battle of the Sexes event

**Principle:** Digital access to humans who are otherwise inaccessible is a zero-marginal-cost upgrade mechanism. If you have premium humans in your network, giving subscribers access to them turns a price discount into a community membership.

### Results
- Launched in US; >50% of purchasers selected 3-month option within the first month
- Rolled out globally shortly after
- Global blended AOV: $280 (up significantly from pre-3-month baseline)
- Payback period maintained under 4 months despite AOV increase (higher per-transaction revenue offsets CAC pressure)

## What to Steal / Application
- If your product requires time to show results: design a subscription tier that gives the minimum viable usage window as a default, not an option
- Price the 3-month tier to be genuinely compelling (15–25% per-unit discount), not tokenistic
- Add non-discount value: identify the premium humans in your network and give access as the upgrade trigger
- For public companies or companies with investor reporting: 3-month prepaid subscriptions also help revenue recognition timing
- Frame the retention conversation internally as a design problem, not a marketing problem — you can't email your way out of a product that people quit before it works
