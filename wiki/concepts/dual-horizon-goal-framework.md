# Concept: Dual Horizon Goal Framework

## Summary / Definition
A goal-setting architecture that operates on two time horizons simultaneously — a near-term operational horizon (3 months) and a long-term aspirational horizon (10 years) — keeping both active and in relationship with each other at all times.

Michael Brandt (HVMN) articulates this as his core planning method: the 3-month horizon anchors daily and weekly execution; the 10-year horizon provides the "why" that makes the ABC work feel worthwhile. Neither replaces the other. The 3-month goal without the 10-year becomes a treadmill. The 10-year goal without the 3-month becomes fantasy.

## Why It Matters
- Solves the tension between urgency and patience that plagues founders — the 3-month horizon demands action, the 10-year horizon provides context for when to slow down
- Prevents "exit-seeking" mentality: when you only see the grind of the next 90 days without a compelling destination, you start looking for shortcuts or exits
- Connects individual tasks to the meaningful mission — visiting 10 Sprouts stores to say hello to a category manager only makes sense if you know the 10-year vision it serves
- Mirrors the marathoner's mindset: pace yourself in training (3-month), but know what race you're training for (10-year)

## How It Works / The Mechanism

**The 3-Month Horizon:**
- "What are we all doing next?" — the planning unit
- Must be concrete: specific launches, specific metrics, specific actions
- Example from Ketone-IQ: "In the next 3 months, visit 10 Sprouts stores, meet the category managers, build sell-through velocity"
- Keeps the team focused on the ABCs — the basic work that actually gets done
- 1 week is too short (not enough gets done). Decades are too long (paralyzing). 3 months is the productive planning unit.

**The 10-Year Horizon:**
- "How big can we go? How audacious can we be?"
- Must be inspiring enough to motivate the grind — ketones as 10% of global calories; making a dent in the metabolic health epidemic; people drinking ketones on Mars
- Doesn't need to be precise — it needs to be directionally exciting
- Functions as a "galaxy brain" sanity check: does what we're doing in Q1 believably track toward this?
- Also useful for investor and team recruitment: the story of where this goes

**The Bridging Question:**
*"Does what we're doing in the next 3 months believably stack toward the 10-year outcome?"*
This is the active integration between the two horizons. If the answer is no, either the 3-month work needs to change or the 10-year vision needs to be interrogated.

## What to Steal / Application
- **For founders:** Explicitly name both horizons in team planning documents. Don't leave either implicit. Teams need to see *both* — the concrete near-term and the inspiring long-term — in the same conversation.
- **For content creators:** The same framework applies to content strategy — "what am I publishing in the next 3 months" paired with "what do I want this platform to mean in 10 years?"
- **Related to [[strategic-growth-model]]:** Dom's annual planning framework operates similarly but within a 1-year window. The dual-horizon model extends the frame further.
- **Running analogy:** The 3-month goal is this training block. The 10-year goal is the running career you're building — the race you want to run at 50.
