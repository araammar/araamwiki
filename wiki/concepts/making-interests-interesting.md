# Concept: Making Your Interests Interesting

## Summary / Definition

The central creative puzzle every personal brand and writer must solve: how do you make what *you* find interesting accessible and compelling to people who have never been exposed to the sequence of ideas that made it interesting to you?

Most people either (a) assume their niche is boring and pursue a different niche they think people "want," or (b) dive deep into their niche without translating it for newcomers. Both are errors.

The insight: if you find something interesting, other people *can* find it interesting too. Your job is not to find people who already share your interests — it is to reverse-engineer the path by which you found something interesting and guide others through that path.

## Why It Matters

This reframes the entire "niche down vs. become the niche" debate. The reason Dan Koe argues for "becoming the niche" is that it forces you to solve the harder, more valuable problem: making your interests interesting to others. This is a skill. Once you have it, the content limitation disappears — you can write about anything that genuinely matters to you, and you can pull people into caring about it.

The product-market-fit mindset (find people who are already interested in what you do) is valuable but incomplete. It creates a cap on your market. The "create customers" approach (make people interested in what you do) is unlimited.

## How It Works / The Mechanism

**The reverse-engineering approach:**
1. Notice what made you interested in a topic
2. Map the sequence of ideas that led you from no awareness → curiosity → genuine engagement
3. Identify where your audience currently is in that sequence (usually: no awareness or early problem awareness)
4. Create content that starts where they are and guides them toward where you are
5. Translate the deep/esoteric insight into a palatable version for the medium (Twitter/Instagram ≠ newsletter ≠ long-form)

**The depth-layering principle:**
- Short-form social: introduce the topic at the accessible entry level
- Newsletter: start at the accessible level, bring beginners up to speed, then deliver the deep insight at the end
- The deep stuff at the end is what builds real trust — "providing the fast track for people understanding these very deep things"

**The esoteric-to-accessible translation:**
Dan Koe's specific strength: taking deep spiritual/philosophical ideas ("very wispy, very clever") that make sense to him and translating the *essence* of that idea into a more palatable version for social media. He knows 80-90% of his audience has never been exposed to the sequence of ideas that led him to see the idea as important. So he provides the compressed version of that sequence.

## The "Create Your Own Customers" Insight

The most underutilized creator insight: you don't need to find people who will pay you. You create people who will pay you. 

- Guide people from no awareness → problem awareness → solution awareness → you as the solution
- This process is: persuasion + content funnel + genuine value delivery
- Most creators try to find the already-aware audience; few try to build the awareness in the first place
- The creators who build awareness own a larger and more loyal audience because they *made* those people care

## Content Medium Calibration

Where you publish determines the appropriate translation depth:
- **Twitter/Instagram/TikTok:** Entry-level accessible — the hook that introduces the topic, the insight translated to its simplest powerful form
- **Newsletter:** Multi-depth — accessible intro, bring beginners along, deliver depth at the end for the curious
- **YouTube/long-form:** Full journey — you can take people through the entire sequence from no awareness to deep understanding

The mistake: posting Twitter-level depth in a newsletter (wastes the medium) or newsletter-level depth on Twitter (gets ignored by beginners).

## What to Steal / Application

- For any topic you want to write about: map the sequence of ideas that made it interesting to you. Your content is that sequence, compressed.
- The question "will anyone care about this?" is less useful than "how can I guide someone from not caring to caring about this?" The second is a creative problem with a solution; the first is an excuse to avoid the creative problem.
- Segment your depth by platform. Use short-form as the entry ramp. Use newsletters and long-form as the deep journey.
- The practice of curating ideas — noting great quotes, saving great posts — feeds this process. When you encounter an idea that made you think "ah, yes," that's the spark for creating content that produces the same reaction in your audience.
