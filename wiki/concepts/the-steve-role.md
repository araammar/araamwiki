# Concept: The Steve Role

## Summary
"The Steve" is a specific, assignable employee function: a non-founder team member whose job is to act as the brand's personal LinkedIn correspondent. They curate every piece of content the brand produces — across every channel — and distribute it through their own personal LinkedIn page, building a personal industry network that gives the brand reach and credibility no brand account could generate on its own. The name comes from a specific person who performed this role so effectively that industry contacts knew him before they knew the brand.

## What the Steve Role Does

The Steve is not a social media manager running the brand's page. They are a brand amplifier operating through a personal account, and the distinction matters enormously.

Core functions:

- **Curates all brand content across every channel** — consumer content, influencer deals, YouTube launches, product campaigns, street team photos, collabs, email campaigns, new locations — everything the brand does
- **Cross-posts that content to their personal LinkedIn** before or alongside the brand's own posting
- **Documents every brand milestone** — new retail accounts, partnership announcements, awards, campaign launches, company news
- **Tags the brand account** in every relevant post, triggering LinkedIn's notification system and activating the company cohort amplification effect (see [[linkedin-company-cohort-effect]])
- **Builds a personal industry network** through trade shows, direct connections, and genuine engagement — accumulating reach and trust that a brand page cannot replicate
- **Posts consumer content, not just business content** — the Steve makes the brand feel alive, exciting, and personality-driven, not just professional and formal

The result, described directly from the source: "People knew Steve before they even knew the brand. They knew about the stories that Steve told and about brand lore in a way that, even if you were on our email list or followed our social media, you probably wouldn't keep up with."

## Why Personal Pages Dramatically Outperform Brand Pages

LinkedIn's algorithm systematically deprioritizes brand page posts relative to personal account posts. The documented reach gap is approximately **13x** — a personal account posting the same content as a brand page generates roughly thirteen times more organic reach.

This gap exists because LinkedIn was designed around professional relationships between individuals. Personal accounts have connection networks, they receive notifications, and the algorithm treats their posts as social interactions. Brand page posts are treated as broadcasts and distributed accordingly.

The Steve Role is the structural solution to this problem. Instead of trying to make the brand page perform better, you route all brand content through a person — where it reaches 13x more people by default.

## The Cheerleader Function

Beyond distribution mechanics, the Steve serves a psychological and social proof function: they are visibly, consistently, publicly excited about the brand.

This matters for B2B audiences — retail buyers, distributors, investors, potential hires — who use LinkedIn to evaluate whether a brand has internal momentum and genuine team belief. A Steve posting enthusiastically and frequently about the brand signals organizational health. It reads as a company people want to work with, buy from, and partner with.

Cheerleading is not hype. It is documented momentum: real milestones, real content, real reactions from a real person who clearly believes in what the brand is building.

## Identifying Your Internal Steve

The Steve is not a specific job title. It is a personality type and behavior pattern that may already exist in your organization:

Signs someone might be your Steve:
- They are already sharing brand wins on their own LinkedIn unprompted
- They are active on LinkedIn and genuinely engaged with industry content
- They have a natural enthusiasm for documenting and sharing what the brand is doing
- They have built (or are building) a professional network in your industry or adjacent spaces
- They are not the founder — they have their own perspective and voice

The Steve can be in marketing, operations, community, sales, or any other function. The role is defined by behavior, not title.

## What to Do If No Natural Steve Exists

If no one on the team is naturally performing the Steve function, there are two paths:

1. **Assign it explicitly** — identify the team member most active on LinkedIn or most enthusiastic about the brand, explain the role and its value, and make it part of their responsibilities
2. **The founder defaults into it** — if no one is available or appropriate, the brand owner takes on the Steve function themselves. As Orin John says: "If there's no one around to do it, then it's probably you."

The Steve Role is not optional. It is the primary mechanism through which a brand generates LinkedIn reach without relying on an underperforming brand page.

## The Steve Role's Relationship to the Brand Account

The brand page is not the Steve's primary posting surface — their personal page is. However, the Steve's posts that tag the brand page serve a secondary function: they activate LinkedIn's team engagement signals.

When colleagues at the company engage with posts that tag the brand page, LinkedIn's algorithm treats this as a strong signal of organizational relevance and amplifies both the personal post and the brand page's visibility. The brand account's job is to be a hub that personal posts orbit and link to — not to originate content for reach on its own.

See [[linkedin-company-cohort-effect]] for the full internal team amplification mechanic.

## What to Steal
Every brand with more than one employee should ask: "Who is our Steve?" If the answer is no one, that is the first LinkedIn problem to solve — before strategy, cadence, or content type. Assign the role, explain the mechanism, and give that person explicit permission (and encouragement) to post all brand content from their personal page. The 13x reach gap means one active Steve outperforms a brand page by default, indefinitely.
