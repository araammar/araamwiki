# Concept: High Agency Development

## Summary / Definition

Agency is the ability to do things without permission. High agency is not a fixed personality trait — it is a developmental stage that can be reached through a specific process of ideological experimentation.

Low agency: following a single path someone else defined, unable or unwilling to break from it.
High agency: the ability to make any path work.

The path from low to high agency runs through deliberate exposure to multiple ideologies, dogmas, and approaches — not to find "the right one," but to extract the universal principles underneath them all and develop the confidence to create your own way.

## Why It Matters

In a world where AI can execute tasks and dispense advice, agency is the meta-skill. Anyone can get instructions from an LLM. The question is whether they can evaluate, adapt, combine, and ultimately author their own path with those instructions as raw material. High agency is what separates the orchestrator from the operator.

It's also the core distinction in Dan Koe's futureproof skill stack: the reason to pursue your own goals rather than assigned goals is that doing so builds agency. Assigned goals suppress it.

## How It Works / The Development Path

**Step 1: The Low-Agency Entry Point**
You start with low agency. This is normal. The best thing a low-agency person can do is take advice — specifically, advice they actually follow through on. Following advice teaches the *feeling* of having the agency of the person whose advice you followed. You live their path, briefly. This is apprenticeship as agency acquisition.

**Step 2: Ideological Sampling**
Rather than committing to one philosophy, business model, health approach, or belief system, deliberately sample multiple. Get sucked into the echo chamber. Feel the conviction. Then fall off. Move to the next.

Dan Koe's own arc: veganism, ancestral diet, IIFYM, sugar-only diet, various political frameworks, different business models. Each was a brief deep dive into someone else's agency — living their path long enough to understand it from the inside.

**Step 3: Pattern Extraction**
After enough ideological samples, you start to notice: "They're all kind of saying the same thing. The underlying principles of all of these are pretty sound." The principles beneath the surface differences are universal. Now you have them, regardless of which surface variation produced them.

**Step 4: Self-Authored Synthesis**
With enough patterns internalized, you develop confidence to make your own decisions — to create your own way that combines and adapts what you've extracted. This is the high-agency state: not commitment to one system, but the capacity to author your own.

**The failure mode: dogma lock-in**
Getting locked into one ideology or approach permanently is how you stay low agency. The ideology gives you a path — which is valuable at first — but it also prevents you from extracting the universal principles beneath it. The test: can you articulate the underlying principle separate from the specific system that taught it to you?

## Games as Agency Education

Reference from the book *Agency as Art*: games are an agentic art form. Music is audible art. Writing is written art. Games are agentic art — they present different paths, different choices, different available agencies, and let you live them. Playing games that show diverse agentic paths educates the player's sense of what's possible, similar to the ideological sampling process.

The practical application: if you want to build agency in a domain, find the equivalent of "games" in that domain — simulations, case studies, experiments, small bets — that let you live different paths quickly.

## The George Mack Test

The most high-agency person in your life: the friend you'd call if you just received news you were going to jail. Not because they're the most connected — because they're the most resourceful, quick on their feet, and oriented toward solving the problem regardless of what it is. Identify that person. Study how they think and respond to constraints.

## What to Steal / Application

- Don't commit to advice-seeking as permanent low agency. Use it as a training ground. Follow advice, extract the principle behind why it worked, add it to your own framework.
- Sample ideologies deliberately but with a defined exit. Give a business model, health philosophy, or creative framework a real trial — then assess.
- The signal that you're building agency: you can explain why advice works in your own terms, not just in the terms of the person who gave it.
- The signal that you're locked into low agency: you defend a system rather than its underlying principles; you can't adapt it when conditions change.
- Periodically ask: "Am I following this path because I chose it, or because someone else assigned it to me and I never questioned it?"
