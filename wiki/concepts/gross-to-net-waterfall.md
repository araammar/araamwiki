# Concept: Gross-to-Net Waterfall

## Summary / Definition
The gross-to-net waterfall is the gap between a CPG brand's gross revenue and the net revenue that actually appears on the P&L. It is made up entirely of deductions retailers take from invoices before paying — and it lives **above the P&L entirely**, making it invisible to founders who only read their financial statements.

Typical gross-to-net components:
- **Trade spend** (promotional allowances, co-op advertising)
- **Discounts** (rollbacks, markdown funding)
- **Rebates** (volume-based payments back to the retailer)
- **Deductions** (late delivery chargebacks, labeling penalties, shortage claims)
- **End cap fees** (paid placement for premium shelf positioning)

Dom's blended gross-to-net across all categories: **above 10%.** On $100M gross revenue, that's $10M that never appears on the P&L and most founders never think about.

## Why It Matters
The waterfall is the #1 leaky bucket in any CPG business. Most founders don't manage it because it doesn't appear on standard P&L reports. The money is gone before the P&L even starts.

At exit, this matters catastrophically: if your EBITDA multiple is 10–20x, every $1M of gross-to-net recovered is worth $10M–$20M in exit value.

Dom: *"Most people don't pay attention to it because it's in La La Land. When it comes time to actually look at the bottom line profitability, you're not looking up there to find more money. And that's where you can usually find the most money."*

## How It Works / The Mechanism

**The mechanism of loss:**
Every retailer contract includes provisions for deductions. Retailers self-deduct from invoices — they pay you less than invoiced and send you paperwork explaining what they took. Each deduction requires your team to dispute, verify, or accept.

At scale, this is a full-time finance function. Dom's dedicated person: an ex-P&G executive whose entire job is managing the gross-to-net waterfall across all channels. This person:
- Tracks every deduction against every retailer
- Manages trade spend by channel (BOGO at Publix, ISB event at Sam's Club, etc.)
- Builds the glide path from gross to net
- Identifies where deductions can be disputed or reduced

**Using trade spend strategically (vs. bleeding it):**
Dom's framework for disciplined trade spend:
- Trade spend should **not be a consistent percentage every month** — that's a sign it's not strategic
- Use it for **new customer acquisition only**: new year/new me, Black Friday, product launches
- Do it in **product** (BOGO) not money discounts where possible — this avoids training customers to expect % off
- Track uplift by retailer: if baseline sales go from $100K/month to $120K/month after a BOGO, you won
- Never discount repeatedly — you become a discount brand. Discounts erode brand equity.

## What to Steal / Application
- **Audit your gross-to-net before your next board meeting.** Ask your accountant to show you gross revenue vs. net revenue, and list every deduction category.
- **Hire a specialist** for this function once you hit retail at scale. An ex-CPG/P&G exec who has done this before will pay for themselves many times over.
- **Build a P&L by retailer and by channel.** This is how you see which trade spend is working.
- **BOGO > % off.** Product discounts protect your pricing integrity better than cash discounts.
- **Time your promotions** around known high-velocity windows (new year, Black Friday, seasonal). Don't run promos continuously.
