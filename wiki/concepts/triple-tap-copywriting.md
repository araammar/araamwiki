# Concept: Triple Tap Copywriting Framework
tags: [cold-email, copywriting, B2B, outbound, subject-line, CTA, deliverability]
source: DDGcd1JoJV0 — Lee Gjenj, Cold Email Masterclass 2025

## Summary / Definition

A three-stage cold email copy framework where each element must be cleared sequentially before the next becomes relevant. Tap 1 gets the email opened, Tap 2 gets it read, Tap 3 gets a response. Failing any tap means the sequence stops.

## Why It Matters

Most cold emails fail at Tap 1. They never get opened because the subject line signals "sales email." The Triple Tap forces you to engineer each gate independently instead of writing a generic email and hoping for the best.

## How It Works / The Mechanism

### Tap 1 — Open (Subject Line + Preview Text)

The only job of Tap 1 is to get the email opened. Nothing else.

**Rules:**
- Never signal the sale. If the recipient can tell it's a sales email from the subject line, they won't open it.
- Write for curiosity or relevance, not for selling.
- Preview text (the snippet that appears after the subject in the inbox) is part of Tap 1 — write it deliberately.
- Avoid spam trigger words: "FREE", "discount", "limited time", "act now", "guaranteed"
- Short subject lines typically outperform long ones
- First-name personalization alone doesn't move the needle anymore — it's expected

**The test:** If someone reads only your subject line, would they know you're pitching something? If yes, rewrite it.

---

### Tap 2 — Read (Email Body)

The job of Tap 2 is to make the body worth reading. If they opened it, they have 3–5 seconds of attention. Spend it entirely on the problem and your credibility.

**Rules:**
- 2–3 sentences maximum for the body. Shorter is nearly always better.
- State the problem they're experiencing. Make them feel seen.
- Add a credibility signal — one specific number, client name, or outcome. Not vague.
- No links in the initial email. Links trigger spam filters AND signal "sales email."
- No images or attachments in initial email. Same reasons.
- **Spin tax is mandatory:** Every sentence that will be repeated across mailboxes must have random word/phrase variations. ESPs detect identical patterns across sending infrastructure.
  - Spin tax format: `{word1|word2|word3}` — one chosen at random per send
  - Every sentence. Not just the key lines. Every sentence.
- No unsubscribe link language. Use soft opt-out: "Let me know if this isn't relevant and I'll leave you alone."
  - "Unsubscribe" keyword is a spam trigger — avoid entirely.

**CAN-SPAM:** Technically requires opt-out, but in practice unenforced for cold B2B email. Soft opt-out language protects you without triggering filters.

---

### Tap 3 — Act (CTA)

The job of Tap 3 is to extract the one action that progresses the conversation. The goal is a reply, not a booking.

**Rules:**
- Ask for a one-word or one-sentence reply, not a call booking
- Examples: "Worth a quick chat?" / "Relevant?" / "Interested?"
- Never ask someone to book a call in the first cold email — it creates friction and signals that you're selling
- The CTA should feel like a natural conversational next step, not a transactional demand
- "Yes/no" questions perform well — they're low commitment
- Make it effortless to say yes and almost as effortless to say no (soft opt-out)

---

### The Warm Email Unlock

Once a prospect replies — even just to ask a question — they have signaled interest and the rules change:

- You can now send links
- You can now send images and videos
- You can use sales language
- You can pitch your offer directly
- They've become a warm lead

**UTM tracking:** Add `utm_source=inst` (or similar) to every calendar/website link shared post-reply. This lets you pixel them for retargeting if they click.

---

### Spin Tax — Deeper Mechanics

Spin tax prevents email service providers from flagging your emails as mass marketing:

```
Example without spin tax:
"I help SaaS companies reduce churn by 30%."

Example with spin tax:
"{I|We|Our team} {help|assist|work with} SaaS {companies|businesses|founders} {reduce|lower|cut} {churn|cancellations} by {30%|around 30%|roughly a third}."
```

Each mailbox receives a slightly different version, making the pattern undetectable. This is not optional for anyone sending at volume.

## What to Steal / Application

- Use the Tap test on every cold email: Does Tap 1 signal selling? Rewrite. Is Tap 2 longer than 3 sentences? Cut it. Does Tap 3 ask for more than a reply? Simplify.
- Run spin tax on every phrase that will repeat across campaigns
- Save your best performing subject lines as templates — but always spin-tax them before scaling
- Once someone replies, switch registers entirely — warmer, more conversational, direct about the offer
- Pair with [[reverse-lead-magnets]] for the most powerful reply-to-conversion stack
