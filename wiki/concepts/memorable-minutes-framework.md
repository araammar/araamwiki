# Concept: Memorable Minutes Framework

## Summary / Definition

Instead of measuring content success by views, measure it by memorable minutes — the number of minutes of content experience that people will actually remember and reference later. Memorable minutes are the output of brand-building. They compound into loyalty, referrals, and pricing power. They justify investments that don't optimize for view counts but that create lasting relationships — like live events, live shows, and deep long-form content.

Samir Chaudry: "We interchanged the term views with minutes. We're like, okay, how do we produce more memorable minutes for people?"

---

## Why It Matters

Views are a lagging indicator of short-term attention, not long-term relationship. The news cycle is a perfect case study: headlines from last week are already forgotten, despite generating millions of impressions. The consumption velocity of the attention economy means most content — even content that performs well by view metrics — is forgotten within 48 hours.

Memorable minutes is a different quality of output. These are the minutes people reference in conversation, that change how they think, that they describe to others. "I watched that episode and it completely changed my business." Those are memorable minutes. One hour of memorable content creates more brand equity than a hundred hours of forgettable content.

---

## How It Works / The Mechanism

**Memorable minutes are designed, not accidental.**

Certain content formats inherently generate more memorable minutes per dollar spent:

**Long-form depth:**
A two-hour podcast conversation that genuinely changes how someone thinks about their business generates more memorable minutes than ten two-minute clips that get more views but are forgotten by dinner. Samir noticed this when Colin and Samir's Chinese micro-dramas episode tanked on YouTube but did 1.4 million downloads on Spotify and was one of their best-ever Spotify episodes. Different distribution, different audience — the minutes were memorable to that audience.

**Live events:**
Standing in a room with 500 like-minded people, experiencing surprise guests, participating in structured conversations — these generate memorable minutes at a density that digital content almost never achieves. Colin and Samir's Press Publish NYC event, with Casey Neistat, Richard Branson, and Steven Bartlett as surprise guests, created memories that attendees would reference for years. That's the product of events: concentrated memorable minutes.

Design of memorable events:
- The onstage programming should function as conversational prompts for attendees (not passive one-to-many programming)
- Structured networking > unstructured networking: give people interaction frameworks (questions on coffee cups, learning groups identified by emblems)
- Surprise guests create shared memorable moments and conversational prompts among strangers ("Who do you think the next surprise guest is?")
- Ask attendees in advance what success looks like — almost universally, "meeting someone new" — and design the event to optimize for that outcome

**IRL experiences:**
Any physical touchpoint — merchandise, events, pop-ups, tours — generates memorable minutes that digital content can't. These are the moments people reflect on at year's end.

**The 70% retention benchmark as a memorable minutes proxy:**
On YouTube, 70% retention at 30 seconds means most people who clicked are still engaged. But even after those 30 seconds, only the videos that generate genuinely memorable content will be referenced later. CTR and retention tell you about immediate attention. Memorable minutes tell you about lasting impact.

## The Event Model

Colin and Samir invested in events specifically to produce memorable minutes at scale. Their insight: "As it gets harder and harder for creators to build things that people remember because we just consume so much in the day, experiences matter."

Their Press Publish NYC format:
- Application-based (paid $400 to apply, answered 13 questions) — designed to ensure a quality audience, not just a large one
- Asked in the application: "What makes this event a 10 out of 10 for you?" — answer was overwhelmingly "meeting someone new"
- Coffee with Creators: coffee cups with interview questions, so every person had a structured way to approach someone else
- Three announced surprise guests, with the final reveal at the last session — sustained open question loop throughout the day
- Partnership with Virgin and YouTube — on-brand associations that extend the brand's identity

Learning: too much mainstage programming is passive. Future iterations would reduce mainstage time and increase structured small-group interaction.

## Scaling the Unscalable

Memorable minutes often come from the least scalable activities: personal conversations, small events, intimate shows, touring. The temptation in media is to optimize for distribution reach (views, impressions). But the asset that compounds isn't reach — it's depth.

Samir: "If it's unscalable, if it's doing events, if it's going on tour... do that. That's cooler. That's memorable. That leaves people with a great experience that they'll reflect on the year and be like, 'That one was cool.' Scale the unscalable."

This directly echoes Gary Vaynerchuk's "scale the unscalable" principle — but grounded in the specific mechanism of why it works: memorable minutes compound into brand equity in a way that scalable reach never does.

---

## What to Steal / Application

**Replace view count with memorable minute quality:**
After every piece of content, ask: "Would someone reference this in a conversation in three months?" That's the memorable minutes test. Optimize for yes.

**Invest in events earlier than feels comfortable:**
Events feel expensive and operationally heavy. But the memorable minutes density per dollar often exceeds any digital content investment. One memorable event can do more for brand loyalty than 100 forgettable videos.

**Ask your audience what success looks like before designing the experience:**
This applies to events, episodes, and product launches. Colin and Samir asked 800 applicants what a 10/10 event looked like — the insight shaped the entire design. Don't assume. Ask.

**Design conversational prompts into your events and content:**
People remember experiences where they felt agency. Give them interaction structures — questions to ask, challenges to complete, groups to find. Don't design for passive reception.
