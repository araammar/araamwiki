# Concept: Retail Sequencing Strategy

## Summary / Definition
The strategic sequence for entering retail channels: the order matters enormously. Going to club or mass retail before building brand validity will destroy your margins, relationships, and potentially your business. Dom Iacovone's framework establishes a clear progression that mirrors how brand credibility is built and how retailer relationships compound.

The rule that overrides everything: **sell-out is harder than sell-in.** Anyone can get a meeting and make a pitch. The only thing that matters is whether the product moves off the shelf once it's there.

## Why It Matters
Retailers have "hurdle rates" — minimum velocity thresholds products must hit on a daily/weekly basis to stay on shelf. Miss the hurdle rate and the retailer forces you into price rollbacks and promotions that destroy your CM1. Miss repeatedly and you get delisted. Once delisted from Walmart, you rarely get a second chance.

## How It Works / The Mechanism

**The correct sequence for CPG (Dom's framework):**

**Phase 1: E-commerce (TikTok + Amazon + DTC)**
- Build brand before touching retail
- Prove velocity, generate social proof, build email list
- See: [[tiktok-to-amazon-to-retail-distribution-model]]

**Phase 2: Specialty Retail**
- Vitamin Shoppe, GNC
- Smaller footprint, easier to manage
- Better margins, premium price points, larger serving sizes
- Primary function: *learning how to manage a retailer* — trade spend, forecasting, buyer relationships, compliance
- If you can't manage a Vitamin Shoppe account, Walmart will eat you alive

**Phase 3: FDM (Food, Drug, Mass)**
- Publix, HEB, Winn-Dixie, regional grocery, CVS
- Lower MSRP than specialty
- Requires price pack architecture to not cannibalize specialty retail
- Here you're learning how to compete for shelf velocity against established brands

**Phase 4: National Mass (Walmart, Target)**
- Huge volume, thin margins
- Buyers are sophisticated, have seen thousands of brands, and are not excited by celebrities or trends anymore
- What retail buyers want to see: TikTok velocity data, DTC revenue, Amazon ranking, sell-through rates from specialty and FDM
- The Walmart buyer's frame: "What's your incremental value to us?" (driving their consumer in, not just competing for existing shelf traffic)

**Phase 5: Club (Sam's / Costco)**
- ~15% retailer margin vs. 40–50% at specialty — but enormous volume
- Cannot be your first retail move — club doesn't build a brand
- Goes in after brand is established; club shoppers are "buy in bulk" customers, not brand discoverers

**The self-in / sell-out distinction:**
Every founder focuses on getting into retailers. The only metric that matters after that is sell-through.

Dom on what drives sell-through in retail (in order):
1. **Product quality** — a bad product fails regardless of brand or celebrity
2. **Shelf packaging** — must communicate the product at a glance; in retail you're competing against a hundred products without the brand context your DTC customer has
3. **Brand** — drives repeat purchases and loyalty

**The "stay in your lane" principle at retail:**
Each retailer has a different shopper profile. The CPG that succeeds across retailers understands that the Costco shopper is not the Target shopper, who is not the Vitamin Shoppe shopper. You need price pack architecture (different SKUs, sizes, price points) to serve each retail environment without cannibalizing others.

**Relationship management:**
Dom's competitive advantage with buyers: he asks "what can we do for you?" at every meeting. Most brands only take. He is among the few who ask what the buyer needs.

*"I've never seen someone besides myself at the end of a meeting say, 'What can we do for you?'"*

Building a partner reputation with buyers compounds over time. When you become someone a buyer wants to do business with, doors open faster, second chances happen, and exclusivity conversations become possible (see: Onside's exclusive with Target).

## What to Steal / Application
- **Don't rush into retail.** Dom: *"You have to have a really big brand, a lot of brand validity before you jump that ship."*
- **Specialty retail is your training ground.** Learn retailer management before entering FDM or mass.
- **Show your DTC/TikTok/Amazon velocity to retail buyers.** This is your proof of demand.
- **Never build SKUs to satisfy a buyer's request.** Dom's biggest mistake: building Walmart-specific SKUs at their buyer's request. The products failed. Hold the brand. A buyer who gets you kicked out of your own positioning is not your partner.
- **Ask buyers "what can we do for you?"** It is rare and it is remembered.
- **Protect margin at each stage.** Club's 15% margin is fine if you have volume; catastrophic if you're still trying to build brand.
