# Concept: Position-Against Strategy — Building Identity Around Displacing an Incumbent

## Summary / Definition

A position-against strategy is a brand building approach where the entire identity is organized around being the better alternative to a specific, dominant competitor. Unlike generic differentiation, position-against puts the competitor at the center of the brand narrative — "we exist because they exist, and because they have a flaw we don't." When executed well, the incumbent's market presence becomes free advertising for the challenger: every purchase of the incumbent is a proof point for why you're needed.

---

## Why It Works

Strong market incumbents carry two liabilities challengers can exploit:
1. **Legacy UX** — they were optimized for earlier consumer expectations and are structurally unable to change without disrupting their core business
2. **Over-indexing on marketing over product** — incumbents often win on distribution and awareness rather than product quality, leaving a product gap the challenger can occupy

The position-against brand doesn't need to win on every dimension — just the one the incumbent has abandoned. When that gap is real and the consumer cares about it, the brand name and messaging write themselves.

## Case Studies

### Grüns vs. AG1
**The setup:** AG1 is a $100M+ supplement brand that built its business almost entirely on creator marketing and podcast sponsorships. The product works but the user experience has always been a pain point — taste, texture, price.

**Grüns' position-against play:**
- "We are the AG1 alternative for people who want a real user experience"
- The enormous AG1 TAM means Grüns doesn't need to steal customers — they just need to capture the significant percentage who tried AG1 and found it lacking, or who are aware of it and want something better
- TAM expansion: AG1 never successfully targeted children. Grüns explicitly positioned as appropriate for kids — creating an entry point that ages up to the adult consumer over time
- The position-against frame is built into the brand DNA from launch

Orin's assessment: "AG1 is a big honking business that has marketed its way to success and they're like hey guess what the user experience still sucks — we are going to hammer that and they will probably never copy us or if they do it'll be too late."

### Poppy vs. Coca-Cola / Pepsi
**The setup:** The carbonated soft drink category is dominated by two players whose core products are increasingly out of alignment with health-conscious consumer values.

**Poppy's position-against play:**
- "We are the soda you can actually drink" — real ingredients, gut health benefits, lower sugar
- The incumbent's market share confirms the TAM; Poppy just needs to capture the consumers who want something different
- Price-to-shelf placement benefits: retail buyers understand the category and can slot Poppy as the better-for-you alternative without needing to explain the category from scratch

Orin's read: "In CPG, if you don't take a massive swing like that, the volume isn't there to ever make any real money."

### David vs. Every Protein Bar
**The setup:** The protein bar category had a decade of products optimizing taste over nutrition.

**David's unassailable metric position:**
- "We have better macros than any bar on the market and we will put the metrics on trucks in New York City where anyone can challenge them"
- The position is not against one incumbent — it's against the entire category's trade-off
- The David Michelangelo branding then layered a premium aesthetic identity on top of the metric advantage

This is a variant of position-against: **position against the category's accepted trade-off** rather than against one specific competitor.

## Three Positioning Types (Orin's Taxonomy)

**1. Position Against One Competitor**
Identity is defined by explicit comparison to a specific dominant player. Messaging constantly implies "vs. [incumbent]." Works when: the incumbent has a known and acknowledged weakness, your product genuinely solves that weakness, and the incumbent is well-known enough that consumers already carry the comparison in their heads.

**2. Unassailable Metric Positioning**
Find one measurable dimension where your product is objectively, verifiably superior and build the entire brand around that claim. The metric itself becomes the marketing. David's calories and protein grams on trucks. The claim is so specific it's either true or it isn't — which is what makes it credible.

**3. Name-as-Strategy (Cultural Reference Capture)**
Building a brand around a term or concept already resonating culturally. Example: Satoshi Nakamoto (fashion brand) — took the crypto founder's name, associated with a global phenomenon, and built an ultra-premium fashion brand on the name alone. "The only reason that brand succeeds is because of the brand name." The name carries an entire ideological ecosystem without the brand having to build it.

## When Position-Against Works vs. Fails

**Works when:**
- The incumbent's flaw is real, felt by consumers, and not just competitive spin
- You have genuine product superiority on the specific dimension you claim
- The incumbent is large enough that their presence validates the category
- Your challenger story is emotionally compelling, not just factually superior
- You can sustain the position as the incumbent eventually responds

**Fails when:**
- Your product advantage is marginal or contested (commodity differentiation)
- The incumbent can easily copy your position without damaging their business
- The market is in an early stage where there is no recognized incumbent to position against
- You are positioning against a competitor's marketing without having product substance to back it

---

## The Price Game Trap

A common mistake in saturated markets (especially health and wellness): positioning on price. Undercutting the incumbent by 10% in a market where you don't have economies of scale is "a very dangerous game." Price is the last lever to pull, not the first.

From Orin: "If you're a new company and you don't have economies of scale, it's a very dangerous game. If you're getting into a super-saturated space, you need to really differentiate yourself through some unique position."

The alternative is exactly the position-against framework — own a dimension (user experience, specific outcome, aesthetic, ingredient philosophy) that the incumbent cannot copy without restructuring their business.

---

## What to Steal / Application

1. **Audit the incumbent's flaw** — talk to their customers, read their negative reviews, identify what they optimize around that leaves something unfulfilled
2. **Test if it's real** — the flaw has to be experienced, not just analytical; if customers have to be convinced it's a problem, it's not a strong enough position
3. **Pick one dimension** — not three; one thing the incumbent got wrong and you got right
4. **Make the comparison implicit, not explicit** — you don't need to name the competitor; the positioning does the work
5. **Build the product first** — the product has to be genuinely better on the dimension you claim; marketing a position-against brand with an inferior product destroys trust faster than any other scenario
