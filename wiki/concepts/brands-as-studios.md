# Concept: Brands as Studios of the Future

## Summary / Definition

Major brands have the opportunity — and arguably the obligation — to become the content studios of the next decade. They have massive budgets, trusted relationships with consumers, and recognizable IP (their brand identity, characters, and values). The brands that figure out how to make genuine YouTube shows — not corporate marketing content, but shows people actually want to watch — will own media real estate that no ad spend can replicate.

Samir Chaudry: "I think major brands have the opportunity to be the studios of the future. The biggest brands in the world — the Walmarts, the Nikes, the Adidas — are doing no branded content. They are movie studios, right?"

---

## Why It Matters

The media landscape is fragmenting away from traditional advertising at an accelerating rate. Ad blockers, subscription streaming, algorithm-driven feeds, and AI-customizable content are all reducing the reach and effectiveness of traditional paid media. At the same time, YouTube is demonstrably beating Netflix in total watch time.

Brands that understand this are building permanent media assets. Red Bull built a media company and now owns cultural relevance with its target audience in a way that no amount of traditional advertising could achieve. Most brands haven't.

The white space argument: the world's most valuable brands are spending enormous media budgets on traditional advertising while YouTube channels sit mostly empty. The opportunity to plant a flag in owned media — shows, series, consistent content that people seek out — is massive and largely unclaimed.

---

## How It Works / The Mechanism

**The Red Bull model:**
Red Bull is the canonical proof of concept. They understood media leverage early — Red Bull TV, a magazine, events — and never confused their content with their advertising. Their YouTube channel makes shows about extreme sports that people actively seek out and watch. The show is about the lifestyle and the audience; the brand is associated but not the subject.

The result: when you think about extreme sports, Red Bull is the first brand that comes to mind. Not because of an ad, but because they've been the media company for that world for decades.

**What makes brand content fail:**
Most brand YouTube channels are "corporate dumping grounds" — press releases reformatted as videos, product announcements filmed awkwardly, behind-the-scenes content with no story structure. This is 2006 television thinking applied to 2026 internet.

The failure pattern:
1. Brand decides to "do YouTube"
2. Goes through an RFP process with large agencies
3. Takes 6 months to build a "strategy"
4. Produces pristine, high-production content that looks expensive and feels cold
5. Gets no views because the audience has no reason to seek it out
6. Abandons the effort and goes back to traditional advertising

**The show-first thinking:**
Every YouTube channel is a show. The question is: what's the show I'm making? Not "how do we leverage our brand on YouTube" but "what show does our brand's audience actually want to watch, and that only makes sense for our brand to make?"

McDonald's could make a show about the drive-through. The drive-through is McDonald's-owned territory — nobody else can authentically make a show about the McDonald's drive-through. The cultural life of the drive-through, the confessionals, the rituals — that's inherently interesting and inherently McDonald's. Samir: "Drive-through confessionals — seeing what people order in a drive-thru is inherently interesting."

Door Dash could make a show you watch while waiting for your delivery. The format serves the context of use — you're already waiting, already in the product experience, primed to watch something.

**Brand trust as studio trust:**
Brands have something most new content studios don't have: established trust. "Our loyalty and our characters that we exist with on a day-to-day basis are brands. They already have so much trust with us." A show from a brand the audience already trusts starts with a warmer audience relationship than a show from an unknown studio. That trust is leverage if brands choose to activate it through genuine content.

**Scripted content as the next frontier:**
YouTube is currently dominated by unscripted content — vlogs, podcasts, tutorials, documentaries. Scripted content (narrative series, animated shows, scripted comedy) is rare. The creators who are pioneering scripted YouTube — and the brands that follow — have an enormous first-mover advantage before the format matures.

## Why Major Brands Haven't Done This Yet

Three barriers:
1. **Agency infrastructure:** Marketing spend flows through agencies that have optimized for TV and paid social placement, not for show development and creator-native production. The incentive structures reward big media buys, not patient content builds.
2. **Fear of low-fi:** Brands are afraid that a YouTube show will look cheap and reflect poorly on the brand. The inversion is true: authentic, native content performs better on YouTube than high-production corporate content. The fear of imperfection is what makes brands produce worse content, not better.
3. **Wrong success metrics:** Brands evaluate content by impressions and reach instead of watch time, returning viewers, and community formation. The right metrics for a brand show are YouTube's three rules metrics, not advertising CPM metrics.

## What to Steal / Application

**For brands:**
Ask "what show does only our brand have the permission and authenticity to make?" That's your brief. Not "how do we show up on YouTube" but "what would people watch that only we could make?"

**Start small with MVP logic:**
Use the YouTube-as-MVP-platform framework ([[youtube-as-mv-platform]]). Don't invest $5M in a show. Invest $100K in a 5-episode pilot. Let the audience tell you if you have something. Scale from there.

**Think like a show, not like a campaign:**
A YouTube show is a series commitment, not a campaign. The brand needs to think weekly/monthly production cadence, not quarterly campaign cycles. This is an operational shift, not just a creative one.

**For creators and agencies:**
The opportunity to position yourself as the creator who helps brands build shows on YouTube — with creator-native instincts, real audience understanding, and channel expertise — is one of the most defensible positions in the next 5 years as brand media budgets shift toward YouTube.
