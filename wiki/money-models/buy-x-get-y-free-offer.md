# Attraction Offer #4: Buy X Get Y Free
tags: [money-models, Hormozi, attraction-offer, bundle, Boot Factory]

## Summary / Definition

> *"In Buy X Get Y Free Offers, when customers buy something, they get other stuff free. The more free stuff they get, and the higher its value, the better it works."*

— Alex Hormozi, *$100M Money Models*

The play that turns discounts into the more powerful frame of *free*. Used to triple effective per-customer revenue without changing the value proposition.

---

## The Boot Factory Story (Nashville 2020)

Downtown Nashville — Bars and shops went in and out of business, but Boot Factory dominated.

> *"Their neon sign cut through the visual clutter on the street like a hot knife through butter. A cowboy boot bigger than my car — directing me to the front door."*

The sign:

> **BUY 1 PAIR GET TWO PAIR FREE**

A decade earlier as a kid, Hormozi thought it was dumb: *"How could they give away so much stuff and stay in business?"*

This time, with offer-design experience, he ran the math:
- Marked-down "final offer": **$600 for the pair**
- Normal boots tripled in price *because* of the two free pairs

> *"They charged three times the price for a single pair of boots because they came with two more pairs. So rather than saying 'come to Boot Factory and buy boots at a fair price' they managed to create a free offer!"*

The setting amplified it: cowboy bars + bachelorette parties + tourists who'd only buy once = perfect Buy X Get Y Free target.

## Free Offers Beat Discount Offers

> *"Free offers get way more attention than Discount Offers. But if you only have one thing to sell, and you give it away, you go hungry. In situations like this, businesses tend to lean on discounts. They run 'sales' relying on holidays, seasonal changes, or whatever, as reasons to temporarily lower prices and get more customers."*

> *"But, by selling more than one thing at once, you can turn Discount Offers into even stronger Free Offers."*

The arithmetic that explains the play:

### Option A — 3 Shirts At Discounted Price
3 × $10 = $30 total, but discounted to ~$20 (33% off)
3 × $6.67 = $20

### Option B — Buy X Get Y Free Framing
Same $20 total. But framed as:
"Buy 1 shirt for $20, get 2 free"

**Same total. Massively different perception. Way more interest.**

## Examples

### Buy 1 Get 2 Free (Boot Factory Pattern)
- One Pair of Boots: $200
- Offer: Buy One Pair For $600, Get Two Pairs Free
- End Result: Three pairs × $200 each = $600 (same outcome, much stronger frame)

### 3 Versions of "18 Months of Services"

| Version | Phrasing | Price |
|---|---|---|
| Good | Buy 12 Get 6 Free | $1,800 |
| Better | Buy 9 Get 9 Free | $1,800 |
| **Best** | **Buy 6 Get 12 Free** | **$1,800** |

Same price. Same total service. But the third option is most compelling because **the most "free" stuff.**

> *"Everyone pays the same price for the same amount of service. But, the third option is the most compelling. (Hint: It has the most free stuff!)"*

## Important Notes

### Buy X Get Y Free Means More Money And More Value

> *"It used to take a whole year for some of my service businesses to make their money. But the 'Buy 6 Months Get 6 Months Free' offer attracted far more customers than the original month-to-month offer. Even better, they got paid up front for it!"*

### Raise Prices Before Giving Stuff Away

> *"If you use this to attract customers, it will work. And since it will work, you need to make money. So, permanently raise prices to accommodate the discount. Don't lie. Actually raise your prices."*

Plus: some people will still pay the higher a la carte price, **breaking your limiting beliefs around pricing.**

### More Free Than Paid Wins

> *"Buy ten get two free is not as strong as buy two get ten free. This seems obvious, but again, people don't do it. To make it work better, give more free than you ask people to buy."*

`Buy 1 Get 2` beats `Buy 2 Get 1` even when the math is identical.

### Free Things Can Be Different From Paid

> *"When people first start doing offers like this, they match the free and paid stuff. But you can mix and match whatever you want."*

Example: socks have a $10 value. They buy a shirt for $10 + get $20 of free socks. Often a better deal than buying 2 shirts.

### More Cheaper Free Things > Fewer Expensive Free Things

> *"Let's say I could only afford to give one shirt away for free, but for the same cost I could give them three pairs of socks."*

Test "Buy 1 Shirt Get 1 Shirt Free" vs "Buy 1 Shirt Get 3 Socks Free." Same cost to you, but the second frame has more "free things."

### Rather Than 33% Off, Try Buy 1 Get 2 Free

> *"Free drives more interest than a discount. More people know the value of free than the value of one shirt."*

Test 1 shirt @ $20 + 2 free vs. 3 shirts @ $6.67 each (33% off).

### Don't Do This If You Can't Manage Money

> *"While Buy X Get Y Free offers create massive cash flow for a business, you need to deliver. So if you get a whole year's worth of payments in a month, make sure you can deliver for the whole year."*

> *"Don't be a goon and buy yourself a house with the cash meant for servicing your customers."*

### Existing Customers Will Take It Too

> *"If you have a recurring business already, and need cash fast, you can make this offer to existing customers. Many will happily 'Buy Ten and Get Two Free' even at their current price. Just limit the offer to 10% of your customers."*

Gives you a cash pop without destroying long-term recurring revenue.

### Prepaid Customers Keep Buying

> *"A lot of people don't want to make more offers to customers who prepay for stuff. This is a mistake. Speaking from experience, these are people who spend the most money."*

Their wallets have been "refreshed" with new money since the original purchase. Make more offers.

### If Customers Only Buy Once, Make Them Buy Big

> *"The Boot Factory in my story catered to tourists that want to fit in the local cowboy bars. This means most of their customers made one purchase. Ever. For that reason, it makes sense to make that purchase as large as possible."*

---

## Summary Points

- Customers buy something, get other stuff free
- Works for stuff customers want more of or longer access to
- Reframes pricing — same total cost, but "free" wins attention
- Always give more free than paid
- Pair different free items with paid items
- Can lengthen customer stay duration (Buy 2 Get 2 Free = 4 months stay)
- Make this offer to existing recurring customers for fast cash
- Keep selling to prepaid customers — they're the highest-value customers

## Application For Caret/Colandrum

For an annual SaaS subscription pricing strategy:

**"Buy 6 Months, Get 6 Months Free" Annual Plan**
- Effective price: $X/mo annualized
- Frame: pay for 6 months, get 6 free
- Stack with [[continuity-bonus-offer|Continuity Bonus]]: exclusive playbook library only available to annual subscribers

For a one-time pitch consult product:

**"Buy 1 Strategy Sprint, Get 2 Follow-Up Workshops Free"**
- $5,000 strategy sprint
- + 2 follow-up workshops normally $2,500 each
- Total perceived value: $10,000 for $5,000
