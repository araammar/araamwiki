## Summary / Definition

> *"A Money Model is a sequence of offers. At their core, we find every opportunity to solve a customer's problem . . . and then offer to solve it. For that reason, Money Models tend to have many offers in a specific order."*

— Alex Hormozi, *$100M Money Models*

If [[grand-slam-offer]] is the answer to *"what should I sell?"*, and [[leads-master-hub]] is the answer to *"who do I sell it to?"*, then $100M Money Models answers **"how do I get them to buy it?"**

> *"In a few pages, I took you from sleeping on the floor to crossing $100,000,000 in net worth. So the natural question is . . . how? Answer: by making more money from customers than it costs to get them. And that's what this book $100M Money Models is all about."*

## The Storage Unit Mentor Origin Story

The book opens with Hormozi sleeping in a concrete utility closet next to his first gym. A storage-unit owner across the street invited him to breakfast at 5 AM.

> *"How much time you got left to live?"*
> *"Huh?"*
> *"How much cash you got saved up?"*
> *"About five grand."*
> *"How much time does that give you?"*
> *"About a month."*

Then the mentor asked the question that would change Hormozi's understanding of business forever:

> *"How much does a free month of storage cost?"*
> *"Uh, nothing?"*

At the storage facility he walked Hormozi through their Money Model:

- Free month of storage attracts customers
- But you need a **lock** → $47 (only Master locks fit)
- You need **boxes**, tape, labels, markers
- You need **moving help** → affiliate kickback from local mover
- You need **insurance** → $500 free, $100,000 for +$10/mo
- And everyone always rents **the wrong size** → upsell one size up
- "Free month" actually costs the customer **$127**

> *"And when you figure out how to make money in one business, and I mean really figure it out, you see ways to make money in any business."*

The kicker: the mentor owned **27 locations.** Hormozi felt two inches tall and never forgot the lesson.

## The Rental Car Example (Money Models Made Concrete)

December 2019. Hormozi rents a $19/day car. The agent runs a 5-offer Money Model:

1. **Vehicle Upgrade** (truck for big guy)
2. **Late Return** (flexible checkout)
3. **Premium Insurance** (no worries about damage)
4. **Minimum Insurance Downsell** (he refuses premium)
5. **Prepaid Gas** ($3.75/gal vs $3.50/gal — he says yes anyway)

> *"I came for a $19/day car and I left paying $100/day. A 5x difference!"*

The agent solved problems he didn't know he had:
- "Big man in small car" → upgrade
- "Worried about scratches" → insurance
- "Late checkout" → flexibility
- "Risk of missing flight" → prepaid gas

> *"As a result, my $19 rental became a $100 rental. I paid more money faster. And now, we can see why the car rental industry brings in billions in the United States alone . . . per month."*

This is what a complete Money Model looks like in execution.

## The Book's Promise

> *"With a $100M Money Model, you make so much money in the first thirty days that the cost of getting more customers will never be a problem again. With so many customers, you'll be forced to work on everything else in your business to just keep up!"*

The unit is **30-day profit per customer**. A $100M Money Model recovers full CAC + delivery cost + extra cash for the *next* customer's acquisition — all inside the first 30 days.

> *"I like to cover my costs of getting a customer within 30 days. Main reason: any business can get interest-free money for 30 days in the form of a credit card."*

This is the operational expansion of [[client-financed-acquisition|Client Financed Acquisition]] from $100M Leads.

## The Bad Money Model Death Spiral

Hormozi outlines what happens when CAC > 30-day profit:

1. Spend money to get customers
2. At end of month, realize you spent more than you made
3. Cut back on advertising
4. Get fewer customers (can't afford them)
5. Cut advertising altogether
6. Float on personal cash, loans, credit
7. Sell percentages of the business to keep lights on
8. Wait months/years to make money back
9. Fall further behind
10. **Lose it all**

> *"But it doesn't have to be that way. There's plenty of money. You just have to go get it."*

The slow-drip-of-profits model only works if you already have lots of cash (large companies + VC-funded companies). For bootstrapped businesses, you need to recover CAC fast.

## The 3 Stages Of A Money Model

The book teaches Money Models as evolution, not assembly:

| Stage | Goal | Offer Type |
|---|---|---|
| **Stage I: Get Cash** | Attract customers, cover costs | Attraction Offers |
| **Stage II: Get More Cash** | Maximize 30-day profit | Upsells + Downsells |
| **Stage III: Get The Most Cash** | Maximize lifetime value | Continuity Offers |

> *"My Money Models develop this way because I make sure each stage pays for the next. We keep improving each stage until it gets reliable."*

Don't build the whole Money Model at once. Build stage by stage. See [[make-your-money-model]] for the full sequence.

## The Four Types Of Offers

Every Money Model combines four offer types in deliberate order:

1. **Attraction Offers** — turn strangers into customers
2. **Upsell Offers** — get them to spend more cash
3. **Downsell Offers** — get them to say yes when they would have said no
4. **Continuity Offers** — keep them buying

> *"You can use one, two, multiples of one, or all four together. You can combine them however you want. But, when I look at my most profitable businesses, I used all four."*

See [[four-offer-types-framework]] for the structural model and 7 ground rules.

## The Famous "Level-10 Skill In A Level-2 Opportunity" Phone Call

When Hormozi had 6 gyms and paid $25,000 for an hour with a famous marketer, he walked the marketer through his "open gyms at full capacity day one" system:

- Put down $3,000 for lease
- Run ads for a few days
- Sell in the empty building
- 20 leads/day at $5/lead
- 50% show, 25% close
- $600 program + $80 supplement profit per customer
- $1 in → $34 out in 48 hours
- $20K/mo membership rolling in for $3K down

The marketer's verdict:

> *"You shouldn't be running gyms. You should be showing other gym owners how to do what you just showed me."*
>
> *"You have a level-10 skill in a level-2 opportunity."*

That phone call → Gym Launch → Prestige Labs → ALAN → $46.2M exit.

## Where To Start

If you're brand new to Money Models:
1. Read [[four-offer-types-framework]] next
2. Then work through [[attraction-offers-overview]] and pick one play
3. Use [[make-your-money-model]] as the build sequence

If you have a business already:
1. Map your current offers to the 4 quadrants
2. Identify gaps (most businesses are missing 2-3 of the 4 types)
3. Add one offer at a time per the stage sequence

If you're applying to Caret/Colandrum:
1. The book's natural application is Stage I + II + IV (Attraction + Upsell + Continuity)
2. Decoy Offer is the highest-fit attraction play for B2B SaaS
3. Anchor Upsell + Continuity Bonus is the highest-fit close mechanism
