# Section: Attraction Offers — Turn Strangers Into Customers
tags: [money-models, Hormozi, attraction-offers, overview]

## Summary / Definition

The first of the four offer types. Attraction Offers generate leads and convert them into customers.

> *"They turn advertising into money by offering something free or at a discount. We do this because everyone wants a great deal. In a great deal, customers get far more value than the price they pay. Strangers can only take your word on the value. But, they absolutely understand the price. For that reason, discounts make anything a great deal to just about anyone. And, the greater the discount, the better the deal. The greatest discount of all being free."*

— Alex Hormozi, *$100M Money Models*

---

## The Free / Discount / $1 Continuum

Hormozi treats these as one mechanic with different intensity:

> *"Any time I say 'free' you can also use 'discount' or '$1.' Any time I use 'discount' you can also use 'free' or '$1' and so on. They exist on a continuum because they all discount a product to some level — even if you discount it by 100%!"*

This is the substrate underneath every Attraction Offer. The play type just changes how the discount is *framed*.

---

## How You Make Money Offering Free Stuff

> *"Think about it this way — people look for one thing and then buy another by accident all the time. Attraction Offers get them to do it on purpose. But what's a better deal than free stuff? More and better free stuff. One free thing is awesome. Two free things are awesomer. And, maybe to get those two free things, they have to buy one. That's how we make money on free stuff."*

The mechanic in plain language: the lead enters because of the free/discounted thing, and the actual money is made on what they buy alongside it (Upsells / Downsells / Continuity).

---

## What They Have In Common

All five plays share a few structural features:

1. **Headline-grade "free" or "discount"** advertising hook
2. **Conditional terms** that filter for serious leads
3. **An adjacent upsell or continuity** that captures the actual revenue
4. **Built-in social proof** mechanism (case studies, testimonials, before/afters, referrals)

The play type changes the framing, but the underlying purpose is identical: get attention at low risk to the lead, then upgrade them once they've engaged.

---

## How To Pick Which Attraction Offer To Use

Hormozi's implicit decision tree:

| If your business is... | Try |
|---|---|
| Effort-dependent (customer must do work) | Win Your Money Back |
| Needs lots of leads quickly | Giveaways |
| Has clear premium/basic versions | Decoy Offer |
| Multi-unit physical product or longer service term | Buy X Get Y Free |
| Digital + measurable result + short delivery | Pay Less Now or Pay More Later |

Many businesses run multiple Attraction Offers simultaneously. Hormozi explicitly recommends rotating them in his portfolio companies.

## Application For Caret/Colandrum

The 5 plays mapped to a B2B SaaS context:

1. **Win Your Money Back** — *"If your pitch win-rate doesn't increase by X% in 30 days, full refund"*
2. **Giveaway** — Free 1-year Colandrum subscription + 1-on-1 strategy call as Grand Prize, $X-off vouchers for the partial-scholarship runners-up
3. **Decoy Offer** — Free DIY playbook vs. Premium done-with-you Colandrum + pitch coaching
4. **Buy X Get Y Free** — Buy 6 months get 6 months free (annual prepay)
5. **Pay Less Now or Pay More Later** — Card on file for free trial; conditional auto-bill if usage threshold met

The Decoy Offer is probably the highest fit because B2B SaaS naturally has a basic-vs-premium spectrum.
