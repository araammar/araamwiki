# Upsell #2: The Menu Upsell System
tags: [money-models, Hormozi, upsell, menu, unselling, prescription, A-B]

## Summary / Definition

A 4-component upselling system that took Hormozi **10 years to learn**.

> *"In a Menu Upsell, you tell customers which options they don't need. Then, tell them what they do need, their preferences, and how to get their value from it."*

— Alex Hormozi, *$100M Money Models*

The 4 components:
1. **Unsell** — tell them what they don't need
2. **Prescribe** — tell them what they do need
3. **A/B** — ask their preferences
4. **Card On File** — make buying frictionless

> *"This took me ten years to learn. May you get the same value in ten minutes."*

---

## The 3-Story Arc (How Hormozi Discovered It)

### Story 1: A/B Upsell Discovery (December 2013)

Selling supplements at his gym. 19 nutrition consults in a row, nobody bought. The 20th was a "diamond ring" client — *"If I can't sell her, I should just quit trying."*

He forgot his script and just asked:

> *"You've got a protein shake for breakfast, do you like chocolate or vanilla?"*

She asked his favorite. He said chocolate. She bought chocolate. He moved on:

> *"Do you want kiwi or strawberry lemonade pre-workout?"*

Two products sold without ever asking "do you want this?" — just choosing between options.

> *"I sold the next twenty customers in a row. At the end of the day, I stared at my empty shelf in disbelief. I know how to sell supplements."*

**Takeaway**: Ask which product they prefer rather than whether they want a product at all. Also — *"Wanna just use the card we have on file?"*

### Story 2: Prescription Upselling (August 2014)

A relentless lady asked for detailed instructions: how many, when, what time, what if, what if. To save time he wrote step-by-step instructions on scratch paper.

Next appointment overheard and asked for the same. He wrote instructions directly on the order form. Without thinking, he said:

> *"I got all your instructions here, do you want to just use the card on file?"*

> *"Yea that's fine."*

She bought all the products without him asking yes/no on any of them. **He told her, and she did it. Like magic.**

> *"My 30-day profits skyrocketed."*

**Takeaway**: Personalized prescriptive instructions outsold general suggestions.

### Story 3: Unselling Discovery (November 2016)

By now Hormozi was selling 40-50 supplement consults a day on the road. One day he ran out of stock. The next customer asked for what the previous bought. He responded:

> *"Sorry, I just ran out. But honestly, you can get something close at the shop down the street for about $20 less. It's not as good, but it'll do OK for the first month. Cool?"*

> *"Thank you so much for helping me out."*

She was grateful. So he kept going:

> *"This other thing, same story. Again, not as good, but it'll get you through the first month."*

She was happier. He started crossing things off her sheet she didn't need:

> *"You're not trying to gain weight, right?"*
> *"Oh god no!"*
> *"Okay great. You won't be needing this," crossing out the weight-gainer.*

She loved it and bought without hesitation.

> *"I went out of my way to cross out what she didn't need. And this built enough goodwill to upsell what she did. Later, I kept products just to cross them out!"*

**Takeaway**: Unselling builds goodwill that converts upsells.

## The 4 Components Explained

### 1. Unsell — What They Don't Need

> *"You unsell by telling customers what they don't need so that you can emphasize what they do."*

Instead of asking if they want a product (yes/no), explain what they *don't* need to get them excited about what they *do* need.

When some options work best, cross out the rest. Builds enormous goodwill.

### 2. Prescription Upsell — What They Do Need

> *"We tell them what they do need. Prescription Upsells work well when offering a choice is inconvenient and you have only one thing that solves the problem."*

Two important components:
- **Explain how it integrates with offers they already bought**
- **Personalize how to maximize value** (this much, this time, this way)

> *"Instead of asking if they want to buy it or not, you explain how to use it as if they already have. Again, we remove the option of not buying to lower the chance they don't buy."*

### 3. A/B Upsell — Preferences Not Yes/No

> *"A/B Upsells work for multiple offers that solve the same problem."*

Instead of "do you want this?" → "would you prefer A or B?"

> *"Basically, when you give people the option to not buy, some don't buy. So, I give the option to pick between buying two similar things."*

### 4. Card On File

> *"Do you want to use the card on file?"*

Instead of asking if they want to pay, refer to ways they already have.

> *"This gets more people to buy because it lowers the 'hidden costs' of buying. Picking which card to use. Taking it out. Being reminded of ugly buying decisions in the past."*

For first purchases: *"What card do you wanna use?"*

## Worked Examples

### Massage Therapist
- **Unsell**: We have a lymphatic massage available, but you're not pregnant or just out of surgery right? So we can cross that out.
- **Prescribe**: Since your shoulder hurts we'll heat you up first, then hit your trigger points, and after that, we'll do some dynamic stretches.
- **A/B**: So would you rather do it before work or on the way home?
- **Card On File**: Wanna just use the card on file?

### Dog Food
- **Unsell**: You're not gonna need this small bag or this puppy stuff — you've got a big dog! You don't need these vitamins either because the food already contains them.
- **Prescribe**: You're also gonna wanna give your dog one of these joint chews at each meal. And every 90 days, give them one of these wafers for heartworms. Also, make sure to bring him back next month. Let's get that booked now.
- **A/B**: So does your dog prefer beef or chicken flavor?
- **Card On File**: Wanna just use the card on file?

### Digital Product
- **Unsell**: You don't need all eight courses yet. You just need to solve X, Y, & Z. Tell ya what. I'll send you some free stuff that'll solve problems X and Y. Then, you'll just need one course for problem Z.
- **Prescribe**: To solve Z, you're definitely gonna wanna do the course this particular way. Can you put an hour a day towards it? Okay — great. This will prevent any other Z problems cropping up later.
- **A/B**: Would you rather have direct message or phone support? Okay great. And would you like to start today or Monday?
- **Card On File**: Awesome. Wanna just use the card on file?

## Important Notes

### Make Anything A/B Sellable

> *"You can turn anything into an A/B offer."*

Examples:
- Quantity (one bottle or two?)
- Start dates (tomorrow or Monday?)
- Payment preference (cash or card?)
- Flavors / sizes / colors / materials
- Time slots (morning or afternoon?)
- Media (read or listen?)
- Delivery (standard or overnight?)
- Personnel (John or Sara?)
- Communication (call or text?)

### Add A Nudge On A/B Offers

If customers have limited experience:
- "This is my favorite"
- "X is usually a safe bet"
- "A lot of people love this"
- "Tuesday's sessions are a little smaller if you like that"
- "Amy does great with high schoolers"

(Hint: if you want to move a particular product faster, nudge that one more.)

### If You've Sold Out, Take Payment And Delay Delivery

> *"Later I learned I could just sell them stuff, order it, and set the expectation of when it will arrive. This allowed me to sell way more selection because I didn't have to carry inventory."*

### Employees Love Unselling

> *"Employees often like helping customers 'game the system.' Let them. Encourage employees to help customers game the system on purpose."*

Insider knowledge becomes the value-creation engine.

## The Economist Play (When You Want Customers To Buy Both)

In the late 1990s, The Economist offered:
- A — Digital Subscription: $59/year
- B — Digital + Print Subscription: $125/year

Print sales plummeted as customers flocked to the cheaper option.

The fix: add a decoy at the same price as the bundle:
- A — Digital: $59/year
- B — Print only: $125/year
- **C — Digital + Print: $125/year**

Customers now took C — getting "both for the price of one."

> *"Present three options. Option A, Option B, and Option C (Both) . . . but you make the price of (C) the same as the more expensive option (B). So long as you price the options to preserve your margins, you make the customer's choice easy and sell both."*

---

## Summary Points

- Menu Upsells work when you have multiple offers available
- 4 components: Unsell + Prescribe + A/B + Card On File
- Unselling lower-margin stuff incentivizes higher-margin upsells
- Encourage employees to unsell and "game the system" on purpose
- Nudge new customers toward what makes sense
- Use The Economist play (3 options, decoy at same price as bundle) when you want both

## Application For Caret/Colandrum

Onboarding upsell flow for an agency:

- **Unsell**: "You don't need the Enterprise tier — you're 5 people. And our white-label add-on won't fit how you operate. So we can skip those."
- **Prescribe**: "For your size, you're definitely going to want the Growth tier + the Pitch Coach add-on. Run 2 pitches per week through the audit, attend the weekly coaching call, and you'll see results in 30 days."
- **A/B**: "Do you want monthly or quarterly billing?"
- **Card On File**: "We have your card on file from onboarding — wanna just use that?"
