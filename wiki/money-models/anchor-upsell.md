# Upsell #3: The Anchor Upsell
tags: [money-models, Hormozi, upsell, anchor, premium, gasp]

## Summary / Definition

> *"With Anchor Upsells, you offer premium stuff first. If the customer gasps, you offer a cheaper but acceptable alternative."*

— Alex Hormozi, *$100M Money Models*

The play where a $16,000 anchor makes a $2,200 main offer feel like a steal — and gets some customers to buy the $16,000 anyway.

---

## The $16,000 Suit Story (2016)

Hormozi had spent five years in sweats and tank tops. A friend told him he should look professional. The friend knew a suit shop owner and called ahead.

Hormozi budgeted $500 — a big purchase at the time.

The owner took measurements and grabbed a suit off the rack. Hormozi tried it on:

> *"I felt cool. Like a rich guy. It was nice."*

Then he flipped the price tag:

> *"$16,000. My face turned red. All I could think was my friend asked the owner to make time for me and I can't even afford anything here. I felt horrified."*

The owner caught the blush:

> *"Do you care about the designer much?"*
> *"Not at all."*

The owner whirled and draped a second suit over Hormozi's shoulders:

> *"Try this on for size."*

Looked good. Price tag: **$2,200**.

> *"It wasn't $500. But it wasn't $16,000 either. Sigh of relief."*

Hormozi bought the $2,200 suit + $300 of accessories.

> *"After seeing the $16,000 price tag on the first suit, everything seemed cheap. I spent five times more than I had budgeted and felt OK about it."*

Later he realized: **the owner used a Price Anchor.**

---

## How It Works (5 Steps)

> *"Basically, if you present your main offer, some people will buy it. Duh. But, if you present a premium version that's 5x-10x the price first, lots of people will say no. Then, when you present your main offer, it looks like a much better deal."*

Two amazing bonuses:
1. **Anchored customers spend more than they normally would**
2. **Some customers still buy the super expensive thing**

### The Steps:

1. **Present the Anchor** — the really expensive thing
2. **Get "The Gasp"** — expect the customer to freak out about the cost
3. **Come to the rescue** — ask if they care about what makes it premium
4. **Present your main offer** — expect relief and "better deal" reaction
5. **Ask how they wanna pay** — Which card do you prefer?

---

## The Most Expensive Lesson Hormozi Learned

> **Pro Tip: The Only Thing Worse Than Making A $1,000 Offer To A Person With A $100 Budget . . . is Making A $100 Offer To Someone With A $1,000 Budget.**

> *"In the first situation you lose $100. In the second, you lose $900. I've lost tons of customers and mountains of cash because customers wanted more than I had to offer."*

The implication: always have a premium offer ready, even if most won't take it.

> *"Only a handful of customers buy them, but that handful of customers bring big profits. So always have premium offers even if most people don't buy. Remember, you won't lose customers by offering premium stuff first, but you WILL lose money if you don't."*

## Examples

### Local Service: Lawn Care
- **Premium Anchor**: Get my cell phone number + fancy mulch + natural pest control + bi-weekly yard maintenance — **$1,000/week**
- **Main Offer**: Get my team's number + generic mulch + normal pest control + bi-weekly yard maintenance — **$200/week**

### Physical Product: A Painting
- **Premium Anchor**: Super protective packaging + 20-year insurance + gift wrapped = **$1,000**
- **Main Offer**: Normal packaging + 1-year insurance + sticker = **$200**

### Digital Product: Newsletter
- **Premium Anchor**: All previous issues + new issues + 24hrs early access = **$199/mo**
- **Main Offer**: New issues only + on time = **$19/mo**

## Important Notes

### Don't Treat The Anchor Like A Fake

> *"Some people hear about this technique. Try it. Gloss over the premium offer. And then say it doesn't work. But if you do that, then the person never really considered it because you never really offered it."*

For this to work, you need to **actually sell it** and they have to actually consider it. Only after they pause, hesitate, or ask for something else do you move to the main offer.

### Make A Premium Offer You Actually Want People To Buy

A friend struggled with this. Hormozi listened to one call and identified the issue: the friend made up a BS premium he didn't want anyone to actually take.

> *"We tweaked the offer to something he would actually feel happy to deliver if someone paid . . . and they did. Tripling his profits."*

If you present like you want it taken, **some will buy.**

### "The Gasp" Is The Tell

> *"When you do an anchor upsell correctly, customers will have mini panic attacks. I call this 'The Gasp.' Gasps used to really stress me out. But then I realized something huge. The bigger the gasp, the more they bought."*

### Have The Rescue Ready

In the suit story, the owner already had the second suit pulled before Hormozi reacted. **He knew Hormozi would probably gasp.**

> *"If your customers don't gasp, then they probably find your premium offer reasonable . . . so just ask them if they wanna use the card on file (ha! Go for it!). Just don't do a gasp of your own when they say yes."*

### Primary vs Secondary Features

> *"To Get More People To Buy Your Main Offer, Make It A Better Deal. Only tweak a few features from your premium offer to make your main offer."*

- **Primary features** — keep the same (everyone wants these)
- **Secondary features** — change these (fewer people care)

Most people just want a suit. A few want a fancy suit. The suit is primary; the material/designer is secondary. After anchoring, offering the primary features for **1/5 the price** makes the main offer a steal.

---

## Summary Points

- Present more expensive offer first → more people buy the cheaper one
- Premium should be 5-10× more expensive than main
- Sequence: Present anchor → Get gasp → Come to rescue → Present main offer → Ask for payment
- Anchored customers spend more than planned
- Don't treat the anchor like a fake — sell it sincerely
- Some customers WILL buy the premium (outsized profits with fewer sales)
- Main offer keeps primary features, drops secondary features
- "Basically the same thing" for way less = irresistible

---

## Why This Connects To Decoy Offer

Note the relationship to [[decoy-offer]]:

- **Decoy** = cheap thing advertised → premium offered → customer takes premium
- **Anchor** = expensive thing offered → gasp → cheaper thing offered → customer takes cheaper (or takes expensive anyway)

Both are decoy structures. The difference is what's marketed vs. what's quietly held. Either play can drive the same final outcome from different angles.

## Application For Caret/Colandrum

For B2B SaaS upsell during a sales call:

- **Premium Anchor**: "$50K annual White-Glove tier: dedicated CSM, custom integrations, monthly strategic reviews with our founder, unlimited pitch coaching"
- **Gasp expected**
- **Rescue**: "Do you need the dedicated CSM and monthly founder calls?"
- **Main Offer**: "$10K annual Growth tier: full platform + group coaching + monthly office hours"

Some will gasp and take Growth. Some will stretch and take White-Glove. Either way, you've captured significant ACV.
