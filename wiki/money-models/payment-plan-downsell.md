# Downsell #1: Payment Plan Downsell
tags: [money-models, Hormozi, downsell, payment-plan, ProfitWell]

## Summary / Definition

> *"Payment Plan Downsells spread the cost of a product by charging some of it up front and putting the rest into scheduled payments."*

— Alex Hormozi, *$100M Money Models*

The play that made Hormozi's first sale. Has earned him tens of millions. Still uses it today.

---

## The First Sale Story (August 2013)

Hormozi's first real month in business. One month's rent in savings. He'd never gotten a stranger to give him money.

> *"I had nightmares about going back home a failure. The idea was unbearable. I got desperate."*

A lead walked in. Hormozi pitched. She said *"I can't afford it."*

In desperation:

> *"OK, when do you get paid?"*
> *"The first."*
> *"Okay, just put half down now, and half when you get paid."*
> *"I can't afford that either."*
> *"Okay — do you really wanna do this program?"*
> *"Yeah, I do."*
> *"What if you do three payments and just put a third down today?"*
> *"I still can't do it."*
> *"Hmm... What can you do?"*
> *"Honestly, nothing. But I can pay for the whole thing on the first."*

Rent was due on the fifth. The first was perfect.

> *"Sounds good. Just give me your card and I'll charge it on the second."*

Two weeks later. Card ran. **First payment plan — a success.**

## The Gamble Structure

> *"Payment plans are a gamble because they can make money in one way, but they can lose money in two."*

| Outcome | Effect |
|---|---|
| Get more customers AND they complete payments | **Money** |
| Customers who would've paid in full take payment plan | **Less money** |
| Customers cancel before you turn a profit | **Lose money** |

This chapter maximizes the win and minimizes the loss.

---

## The Underlying Insight

> *"Many people reject offers because they 'cost too much.' Sometimes true. But in response to this, business owners and other sales professionals will immediately discount or sell cheaper stuff just to get people to say yes."*

> *"However, a huge percentage of the time 'it costs too much' really means 'this costs too much up front.'"*

Payment plans **get the best of both worlds**:
- More buyers because customers pay less in the moment
- Boost profits because customers still pay full price over time

---

## The 7-Step Process

Hormozi's full process makes up to 9 offers. He stops when they buy.

### Step 1: Reward For Paying In Full Rather Than Punish For Paying Over Time

Don't say: *"It's $10 if you get it right now, but it's $15 if you pay over time because we charge $5 in interest."*

Say: *"It's $15 . . . but it's $10 if you prepay it. You save $5 . . . that's what most people do."*

Same math. But the second framing benefits from a price anchor and friendly tone.

### Step 2: Offer 3rd-Party Financing, Credit Card, Layaway

**3rd-Party Financing**: Another company pays you now, customer pays them over time. Car dealers' standard play.

**Credit Card**:
> *"Would you rather I decide your payment terms or you decide?"*

They prefer to decide. Tell them to use a credit card. **You get paid today, they pay the credit card company over time.**

**Layaway**: Customer pays off before getting the product. As many installments as they want.

### Step 3: Offer Half Now, Half Later

Ask: *"When's the next time you get paid?"*

Then: *"Wanna just put half down today and the rest down when you get paid?"*

If no: *"What's the most you can put down today?"*

Schedule payments off paychecks (most people get paid every two weeks).

### Step 4: Check To See If They Still Want The Thing

> *"No payment plan will satisfy a customer who doesn't want the thing."*

Script:
> *"Got it. So money is tight right now. Real quick. I want to make sure. On a scale from 1-10 how bad do you wanna do this?"*

- 8+: *"Awesome. Don't worry. We're gonna figure out a way to make this happen for you."*
- 7 or below: *"Why not a 10?"* Then: *"You're right. I think we may have something that could be a better fit for you."* Pivot to [[feature-downsell|Feature Downsell]].

### Step 5: Offer To Split Into Three Payments

If 8-10: One-third now, one-third on the next two paychecks (or monthly).

### Step 6: Offer Evenly Spread Payments

If still no: Evenly spread payments over the rest of the service term.

(Gym Launch was 16 weeks → 16 weekly payments.)

### Step 7: Offer A Free Trial

If still no: [[trial-with-penalty-downsell|Trial With Penalty]]. The sale ends here (at least for now).

## Seesaw Downselling (Simplified Version)

For fewer steps or less experienced salespeople:

Start with:
> *"Would you rather have giant monthly payments or tiny ones?"*

They say tiny. Then:
> *"It normally costs XXX. And if you prepay it today, you get a huge discount and zero monthly payments. That work?"*

This frames the payment plan as the *negative* and highlights prepayment benefits.

If they can't afford it:
> *"If you can't afford it up front, I totally get it. We'll simply adjust the down payment until you get a monthly rate you like."*

This incentivizes bigger down payments. If still no, ask if they still want the product. If yes, pull your chair to their side of the table and walk through options together. *"The sale becomes a team effort."*

## Important Notes

### Payment Plans Have Built-In Upsells

> *"Make periodic offers for the original paid-in-full-discount during their payment plan. If they pay off the balance, they can still get the original 'prepay discount.'"*

Customers forget they have this option. When you remind them, some take it.

> *"Give your sales guys the same bonus to close the balance to incentivize follow up."*

### Align Payment Schedules With Paychecks

> *"If you charge on days people get paid, they have a higher chance of paying. Also, people's paychecks get deposited at different times, so if at first it gets declined, run it a few times that day. I learned this strategy from John (my early mentor). I often recoup a third of my declined payments by adding this little process."*

### How To Make Sure Payment Plans Make You Money

> *"After implementing payment plans, your close rate should increase. Duh. But, if the number of paid-in-fulls goes down, you have a problem. You've just put people who would've paid in full on payment plans!"*

> *"So, you want to close more appointments overall but with the same percentage of appointments paying in full."*

Worked example:
- 10 leads → 3 sales (all full price)
- After payment plan downsell: 10 leads → 3 sales at full price + 3 sales on payment plans = **6 total**
- Up-front cash from the first 3 + payment from the second 3

### Another Reason To Start High Before Working Down — ProfitWell Data

Hormozi cites ProfitWell data from 14,000 subscription businesses:

| Billing Cadence | Monthly Cancellation Rate |
|---|---|
| Monthly (12×/year) | **10.7%** |
| Quarterly (4×/year) | **5%** |
| Annual (1×/year) | **2%** |

> *"I already present pricing in order of most cash up front to least. It just so happens this also makes customers more valuable over the long term. So start high (fewer bigger payments) and work your way down."*

**Annual billing has 5× lower churn than monthly billing.** Massive implication for SaaS pricing.

## Summary Points

- Same product, same total price, different payment timing
- Get more buyers like discounts, but full revenue preserved
- Only grows business if customers actually pay
- Step 1: Present at full price, offer discount for paying in full
- Step 2: 3rd-party financing → credit card → layaway
- Step 3: Half down, half on next paycheck
- Step 4: 1-10 want-it check (8+ continue, 7 or less feature downsell)
- Step 5: Three payments
- Step 6: Equal payments over the service term
- Step 7: Trial with penalty
- Seesaw downselling = gradually shift from paid-in-full to equal payments
- Payment plan upsell: original discount if they pay balance today
- Align payment with paychecks for fewer declined payments
- Annual billing = 5× lower churn than monthly (ProfitWell)

---

## Why Annual Billing Matters

The book's casual mention of the ProfitWell data is one of the most important data points in the entire trilogy. Most SaaS businesses default to monthly billing because it's the lowest-friction sale.

**They are accepting 5× higher churn for slightly higher conversion at sign-up.**

For Caret/Colandrum: incentivize annual billing aggressively — bigger discount, exclusive bonuses, longer onboarding included.

---

## Application For Caret/Colandrum

When prospect rejects the $12,000 annual prepay:

1. *"You save $2,000 if you prepay. What would 12 monthly payments of $1,000 each look like?"*
2. *"Half now, half in 30 days?"*
3. *"On a scale 1-10..."*
4. Quarterly payments?
5. Monthly payments?
6. Trial with penalty.

At each step, BAMFAM the follow-up payment-plan upsell — *"You can lock in the original discount if you pay off the balance any time."*
