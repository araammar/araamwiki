# Idea: Why Your Low Price Is Killing Your Brand

## Concept
A short-form video or carousel built around the counterintuitive insight that underpricing is a brand problem, not generosity. Most small brand owners and first-time entrepreneurs assume that offering good product at a lower price is a competitive advantage. This video argues the opposite: low pricing signals low quality before the customer has a chance to experience the product, which actively reduces the number of people who buy in the first place.

The video walks through:
1. The psychology of price as quality proxy (people can't evaluate most products before purchase, so they use price as the signal).
2. A real example — an $80 quality shirt that loses to a $500 lower-quality shirt in boutique channels because $80 reads as "not designer."
3. The concept of charm vs. rounded pricing: $9.99 vs $10, and why luxury brands never end in .99.
4. Practical fix: where to price, and how to use the product mix to anchor perception without changing a single core product.

## Hook Angle
"Lowering your price doesn't make more people buy. It makes them trust you less. Here's the counterintuitive reason your product isn't selling — and it has nothing to do with quality."

Or for a broader take: "You're underselling yourself on purpose. You think it's generous. It's actually hurting you."

The hook is pure pattern interrupt — it inverts an assumption so common that people don't even recognize they're holding it.

## Why It Works
- The insight is counterintuitive but immediately makes sense once stated. That combination drives both shares and saves.
- It is directly actionable — a founder can adjust their pricing TODAY based on this video.
- It generates debate: "I tried raising my prices and it worked" stories in the comments are organic social proof.
- The visual/numerical examples (good/better/best tier, charm vs. rounded pricing, lost price points like $69) make abstract psychology concrete and teachable.
- High resonance for first-time founders, Shopify store operators, freelancers, service businesses — anyone who has second-guessed their price.

## Target Audience
First-time and early-stage brand founders, DTC store operators, freelancers and service providers, creative professionals pricing their work.

## Format Suggestion
Carousel (Instagram/LinkedIn) works exceptionally well — "Slide 1: The wrong assumption. Slides 2-5: Why it's wrong. Slide 6: What to do instead." Static, sharp copy with minimal design. Alternatively, a 60-90 second vertical video with on-screen text support showing the pricing tiers visually. The numbered list format (5 pricing mistakes that hurt your brand) is also highly searchable and saveable.
