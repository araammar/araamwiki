# Idea: Two Internets, One Brand

## Concept
A short-form video (reel or TikTok) that visually demonstrates what happens when a brand runs the same product through two completely different algorithmic universes — the women's feed vs. the men's feed — and shows why the exact same creative fails in one and succeeds in the other. This is a meta-education piece: it teaches marketers why their creative is underperforming without ever using jargon.

The format could be a split-screen: on one side, what the ad looks like when it lands in a women's algorithm (problem-validation → solution framing, feeling seen, community signal); on the other, what the men's algorithm receives (quantified inadequacy, social proof of other men succeeding). Same product. Completely different emotional context and hook.

Alternatively: a single-camera explainer showing how [brand X] built two separate creative strategies for the same product and what each one actually said.

## Hook Angle
Open with a question: "What if I told you your male and female customers are living on completely different internets — and your ads are only showing up on one of them?"

Or a more provocative variant: "Your men's ad and your women's ad should have almost nothing in common. Here's why."

Immediate pattern interrupt for any marketer watching. The stated assumption ("marketing is marketing") is wrong, and the video immediately offers to prove it.

## Why It Works
- Most brand marketers and small business owners genuinely don't know this. The insight is real and actionable.
- It generates debate in comments ("this isn't true" vs "this is exactly what I've experienced") — engagement signal.
- It is inherently visual and demonstrable, not just explanatory.
- The "burner account" research method (set up an account following your target customer's follows to see their actual feed) is a concrete, novel tactic people can apply immediately.
- Saves the creator from being abstract — there are specific, relatable examples (Stanley cups, sneakers, creatine, supplements) that any viewer can visualize.

## Target Audience
Brand marketers, DTC founders, creative directors, agency account managers, content creators trying to understand why their reach skews one gender.

## Format Suggestion
60-90 second vertical video with a split-screen visual or a simple "two phones, side by side" prop. The contrast is the hook. Could also work as a longer YouTube essay (8-12 minutes) with data and brand-specific examples. As a carousel: left side / right side framing works well in static format for LinkedIn and Instagram.
