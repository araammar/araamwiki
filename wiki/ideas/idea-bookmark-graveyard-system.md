# Idea: The Bookmark Graveyard

## Concept

Every creative professional has thousands of bookmarks they'll never look at again. The problem isn't saving content — it's that standard bookmarking has no retrieval layer. A reel showing what a properly organized reference system looks like vs a standard Instagram saves folder. The fix is simple: category tags + one-line note + tried/untried column.

## Why It Works

- Immediately relatable pain: every creator nods at "thousands of bookmarks I never look at"
- High save rate: the post IS advice worth saving (meta-irony of saving a post about saving)
- Visual proof is strong: screen recording of a real Notion database with hundreds of entries is compelling
- Actionable in one viewing: three steps the viewer can do today

## Hook Options

**Option 1 (Problem Reframe):**
"Every creative I know has thousands of bookmarks. None of them can find anything."

**Option 2 (Specificity):**
"I have 847 saved posts on Instagram. I can find any of them in 10 seconds. Here's why."

**Option 3 (Stakes):**
"You're saving all the right content. You'll never use it. The problem isn't discipline — it's architecture."

## Target Audience

- Content creators with disorganized reference libraries
- Marketing strategists and consultants who save references for client work
- Anyone who's opened their Instagram saves and immediately closed it

## Visual Proof

**Primary:** Screen recording walkthrough of the Notion database — showing filtering by category, the tried/untried column, a search returning 40+ relevant results in seconds.

**Contrast:** Open Instagram saves (grid of random unorganized thumbnails) → cut to Notion database (structured, queryable). No voiceover needed for this cut.

## Recommended Structure

1. Hook — "Every creative I know has thousands of bookmarks. None of them can find anything." (3 seconds)
2. Problem: Standard bookmarking has no retrieval layer — you save, it disappears (10 seconds)
3. Screen rec: Instagram saves folder — just a wall of thumbnails (5 seconds)
4. Transition: "Here's what mine actually looks like" (2 seconds)
5. Screen rec: Notion database — filter to "launch content" → 40 results appear (15 seconds)
6. The system: 4 columns — Category / Notes / Link / Tried or Not (10 seconds)
7. The key insight: Tried/untried is the column that makes this a strategy tool, not a junk drawer (5 seconds)
8. Close: "You're not saving the wrong things. You're saving them in the wrong place." (3 seconds)
