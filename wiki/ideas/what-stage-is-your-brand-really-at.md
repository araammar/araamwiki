# Idea: What Stage Is Your Brand Really At?

## Concept
A framework-led piece of content that walks the viewer through the 7 Levels of Brand Building as a self-diagnosis: "Which stage are you actually at, and why does it explain why your brand feels stuck?" The hook is not teaching the framework as a curiosity — it is positioning it as the answer to a frustration the viewer already has.

This content works best as a choose-your-own-journey format:
- "Are you making money from at least one channel consistently? No? You're at Stage 1. Here's your only job."
- "Are you making money but not growing fast? You skipped Stage 2. Here's what that means."
- Each stage is a diagnosis + prescription.

The video can also be structured as a "mistakes" format — not "here are the 7 stages" but "here are the 7 signs you skipped a stage and it's costing you." This creates more tension and urgency.

The key insight: almost every brand that is stuck has not entered Stage 5 (secret sauce) failure — they have entered Stage 2 or Stage 4 failure. They are experiencing the downstream consequence of skipping infrastructure, not the upstream problem they think they have.

## Hook Angle
"If your brand feels stuck, you didn't fail at marketing. You skipped a stage. Here's the one you're probably missing."

Or more blunt: "Most brands trying to go viral are at Stage 1 and don't know it. You can't have a secret sauce if you don't have a foundation."

Or as a self-selection hook: "What stage is your brand at? If you're not sure, you're probably at Stage 2. Here's what that means."

## Why It Works
- The diagnostic format is inherently engaging — viewers want to locate themselves in the framework.
- The insight that "stuck = skipped stage" reframes a frustrating experience as solvable, which generates saves and shares.
- The framework is concrete and sequential, making it easy to teach in short-form without oversimplifying.
- Every marketer, founder, and brand employee has an implicit framework for brand building. This one is better than what most people have, which means it displaces an existing belief — the highest-impact type of content.
- The specific examples (David protein bars at Stage 1, Bad Ombres at Stage 1, SKIMS at Stage 7) ground the abstract framework in recognizable brands.

## Target Audience
DTC brand founders, marketing managers at growth-stage brands (Stages 2-5 range), freelance marketers, brand consultants who advise clients on growth strategy.

## Format Suggestion
Long-form YouTube (10-15 minutes) is the natural home for a seven-part framework — each stage gets enough room for explanation and example. For short-form, the best approach is a "pick one stage" format: one 60-90 second video per stage, released as a series. Each stands alone but rewards watching the full series. LinkedIn carousel could cover 2-3 stages per post (3-4 posts in a series). The diagnostic angle also works well as an email series: "Day 1: Are you at Stage 1? Here's how to tell." etc.
