# Idea: The Halo Product Secret

## Concept
A video that reveals how the most sophisticated brands use products they never expect to sell to make everything else sell better. The insight: a $10,000 bike, a $40,000 car in a lookbook, a $200 candle on a shelf — these are not mistakes or vanity. They are deliberate pricing architecture tools that reframe the customer's perception of everything else in the line.

The content walks through:
1. What a halo product is and why it exists.
2. Three examples across different brand sizes: Apple Vision Pro ($3,500) making the $1,600 iPhone feel reasonable; Loro Piana's $12,000 King's Wool jacket making the $3,000 sweater feel accessible; District Vision's $10,000 one-of-one bike at launch establishing the brand's ceiling.
3. The Oreo collab effect — limited-edition products that sell modestly but drive dramatically more sales of the core product by keeping people engaged and bringing them to the shelf.
4. How any brand — not just luxury — can apply this: what is the "aspirational SKU" they could add tomorrow that would reframe their core product?

## Hook Angle
"The $900 candle a luxury store stocks isn't there because they think you'll buy it. It's there to make the $200 candle feel affordable. Every store you walk into is doing this to you."

Or for a brand-builder audience: "Apple's Vision Pro was never meant to be a hit product. It was meant to make a $1,600 phone feel reasonable. Here's how to run the same strategy at any price point."

The insight is immediately applicable and slightly conspiratorial — which is the ideal combination for high-save, high-share content.

## Why It Works
- Most people have encountered this tactic but never had it named or explained. Naming it creates an "aha" moment that drives sharing.
- The Apple example makes it feel universal and relatable — everyone knows the Vision Pro, most people have bought an Apple product.
- The Oreo example makes it feel counterintuitive — who knew that a weird limited collab drives sales of regular Oreos? — which creates the "I'm learning something real" reward.
- The practical takeaway (add an aspirational SKU to your current lineup) is immediately applicable by the watching founder or marketer.
- Cross-category applicability: fashion, food, tech, home goods, beauty — this works everywhere.

## Target Audience
Small-to-mid DTC brand founders, e-commerce operators, freelancers building out service packages, anyone who has never thought systematically about pricing architecture.

## Format Suggestion
Carousel (Instagram/LinkedIn) is ideal: Slide 1 hook, Slides 2-4 examples (Apple / Loro Piana / District Vision), Slide 5 the Oreo effect, Slide 6 "what's YOUR halo product?" CTA. Visually driven with product photos of the anchor items. For video: a rapid-fire "they're not selling what you think they're selling" format with examples in quick succession. The reveal structure (here's what you thought → here's what's actually happening) is inherently watchable.
