# Idea: World Builder — Fictional Brand Episodic Series

## The Idea
Create a scripted, recurring short-form series set in a fictional world that reflects the brand's values, aesthetic, and audience's aspirations — without the brand's product being the topic of the content. The brand is omnipresent in the world (worn, used, visible in the environment) but the show is the draw. The product earns attention by association with content people actually seek out.

## Format
- **Length:** 60–120 seconds per episode, designed for TikTok and Instagram
- **Structure:** Recurring cast of 2–4 characters, consistent setting, episodic narrative tension that resolves within each video but carries forward across episodes
- **Production level:** lo-fi is viable — all-iPhone production with scripted dialogue and consistent set design outperforms high-budget content with weak concepts (Cluey achieved millions of likes with iPhone-only episodic content)
- **Cadence:** Batch 4–6 episodes before launch. Release 2–3 per week to build narrative momentum.

## Example Premises by Brand Category

| Brand Type | Series Premise |
|------------|---------------|
| Coffee brand | A fictional indie coffee shop — recurring customers, ongoing relationship drama, the owners' ongoing argument about the menu |
| Outdoor / apparel | A group of friends planning an increasingly ambitious trip — each episode one step closer, the gear always part of the story |
| Fitness supplement | A fictional gym — recurring characters: the guy who gives unsolicited advice, the silent regular who's clearly the most fit, the new member discovering things |
| B2B SaaS | A fictional office — workplace comedy in the vein of The Office, product visible in the environment but not lectured about (Mohawk Chevrolet model) |
| Fragrance / luxury | A short film aesthetic — character studies of the kinds of people the brand imagines as its audience, each a short portrait with loose narrative |

## Why It Works
- Series content compounds: each episode benefits from everyone who watched previous episodes (return viewership drives hook rate from the first frame)
- The World Builder archetype builds the deepest brand affinity of any content model — audiences feel ownership over the characters and the world, which transfers to the brand
- Lo-fi production removes the budget barrier — what matters is concept quality and narrative consistency, not production value
- Fictional framing allows the brand to move beyond the product's natural content limits — a candle brand can tell a love story; a protein bar brand can build a character universe

## Production Model
For brands without in-house creative resources:
- Hire one creator on retainer at $3–10K/month to develop and execute the format
- Pilot phase: shoot 4 test episodes, measure hook rate and share rate after each, iterate the format
- Lock the format when a consistent pattern emerges, then batch-produce 20–30 episodes
- Keep the creator's editorial voice — over-directing creates content that sounds like an ad

## Execution Checklist
- [ ] Develop the fictional world premise — what is the setting, who are the characters, what is the ongoing tension
- [ ] Write episode 1 as a standalone that also sets up a reason to come back
- [ ] Set and costume design — what does this world consistently look like
- [ ] Cast: who can play recurring characters (internal team, hired actors, the creator themselves)
- [ ] Batch 4–6 before posting; do not launch with a single episode
- [ ] Define the narrative arc across a 10-episode initial season
