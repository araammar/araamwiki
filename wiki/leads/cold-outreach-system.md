# Framework: Cold Outreach — The 3-Problem System
tags: [leads, Hormozi, cold-outreach, core-four, framework]

## Summary / Definition

The third of the Core Four. Cold Outreach = 1-to-1 private communication with strangers.

> *"Cold outreach has one key difference from warm outreach: trust. Strangers don't trust you."*

— Alex Hormozi, *$100M Leads*

Strangers create three new problems versus warm audiences. The system solves each in sequence.

---

## The COVID Cold Outreach Origin Story

July 2020. COVID raged. 30% of customers went out of business. Apple's iOS update crippled paid ads. All three of Hormozi's companies (Gym Launch, Prestige Labs, ALAN) relied on brick-and-mortar businesses staying open. They were closed.

Saturday morning, Hormozi got a DM from a sales candidate Cale had rejected. The candidate had quit his job to accept Hormozi's role; now he had nothing.

Phone call:

- He was top producer at a gym software company doing $10M/mo
- 100% cold outreach
- Team of 30
- Selling retention software (harder than Hormozi's gym work)

> *"A team of 30!? Just from cold outreach?"*

Hormozi gave him the runway:

> *"Well, given our current lead volume, he's probably right. But, if you can get your own leads, I'll give you the runway to get cold outreach going."*

What happened:

| Month | Sales | Revenue |
|---|---|---|
| September | 0 | — |
| October | 2 | $32K (team asks Hormozi to pull plug) |
| December | 4 | $64K (team asks again) |
| January | 6 | $96K |
| February | 10 | $160K |
| March | 14 | $224K |
| April | 20 | $320K |
| May | 30 | $480K |
| Today | — | Millions/month |

> *"Making this work took every (legal) cold outreach method we knew. Cold calls . . . Cold emails . . . Cold Direct Messages . . . Voicemails. Everything. But, piece by piece, we built a reliable customer-getting machine."*

### The 5 Lessons From The Experience

1. There was another company in his space making far more money — broke his belief about market size
2. They made it all through **private** advertising (invisible to him)
3. They built a profitable cold outreach machine in his space → he could too
4. **Cold outreach veterans said a year to scale. He thought 12 weeks. Actually took ~9 months.** Expectations matter
5. He'd tried twice before and failed. Working with someone who'd done it was the unlock

## The 3 Problems Strangers Create

1. **You don't have a way to contact them.**
2. **Even if you can contact them, they ignore you.**
3. **Even if they give you their attention, they're not interested.**

> *"If you're knocking on doors, you don't have the addresses. Then, even if you do, they don't open the door when you knock. If they open, they still tell you to pound sand."*

The same 3 problems apply to phone calls, emails, DMs, voice memos, texts.

## Solution #1: Build A List — 3 Levels Of Access

> *"There are three different ways I get my targeted lead lists. First, I use software to scrape a list of names. Second, I pay brokers to assemble me a list of targeted leads. And if neither of those work, I manually scrape a list of names myself."*

### Level 1: Software Scrapers

Subscribe to as many lead-scraping platforms as you can. Search by criteria. Test a sample (a few hundred) for each:
- Up-to-date contact information?
- Responsive leads?
- Match the persona claimed by the software?

If yes → get as many as the software will give you.
If no → move to Level 2.

### Level 2: List Brokers

Go to multiple brokers. Ask each to assemble a list to your criteria. Test samples. Pick the broker(s) producing good results.

If still failing → move to Level 3.

### Level 3: Elbow Grease

Join groups and communities likely to host your audience. Find qualifying members. Get contact info from their group directory or social profiles.

Hormozi's preference: find contact info **outside the group** so you don't appear to be milking the group for business — but use the platform itself if you have to.

### The Strategic Insight

> *"If you can search the database, so can everyone else. But if you assemble a list of names yourself, it's less likely that person has already received many cold reach outs from other companies. So they're the freshest."*

Hand-built lists outperform purchased lists on response rate — at the cost of time. Once you've proven the workflow, you can pay someone else to do it.

## Solution #2: What To Say — Personalize + Big Fast Value

Two factors dominate:

### A) Personalize (They Don't Know Us)

> *"To get more leads to engage, we want the message to look like it's from someone they know."*

The standard: 1-3 pieces of information a friend might know. Compliment them on it. Ideally show how it benefited you.

Example (dog trainer outreach):
> *"<Your name?>... <yea, who's this?> ... It's Alex... I watched a few of your videos and read that recent blog post you wrote on dog training. It was killer! Really helped me out with my doberman. She's a beast! That peanut butter trick really helped. Thanks for that."*

Then pivot to relevance:
> *"Oh yea, sorry, I got ahead of myself. I work for a company that helps dog trainers fill up their books. We like to partner with the best in the area..."*

Then the bridge:
> *"We worked with someone about an hour north from you... John's Doggy Daycare... heard of them?"*
>
> "We ended up getting them 100 appointments in 30 days using a combination of text email and some ads. Do you offer similar services to them?"

Then the soft close:
> *"If you got a boatload of high paying new dog training customers you wouldn't be upset with me would you?"*

Compare this to "Hey man, wanna buy some marketing services?" — the first version produces a meeting. The second produces a hang-up.

### B) Big Fast Value (They Don't Trust Us)

> *"Strangers give you far less time to prove your worth. And, they need a lot more incentive to move towards you. So make your life easier by 'giving away the farm.' We're not trying to tickle their interest, we're trying to blow their minds in under thirty seconds."*

The first 4 months of Hormozi's cold outreach were torture. The lead magnet was a "game planning session" (code for "sales call"). Few took it.

> *"We swapped from 'game planning' — code for 'sales call' — to actually giving them as much free service as we could possibly afford. Our take rates 3x'd and cold outreach became a monster channel for us."*

The principle:

> *"Give yourself a downhill battle by giving away something crazy. Give away something for free people would normally pay for and they will want it. Note: I didn't say, 'so good they should pay for it,' I said, 'stuff they actually pay for.' Big difference."*

## Solution #3: Volume — Automate + Follow Up

### A) Automate Delivery

Manual: a live person says a script over the phone, writes a handwritten letter, etc.

Automated: pre-recorded voice memo, pre-recorded voicemail, templated email/text, pre-recorded video. Record once, send to everyone.

### B) Automate Distribution

Manual: dial each number, click send on each message.

Automated: dialers that hit multiple numbers, blast 1,000 messages at once.

### The Personalization Tradeoff

> *"Generally speaking, you sacrifice personalization for scale. You get a higher response rate with personalized messages. The fewer leads you have, the less automation you should use."*

Heuristic:
- 1,000 hedge fund managers? Personalize every one
- 10 million weight-loss prospects? Less personalization, more automation
- Personalization on top of automation = best of both worlds

### C) Follow Up. More Times. More Ways.

> *"Imagine you really needed to get a hold of your parents because something important came up. What would you do? You'd probably call them, text them, leave a voicemail, etc. And if they still haven't responded, what would you do? You'd call and text them again."*

Same with prospects. They are "in danger of living life without your solution. Be a hero. Save them!"

Hormozi's preferred sequence:
- Email first (most people don't respond)
- Use no-reply as a reason to call: *"Hey I'm calling you to follow up about my email."*
- Win either way (response or a real excuse to reach out again)

Expect 2-3 conversations before a higher-ticket sale.

### D) Restart The List Every 3-6 Months

After contacting everyone multiple times multiple ways, wait 3-6 months. Then start the list over.

Why this works:
1. **They may not have seen your first series** — make-up for missed exposure
2. **Their schedule may have prevented response then** — different time, different outcome
3. **Their circumstances may have changed** — the person who didn't need weight loss before the holidays needs it desperately after

Example: A weight-loss prospect ignores your message in November (skinny jeans fit). After 10lbs of holiday weight, they respond enthusiastically to the exact same message in January.

> *"Everything may be right except the timing. So the more times we contact them, the more likely we will catch them at the time they're ready to engage."*

## Benchmarks Per Platform

### Phone
- 100 calls/day → 20% pickup → 25% take lead magnet → 4 engaged leads
- 4 hours of work → 1 engaged lead per hour
- Scale once: lifetime profit per customer > 3 × cost to acquire

### Email
- 100 personalized emails/day → 30% open → 10% reply with interest → 3 engaged leads
- Aim for 3% of list turning into engaged leads
- Hormozi cites a real campaign at 4% lead engagement rate; 1/3 conversion = 1 new customer per 100 outreach attempts

### Direct Messages
- 100 personal video/voice memos → 20% reply → 20 engaged leads
- Hormozi's portfolio example: 30:1 returns from outreach efforts

### Cold Call Team Example (Cost Walkthrough)
- $15/hr base + $50/show commission
- 200 leads/day @ $0.10/lead → 2 shows → $240 cost → $120/show
- 33% close rate → $360/client (excluding commissions)
- $3,600 LTGP → **10:1 return**

## The 7 Strategic Benefits Of Cold Outreach

> *"Most people dramatically underestimate the amount of volume it takes to use cold outreach. They also underestimate how long it takes. But there are seven enormous benefits."*

1. **No content/ads required** — one perfectly-crafted message. No ad fatigue. No banner blindness.
2. **Competition can't see what you're doing** — everything is private. Operate in secrecy.
3. **Incredibly reliable** — input ratio is fixed: X people contacted = Y customers. Reverse-engineer revenue from inputs.
4. **Fewer platform changes** — rules for cold calling, door knocking, cold email have hardly changed in 30 years
5. **Compliance is easier** — no platform claim restrictions; just FTC. Banned industries can advertise (tobacco, firearms, cannabis, weight loss). "If you have a phone, you can make money."
6. **No spokesperson = Sellable business** — Gym Launch was sellable because the outreach team kept producing without Hormozi
7. **Hard to copy** — competitors can't see how you scrape lists, personalize, distribute. Hidden complexity = durable lead

## Application For Caret/Colandrum

Cold outreach is the highest-leverage early-stage method for any B2B product:

- **List**: agency directories, marketing platforms, Clay enrichment (see [[cold-email-masterclass-2025]])
- **Personalize**: reference their recent campaign, their portfolio, a specific result
- **Big Fast Value**: free audit of their last campaign + 3 specific opportunities
- **Volume**: 100 contacts/day minimum, multi-channel follow-up, restart list every 6 months
