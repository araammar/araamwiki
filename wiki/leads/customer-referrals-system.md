# Framework: Customer Referrals — 6 Ways To Give + 7 Ways To Ask
tags: [leads, Hormozi, referrals, lead-getters, framework, word-of-mouth]

## Summary / Definition

The first Lead Getter. Customer referrals are the **lowest cost, highest profit, best quality leads** available — when you can produce them.

> *"The best advertising is a happy customer. An amazing product turns every customer into a lead getter."*

— Alex Hormozi, *$100M Leads*

But most businesses don't get them. Two reasons: their product isn't good enough, and they don't ask.

---

## The Origin Story — October 2019

Hormozi and Leila reviewed weekly reports on her parents' couch:

> *"Hey, sales numbers look a little soft this week."*
> *"How many did we close?"*
> *"Fifteen."*
> *"Is there anything different on your end?"*

Hormozi opened Facebook ads. Red rejection notifications filled the screen.

> *"Wait. The ads got shut off two weeks ago, so that explains the dip. The good news is . . . our product is so good that we're still doing $500,000 a week from word of mouth alone."*

> *"You ignored the ads for two weeks!?"*

Two weeks of zero paid advertising. $500K/wk in pure word-of-mouth revenue.

A few months later, on stage at Gym Lords (700+ owners paying $42K/yr each):

> *"Who here learned about Gym Launch from another gym owner? Raise your hand."*

The room paused. Hormozi panicked internally. Then hands started raising — a few, then more, then *almost the entire room*.

> *"That . . . is the power of word of mouth."*

## How Referrals Grow A Business (2 Ways)

1. **Higher LTGP**: Referrals buy more expensive stuff, buy more times, often pay cash up front
2. **Lower CAC**: Free customers cost nothing → much cheaper than acquired ones

### The Math Made Visible

Imagine your normal LTGP:CAC is 4:1 (25% of LTGP spent on acquisition).

Now imagine every customer brings 2 more customers:
- LTGP:CAC effectively becomes 12:1
- You use **8.3%** of LTGP on acquisition instead of 25%

> *"You get three customers for the price of one. Now we're talking."*

### Exponential vs Linear Scaling

Other Core Four methods are **linear**: 2× the input → 2× the output.

Referrals are **exponential**: 1 customer brings 2; those 2 bring 4; those 4 bring 8.

But there's a catch: the math only works if referrals exceed churn.

> **The Referral Growth Equation:**
> - Referrals > Churn → you grow without other advertising ✨
> - Referrals = Churn → you need other advertising to grow
> - Referrals < Churn → you advertise just to break even (most businesses)

PayPal and Dropbox went billion-dollar from referrals because their referral rate vastly exceeded churn. Most small businesses scrape by because their churn equals their referrals.

## Why Most Businesses Don't Get Referrals

### Reason 1: The Product Isn't Good Enough

> *"'Everyone loves our stuff, we just need to get the word out!' — says every small business owner with a product that's not as good as they think it is."*

The hard truth:

> *"If your product were exceptional, people would already know about it and you'd have more business than you could handle. So if you sell direct to consumers and they are not bringing you more customers, your product has room to improve."*

Hormozi's reframe question to himself:

> *"Why are my customers too embarrassed to tell everyone they know about my product?"*

Examples of "unremarkable" Hormozi pays for now:
- Pool guy forgets stuff half the time
- Landscapers make noise at the worst hours
- Cleaners put his clothes in his wife's closet

> *"The list goes on."*

### Reason 2: They Don't Ask

> *"Do you know why businesses have so few referrals compared to what they could have? They never ask for them. Your customers, like any audience, can only know what to do if you tell them."*

The fix is in two parts: **give more value** (so the product is referral-worthy) + **ask explicitly** (so they actually refer).

---

## The Goodwill Equation

> *"Price is what you charge. Value is what they get. The difference between price and value is goodwill."*

Goodwill drives word of mouth. Two ways to build it:

1. **Lower price** — temporary fix, race to zero
2. **Give more value** — sustainable, unlimited upside

> *"Any fool can sell something for less."* — Rory Sutherland (quoted by Hormozi)

The book focuses entirely on option 2.

---

## Part 1: 6 Ways To Give More Value (Mapped To Ad Parts)

The framework that makes this elegant: **the 6 ways map directly to the parts of an ad** (Callouts, Value Elements, CTA).

### 1. Call Outs → Sell Better Customers

The customers who get the most value have the most goodwill. The customers with the most goodwill refer most.

#### The PR Portfolio Company Story

A portfolio company did public relations for generic small businesses. Lots of sales. Lots of churn. Plateaued for years.

They looked at lowest-churning customers. Common pattern: all in a specific niche raising funds from investors.

Concern: those were only **15% of business**. Changing targeting risked losing 85%.

> *"After reviewing the data many times, he agreed to change the advertising call outs to match this narrower, 'perfect fit' customer."*

Results:
- Broke through the plateau
- Adding millions per month
- Lower CAC (more specific messaging)
- Cheaper leads got even more value (better fit)
- Referrals started clockwork

**The action**: figure out what your most successful customers have in common. Target *only* them in your advertising.

### 2. Dream Outcome → Set Better Expectations

> *"The fastest, easiest, and cheapest way to make your product remarkable — make it better than they expect. And that's easier than you might think because you set the expectations."*

The movie analogy:
- Stranger says "this movie is awesome" → you see it → "not as good as expected"
- Stranger says "this movie is terrible" → you see it → "not as bad as expected"

> *"Our expectations of an experience can dramatically affect the experience itself. We can increase goodwill by lowering expectations."*

Hormozi started by promising everything. Fulfilling that was a nightmare. He began lowering promises while maintaining quality.

**The action**: slowly lower your promises until close rates start to drop. Then stop. That's the sweet spot of maximum conversion + maximum room to overdeliver.

### 3. Perceived Likelihood → Get More People Better Results

> *"The customers with the best results get the most value from your product. Figure out what they do to get the most value, and you can help your other customers do the same."*

This is the same mechanic as [[reverse-engineer-buying-process|the Lost Chapter's Step 4b]] — applied to customer outcomes instead of customer journey.

#### The Gym Launch Discovery

Tracked customer activities. Found: **if a gym ran paid ads + made a sale in the first 7 days, LTGP tripled.**

Forced everyone to launch ads and make sales in 7 days. Average customer results skyrocketed. Testimonials and referrals followed.

#### The 6-Step Process

1. Survey customers to find best results
2. Interview them to find what they did differently
3. Look at actions they had in common
4. Force new customers to repeat those actions
5. Measure the improvement in average results
6. **Match guarantee conditions to the success actions** to get more people to do them

**The action**: don't leave customer success to chance. Engineer it from your data.

### 4. Time Delay → Make Faster Wins ([[bamfam-book-meeting-from-meeting|BAMFAM]])

> *"To make wins feel faster, we give them wins more often."*

Same amount of progress, more "win moments" = perception of speed + trust + adherence.

#### 5 Ways To Make Wins Faster

1. **Split deliverables into smaller intervals** — 7 wins beats 1 win at the end of a week
2. **Updates are wins** — share progress as frequently as possible. "You can never give someone too much good news."
3. **First 48 hours decide the lasting impression** — force as many wins as you can in that window
4. **They should always know the next time they'll hear from you** — **[[bamfam-book-meeting-from-meeting|BAMFAM]]: Book-A-Meeting-From-A-Meeting**
5. **Never expect customers to forgive you** — deliver early, never late. Hormozi adds 50% to timelines so on-time-for-him = early-for-them

### 5. Effort & Sacrifice → Keep Making Your Stuff Better

> *"If the customer does less stuff they hate to benefit from your product, you've made it better."*

There's no such thing as a perfect product. Always more to improve.

#### The Monthly Improvement Process

1. Use customer service data, surveys, reviews → find most common problem
2. Figure out your fix → get feedback from customers who made it work despite the problem
3. Implement → use feedback to build the new version
4. Test → give it to a small group of struggling customers
5. Validate → if it solves the original problem, roll to all customers; if not, back to step 2
6. Move on → next most common problem, repeat

> *"Set this as a recurring monthly process."*

### 6. Call To Action → Tell Them What To Buy Next

> *"If you have an amazing product, they'll want more. You have to satisfy their desire to buy. If you don't, they'll still buy . . . but from someone else."*

#### The Weight Loss Example

In a weight loss company, lots of customers referred friends to the **first** tier product. Some didn't. Of those non-referrers, when they bought the **more expensive** thing, they referred their friends to *that*.

The lesson: keep selling. People obsess over front-end offers and neglect the back end.

> *"Customers that fall off your product are unlikely to refer — so keep selling them so they don't."*

**The action**: treat every customer like it's the first time you've sold them. Make the next offer more compelling than the first.

## The One Question To Rule Them All

> *"You've lost all your customers but one. The gods of advertising ban you from doing the core four and decree: All customers must come from this one customer. Violate our terms and we will destroy your business, and every other business you start, for eternity."*

> *"Tough break. But, the question remains, how would you treat this customer?"*

The thought experiment forces clarity on:
- What experience makes them refer their friends?
- What results would they need to get?
- What's the onboarding?
- What type of customer would you pick?

> *"Start acting like the advertising gods will revoke your core four privileges at any moment."*

This is the [[reverse-engineer-buying-process|Lost Chapter quality-first principle]] taken to its logical extreme.

## Part 2: 7 Ways To Ask For Referrals

> *"Asking for referrals only works when you treat it like an offer."*

### Case Studies

**Dropbox**: Free storage to customers and free storage to friends they refer. **39× business in 15 months.**

**PayPal**: $10 credit to customers and $10 to friends. Reached 1M users in 2 years, 100M in 6 years. **Still in use today.**

> *"So how do we harness the same viral growth in our own small businesses? We do what they did. We ask for it."*

### The 7 Methods

#### 1. One-Sided Referral Benefit
Pay your CAC to *either* referrer or friend.

> *"Imagine it costs $200 to get a new customer. Ask the current customer to make an actual three-way introduction to a friend — via call, SMS, or email. Not just a name and number. Also, ask them to do it right when they buy . . . don't wait."*

Variations:
- Write the referrer a check for $200 when the friend signs up
- OR give the friend $200 off

**Spouse variant**: ask for the spouse and give a household discount.

#### 2. Two-Sided Referral Benefits (Dropbox/PayPal Style)
Half goes to referrer, half to friend.

> *"We sell $500 programs. Our cost to get a customer is $200. For every friend someone refers, we give them $100 cash, and give their friend $100 off signing up. Good for up to 3 friends."*

#### 3. Ask Right When They Buy
On the sales contract or checkout page. Show how they'll get better results doing it with a friend.

#### The "How Are You Selling So Many?" Story

A new salesman shattered all sales records for an upcoming event. Hormozi called to ask why.

> *"I'm doing the same thing as everyone else. I just make sure I ask them who else they'd want to have come with them. Then ask them to introduce me."*

**Half his sales were referrals.**

> *"Script: People who do our program with someone else tend to get 3× the results. Who else could you do this program with?"*

#### 4. Add Referrals As A Negotiation Chip
For someone who wants to pay $400 when your price is $500:

> *"I can't do anything less than $500 down, but if you make a 3-way text introduction to a few of your friends right now, I'd be happy to cut that initiation fee."*

If full-priced customers find out, respond:

> *"Yea — Stacy got $100 off because she referred three friends. I'm happy to give you $100 if you refer me three friends. Who do you have in mind?"*

They either back off or give you three friends. Win-win.

#### 5. Referral Events
Time-bound (1-4 weeks). Points, credits, dollars, or bragging rights.

Hormozi's names:
- "Bring-a-friend" Promotion
- "Spouse Challenge" Promotion
- "Accountability Buddy" Promotion
- "Coach Challenge" Promotion (creates teams with employees + customers)

#### 6. Ongoing Referral Programs
Talk about benefits of doing things with others **all the time** — in free content, outreach, paid ads.

A friend who did this: **33% boost in total signups.** Context: 1M ticket buyers, 250K of them referred.

#### 7. Unlockable Referral Bonuses
For people who 1) refer and 2) leave a testimonial.

Examples: VIP bonuses, courses, tokens, status, training, merchandise, service levels, premium support.

> *"Unlockable referral bonuses work well if you don't like paying out cash."*

## The Gift Card Promotion (Combined Strategies)

Hormozi's "killer combo" example:

> *"Give everyone a gift card for one-third the cost of their program. Tell them they can give it to a friend of theirs if they sign up with them. Give the gift card an expiration date within seven to fourteen days from the date you give it to them — it'll force them to use it. This gives the referrer status when they give it to their friend."*

The status angle: instead of saying *"join my program for $2000 off,"* they say *"I got this gift card for $2000. Do you want it? I don't want to waste it."*

Bonus: write the friend's name on the gift card and text a picture. Gives a legitimate reason to ask for the friend's name.

You can also **sell** the gift cards at 90% off to existing customers as a gift for their friend. Referrer looks generous; you get paid to acquire customers.

## Final Mantra

> *"Referrals aren't an advertising method you can 'do.' It's not one trick or hack. It's a way of doing business. And it starts with you."*

> *"Referring is always a risk for the customer. They risk their goodwill with their friend in the hopes of getting more by showing them something cool (your stuff)."*

The compounding loop:
- Goodwill = the asset
- Value delivered + asks made = the deposits and withdrawals
- Surplus goodwill = referrals
- Referrals = exponential growth

> *"I am compensated tomorrow for the value I provide today."*

## Application Checklist

1. Calculate current referral % and churn %
2. Implement the 6 "give value" steps to build goodwill
3. Pick 1-2 of the 7 ways to ask
4. Track movement on both metrics monthly
