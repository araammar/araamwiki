# Framework: Paid Ads — Money Mechanics
tags: [leads, Hormozi, paid-ads, LTGP, CAC, economics, client-financed-acquisition]

## Summary / Definition

The economics side of paid ads. Hormozi's claim: efficiency matters far more than creativity. You can win at paid ads with mediocre creative if your math is tight; you can lose with brilliant creative if your math is loose.

> *"All advertising works. The only thing that differs between advertisements is how well they work."*

— Alex Hormozi, *$100M Leads*

This page covers:
1. The 3 phases of scaling paid ads
2. The LTGP:CAC efficiency framework
3. Client Financed Acquisition (the cash-flow unlock)
4. 3 personal lessons

## The Three Phases Of Scaling

### Phase 1: Track Money

> *"Before spending a dollar on ads, set everything up so you can accurately track your returns."*

Without tracking, you're in a casino without knowing your account balance. Tracking lets you do more of what works and less of what doesn't.

> *"Get a consultant, watch tutorials, and get it set up. End of story."*

Phase 1 is non-negotiable. If you skip it, every subsequent phase becomes guesswork.

### Phase 2: Lose Money

> *"I prefer to call it 'investing in a money printing machine.'"*

> *"I emphasize this because I'd rather prepare you: you're gonna lose money. In fact, I've lost money more times than I've made money running paid ads."*

The math behind why losing is OK:

**The Pareto Of Ads (Hormozi's Example)**
- 10 ads × $100 each = $1,000 spent
- 9 lose all $100
- 1 makes $500 back on $100 spent
- **Net: down $500**

Many stop here. They see "$500 loss." **Hormozi sees a winner.**

> *"So now we buckle up and 100× down. We spend $10,000 on the winning ad and make $50,000 back."*

The pattern: many small losses + a few large wins = net huge profit.

### How To Lose Smart — The 2× Cash Budget

> *"I budget two times the cash I collect from a customer in thirty days (not LTGP) when testing new ads."*

The rule:
- If you make $100 in profit from a customer in the first 30 days, let an ad spend up to $200 before shutting off — **as long as you're getting leads**
- If you're not getting any leads at all, shut off before spending 1× thirty-day cash ($100 in the example)

Hormozi worked with a business that took a **year** to get paid ads profitable. Other businesses in their space were profitable, proving it was possible.

> *"Once they were profitable, they made their year's worth of 'wasted' money back in the next month. It costs money to build an advertising machine . . . and that's normal."*

> *"Can you think of anything more valuable than a machine that prints money? It would be unreasonable for it to be cheap (or easy)."*

### Phase 3: Print Money

> *"If you're making back more money than you spend — the answer is simple — spend as much as you can."*

The reframe Hormozi prescribes:

> *"Instead of asking 'How much money should I spend on an ad?' I ask 'How many customers do I want?' or 'How many customers can I handle?'"*

Reverse-engineering the budget:
- 100 customers max next month
- $100 CAC
- Need $10,000 ad budget
- Pad 20% for scaling inefficiency → $12,000 → $400/day

> *"If the number terrifies you, then you're doing it right. Trust the data. This is how you scale. And that's why most people never do."*

## The LTGP:CAC Framework

The unit of efficiency. Hormozi tracks:
- **LTGP** — Lifetime Gross Profit (not LTV, see below)
- **CAC** — Cost to Acquire a Customer

### Why LTGP, Not LTV

> *"Lifetime gross profit is all the money a customer ever spends on your stuff minus all the money it takes to deliver it."*

Example:
- Customer buys $15 of stuff that costs $5 to deliver → $10 gross profit per purchase
- 10 purchases over lifetime → $150 total revenue, $50 cost
- **LTGP = $100**

> *"Gross profit is important in general because it's the actual money you use to acquire customers, pay rent, cover payroll, and... everything else to run your business."*

LTV (total revenue) is misleading because it doesn't account for delivery cost. LTGP is the actual money available.

### The 3:1 Minimum Ratio

> *"Every business I invest in that struggles to scale has at least one thing in common — their LTGP to CAC ratio was less than 3 to 1. As soon as I get it above 3 to 1 (either through decreasing CAC or increasing LTGP), they take off. This is a pattern I personally observed, not a rule."*

Below 3:1 → struggle. Above 3:1 → scale.

### Two Levers To Improve LTGP:CAC

1. **Make CAC lower** — get cheaper customers (better ads following the steps in [[paid-ads-callout-value-cta]])
2. **Make LTGP higher** — make customers worth more (better business model)

> *"For maximum money . . . I prefer to do both."*

### Diagnosing Whether It's Ads Or Business Model

> *"They often think they have crappy ads (high CAC) when, in reality, they have a crappy business model (Low LTGP)."*

The surprise:

> *"The cost to acquire customers, between competitors in the same industry, is much closer than you'd think. The difference between the winners and the losers is how much they make off each customer."*

The diagnostic rule:
- Research your **industry average CAC**
- If your CAC is **below 3× industry average** (good) → focus on LTGP (business model)
- If your CAC is **above 3× industry average** (bad) → focus on advertising

> *"Things can only get so cheap. Eventually you just gotta make more."*

CAC can only approach $0. LTGP can grow to infinity. Make the lever that has unlimited upside the focus once basic efficiency is achieved.

## Client Financed Acquisition

The cash-flow concept that unlocks unlimited scale.

### The Problem

For many businesses, LTGP > CAC over time but **not in the first 30 days**. You spend $X on ads, get a customer, but they pay you back over 6+ months.

> *"This cash flow problem cripples your ability to scale ads and get more customers. Boo again."*

### The Solution

> *"If your customer spends more than it costs you to get and fulfill them — in the first 30 days — then you have the funds to scale now and forever. I call this client financed acquisition."*

Why 30 days specifically:

> *"I pick thirty days because any business can get interest free money for thirty days in the form of a credit card. And if we make more than the cost to get and fulfill the customer in the first thirty days, we square our balance."*

Now you have:
- A new customer at zero net cash cost
- Continued profit from them forever
- The same starting bank balance
- Repeat the cycle indefinitely

> *"Money is no longer your bottleneck. This is the key to limitless scale."*

### Worked Example

**Step 1 — The membership business:**
- $15/mo membership, $5/mo to deliver = $10 gross profit/mo
- Avg member stays 10 months = **LTGP $100**
- CAC = $30 → LTGP:CAC = 3.3:1 ✓ ads make money

**Step 2 — The cash flow problem:**
- Spent $30, got $10 back in month 1
- Don't break even until month 3
- That's tough

**Step 3 — Add the upsell:**
- $100 upsell at 100% margin
- 1 in 5 customers take it
- Average upsell value = $20/customer
- First-30-day gross profit = $10 (first month) + $20 (upsell) = **$30**
- $30 CAC fully recouped within 30 days
- **Free customer**

> *"Every $10 a month that comes in thereafter is 'gravy.' Now, I can go get another customer while I keep collecting that $10 profit per month for the next nine months. This is how you print money."*

### Hormozi's Personal Track Record

> *"It's how I've scaled every company I've started for the past seven years past $1M/mo in the first twelve months — without outside funding."*

The mantra:

> *"Figure out a way to get your customers to pay you back in the first thirty days so you can recycle your cash to get more customers."*

The unlimited upsells:
- Higher tiers
- Annual pre-pays
- Adjacent products
- Service packages
- Premium support

If you can recover 100% of CAC in 30 days, you can scale ads as fast as you can hire to fulfill.

## 3 Personal Lessons

### 1. Don't Confuse Sales Problems With Advertising Problems

A company invested $150,000 over 12 weeks in paid ads. Right leads were getting on the phone but weren't buying. The owner said "ads don't work" and gave up.

> *"The ads worked fine, great even, their sales sucked. The owner threw up his hands and gave up . . . six inches from gold."*

Cost of the wrong diagnosis: **~$30M in enterprise value.**

The diagnostic:
> *"If your engaged leads have the problem you solve and the money to spend, and they're not buying, then your ads work fine — you have a sales problem."*

### 2. Your Best Free Content Can Make The Best Paid Ads

> *"Some of the best paid ads I've ever run came from free content. If you make a free content piece that generates sales, or performs very well, nine times out of ten it'll make a great paid ad."*

**User Generated Content (UGC)** is a special case:
- If you can get customers to create testimonials/reviews using your product, post them
- If they perform well as free content, they often make killer ads
- System in place to encourage this = steady stream of potential ads at no extra cost

This connects to [[content-monetization-strategies|Lesson #4]] — content creates tools for salespeople — but extends it: content creates tools for *paid ads* too.

### 3. If You Say You Suck At Something, You Will Suck At It

> *"Never say 'I'm not techy' or 'I hate tech stuff.' It just keeps you poorer than you should be."*

Hormozi said this for **four years**. Then snapped: *"If this idiot can do it, so can I."*

**Four years of wasted time and lost money reversed with four hours of concentrated effort.**

## How To Place Your First Ad — 5 Steps

> *"I can teach you how to place an ad in twenty minutes. It'll cost ya $100."*

1. Search "HOW TO PLACE A [PLATFORM] AD"
2. Place one for $100
3. Don't chicken out at checkout. Spend the money. Rip off the bandaid
4. Now you're in the game
5. Spend an acceptable monthly amount you're willing to lose. **You won't be earning, you'll be learning.**

## Why Run Paid Ads Last Of The Core Four

> *"I recommend doing paid ads last for two reasons. First, skills from the other three methods transfer to this one. And second, paid ads cost money. Money you will have if you start with the other three methods first."*

The progression makes paid ads the easiest to win at — because the prior methods have already taught you offers, audiences, messaging, and CTAs.
