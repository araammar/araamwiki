# Concept: BAMFAM — Book A Meeting From A Meeting
tags: [leads, Hormozi, BAMFAM, customer-success, retention, meetings]

## Summary / Definition

A principle Hormozi credits to a public CEO friend. **Never leave a customer in no-man's-land.** They should always know the next time they'll hear from you.

> **B**ook **A** **M**eeting **F**rom **A** **M**eeting.

Use it at the end of every customer-facing interaction to schedule the next one, on the spot, before either party leaves.

---

## The Mechanic

The end of every meeting / call / session asks one question:

> *"When should we sync next?"*

Pull up calendars. Pick a date. Lock it. Don't let either party leave without the next touch on the calendar.

The customer leaves knowing:
- Exactly when they'll hear from you next
- What that next conversation will cover
- That they don't need to chase you

---

## Why It Works

### 1. Reduces Customer Anxiety
> *"Never leave a customer in no man's land. They should always know what happens . . . next."*

Customer anxiety = friction in the relationship. The cure for "when will I hear back?" is "you'll hear from me Tuesday at 3pm."

### 2. Accelerates Customer Wins (Time Delay Reduction)
The customer can mentally count the days until the next milestone. Anticipated future wins feel more concrete than vague "we'll keep in touch."

This is one of the 5 "Faster Wins" methods in [[customer-referrals-system|Way 4 — Time Delay]].

### 3. Builds Goodwill
A customer who never wonders when they'll hear from you trusts you more. Trust is the substrate that produces referrals.

### 4. Eliminates Drop-Off
Without a next meeting, customers go quiet, fall off the radar, churn. With one always on the calendar, they stay engaged through the natural commitment of a scheduled future event.

---

## The Rule

> *"They should always know the next time they'll hear from you."*

The implementation:
- Every email response ends with "Next check-in: [date]"
- Every meeting closes with "Next meeting: [date]"
- Every project milestone includes the next milestone date
- Every product delivery includes the next interaction date

---

## When To Apply It

### Internal-Facing (Customer Success / Service)
- Onboarding calls → Book the kickoff
- Kickoff calls → Book the 30-day review
- 30-day reviews → Book the 60-day check
- Quarterly reviews → Book the next quarterly

### Sales (External)
- Discovery call → Book the demo
- Demo → Book the decision call
- Decision call → Book the kickoff
- Closed-lost call → Book a 6-month re-engagement check

### Customer Service / Support
- Ticket resolution → "We'll check back with you in 7 days to make sure this stuck"
- Onboarding ticket → Book the next milestone touchpoint

## How It Connects To The 78% Insight

The [[reverse-engineer-buying-process|78% / 2-long-form-content-pieces]] data point is about **the buyer's journey**. BAMFAM is about **the customer's post-purchase journey**.

Both share the same underlying principle: **engineer the touchpoints rather than leaving them to chance.**

A customer who consumes 2 pre-purchase content pieces + has BAMFAM scheduled throughout their lifecycle is dramatically more likely to:
- Stay (low churn)
- Get results (high adherence)
- Refer (high goodwill)

---

## Combined With "Add 50% To Timelines"

The companion practice Hormozi prescribes:

> *"Never expect customers to forgive you. Ever. So act like it. For example, you can deliver early, but never late. I add fifty percent to my timelines so I always deliver early. That makes 'on time' for me early for them."*

BAMFAM + Padded Timelines = Customers who are surprised by on-time delivery and certain about future engagement.

## Application For Caret/Colandrum

### Sales Cycle
- Demo ends → Book pilot proposal review (date locked)
- Pilot proposal ends → Book contract review
- Contract review ends → Book kickoff
- Kickoff ends → Book 30-day strategic review

### Customer Lifecycle
- Onboarding ends → Book 30/60/90-day Quarterly Business Review schedule
- QBR ends → Book next QBR
- Renewal moment → Book renewal call 90 days out

Every meeting closes with a date locked. No customer is ever in no-man's-land.
