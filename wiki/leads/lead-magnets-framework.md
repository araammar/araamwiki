# Framework: The 7-Step Lead Magnet Framework
tags: [leads, Hormozi, lead-magnets, framework, salty-pretzels]

## Summary / Definition

> *"A lead magnet is a complete solution to a narrow problem. It's typically a lower-cost or free offer to see who is interested in your stuff. And, once solved, it reveals another problem solved by your core offer."*

— Alex Hormozi, *$100M Leads*

The lead magnet is how you get leads to **engage** when your core offer is too expensive, too complex, or too uncertain for them to commit on first exposure.

---

## The Salty Pretzels Metaphor

> *"Think of it like salty pretzels at a bar. If somebody eats the pretzels, they'll get thirsty and order a drink. The salty pretzels solve the narrow problem of hunger. They also reveal a thirst problem solved by a drink, which they can get, in exchange for money."*

The pretzels:
- Cost something to deliver (the bar buys them)
- Are free to the customer (engagement-friendly)
- Solve a real, narrow problem (hunger)
- Reveal the next problem (thirst)
- The drink covers the cost of pretzels and nets a profit

A lead magnet works the same way.

---

## The Foundational Principle

> *"Your lead magnet should be valuable enough on its own that you could charge for it. And, after they get it, they should want more of what you offer."*

> *"A person who pays with their time now is more likely to pay with their money later."*

The lead magnet is not a teaser. It's not "almost the answer." It's a **complete solution to a narrow problem.** If it doesn't fully solve something, it fails as a lead magnet.

---

## Grand Slam Offer Logic Applies To Free Stuff Too

> *"Grand Slam Offers work for free stuff as much or better than they do for paid stuff. So make your lead magnet so insanely good people will feel stupid saying no."*

The free lead magnet should follow [[grand-slam-offer]] principles: outsized perceived value, low time delay, low effort, high likelihood of result. This is what makes the difference between a lead magnet that produces 100 engaged leads and one that produces 10,000.

---

## The 7 Steps

### Step 1: Figure Out The Problem To Solve & Who To Solve It For

Use the **Problem-Solution Cycle**:

> *"Every problem has a solution. Every solution reveals more problems. This is the never-ending cycle of business (and life)."*

The framework:
1. Pick a problem that is **narrow** and **meaningful**
2. Solve it
3. The solution reveals a new problem
4. **The new problem must be solvable by your core offer**

If the next problem isn't solved by your core offer, the lead magnet won't pay back. Pick a different problem.

#### Hormozi's Worked Example (Selling Homes)

Broad solution: "We help homeowners sell their homes."

Narrow lead magnet problems:
- What's my house worth?
- How do I increase its value?
- They need pictures
- They need cleaning
- They need landscaping
- They need minor things fixed
- They need moving services
- They may need staging

Solve any one of those narrow problems for free — and now you've earned trust to charge to solve the remaining ones with your core offer (selling the house).

#### Hormozi's Own Example
> *"This book is a lead magnet. You are a lead. I want to solve an engaged lead problem. And I want to solve it for businesses making less than $1,000,000 in annual profit. With enough engaged leads, they can make more than $1,000,000+ in annual profit. Then, they qualify for my core offer: me investing in their company to help them scale."*

The book is the lead magnet; investment is the core offer.

### Step 2: Figure Out How To Solve It (3 Types)

Three types of lead magnet solutions:

#### Type 1: Reveal Their Problem (Diagnosis)
Best when revealing the problem makes them urgently want to fix it.

**Examples:**
- A website speed test that shows them loading at 30% below standard, with money lost spelled out
- A posture assessment with a clear contrast to a pain-free life
- A termite inspection that reveals what bugs do to a home; works whether termites are present (treat them) or not (preventive contract)

#### Type 2: Samples & Trials
Full but brief access to your core offer. Works when your core offer is a *recurring* solution to a recurring problem.

**Examples:**
- Hook them to your faster server, show their site loading at lightning speed → they keep paying or it slows down
- A free chiropractic adjustment that relieves pain → they need to keep coming back
- Consumable samples — food, cosmetics, medicine. "Costco sells more food than other stores because they give out samples!"

#### Type 3: One Step Of A Multi-Step Process
When your core offer solves a complex problem with multiple steps, give one valuable step free.

**Examples:**
- This book (Step 1: how to get leads. Core offer: scaling to $100M+)
- A free wood sealant coat for a garage door — but full protection needs 3 coats; the other 2 are paid
- Free finance courses, guides, calculators, templates — valuable enough that they could DIY, but the time/effort reveal makes them buy your financial services

### Step 3: Figure Out How To Deliver It (4 Methods)

Four delivery vehicles. Each works with each of the three solution types — that's **12 possible lead magnet variants** for any single narrow problem.

| Method | What | Hormozi's Gym Owner Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Software** | Give them a tool | Spreadsheet/dashboard with gym business stats vs industry averages + rank |
| **Information** | Teach them something | Mini-course on how to write an ad |
| **Services** | Do work for free | Run their ads free for 30 days |
| **Physical Products** | Something they can hold | A book — "Gym Launch Secrets" |

> *"With three different types of lead magnets and four ways to deliver them, that's up to twelve lead magnets that solve a single narrow problem. So many magnets, so little time!"*

Hormozi's strategy: **make many versions and rotate them.** Keeps advertising fresh, low-effort, and lets you discover which versions work best.

### Step 4: Test What To Name It

Quoting David Ogilvy:

> *"When you have written your headline, you have spent 80 cents of your (advertising) dollar."*

Five times more people read the headline than any other part. The name is the highest-leverage component.

**Test in this order:**
1. Headline (most important)
2. Image
3. Subheadline

#### Hormozi's Own Headline Testing (This Book)
- Round 1: "Advertising" vs "Promotion" → Advertising won
- Round 2: "Advertising" vs "Leads" → Leads won
- Round 3: "Marketing" vs "Leads" → Leads won

#### Subheadline Tests
- "How to get strangers to want to buy your stuff" beat "How to get more people to want to buy your stuff"
- "How to get strangers to want to buy your stuff" beat "Get strangers to want to buy your stuff"
- Small changes (adding "how to," removing "more") make big differences

> *"You don't need a lot of votes to get a directional idea."*

If you have any audience, run polls. If not, post on every platform and ask for "1" or "2" replies. If not, message people directly. There's always a way.

### Step 5: Make It Easy To Consume

> *"People prefer to do things that take less effort. So if we want more people to take us up on our lead magnet, and consume it, we gotta make it easy. You can see 2x, 3x, and even 4x+ increases in take rates and consumption simply by making it easier to consume."*

Per delivery method:
- **Software**: Mobile + computer + multiple formats
- **Information**: Watching + reading + listening — offer them all
- **Services**: More times of day, more days/week, more channels (video call, phone, in-person)
- **Physical products**: Easy to order, fast to deliver, simple to open, simple directions (Apple example: packaging so good people keep the boxes)

> *"Fun fact: My book $100M Offers has a near perfect ¼, ¼, ¼, ¼ split between ebooks, physical books, audiobooks, and videos."*

Making it available in multiple formats yields 2-3-4× the leads for the same work.

### Step 6: Make It Darn Good — Give Away The Secrets, Sell The Implementation

> *"The marketplace judges everything you have to offer — free or not. And you can never provide too much value. But, you can provide too little."*

The lead magnet should provide more value than the **cost of your core offer** before they've bought it.

The fear: "If I give away my secrets, no one will pay me."

The reality: If you give away sucky fluff, people decide *this person sucks* and tell others not to buy from you. The vicious cycle of low-quality free content destroys reputation.

> *"People buy stuff based on how much value they think they'll get after they buy it. And the easiest way to get them to think they'll get tons of value after they buy is . . . drum roll please . . . to provide them with value before they buy."*

> *"99% of people aren't gonna buy, but they will create (or destroy) your reputation based on the value of your free stuff."*

### Step 7: Make It Easy For Them To Tell You They Want More — The CTA

A Call To Action tells the audience what to do next. Good CTAs have **two components**:

1. **What to do** (call this number, click this button, give this info, book this call, etc.)
2. **Reasons to do it right now** (urgency mechanic)

#### Three Reasons That Work

**a) Scarcity** — fewer available means more wanted
- "The most convenient class times fill up fast. Call now to get the one you want."
- "I can only handle five people per week, so if you want it solved soon, do XYZ..."
- "We only printed one batch of shirts and will never reprint this design."

Use **ethical/real scarcity**: if you actually can only handle 5 customers/week, say so.

**b) Urgency** — limited time window
- "Our July 4th promotion ends Monday at midnight."
- "Through Friday, I'll throw in a free hat to anyone who buys more than three books."

**c) Fraternity Party Planner (Hormozi's favorite)** — Make Up A Reason
- Fraternities party for any reason: "John got his wisdom teeth removed → kegger!" "Margherita Monday!" "Toga Tuesdays!"
- Harvard study: people let strangers cut in line more often if any reason was given — *even bad ones*
- "Because moms know best."
- "Because your country needs you."
- "Because it's my birthday and I want you to celebrate with me."

> *"Don't be clever, be clear."*

## The Economics

> *"Even if your lead magnet costs money to deliver, it should still lower your cost to get a new customer."*

Hormozi's worked example:

**Scenario A — Advertise Core Offer Directly:**
- $10,000 profit on core offer
- $1,000 ad spend to get a call
- 1/3 close → $3,000 CAC
- 3.3× return

**Scenario B — Advertise Free Lead Magnet First:**
- Lead magnet costs $25 to deliver
- Because free, more people engage
- $75 in advertising to get on a call
- $100 total per call (including the magnet)
- 1/10 buy core offer → $1,000 CAC
- **10× return — triple the business at same ad budget**

> *"This is where experienced business owners beat newbies. With a $25 budget to deliver your lead magnet, you can provide FAR more value than a $0 budget."*

The lead magnet that costs money to deliver outperforms the free-to-deliver one because it can be **so much better**.

---

## The 4-Point Lead Magnet Standard

A good lead magnet does four things:

1. **Engages ideal customers when they see it.**
2. **Gets more people to engage than your core offer alone.**
3. **Is valuable enough that they consume it.**
4. **Makes the right people more likely to buy.**

---

## Connection To $100M Offers

The 7-Step Lead Magnet Framework is functionally the same as the [[5-step-grand-slam-creation]] process applied to a free/cheap product:
- Pick the avatar (see [[lost-chapter-avatar-refinement]])
- Identify the dream outcome (narrow version)
- Identify the problems en route
- Solutions = lead magnet content
- Delivery vehicle = how you give it
- Stack value = make it darn good
- Name it well = test the wrapper
- Reverse risk + add urgency = the CTA

Lead magnets are Grand Slam Offers at the entry point of your funnel.
