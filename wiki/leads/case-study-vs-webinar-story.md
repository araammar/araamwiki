# Story: The Case Study That Replaced The Webinar
tags: [leads, Hormozi, story, lead-magnets, origin]

## Summary

The origin story of Hormozi's understanding of lead magnets. The moment he discovered that **what he thought people wanted (a polished webinar)** was different from **what they actually wanted (a no-frills behind-the-scenes case study)**.

Setting: April 2016. Hormozi had paid $25,000 to be in a marketing group. His mentor told him: *"Do a webinar every week until you make a million dollars. Until then, don't ask me about anything else."*

---

## The Original Setup (8 Weeks Of Failure)

The webinar concept overwhelmed him:
- Landing pages
- Registration pages
- Follow-up emails
- Replay emails
- Cart close emails
- Presentation software
- Website integration
- Writing ads
- Making ad creative
- Targeting decisions
- Building payment pages
- Processing payments
- And making the actual webinar

He started with what he understood — a landing page (modeled after his mentor's). Then a thank you page (took an entire Sunday). Then the rest of the funnel.

**Eight Sundays later**, the green "active" indicator appeared next to his $150/day ad campaign.

**Three days, $450, 80 leads, and 0 sales later** — he shut it all down.

> *"No one even watched my webinar. Meanwhile, that guy posted again about how much money he was making off this webinar stuff. Why do I suck so much?"*

---

## The Accidental Discovery

Scrolling his newsfeed in defeat, an ad caught his eye:

> *"Free Case Study on How I Spent $1 and Made $123,000 in a Weekend"*

He punched in his email, watched the video. **No slides. No presenting. Just a dude explaining how his stuff worked.**

> *"This, I can do."*

He fired up his screen recorder and made a 13-minute video:

> *"Okay everyone. So here's the ad account of a gym we just launched. Here are the ads we ran. This is how much we spent. We sent them to this page with this offer. You can see how many leads we got here. They got this many people scheduled. This many showed. This is how many they sold. This is how much the gym owner made. This is everything we did. If you want help setting something like this up, we'll do the whole thing for free. And we only get paid off the sales you make. If that sounds fair, book a call."*

He swapped the webinar out for the video and changed the headline:

> *"FREE Case Study: How we added 213 members and $112,000 in revenue to a small gym in San Diego."*

Booking page on the next step. New ad campaign live. He went to bed.

---

## The Next Morning

Leila woke him: *"Alex . . . what did you do?"*

> *"Strangers have booked my calendar solid for like the next week."*

The campaign produced an avalanche of engaged leads while he slept.

---

## The Lesson

> *"They didn't want my webinar. But they did want my case study."*

> *"This accidental discovery showed me how getting leads actually works . . . you have to give people something they want. The best part is — it's easier than you think."*

The webinar was what *Hormozi's mentor* believed worked, what *Hormozi himself believed* people wanted, what the broader marketing community pushed. It failed.

The case study was a no-frills, behind-the-scenes, raw recording of how something actually happened. It worked instantly.

---

## What This Story Establishes

This is the foundational story for the entire lead magnets section of $100M Leads. It establishes:

1. **People don't always want what you think they want.** Test, don't assume.
2. **Polished doesn't always beat raw.** Authenticity converts.
3. **A lead magnet is something they want now**, not what you think they should want later.
4. **Specificity sells.** "$1 → $123,000" or "213 members + $112K in San Diego" beats generic claims.
5. **A 13-minute video can outperform 8 weeks of webinar production.** Effort ≠ effectiveness.

---

## How It Maps To The 7-Step Lead Magnet Framework

Re-reading the case study through the [[lead-magnets-framework]] lens:

| Step | Hormozi's Application |
|---|---|
| 1. Problem & who | Gym owners want more members; he picks one specific gym story |
| 2. How to solve | Type 1: Reveal Problem ("here's how someone else solved it, here's the gap you have") |
| 3. Delivery | Information delivery via video |
| 4. Name | Tested headline with specific numbers (213 members, $112K, San Diego) |
| 5. Easy to consume | 13-minute video, no slides |
| 6. Darn good | Full transparency — ad account, spend, leads, schedules, shows, sales, owner revenue |
| 7. CTA | "Book a call" with implicit performance-pricing reverse-risk ("only get paid off the sales you make") |

The original webinar attempt failed every one of these. The case study nailed all 7.

---

## The Bigger Implication

> *"I learned an important lesson. They didn't want my webinar. But they did want my case study."*

Hormozi spent **8 weeks building the wrong thing** because that's what authorities told him to build. He spent **one afternoon** building the right thing because he paid attention to what was working in his actual newsfeed.

The implication for any reader: **stop building what marketing pundits told you to build. Start building what makes your actual prospects raise their hand.**

This is the same lesson [[logical-vs-psychological-solutions]] teaches from the offers side — the logical solution (polished webinar) had been tried. The psychological solution (raw behind-the-scenes case study with specific numbers) was the unlock.

---

## Application Notes

When designing a lead magnet:

1. **Look at what's working in your own newsfeed.** If something hooked you, study why.
2. **Try the rough version before the polished version.** A 13-minute screen recording can outperform an 8-week webinar build.
3. **Lean into specificity.** "$1 → $123K in a weekend" with a real case beats "How to make money advertising."
4. **Show your work transparently.** Open ad accounts, show numbers, walk through the actual mechanics.
5. **Test in days, not weeks.** Run the cheap version first. Validate. Then invest.
