# Framework: Warm Outreach — The 10-Step System
tags: [leads, Hormozi, warm-outreach, core-four, framework]

## Summary / Definition

The first of the Core Four. Warm Outreach = 1-to-1 contact with your warm audience (people who know you).

> *"It's the cheapest and easiest way to find people interested in the stuff you sell. It's super effective — and most businesses don't do it. Don't be like most businesses."*

— Alex Hormozi, *$100M Leads*

The starting point of every business Hormozi has ever built. Also the starting point he recommends for every reader.

---

## Why Start Here

> *"Also, you do have a warm audience, even if you don't know it. Everybody knows somebody. So your personal contacts are the easiest place to start."*

The book opens this chapter with Hormozi's own first business (Free Training Project, 2013) — built entirely on warm outreach to people he already knew. **Six initial yeses became referrals; referrals became the $4,000/mo income that replaced his job.**

That little business was built on:
- A tax ID
- A bank account
- A way to take payments
- A way to communicate with people

The last one is what warm outreach uses.

## The 10 Steps

### Step 1: Get Your List ("But I don't have any leads...")

Everyone has a list. Pull from:

- **Your phone contacts** — each one has given you permission
- **Every email account you've ever used** — pull contacts + address lists
- **All your social media profiles** — followers, subscribers, friends, connections

> *"Add up all your contacts from all the platforms. Seriously, figure out the number. Between your phone, email, social media, and other platforms you will have more than enough contacts to get started. For many of you, this will be your first 1000 leads."*

### Step 2: Pick A Platform ("But I don't know where to start...")

Pick the platform with the most contacts. Phone, email, social media, mail. Doesn't matter — you'll hit them all eventually.

### Step 3: Personalize Your Greeting ("But what do I say?")

Use something you know about the contact as your actual reason to reach out. If you don't have personal info, glance at their social media profiles.

> *"Don't be a weirdo. Pay your social dues. Remember, you haven't asked for anything. You're just checking in and providing value. So . . . relax."*

Example: *"Saw you just had a baby! Congrats! How is the baby doing? How are you?"*

### Step 4: Reach Out To 100 People Per Day

> *"To get what you want, you have to deserve what you want."*
>
> — Charlie Munger (quoted by Hormozi)

100 personalized messages per day. Across calls, texts, emails, DMs, postcards, etc. Up to 3 attempts per person (once per day for 3 days, or once per week for physical mail), or until they respond — whichever comes first.

### Step 5: Reply Using The ACA Framework

When someone responds, use **A-C-A**:

- **A**cknowledge what they said. Restate it in your own words.
  - *"Two kids. And you're an accountant..."*
- **C**ompliment them on whatever they tell you. Tie to a positive character trait.
  - *"...Wow! Supermom! So hardworking! Managing a full-time career and two kids..."*
- **A**sk another question. Lead the conversation toward a topic closer to your offer.
  - Therapy/Coaching: *"...Do you get time for yourself?"*
  - Fitness: *"...Do you have time to get workouts in?"*
  - Cleaning Services: *"...Do you have anyone who helps you keep the house tidy?"*

> *"People love talking about themselves. So let them. They also love to be complimented, so do that too."*

### Step 6: Make Them An Offer ("How do I know if they're interested?")

After ~3-4 exchanges (or 3-4 minutes in person), make an offer. Use the [[value-equation]] from $100M Offers:
- **Dream Outcome** — state best possible results, ideally with relatable case study
- **Perceived Likelihood** — proof, results, reviews, guarantees
- **Time Delay** — how fast results come, how often
- **Effort & Sacrifice** — what they can keep doing, what they can avoid

#### The Script Template

> *"By the way, do you know anybody who is (describe their struggles) looking to (dream outcome) in (time delay)? I'm taking on five case studies for free, because that's all I can handle. I just want to get some testimonials for my service/product. I help them (dream outcome) without (effort and sacrifice). It works. I even guarantee people get (dream outcome) or I work with them until they do. I just had a girl named XXX work with me (dream outcome) even though she (describe the same struggle your contact has). I also had another guy who (dream outcome) and it was his first time. I'd just like more testimonials to show it works across different scenarios. Does anyone you like come to mind?"*

If they say no: *"Haha, well . . . does anyone you hate come to mind?"* (Breaks awkwardness.)

#### The Mechanic Underneath

> *"There's an important feature here. We're not asking them to buy anything. We're asking if they know anyone. And of the people who say yes, most say they are interested."*

By asking about **someone else**, you eliminate pressure on the prospect. They either:
- Express interest themselves
- Refer you to someone who might
- Do both

All three outcomes are wins.

#### The Short Version

If space is limited (text, DM, short email):

> *"I help (ideal customer) get (dream outcome) in (time period) without (effort and sacrifice) and (proof / increased perceived likelihood)."*

### Step 7: Make It Easy To Say Yes — Make It Free

> *"After people show interest, make your offer easy to say yes to. I like to start with the easiest offer enhancer in the world — FREE."*

Script:

> *"Since I'm only taking on five people, I can give you all the attention you need to get brag-worthy results. And I'll give it all for free so long as you promise to: 1) Use it 2) Give me feedback and 3) Leave a killer review if you think it deserves one. Does that sound fair?"*

#### Why First Five Free

Whenever you launch a new product or service — make the first five free. The exact number matters less than the reasons:

1. **You get reps** — calms nerves, builds the skill
2. **You probably suck (for now)** — people are forgiving when free
3. **You suck less by doing more** — guinea pigs let you iterate
4. **Free customers create paying customers via:**
   - Positive reviews and testimonials
   - Detailed feedback
   - Referrals to paying friends

> *"If people get value, especially for free, they're far more likely to: leave positive reviews and testimonials, give you feedback, send their friends and family."*

#### Free Customers Make You Money Three Ways

1. They convert into paying customers themselves
2. They send referrals
3. Their testimonials bring in paying customers

#### What If They Say No (To Free)?

> *"Often, the most expensive part of what you sell isn't the price — it's the hidden costs."*

Hidden costs = time, effort, sacrifice (the bottom of the [[value-equation]]).

If you can't even give it away free, ask:

> *"What would I have to do to make it worth it for you to continue?"*

Their answer gives you ammunition. Either you solve their objection and they buy, or you learn what to address with the next prospect.

> *"Yeses give me opportunity. Nos give me feedback. Either way, I win."*

### Step 8: Start Back At The Top

After reaching out to all leads on platform #1, switch to platform #2. Then platform #3.

> *"Let's say you follow this to the tee because being poor sucks more than helping people for free. If between all platforms, you have 1000 leads, that gives you ten solid days of work. A month of work including follow-ups."*

By the end, five-plus people will have accepted your free offer. Some will convert. Those will refer.

### Step 9: Start Charging

Litmus test: when people start referring, start charging.

Progression:
- Original script: *"...free..."*
- After first 5: *"80% off for the next 5"*
- After next 5: *"60% off for the next 5"*
- *"40% off for the next 5"*
- Continue raising 20% every 5 customers until you find the sweet spot

> *"The 'I increase my prices every five' rule also adds urgency because prices actually go up."*

### Step 10: Keep Your List Warm

Give regular value through email, social media, etc. A warm list stays primed for future reach outs.

Once warmed for a while, probe with **Dean Jackson's 9-word email** (see [[9-word-email]]):

> *"Are you still looking to [4 word desire]?"*

No images. No frills. No links. Just a question.

The replies are your next round of high-priority warm outreach.

## Benchmarks

> *"Warm reach outs should get about one in five contacts to engage. So one hundred warm reach outs should get about twenty replies. Of the twenty who reply, another one in five-ish will take you up on your free offer. So, four people. Of the four who take your free offer now, you should be able to convert one into some sort of paid offer later."*

The funnel:
- 100 reach outs → 20 replies → 4 take free → 1 paying customer

You can predict revenue by reverse-engineering this math.

---

## The $100K/Year Math

> *"This process alone can take you to $100,000+ per year with nothing else."*

Worked example:
- 500 reach outs per week → 5 paying customers per week (at 1% conversion)
- $400 average sale × 5 customers/week × 52 weeks = **$104,000/year**

> *"Which, as of this writing, is still two times the median household income in the US. Not bad."*

---

## The Time/Skill Investment

> *"You'll learn more in the first ten days of doing 100 reach outs than you did from everything you've ever read or watched."*

This isn't a skill you read — it's a skill you do. The first 100 are awkward. The next 100 are better. By 1,000, you've internalized the pattern.

---

## When To Stop Doing Warm Outreach

You don't. You just shift it.

> *"Warm reach outs have two limitations. The first is time. When you're starting out, getting new customers should take the majority of your time. Think four hours per day, minimum."*

> *"The second limiter is the number of people who know you. You'll eventually 'run out.' Don't worry though. We can get more."*

The fix: combine warm outreach with [[content-unit-framework|Posting Content]] (which grows your warm audience).
