# Concept: Engaged Leads — The True Output Of Advertising
tags: [leads, Hormozi, definitions, foundational, advertising]

## Summary / Definition

The book opens with a confession: Hormozi spent six months trying to define what a "lead" actually is. The exercise produced the foundational distinction that drives every subsequent chapter.

> **Lead** = a person you can contact.
>
> **Engaged Lead** = someone who *shows* interest in the stuff you sell.

> *"Engaged leads are the true output of advertising."*

— Alex Hormozi, *$100M Leads*

---

## Why The Distinction Matters

> *"Words matter because they affect how we think. How we think affects what we do. And if words have us thinking the wrong way, then we will probably do the wrong stuff. I hate doing the wrong stuff."*

If you measure "leads" generically, you count phone numbers in a list, footsteps past a billboard, emails on a spreadsheet — none of which is moving you toward revenue.

If you measure **engaged leads**, you count the moments where a stranger raised their hand and said *"tell me more."* This is the unit that compounds into customers and revenue.

---

## What Counts As A Lead

A "lead" by Hormozi's definition is any person you have the means and permission to contact:

- A purchased email list → leads
- Contact info scraped from a website → leads
- Numbers in your phone → leads
- People on the street → leads (proximity counts as a contact channel)
- Anyone whose contact info you have, in any form → leads

Note: **lead ≠ qualified buyer.** A lead is just an address you can reach.

---

## What Counts As An Engaged Lead

A lead becomes an *engaged* lead the moment they show interest in what you sell:

- They give their contact information on your website
- They follow you on social media (and you can contact them there)
- They reply to your email campaign
- They click your ad and submit a form
- They text "YES" to your CTA
- They walk in your door asking about the product
- They schedule a call

**Engagement is the act of raising a hand.** Without it, you don't have a true lead — you have a name.

---

## The Implication For Everything That Follows

Once you accept this definition, three implications fall out:

1. **Advertising's job is to produce engaged leads** — not awareness, not reach, not impressions. Engaged leads.
2. **Every advertising method should be measured in engaged leads** — not vanity metrics.
3. **The cost-per-engaged-lead becomes the foundational economic unit** of any lead generation system.

This is why every Core Four chapter in the book has a benchmarks section measured in engaged leads — and why Hormozi's whole [[paid-ads-money-mechanics|LTGP:CAC]] framework starts from this unit.

---

## How Engaged Leads Are Created

You don't create engaged leads by hoping someone notices you. You create them by **giving people a reason to raise their hand.**

The next question is: how do we get leads to engage?

The book's answer is two-part:

1. **Your core offer** (the main thing you sell) — sometimes this alone is compelling enough
2. **A lead magnet** (a free or low-cost narrow-problem solution) — when the core offer needs more lead-in

See [[lead-magnets-framework]] for the lead magnet system. See [[grand-slam-offer]] for the core offer system.

## Common Mistake: Confusing Leads And Engaged Leads

> *"Engaged leads are the true output of advertising."*

If you say "we got 5,000 leads from that campaign" but only 50 raised their hand, you got 50 leads of value — not 5,000. The other 4,950 are just names you have access to. They're worth zero unless you do something to engage them.

This is the same mistake the chiropractor in [[size-of-the-pie-fallacy]] makes — confusing "ad volume" with "lead production."

---

## Why The Book Couldn't Be Called "Engaged Leads"

> *"Getting more engaged leads is the point of this book. But I couldn't call the book 'engaged leads' because no one would get it."*

Hormozi titled the book *$100M Leads* for marketability but defines his real subject as **engaged leads** from chapter 1. Every subsequent chapter assumes you've internalized the distinction.

When this section of the wiki uses the word "lead," it means *engaged lead* unless explicitly noted otherwise — same convention as Hormozi's.

---

## Application

When designing any advertising activity, ask:

1. **What action will indicate engagement?** (Click, submit, reply, walk in, etc.)
2. **How will we count those actions?** (Tracking, attribution)
3. **What's the cost per engaged lead at this rate?** (Spend ÷ engaged leads)
4. **What's the conversion of engaged lead → customer?** (To get cost per customer)
5. **What's the LTGP per customer?** (To compare to CAC and decide if it's profitable)

This sequence runs through every page in this section.
