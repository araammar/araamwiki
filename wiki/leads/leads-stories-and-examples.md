## The Big Origin Stories

### March 2017 — The $150K Refund Disaster + Boise Gym Pivot

Two gyms refunded $150,000 of customers in a week:
- One owner stood on a chair telling customers to refund and go home
- The other owner offered customers half price to refund and pay him instead

Leila: *"It's a hundred and fifty grand. We won't be able to pay my friends."*

The next morning at 3 AM, Hormozi scribbled ideas. None of his planned moves would generate $150K in 30 days.

**The breakthrough**: a phone call from a Boise gym owner who refused to take no for an answer. Hormozi quoted **$6,000** as the "biggest number I could think of to get him off the phone." The owner said yes.

> *"What. I stood there slack-jawed, frozen in disbelief. Six. Thousand. Dollars."*

Then he raised: $8,000 → yes. $10,000 → yes. **In a single day, all 8 planned gym launches converted to licensing deals = $60,000 in pure-profit cash.**

> *"I think we're still in the gym business . . . I think we were just doing it wrong."*

That month: **$215,000 profit.** First full year: **$6.82M revenue.** Year 2: **$25.9M revenue / $17M profit.** Built into **4,500+ gym locations.**

## Lead Magnet Stories

### Case Study That Replaced The Webinar (April 2016)

8 Sundays of webinar building → $450 + 0 sales. Scrolling his feed in defeat, Hormozi saw an ad: *"Free Case Study on How I Spent $1 and Made $123,000 in a Weekend."*

He recorded a 13-min screen video walking through one gym's actual ad account. Swapped the webinar URL for the case study URL. Went to bed.

Next morning Leila: *"Strangers have booked my calendar solid for like the next week."*

> *"They didn't want my webinar. But they did want my case study."*

## Warm Outreach Stories

### The 2013 Free Training Project

Hormozi at 23, with $51,128.13 in savings and a Harvard MBA application in hand. He chose: quit the job and start a business.

LLC: "Impetus Group LLC" (someone heard "Impotence" → renamed to "The Free Training Project").

He messaged friends:
> *"Hey, do you know anyone who's trying to get into shape? I'm training people for free for twelve weeks. On top of that, I'll make them a custom nutrition plan and grocery list. All they have to do is donate to a charity of their choice and let me use their testimonial."*

**6 yeses** (2 high school friends + 1 college friend + 3 of their referrals). When the 12 weeks ended: *"I asked them to pay me instead. I was the charity now."*

The referrals produced 5-6 more clients. **$4,000/mo income** replaced his job salary.

> *"That little business made about $4000 per month and replaced the income from my first job."*

### The 78% / 2-Long-Form-Content Insight (January 2020)

Paid ads stopped performing. Team debated copy, creative, offer. Leila asked: *"What did we stop doing in the months before the decline?"*

Unanimous answer: Hormozi stopped making gym-specific content.

He surveyed clients:

> **78% of all clients had consumed at least TWO long-form pieces of content prior to booking a call.**

> *"I had fallen into my old ways and given paid ads all the credit. But, our free content was nurturing the demand."*

## Paid Ads Stories

### Sam's Gym + The $1,000 Facebook Bet (June 2013)

Hormozi convinced Sam, his gym mentor (where he worked for minimum wage), to bet $1,000 on Facebook ads.

> *"I'll give you a thousand bucks to play with. If you lose it, then you have to shut up about this Facebook stuff. If you make more, I'll split the profit with you."*

The ad — the ugliest you've ever seen:
> *"I'M LOOKING FOR 5 CHINO HILLS RESIDENTS TO TAKE PLACE IN A FREE 6 WEEK CHALLENGE."* (ALL CAPS, no images.)

Result: **19 sales × $299 = $5,700 from $1,000.** Sam wrote Hormozi a check for $2,500 (more than half).

> *"Nice work, Hormozi. Do it again."*

The "6-week challenge" became the biggest gym promotion for 7 years — driving **$1.5B+ in revenue.**

## Employee Stories

### The 40-Interviews-Per-Hire Diagnosis (June 2021)

Quarterly meeting. Cold outreach team missed goal 2 quarters in a row. Hormozi traced the bottleneck through HR:
- Lose a rep every 4 weeks → hiring can't keep up
- Hire 1 in 4 candidates → ~1 candidate/week
- 1 qualified in 10 screening interviews → **40 interviews per hire**

The fix: switch from 1-on-1 to **group interviews**. Look for crazies there. Push everyone else with work ethic + basic social skills to sales.

Within 6 weeks: hiring outpaced churn. Cold outreach sales **doubled** in one quarter.

→ Concept: [[employees-as-lead-getters]]

## Execution Stories

### The 300 Flyers (June 2014)

Mentor suggested flyers. Hormozi printed **300**. Got one call — *"You scratched my Mercedes."* He hung up.

Called the mentor frustrated. Mentor: *"I test with 5,000 in a single day. Then 5,000 per day every day for a month."*

> *"I was doing 1/1500th the level of effort required to make a flyer campaign work — I just didn't know it."*

The lesson:
> *"I promised myself I would never let effort be the reason anything didn't work for me."*

### Cool Kids Meet-Up + The Permission To Fail (June 2017)

Three months after switching Gym Launch to licensing, Hormozi was invited to an 8-figures-and-up entrepreneur event (10 people, private resort, additional fee). He and Leila couldn't really afford it. They went anyway.

The first speaker (a "$3M/mo" guy in yoga clothes) presented over Hormozi's head. He felt like a fraud.

Q&A. Someone asked what courses the speaker took.

The answer that changed Hormozi's life:

> *"At this point, I don't expect to learn anything new from courses. I have to learn by doing. And I 'do' by spending a percentage of my revenue to test new campaigns, new channels, new pages, or just plain crazy ideas. So whether it's 1%, 5%, or 10%, set some percentage of your advertising budget aside to try new things without expecting a return. Consider it an investment in your education."*

> *"None of this is magic. If he can do this, so can I."*

The next week, Hormozi tripled his advertising budget. From $400K (June) → $780K (July) → $1M → $1.2M → $1.5M/mo → $1.8M (after adding text follow-up) → $2.5M+ (paid ads testing) → $4M+ (after affiliates).

> *"You either win or you learn."*

→ Concept: [[open-to-goal-vs-rule-of-100]], [[seven-levels-of-advertisers]]

## The Chiropractor Saturation Story

A business owner at one of Hormozi's events claimed he'd saturated the chiropractor niche at $2M/yr revenue, $30K/mo on Facebook ads only.

Hormozi's questions:
- Other platforms? **None.**
- Content for chiropractors? **None.**
- Cold outreach? **None.**

> *"And the $30k you spend, on one platform, for a two million dollar business, saturated the $15.1B chiropractor industry? Does that sound reasonable?"*

A second chiropractor in the audience volunteered: *"I'm in the chiropractor niche too, and I spent $30k in advertising, across four platforms, last week..."*

> *"He got the point."*
