# Framework: The Give:Ask Ratio
tags: [leads, Hormozi, content, monetization, give-ask, Gary Vaynerchuk]

## Summary / Definition

The ratio of **value-giving content** to **asks** (offers, CTAs, promotions) when monetizing an audience. Hormozi's central principle:

> **"Give until they ask."**

A slight evolution on Gary Vaynerchuk's "jab, jab, jab, right hook" — instead of a fixed cadence, you keep giving until the audience itself asks how to buy from you.

---

## The Ratio Across Platforms

Hormozi cites the well-studied baseline:

| Platform / Stage | Give:Ask Ratio |
|---|---|
| **Television** | 3.5:1 (47 min content / 13 min ads per hour) |
| **Facebook newsfeed** | 4:1 (4 posts per 1 ad) |
| **Growing platforms** | ~10:1+ (massive over-give) |

> *"This gives us an idea of the minimum give:ask ratio we can sustain. After all, television and Facebook are mature platforms. They care less about growing their audiences and care more about making money from them."*

The implication: **mature platforms give less and ask more. Growing platforms give far more and ask rarely.** If you want to grow, model the growing platforms.

---

## The "Give Until They Ask" Strategy

> *"People are always waiting for you to ask for money. And when you don't, they trust you more. They share your stuff more. You grow faster, etc. But I'm not some altruistic saint. I'm here to make money."*

> *"So, it's simple. If you give enough, people start asking you."*

The mechanic:
- Constant value-giving creates a goodwill imbalance in your audience's minds
- People are wired to reciprocate (they get uncomfortable receiving without giving back)
- They reach out to you — DM, email, your website — to ask for the next step
- You give in public, **ask in private**
- The audience self-selects when they're ready to spend money

> *"That's why, in my opinion, give until they ask is the best strategy. But, if you feel like asking, I get it."*

The strategy produces **the best customers**: the ones who feel they got the better end of the deal even after paying.

---

## The Cost Of Asking Too Early

> *"The moment you start asking for money is the moment you decide to slow down your growth. So the more patient you are, the more you will get when you finally make your ask."*

Hormozi tells the story of a friend whose podcast blew up quickly. Eager to monetize, the friend made offers (asks) too frequently *inside* the content. The podcast **stopped growing — then actually shrank.**

> *"Don't be like that. Don't kill your golden goose."*

The cost of asking too early isn't just slowed growth. The audience can actively shrink — you go backwards.

---

## When You Must Ask — Two Methods

For situations where you genuinely need revenue now, Hormozi gives two structures:

### Method 1: Integrated Asks

The ask lives inside the content piece.

> *"You can advertise in every piece of content so long as you keep your give:ask ratio high."*

Example math: an hour-long podcast with 3 × 30-second ads = 58.5 min give to 1.5 min ask = well above 3:1.

**Where to integrate:** Hormozi prefers placing CTAs:
- After a valuable moment (when goodwill is peaked)
- At the end of the content piece

Add one location first. Watch audience growth. If it doesn't slow, add the second. Iterate.

### Method 2: Intermittent Asks

The ask gets its own piece. The give pieces are 100% give.

> *"You make many pieces of content of pure 'gives' then occasionally make an 'ask' piece."*

Example: 10 "give" posts, then on the 11th, promote your stuff.

### Choosing Between Methods

> *"On short platforms, the intermittent way will dominate. On long-form platforms, integrations are often your best bet."*

- **Short-form content** (tweets, Reels, Shorts, TikTok) → intermittent
- **Long-form content** (podcasts, YouTube longs, newsletters) → integrated

## What To Ask For

When you do ask, you advertise one of two things:

1. **Your lead magnet** (lower risk, builds the engaged-lead list first)
2. **Your core offer** ("go for the jugular" — direct path to money)

> *"If you're not sure, do the lead magnet. It's lower risk."*

### Lead Magnet Ask Example

> *"I have 11 more tips that have helped me do this. Go to my site to grab a pretty visual of them."*

Then the thank-you page after lead magnet opt-in displays the paid offer.

> *"Bonus points if your lead magnet is relevant to your content advertising it."*

### Core Offer Ask Example

> *"I'm looking for 5 (specific avatar) to help achieve (dream outcome) in (time delay). The best part is, you don't have to (effort and sacrifice). And if you don't get (dream outcome), I will do two things: 1) I will hand you your money back 2) I will work with you until you do. I do this because I want everyone to have an amazing experience with us and because I'm confident I can deliver on my promise. If that sounds fair, DM me/book a call/comment below/reply to this email/etc."*

This uses the [[value-equation]] structure plus risk reversal.

---

## The Underlying Logic

> *"You deposit goodwill with rewarding content, then withdraw from it by making offers."*

Goodwill is a balance. Content adds to it. Asks subtract from it. If you withdraw faster than you deposit, the balance goes negative — your audience tunes out, unfollows, or actively reduces engagement (the platform algorithm notices).

The healthy balance is **always running a surplus** — depositing more than you withdraw.

---

## The "Give in Public, Ask in Private" Principle

> *"When you use this strategy, you give in public, ask in private. You let the audience self-select when they're ready to give you money."*

Public asks (large promotions, sales events, launches) work too — but the *give until they ask* strategy front-loads private asks first. The audience that DMs you to ask for the next step is far higher intent than the audience that responds to a public CTA.

---

## Why This Matters For Caret/Colandrum

The give:ask principle applies directly to any business building an audience:

- **The first 6 months should be give-heavy** — establish trust, expertise, identity
- **Once the audience starts asking** ("how do I work with you?" DMs / "what's your offer?" comments / "can I buy that?" emails), you have permission to ask publicly
- **Even then, private asks (DMs) outperform public ones** because they meet the prospect at their moment of expressed intent
