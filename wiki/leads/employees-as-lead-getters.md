## The Origin Story — 40 Interviews Per Hire

June 2021. Quarterly review meeting. The cold outreach team had missed its goal two quarters in a row. Sales director's defense: *"I don't think we need to change anything, we'll hit it this quarter."*

Hormozi pushed:

- Why missed two quarters? → Lose a rep every 4 weeks
- HR says churn is below industry average → True, but hiring can't keep up
- Hiring 1 out of 4 candidates → ~1 candidate per week
- HR: 1 qualified out of 10 screening interviews → 40 interviews per single hire

> *"We're bottlenecked at the one-on-one screening. Start interviewing in groups and look for crazies there. Push everyone else with a good work ethic and basic social skills over to sales. We can teach the rest."*

Within 6 weeks: hiring outpaced churn. Cold outreach sales **doubled** in one quarter. Made up more than half of total sales.

> *"The issue wasn't our cold outreach method, skills, or offer at all. We just didn't have enough people doing cold outreach."*

## Why Employees Make You Wealthy (Not Just Rich)

> *"You get rich from what you make. You become wealthy from what you own."*

### Two Scenarios

**Scenario 1**: $5M revenue, $2M profit. You work around the clock. Business burns down if you take a vacation.
- You have a high-paying job
- Business is worth almost nothing to anyone else
- Net worth = mostly the cash you've saved

**Scenario 2**: Same $5M revenue, $2M profit. **Business runs without you.**
- Time back → can invest, buy other businesses, take vacations
- $2M profit business worth $10M+ to an investor
- Net worth = cash + a $10M+ asset

> *"Learning how to get other people to do it for you makes a $10,000,000 difference to your net worth. I'd say it's worth learning how to do it."*

This is the fundamental wealth equation of the book. Employees aren't a cost — they're the conversion mechanism from "high-paying job" to "valuable asset."

## Everything Hormozi Thought He Knew About Employees Was Wrong

> *"Have you ever heard . . .*
> *• If you want it done right, you gotta do it yourself.*
> *• No one can do it like I do it.*
> *• Nobody can replace me.*

> *I have. I said all that stuff. I lived all that stuff."*

For years, every hire became a competition: "me vs them." Gloating when better than them. Complaining when they weren't as good. Never noticing he was the one who hired and trained them.

> *"My entire workforce always ended up looking like a ragtag group of misfits who could kind of do one of the many things I could do. This got me up and running at first, but I fell into the trap of believing I was better than everyone else."*

The reality:
- Hormozi didn't have the skills to train or lead properly
- He was too poor (then too cheap) to hire anyone better
- It was his fault they sucked

### The Belief Switch

Old beliefs → New beliefs:
- "If you want it done right, do it yourself" → **"If you want it done right, get someone to spend all their time doing it."**
- "Nobody can do it like I do it" → **"If I can do it, someone else can do it better."**
- "Nobody can replace me" → **"Everyone is replaceable, especially me."**

> *"Trusting my employees to succeed made my time and attention far more valuable."*

The implication: **replace yourself as soon as you can.** Then make yourself useful somewhere else.

## The Internal Core Four

> *"The core four works for getting employees too."*

The mapping:

| Customers | Employees |
|---|---|
| Warm Outreach | Asking Your Network |
| Cold Outreach | Recruiting |
| Post Content | Posting Job Openings |
| Paid Ads | Promoting Job Postings |
| Customer Referrals | Employee Referrals |
| Affiliates | Associations, Guilds, Listservs |
| Agencies | Staffing Firms |
| Employees | Employees (unchanged) |

> *"By changing the frame from 'letting potential customers know about your stuff' to 'letting potential employees know about your stuff' it immediately turns into something you already know how to do."*

The implication: **you do not need a separate "HR strategy."** You apply the Core Four to hiring.

## How To Train Employees To Get Leads

### Option A: Hire People Who Already Know
"Awesome." Also "will cost more." If starting, may not be affordable.

### Option B: Hire And Train (Hormozi's Default)

> *"You have an entire book of lead-getting at your fingertips. So the next step is training your employees on how you do those lead-getting activities."*

The 3Ds Training Method:

#### Step 1 — Document
Write down the steps exactly as you do them. Better: have observers watch you do it. Best: record yourself doing it multiple ways across multiple shifts.

Then run a real work block following only your checklist. Can you do an A+ job following just the directions? If yes, you have the first draft.

#### Step 2 — Demonstrate
Do it in front of them. Sit, walk through the checklist step by step.

When they slow you down to understand something → adjust the checklist for that.

#### Step 3 — Duplicate
They do it in front of you. Same checklist, but now they're driving and you're observing.

If the checklist is right, the outcome will be the same. If it's off, you'll find out fast. Fix the checklist until it's right.

> *"Once they nail it, you now have a bonafide lead-getter on your payroll."*

### The "Vanish Test"

> *"If you vanished tomorrow, could a stranger get the results you get if they only followed your checklist? That's the level of clarity to shoot for."*

This is the clarity target. If the answer is no, the checklist needs more iteration.

## Training Best Practices

Hormozi's notes on training:

### Diagnosis Rule
> *"If they get it wrong or get confused then we got it wrong or made it confusing."*

If you need a long explanation for a step, the step is too complicated. More likely, you packed multiple steps into one.

### Competence vs Performance
You can know what to do and not be that good at it yet. *"Slow then smooth then fast."* They don't need new instructions, they need more reps.

### Focus On Direction-Following
> *"Focus on your employee's ability to follow directions more than whether they get the right result."*

The reasoning: if they follow directions and get the wrong result, **you know it's the directions.** That's a problem you can solve. If they don't follow directions, you don't know where the problem is.

### Praise + Correct
- Every successful step → praise them
- Goofs are OK; that's what training is for
- Don't take over for them; pause, step back, let them try again
- If they follow directions exactly and get the wrong result → **praise them for following, then fix the checklist on the spot**

### No Punishment During Training
> *"Reward the good stuff you want them to do more of and they'll do more of it. Learning a new skill is punishing enough."*

### One Step At A Time
You can't fix multiple things when you've never done something before. One piece of feedback per step. Master it. Move on.

### Retrain On Performance Dips
> *"Whenever there is a major dip from normal performance, retrain the team. They stopped doing an important step in the process (often because they didn't know it was important)."*

## How To Calculate Returns

Excluding paid ad costs, employee advertising cost = payroll.

The simple formula:

> **Total Payroll / Total Engaged Leads = Cost per engaged lead.**

Worked example:
- $100,000 payroll
- 1,000 engaged leads
- → $100 per engaged lead

If 1 in 10 leads converts:
- → CAC = $1,000

If LTGP = $4,000:
- → LTGP:CAC = 4:1 ✓

### Hormozi's Acquisition.com Numbers

At the time of writing:
- **~30,000 engaged leads/mo** at Acquisition.com
- $0 paid ads, $0 outreach
- Content team payroll: **~$100,000/mo**
- → $100,000 / 30,000 = **$3.33 per engaged lead**

> *"We make much more than $3.33 per lead, so we're profitable."*

## Diagnosing: Sales Problem Or Advertising Problem?

The single-question diagnostic:

> *"Do my engaged leads have the problem I solve and the money to spend?"*

- **No** → they're unqualified → **advertising problem**
- **Yes + buying but not enough of them** → **advertising problem (volume)**
- **Yes + qualified but not buying** → **sales problem**

> *"Don't fire your sales guy if you've got advertising problems. And equally, don't fire your advertising employees if you've got a sales problem."*

## Why The Big Money Lives In Employees

> *"It's your job to advertise and sell the vision of your company. You advertise it publicly and privately to employees and customers alike. That's the job. And once you get good at it, you become unstoppable."*

The bigger frame: **you advertise to two audiences** — customers (for revenue) and employees (for capacity). The same skill (the Core Four) works on both.

> *"Anyone can be taught to do 'ground level' jobs for any business — advertising or otherwise. So who you pick is not as important as how you train the ones you do."*

> *"It doesn't take a genius to advertise. I'd even say it hurts. We've got plenty more iron-wills than brainiacs anyways. This isn't about brains, it's about guts."*

---

## When To Be Picky

For low-level frontline jobs: hire and train almost anyone willing.

> *"Get picky when you have to make massive investments in hyper-specific-multiple-six-figure-C-suite employees."*

The picky bar matches the cost of mistake. Frontline = low; C-suite = enormous.
