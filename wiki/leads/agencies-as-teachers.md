# Framework: Agencies As Teachers (Not Service Providers)
tags: [leads, Hormozi, agencies, lead-getters, framework]

## Summary / Definition

The third Lead Getter. Hormozi's framework inverts the conventional way to use agencies. Instead of paying an agency to *do* the work forever, you pay them to *teach* you (and your team) how to do it — then graduate to consulting.

> *"After my first experience with an agency, that I mentioned earlier and went quite well, I decided to use more. But my experience with the next ten plus agencies was different because I used them 'the right way.'"*

— Alex Hormozi, *$100M Leads*

The chapter is two stories: the agency he accidentally used right the first time, and the 10+ agencies he used wrong before figuring out the system.

---

## The Origin Story — $750/Hour Lesson

Summer 2016. Hormozi had 5 gyms, opening his 6th. Facebook released new features (retargeting, interest groups, pixels). Courses confused him more.

He got 2 agency referrals. Heard horror stories: high cost, never works, traps you forever. The first agency was unaffordable. The second call was going the same way when Hormozi panicked:

> *"Can you just show me in a few hours how you would run ads on my account?"*
> *"No. My time's not for sale."*
> *"What sort of arrangement could we come to?"*

Eyebrow up, smirk:

> *"Fine. $750 an hour."*

Hormozi accepted. Paid for the first 4 hours up front — **$3,000 he didn't have.**

The structure:
- Meet 1 hour per week
- Hormozi takes notes, asks questions
- Records and rewatches every call
- Homework between calls

The arc:
- Calls 1-2: Agent drove, Hormozi watched
- Calls 3-4: Hormozi in driver's seat
- Calls 5-6: Clicked — Hormozi understood the decision logic and data tracking
- Calls 7-8: Hormozi realized he didn't need help anymore

> *"I had learned how to run paid ads, at least on Facebook, like a pro. And, if I had to make a guess, it was because I learned it . . . from a pro."*

**8 hours and $6,000 to learn a skill that has made him millions.**

## How Agencies Want You To Think They Work

The conventional model — Hormozi went through this 10+ times:

| Step | What Happens |
|---|---|
| 1 | They get me excited about all the new leads |
| 2 | Onboarding feels valuable (and sometimes is) |
| 3 | Their "best" senior rep assigned to my account |
| 4 | I see some results |
| 5 | Senior rep moves to the newest customer |
| 6 | Junior rep starts managing my account. Results suffer |
| 7 | I complain |
| 8 | Senior rep visits to make me feel better |
| 9 | Results still suffer. I cancel |
| 10 | I search for another agency and repeat |
| 11 | For the zillionth time — wonder why it's not like the first time |

The pattern: **the first time worked because Hormozi accidentally used the agency to learn.** Every subsequent time he used them to *do*. And was disappointed every time.

## The Reframe

> *"It's frankly ridiculous that it took me so many years to figure out that I actually used an agency the right way . . . the first time! But now, after playing their game so many times, I feel I cracked the 'how to use an agency' code. And it doesn't come from playing their game at all. It comes from playing a different one."*

The reframe in three steps:

1. Hiring an agency vs doing it yourself
2. How Hormozi uses agencies now
3. How to pick the right one

---

## When To Use Agencies

> *"Good agencies cost money. So if you have no money, then agencies are out of the question. You've gotta learn through trial and error."*

If you have money, **use agencies for two things**:

1. **Learning new methods** — they've already made the big mistakes
2. **Learning new platforms** — they do the early setup and maintenance, and teach you while doing it

> *"Hiring an agency is all about investing in important skills you can't really learn anywhere else."*

The alternative — DIY trial-and-error — costs the time and attention you could have used to learn the *other* things that scale your business.

---

## How To Use Agencies Right

The opening pitch Hormozi gives to every agency:

> *"I want to do what you do in my business, but I don't know how. I'd like to work with you for 6 months so I can learn how you do it. Plus, I'll pay extra for you to break down why you make the decisions you do and the steps you take to make them. Then, after I get a good idea of how it all works, I'll start training my team on it. And once they can do it well enough, I'd like to change to a lower cost consulting arrangement. This way, you can still help us if we run into problems. Are you opposed to this?"*

Most agencies are not opposed.

If they are → move on. If they need to negotiate → negotiate. At some price, it's worth it for both of you.

### The Two-Agency Strategy

For learning YouTube, Hormozi hired **two** agencies:

1. **The "good enough" agency** — kept him committed to making videos while doing some legwork on the platform
2. **The elite agency** (at 4× the price) — taught the in-depth ideas behind making the best content possible

> *"Once our videos beat their videos, we dropped down to consulting only."*

> *"I hire one 'good enough' agency to learn the ropes of a new platform. Then, I hire a more elite agency to learn how to maximize it — and I cannot recommend this strategy enough."*

### Why This Works For Both Parties

You get:
- Better short-term results (they probably know more than you)
- Better long-term results (you and/or your team can do it forever)
- Maximum time with their best reps (you're a current learning client, not a maintenance client)

The agency gets:
- A customer they otherwise might not have had
- Clear scope (6 months)
- An upgrade path (consulting after the transition)

### The Comparison Test

> *"Compare your team's results to the agency's until you beat them. Then, cancel the relationship and put the money into scaling everything you just learned."*

This is the trigger. You know it's time to graduate when your team consistently outperforms the agency.

## How To Pick The Right Agency (10 Criteria)

Hormozi's hard-won checklist:

1. **Somebody you know got good results with them** — best signal. If you only know them from paid ads/cold outreach, they probably aren't as good as ones that rely on word of mouth.

2. **Prominent companies got good results with them** — even if you don't know personally, recognized names are a good sign.

3. **A waiting list** — demand exceeds supply → probably good.

4. **Clear sales process that sets realistic expectations** — no funny business.

5. **No short-term hacks** — they keep talk on long-term strategy. Clear timelines for setup, scaling, results.

6. **They tell you exactly what they need, when, and how they'll use it.**

7. **They suggest a regular meeting schedule + multiple ways to update you.**

8. **They give updates in simple terms with clear tracking so you can compare costs and results.**

9. **They make a good offer:**
   - Dream outcome: is what they promise what you want?
   - Perceived likelihood: how many people like you have they gotten there?
   - Time delay: how long?
   - Effort & sacrifice: what do they require? Can you sustain it?

10. **They are expensive.** *"All good agencies are expensive . . . but not all expensive agencies are good. So talk with as many as it takes."*

> *"If an agency checks those boxes, they're worth considering."*

---

## The Cost-Benefit Math

> *"Even though this isn't the 'traditional' agency model, both businesses benefit. They get a customer they otherwise wouldn't have. And we get a money-making skill for life. In the story at the beginning of the chapter — it cost me eight hours and $6000 to learn a skill that's made me millions. Does that seem worth it to you? It better."*

To make this work at scale, expect to pay **both the agency and your team** for the same work for a period. This is intentional:
- Agency gets results while team learns
- Team learns by doing alongside (or under) the agency
- After the transition window, agency goes to consulting, team owns the work

> *"Yes, it costs a lot of money. And yes, it's totally worth it when you get it right."*

---

## Hormozi's Personal Track Record

> *"At first, it took about a year to get my team better than an agency. As I got better, it went down to ten months, then eight. And now, I've got it down. I can get my team as good or better than the agency in less than six months or less."*

The skill of using agencies improves with reps. Each use shortens the next.

---

## Next Steps Checklist

1. Decide if an agency makes sense for you right now
2. Talk to a lot of agencies to get a feel for the market. **Don't be cheap.**
3. Use the agreement framework above (the 6-month learning then consulting transition)
4. Set a clear deadline to force you and your team to learn the skills
5. Use both teams until yours beats theirs regularly
6. Switch to discounted consulting until you feel like you're teaching them — then cut them loose

---

## Application For Caret/Colandrum

If you wanted to scale paid ads or content for Colandrum:

1. **Pick a method/platform** you don't yet master (e.g., LinkedIn ads)
2. **Hire one "good enough" agency** that targets B2B SaaS — frame as a 6-month learning engagement with a transition plan
3. **Hire one "elite" agency** at 4× the price for the truly advanced layer
4. **Document everything** — every decision, every test, every campaign structure
5. **Train your team** alongside the agency from month 2
6. **Compare results** monthly
7. **Graduate to consulting** once your team consistently outperforms

The total cost might be $50K-$100K in agency fees. The skill stays in-house forever.
