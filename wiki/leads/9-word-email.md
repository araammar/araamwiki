# Concept: Dean Jackson's 9-Word Email
tags: [leads, Hormozi, Dean Jackson, warm-outreach, re-engagement, templates]

## Summary / Definition

A re-engagement template Hormozi credits to **Dean Jackson** and calls "timeless." Used in Step 10 of [[warm-outreach-system]] to probe a warmed list and identify who is currently in-market.

> *"Are you still looking to [4 word desire]?"*

That's it. No images. No frills. No links.

> *"This message is money for getting leads to engage. And it's among the first things I do when I invest in a new business."*

— Alex Hormozi, *$100M Leads*

---

## Why It Works

Three mechanics converge:

### 1. Brevity Signals Authenticity
A 9-word email from someone you know doesn't look like marketing. It looks like a check-in. Open rates and response rates spike.

### 2. The Question Forces A Reply
You can't passively scan and forget. The question demands a yes/no answer in the recipient's head, which often translates to a typed reply.

### 3. It Filters For Current Intent
Many people had the desire 6 months ago and bought it elsewhere. Many people had the desire 2 weeks ago and are now actively shopping. The question surfaces only the second group.

> *"You make the ask to see who replies — aka — engaged leads. And these replies should be your top priority for warm reach outs."*

---

## The Template Examples

Hormozi's example variants:

> *Are you still looking to . . .*
> - *. . . buy your dream home?*
> - *. . . get more sales leads?*
> - *. . . tone up your arms?*
> - *. . . open an online store?*
> - *. . . start a YouTube channel?*

The [4 word desire] slot is filled with whatever your prospect would actually be searching for. The narrower the desire, the higher the response rate.

---

## When To Use It

### Best Use Cases
- **Re-engaging a dormant list** — old subscribers, past inquirers, lapsed customers
- **First contact in a new investment** — Hormozi's first move when buying into a company
- **Warming up a cold-but-permission-given list** — people who opted in for something else, now you have an adjacent offer
- **Periodic re-checks** — every 3-6 months on a list, like the multi-touch cold outreach principle in [[cold-outreach-system]]

### Bad Use Cases
- Cold lists (no permission) — looks like spam without prior context
- Lists where you've never given value — feels presumptuous
- Audiences who don't recognize your name — adds confusion

## How To Action The Replies

> *"I'll end step 10 here because I break down this 'give-ask' process in the next chapter. The main point is that a warm list is a huge asset because it's a consistent and growing source of engaged leads."*

When someone replies "yes":
1. Treat them as the top-priority engaged lead on your list this week
2. Apply the [[warm-outreach-system|ACA Framework]] (Acknowledge / Compliment / Ask)
3. Move into the value-equation pitch (Step 6 of warm outreach)

When someone replies "no":
- They've voluntarily disqualified themselves — don't push
- File for the next 9-word email in 6-12 months

When no reply:
- Standard list hygiene — they may come back to-life on the next probe

## Why It's Not Just A "Cute Trick"

Hormozi treats this as a serious tool, not a gimmick:

> *"Swipe and deploy."*

The 9-word email is one of the few templates in $100M Leads that he prescribes verbatim with no variations. The reason: it's been tested across enormous lists for over a decade by Dean Jackson and works reliably in every industry.

## Application

The simplest way to use this today:

1. Open your CRM or email list
2. Identify the 4-word desire most relevant to your offer
3. Send the 9-word email to 100 dormant contacts
4. Reply to every "yes" using the [[warm-outreach-system|ACA framework]]
5. Repeat every 3-6 months

Expected return on time: this is one of the highest-ROI activities documented in the book.
