# Framework: The Content Unit — Hook + Retain + Reward
tags: [leads, Hormozi, content, framework, posting, core-four]

## Summary / Definition

The mechanical structure of all audience-growing content. Hormozi's claim: **every piece of content that grows an audience does three things in this order: Hook → Retain → Reward.**

> *"The smallest amount of material it takes to hook, retain and reward attention is a content unit."*

— Alex Hormozi, *$100M Leads*

A content unit can be as small as a single sentence or as large as a multi-hour podcast. Longer pieces are just content units chained together.

## The Three Components

### 1. Hook — Get Their Attention

> *"They cannot be rewarded unless we first get their attention."*

The hook gives them a reason to redirect attention from whatever they're doing toward your content. Measured by the % of people who *start* consuming.

**Three sub-components of a good hook:**

#### A) Topics (5 Categories)

1. **Far Past** — Important past lessons from your life. *"Give them the story without the scar."*
2. **Recent Past** — Things you did recently. Look at last week's calendar, meetings, conversations.
3. **Present** — Capture ideas the moment they happen. Always have a way to record.
4. **Trending** — Apply your expertise to what's already getting attention.
5. **Manufactured** — Pick a topic, then create the experience (lived on $100/mo example).

> *"The easiest way to differentiate is to say something no one else can say. And no one else has lived your life but you."*

#### B) Headlines (7 News Components)

From meta-analysis of news. Include at least 2:

1. **Recency** — As new as possible (literally "the *news*")
2. **Relevancy** — Personally meaningful to the audience
3. **Celebrity** — Prominent people (authorities, celebrities)
4. **Proximity** — Geographically close to home
5. **Conflict** — Opposing ideas, people, nature (Pineapple vs no pineapple on pizza)
6. **Unusual** — Odd, unique, rare, bizarre
7. **Ongoing** — Stories still in progress, evolving, with plot twists

> *"Include one or more of these components to give yourself meatier, attention-grabbing headlines."*

#### C) Format — Match The Platform

> *"You've gotta make your content look like what they expect will reward them. Otherwise, no matter how good it is, better-looking content will hook them before yours even has a chance."*

Study the best-performing content on the platform targeting your audience. Match the format. Tweak only after matching.

### 2. Retain — Get Them To Consume It

> *"My favorite driver of retention is curiosity."*

The technique: **embed unresolved questions in their minds.** Three methods:

#### A) Lists
Things, facts, tips, opinions presented in sequence. Follow a theme. Use a number in the headline.

- "7 Ways I Invested $1000 in My 20s That Paid Off Big"
- "28 Ways To Stay Poor"
- "A content unit has three pieces..."

#### B) Steps
Actions in order accomplishing a goal. The reward is explicit (completed result).

- "3 Steps to Creating a Great Hook"
- "How I Create a Headline in 7 Steps"
- "The Morning Routine That Boosts My Productivity"

#### C) Stories
Real or imaginary events with a lesson. Curiosity comes from "what happens next?"

- Every chapter in $100M Leads has a story
- "My editor made me do 19 drafts of this book — here's what I did to him"
- "My journey from sleeping on the bottom floor of a gym to the top floor of a 5-star hotel"

**You can interweave all three.** Lists within steps. Stories about each list item. Stories reinforcing each step.

### 3. Reward — Satisfy The Hook's Promise

> *"How good your content is depends on how often it rewards your audience in the time it takes them to consume it. Think value per second."*

> *"There is no such thing as too long, only too boring."*

The same viewer who bounces from a 10-second video binges a 900-page book. Streaming services proved this — people will spend 8 hours straight on content they love.

**Reward = match or exceed expectations created by the hook.**

#### Examples Of Failed Rewards

- Hook promises "7 Ways to Make Up with Your Spouse" but you only give 4 → fail
- 7 are given but they stunk or are well-known → fail
- The 7 are great but your audience is all single guys → fail (wrong audience hooked)

> *"No matter how good you think your content is, the audience decides."*

#### How To Know If You Succeeded

> *"Here's how you know if you succeeded: your audience grows. If it's not growing, your stuff isn't that good. Practice and you'll get better."*

## Short Vs Long Form Content

> *"The difference between short and long form content? Answer: not much."*

A content unit is the smallest unit that hooks-retains-rewards. Longer content = multiple content units chained.

> *"Shorter content hooks, retains, rewards fewer times. Longer content does it more times. And doing it more times takes more skill because you have to string more 'good' content units in a row."*

Comedian analogy: new comics get 3 minutes; masters get an hour. They had to learn to reward attention frequently enough to hold it.

Hormozi's recommendation: **start short.** Even authors of epic novels started with short stories.

---

## How To Apply (Start Today)

1. **Pick one topic** from the 5 categories (start with Far Past — your own lessons)
2. **Write 1 headline** that uses 2+ news components
3. **Decide your retention structure** — list, steps, or story
4. **Match the platform format** of the best content in your space
5. **Make sure the reward delivers** on what the hook promised
6. **Post and observe** — the audience tells you if it worked

Then repeat. Every day. Forever.
