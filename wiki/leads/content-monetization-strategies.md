## Two Scaling Strategies

After the give:ask ratio is locked in, you face the question of how to scale beyond one platform.

### Strategy A: Depth Then Width

**Maximize one platform fully before adding another.**

Steps:
1. Post on a relevant platform
2. Post regularly on that platform
3. Maximize quality + quantity (short-form: sometimes 10×/day per platform; long-form: 5×/week)
4. Add another platform while maintaining the first
5. Repeat until all relevant platforms are maximized

**Advantages:**
- Maximum return on a given effort (audiences compound faster the more you do)
- Fewer resources required to make it work
- You learn one platform deeply before splitting attention

**Disadvantages:**
- Less low-hanging fruit from new platforms
- No "omnipresence" feeling
- Risk of business being reliant on a single channel (platforms ban, change, vanish)

### Strategy B: Width Then Depth

**Get on every platform early, then maximize them together.**

Steps:
1. Post on a relevant platform
2. Post regularly on that platform
3. **Instead of maximizing, move to the next relevant platform while maintaining the previous**
4. Continue until on all relevant platforms
5. Now maximize content creation on all platforms at once

**Advantages:**
- Reach a broader audience faster
- Repurpose content efficiently (one short video can run on Reels, TikTok, Shorts, LinkedIn, etc.)
- Diversified risk

**Disadvantages:**
- More labor, attention, and time
- Often produces bad content everywhere ("sucky fluff")

### Which To Pick

> *"If you already have a sizable business, scale up faster and reap the rewards of an asset that only gets better with time."*

The choice depends on resources:
- **Solo/small team** → Depth-then-Width (focus pays better)
- **Larger team or sizable business** → Width-then-Depth (parallelize the work)

> *"I said it before and I'll say it again. The best day to start posting content was the day you were born. The second best day is today. Don't wait like I did."*

## Why Content Multiplies Every Other Method

January 2020. Hormozi's portfolio companies' paid advertising stopped performing. The team debated creative, copy, offer, pages, sales process, price. Leila asked a different question:

> *"What did we stop doing in the months before the decline?"*

Unanimous answer: **Alex stopped making gym-specific content** and started talking about general business.

He surveyed gym owners. The result:

> **"78% of all clients had consumed at least TWO long form pieces of content, prior to booking a call."**

(This is the same data point captured in [[reverse-engineer-buying-process]] from the Lost Chapter, re-quoted in $100M Leads.)

> *"I had fallen into my old ways and given paid ads all the credit. But, our free content was nurturing the demand."*

The lesson:

> *"Don't make the same mistake I did. Your free content gives strangers an opportunity to find, get value from, and share your stuff. And, it warms people on the fence that go to and come from the cold audience methods we dive into next. So even if it's hard to measure, free content gets you better returns on all advertising methods."*

**Free content multiplies every other Core Four method.**

## The 7 Content Lessons

Hormozi's hard-won insights:

### 1. Switch From "How To" To "How I"

From "This is the best way" to "These are my favorite ways."

- *"I make my oatmeal this way"* vs *"You should make your oatmeal this way"*
- *"How I Built My 7-Figure Agency"* vs *"How To Build a 7-Figure Agency"*
- *"My favorite way to generate leads for my business"* vs *"This is the best way to generate leads for your business"*

> *"When you tell your experience, you are sharing value. When you tell a stranger what to do, it's hard to avoid coming off preachy or arrogant."*

**Why this matters:** When you speak from experience, no one can question you. **This makes you bulletproof.**

### 2. We Need To Be Reminded More Than We Need To Be Taught

> *"You're a silly goose if you think 100 percent of your audience listens 100 percent of the time."*

Hormozi posts about his book every single day. Surveyed his audience — **1 in 5 viewers said they didn't know he had a book.**

> *"Keep repeating yourself. You'll get bored of your content before your whole audience even sees it."*

### 3. Puddles, Ponds, Lakes, Oceans

Narrow your content focus when starting.

> *"If you have a small local business, you probably shouldn't make general business content. Not at first, at least. Why? The audience will listen to people with better track records than you."*

Instead:
- Start with **plumbing in a specific town** (puddle) — become king of that puddle
- Expand to **general local business** (pond)
- Expand to **brick & mortar chains** (lake)
- Eventually expand to **general business** (ocean)

Each expansion only happens after you dominate the smaller scope.

### 4. Content Creates Tools For Salespeople

Some content will perform well. That content helps your sales team.

> *"Create a master list of your 'greatest hits.' Label each 'hit' with the problem it solves and the benefit it provides."*

Your sales team sends specific pieces before or after sales calls to help close. Works especially well when content resolves common objections.

This is the same mechanic as the "all time greatest hits" library in [[reverse-engineer-buying-process]].

### 5. Free Content Retains Paying Customers

> *"Imagine a person pays for your thing and then consumes your free content. If your free content is valuable, they will like you more and stay loyal to your business longer."*

> *"Somebody who buys your stuff is more likely to consume your free content."*

Customers calculate ROI partly from your free content — make it as good as your paid stuff.

### 6. People Don't Have Shorter Attention Spans, They Have Higher Standards

> *"Repeated for emphasis: there's no such thing as too long, only too boring."*

Streaming platforms proved people will binge long-form for hours if they like it. Biology hasn't changed — competition has.

> *"Make good stuff people like and reap the rewards rather than whining about people's 'short attention spans.'"*

### 7. Avoid Pre-Scheduling Posts

> *"The posts I manually post perform better than ones I pre-schedule."*

Hormozi's theory: manual posting creates a real-time feedback loop. You know within seconds if it lands. That close feedback pressure makes you try harder. Pre-scheduling removes that pressure.

> *"Whenever I post, or my team does, we strongly believe in someone pressing the 'submit' button because it gives that last bit of pressure to get it right. Try it."*

## Benchmarks — How To Measure

Two metrics, measured monthly:

### 1. Total Followers And Reach (How Big)

- **Followers**: 1000 → 1500 = grew by 500
- **Reach**: 10,000 → 15,000 people seeing your stuff = grew reach by 5,000

### 2. Rate Of Growth (How Fast)

- 500 new / 1000 starting = **50% growth month**
- 5,000 new reach / 10,000 starting = **50% growth month**

> *"Remember, we can only control inputs. Measuring outputs is only useful if we are consistent with inputs."*

Pick posting cadence. Pick ask cadence. Then **Do. Not. Stop.**

### The Long-Game Data Point

> *"I posted a new podcast twice a week for four years before even getting picked up on the Top 100 list."*

Year 5: top 10 podcast in US for business, top 500 in the world.

> *"This was only possible after 5 years of multiple podcasts per week every week. Remember, everyone starts at zero. You just gotta give time, time."*

## Your First Post

> *"You've probably been providing value to other humans knowingly or unknowingly for a while. So the first post you make, you can make an ask. My hope is that it gets you your first engaged lead."*

If it doesn't get one, switch to giving for a while before asking again.

> *"If someone won't speak at your funeral, you shouldn't care about their opinion while you're alive. Honor the few who believe in you by having courage."*

Hormozi includes his own first business post in the book — describes it as "you goon" cringe — and uses it to make the point: **post anyway.**

---

## Why This Works In Tandem With Warm Outreach

> *"Posting free content is less predictable than, but complementary to, warm reach outs. So keep doing warm reach outs. Also, posting free content grows your warm audience. And a bigger warm audience means more people for warm reach outs."*

Don't ditch one for the other. **Stack them.**
