# Framework: Paid Ads — Callout + Value + CTA
tags: [leads, Hormozi, paid-ads, core-four, framework, callouts]

## Summary / Definition

The fourth of the Core Four. Paid Ads = 1-to-many public communication to strangers, paid for by you.

> *"Advertising is the only casino where, with enough skill, you become the house."*

— Alex Hormozi, *$100M Leads* (chapter epigraph)

This page covers the **creative side** of paid ads — how to make an ad that works. The economics side is in [[paid-ads-money-mechanics]].

---

## The Origin Story — The 6-Week Challenge

June 2013. Hormozi convinced Sam (his gym mentor) to bet $1,000 on Facebook ads. The deal: if it loses, Hormozi shuts up. If it makes money, they split.

The ad was the ugliest you've ever seen:

> *"I'M LOOKING FOR 5 CHINO HILLS RESIDENTS TO TAKE PLACE IN A FREE 6 WEEK CHALLENGE. YOU MUST LET US USE YOUR BEFORE AND AFTER PICTURES IN OUR MARKETING IN EXCHANGE FOR THE PROGRAM. CLICK LINK TO SIGN UP."*

No images. No videos. No frills. ALL CAPS.

Result: 19 sales × $299 = **$5,700 from $1,000**. Sam wrote Hormozi a check for $2,500.

> *"Sam, this is —"*
>
> *"Nice work, Hormozi. Do it again."*

The "6 week challenge" became the biggest promotion in the gym industry for 7 years. Drove **$1.5B+ in revenue.** Taught to 4,500+ gyms. Used (without licensing) by 10,000+ more.

## What Makes Paid Ads Different

> *"Paid ads work by paying another person or business to put your offer in front of their audience. Think of it like renting eyeballs or earballs."*

The trade: money for guaranteed reach. The risk: the reach is guaranteed; the profit isn't.

> *"In principle, if you paid enough money, you could get every person in the world to see your ad. And, if every person in the world saw your ad, someone would buy."*

So the question isn't "do ads work?" — it's "how well can you make them work?" Efficiency over creativity. Profitability over reach.

---

## The 4 Problems Paid Ads Create

1. Knowing where to advertise (platform selection)
2. Getting the right audience to see it (targeting)
3. Making the best ad for them to see (creative)
4. Getting permission to contact them (capture)

---

## Step 1: Pick A Platform (4 Criteria)

Look for a platform where **all four** are true:

1. **You've used it yourself** — you know how it works as a consumer
2. **You can target people interested in your stuff**
3. **You know how to format ads specific to the platform**
4. **You have the minimum money required to place an ad**

> *"Start with one platform that meets the four requirements. And start watching, listening, or reading ads on the platform as a first step to learning how to make one."*

---

## Step 2: Target — Find The Needle In The Haystack

Two ways to target on modern platforms:

### A) Lookalike Audience

Upload a list. The platform builds a much bigger audience similar to it.

Order to use lists:
1. Current and previous customers (best quality)
2. Add warm reach out list (if list too small)
3. Add cold reach out leads (if still too small)

Force the list to hit the platform minimum.

### B) Targeting Filters

Layer on:
- Age
- Income
- Gender
- Interests
- Time of day
- Location

Examples:
- Sleep product → exclude under 25 and over 45 if data shows that's optimal
- Car parts → run during car shows on car channels
- Pet products → include pets as interest

> *"The more filters you use, the more specific the list. The more specific the list, the more efficient your ads but the faster you will 'burn' through it."*

Specificity sets you up for early wins; broader audiences come later when you can afford lower efficiency at higher absolute profit.

## Step 3: Make The Ad — Callout + Value + CTA

> *"I want to see how businesses do three things. 1) How they call out their ideal customers. 2) How they present the value elements. 3) How they give their audience a call to action."*

This is the structural framework for every ad.

### 1) Call Out — Get Them To Notice It

> *"People noticing your ad is the most important part of the ad . . . by a lot."*

> *"After you've written your headline, you've spent eighty cents of your advertising dollar."* — David Ogilvy

> *"As crazy as this sounds, my advertising became 20× more effective when I focused the majority of my effort on the first five seconds."*

#### The Cocktail Party Effect

In a loud ballroom, only one thing pierces through: **your name.** Callouts harness this.

#### Verbal Callouts (4 Types)

1. **Labels** — Words/phrases putting people in a group
   - *Clark County Moms*, *Gym Owners*, *Remote Workers*
   - Most powerful: **local area + type of person** (*Irving Residents — Plumbers*)

2. **Yes-Questions** — Where "yes" qualifies them
   - *Do you wake up to pee more than once a night?*
   - *Do you have trouble tying your shoes?*
   - *Do you have a home worth over $400,000?*

3. **If-Then Statements** — Meeting conditions = help making a decision
   - *If you run over $100,000/mo in ads, we can save you 20%+*
   - *If you were born between 1978 and 1986 in Muskogee Oklahoma, you may qualify for a class action lawsuit*
   - *If you want to XYZ, then pay attention*

4. **Ridiculous Results** — Bizarre, rare, out-of-ordinary outcomes
   - *Massage studio books out two years in advance. Clients furious.*
   - *This woman lost 50 pounds eating pizza and fired her trainer*
   - *The government is handing out thousand dollar checks*

#### Nonverbal Callouts (3 Types)

1. **Contrast** — Visual elements that "stick out"
   - Bright shirt > dull shirt for attention
   - Attractive people > plain people for attention
   - Moving stuff > still stuff for attention

2. **Likeness** — Visual labels (features, traits, titles, places)
   - "Quack like a duck" — to attract plumbers, dress like one, talk like one, be in a plumbing environment
   - Doctors' ads use lab coats, stethoscopes, medical facilities
   - **Mascots** work great (Mickey Mouse, Geico Gecko, Tony the Tiger, Michelin Man) — they don't age, don't ask for raises, don't take days off
   - Advanced: pretty people convert better and cost less than you might think

3. **The Scene** — Visual representations of Yes-Questions and If-Then
   - Person tossing in bed → people with sleep troubles
   - Pear next to hourglass → pear-shaped body
   - Room stacked with junk → people with clutter
   - Rock hitting window → people with broken windows
   - Local landmark → locals: "Hey, I know that place!"

### 2) Value — Get Them Interested

Covered in detail at [[what-who-when-framework]]. The framework: 8 key elements × Who perspectives × When timeline.

> *"A good advertisement, paid or not, uses clear and simple ways to answer the question: why should I be interested in your thing?"*

### 3) CTA — Tell Them What To Do Next

> *"If your ad got them interested, then your audience will have huge motivation . . . for a tiny time. Take advantage. Tell them exactly what to do next. S-P-E-L-L it out."*

Make CTAs quick and easy:
- Easy phone numbers
- Obvious buttons
- Simple websites

> *"Instead of: alexsprivateequityfirm.com/free-book-and-course2782*
>
> *Use: acquisition.com/training"*

(Note: Hormozi spent $370,000 on the single-word domain Acquisition.com.)

Add the urgency mechanics from [[lead-magnets-framework|Step 7 — CTA]]: scarcity, urgency, bonuses.

## Step 4: Get Permission To Contact Them — The Landing Page

After they take action, **get their contact information.**

Simplest mechanism: a landing page. Focus on **words and image only.**

### Match Ad To Landing Page

> *"Make your landing pages match your ads. People click an ad because you promised them some benefit. So carry that same look and language over to your landing page. Make sure what you promised in your ad is what you deliver."*

A continuous experience from "click to close." Frankensteins lose conversion.

### The Cialdini Consistency Principle

Robert Cialdini (*Influence*): people like to think of themselves as consistent. Reminding them of an action they just took makes the next action easier.

Example phrasing:
- *"Now that you just did A, you need to do B to get the most of A."*
- *"Doing A makes you a 'doing A' kind of person. Doing A kind of people do B."*

### The Mindset

> *"To be clear, we aren't selling anything. We are asking if they're interested in the stuff we sell. And if they're interested, they'll give us a way to tell them more about it."*

The landing page produces engaged leads, not sales. Sales happen later.

---

## Long Ads vs Short Ads

> *"The only difference between long ads and short ads is how many angles we have time to cover from the copywriting framework. Longer ads use more. Shorter ads use fewer."*

Add or remove angles based on the platform — **but always keep the callout (first 5 seconds) and CTA (what to do next) identical.**
