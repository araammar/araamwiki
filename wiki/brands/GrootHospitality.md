# Brand: Groot Hospitality

## Voice & Tone
Aspirational but warm. Luxury without pretension. The brand occupies the intersection of glamour and genuine hospitality — it wants guests to feel like insiders, not just customers. David Grutman's personal ethos ("take it personal") permeates the brand: obsessive care about details, genuine relationships, and the conviction that hospitality is ultimately about serving others.

## Target Audience
- High-net-worth locals and Miami regulars
- Transient guests (tourists, event visitors, convention attendees) — a deliberate mix Grutman considers essential for venue longevity
- Celebrity and entertainment circles
- Young affluent professionals drawn to destination dining and nightlife
- International visitors to Miami and Las Vegas markets

## Brand Entities
- **LIV Nightclub** — flagship Miami Beach nightclub at Fontainebleau; management deal with Jeff Sofer; among the most storied clubs in the US
- **Komodo** — flagship restaurant; 18,000–19,000 sq ft in Miami; now in Vegas and Dallas; Grutman's first restaurant; origin of the ecosystem model
- **Poppy Steak** — steakhouse partnership with David "Poppy" Einhorn; known for the $1,000 steak experience; wildly successful ($20M+ Miami, $30M+ Vegas); 7–8 years in, still culturally hot
- **Strawberry Moon** — hotel-integrated venue
- **Story** — club that was shuttered by city ordinance (forced 2 a.m. closure)
- Additional venues across Miami, Las Vegas, and expanding markets

## Strategy & Positioning
Groot operates a **full-night hospitality ecosystem**: restaurants prime guests pre-club, lounge concepts fill the gap between dinner and late-night, and the club is the capstone of the evening. The strategy is explicitly about never losing the guest — owning every touchpoint from 8 p.m. through 5 a.m.

The brand occupies an unambiguous luxury tier. Every attempt to go mid or low-price has failed (Winkers diner, Key Club). The market has repeatedly told Grutman: we want glamour, spectacle, and the high end from you. He now leans into this completely.

**Deal architecture:** Mix of:
- **Owned leases** — highest upside, highest capital risk; Komodo and Poppy Steak are the crown jewels
- **Management deals** — piece of gross + piece of net, no capital required; how most casino/resort venues work (LIV model); valuable for cash flow and market penetration without bet-the-company risk

## Brand Superpowers
1. **Area activation** — Grutman has proven he can make a cold or undiscovered area hot (Brickell at Komodo's launch); landlords call him specifically for this
2. **Celebrity gravity** — relationships with A-list entertainers, athletes, and moguls mean programming generates cultural news on its own
3. **IP discipline** — Grutman learned early (Komodo Series A lesson) to control IP at the holdco level; this is how Komodo expanded to Vegas and Dallas with equity intact
4. **Sensory design obsession** — acoustics and lighting are non-negotiables that never get value-engineered out; these create the experiential foundation that makes everything else work

## Do's
- Go big — only large-scale venues (18K+ sq ft) generate the revenue needed to cover the infrastructure
- Mix transient and local business; purely neighborhood venues cap out
- Obsess the details: lighting, acoustics, pacing of service, cleanliness, body language of guests
- Protect IP at the holdco level; never give away IP for a single-venue investment deal
- Keep venues fresh through periodic closures and renovations (Poppy Steak 4-month refresh)

## Don'ts
- Mid or low price point — Groot's audience does not want this from them
- Purely local neighborhood positioning — needs transient/tourist demand to sustain
- Pure management deal structure — needs owned leases in the portfolio for real exit value
- Over-dilute investors; IP should stay at holdco level; single venue investors get investment returns, not perpetual IP rights
