# Brand: Cadence

## Overview
Cadence is a sports hydration and nutrition brand founded by Ross Mackay (and co-founder George Eaton of Represent). Positioned as "the world's first $2 luxury product" and "the champagne of electrolytes." Goal: disrupt Gatorade at mass retail scale (Target, Walmart, CVS) while maintaining premium brand positioning. ~$10M in first 600 days; projecting $40M next year. Loading into Target (February 2026), Walmart (March), CVS and Walgreens (April).

## Voice & Tone
- Inspirational yet achievable — "fuel the goal-oriented athlete"
- Premium without being inaccessible
- Scientifically credible (data-backed formulation) but not clinical
- Founder-voiced content (Ross on YouTube + Instagram)
- Almost zero direct response advertising in early phase — brand first

## Target Audience
- Goal-oriented endurance athletes: runners, cyclists, Hyrox participants, triathletes
- Run club culture (Brooklyn Half Marathon, Chicago Marathon, NYC Marathon community)
- Amateur athletes training seriously — not elite, but highly motivated
- Eventually: mass America (Walmart/Target shopper demographic)

## Brand Colors & Aesthetics
- Minimal, sleek can design
- Half-inch colored rim for shelf-recognition and flavor differentiation
- USPs on can: no sugar, no caffeine (visible at glance)
- Printed can (not sleeve) — deliberate premium signal; higher MOQ accepted as brand investment
- 35% of can is regulatory (barcode, FDA callouts, fluid ounce size) — the brand fights for every inch of the remaining 65%

## Strategy & Positioning
- **The $2 Luxury Paradox**: Price within category norms ($2.79 at Target), positioned as premium through brand, content, and partnerships — not price point
- **Premium at Scale** mission: maintain Represent/On Running-level brand equity while achieving Gatorade-level distribution
- **Formulation first**: 500mg sodium (vs. Gatorade's ~100mg equivalent) — backed by sports science (Boston Celtics nutrition advisor Matt Jones); full custom IP on all formulas
- **Product system**: Core / Race / Recover — covers the full 24-hour athlete day
- **Retail as the game**: 86% of beverages bought in-store; D2C builds brand and 65% subscription base, then converts to retail drive
- **SKU discipline**: One SKU to $10M before launching the next; anchor customer must commit before pressing go on production

## Brand Inspirations
- **On Running**: premium at scale; dentist and Olympic athlete both wear it
- **Red Bull**: lifestyle brand at billion-dollar volume — content/sponsorship drives can sales
- **Nespresso**: premium content and retail experience, accessible price, scale
- **RXBAR / Clif Bar**: community-first, never loses the core ICP even at mass distribution
- **George Eaton / Represent**: co-founder connection, fashion/lifestyle brand sensibility applied to CPG

## Do's
- Build for where the product will sit (retail shelf, not D2C hero image)
- Keep the brand bigger than the founder's face — Cadence can exist without Ross
- Use personal founder content to drive audience into retail channels
- Work with influencers who represent your aspirational positioning — not just whoever converts
- Shoot content WITH athletes (bring the team); don't just hand them product
- Underwrite new SKUs with retailer commitments before production
- Keep the team small and fast — speed is the competitive advantage below $100M

## Don'ts
- Don't launch a clothing line or do category-crossing collabs for a quick million
- Don't run ads that make you cringe — brand equity is the long game
- Don't outsource retail sales to brokers who won't fight for you
- Don't build the brand on Ross's name — "It's not McKie Nutrition, it's Cadence"
- Don't confuse "sold out" as good for a daily-consumption brand scaling into B2B

## Collaborations
- **Bandit Running**: run community collab — non-competitive categories, same ICP; elevates brand in NYC running scene
- **RAW Supplements**: overlapping categories but different demographics (bodybuilding vs. endurance); relationship-driven
- **24/7 (George Eaton's brand)**: natural community overlap; co-founders bring cross-audiences
- Dream: **Nespresso × Cadence** sleep capsule (hot cocoa format for Cadence's sleep product)

## Retail Economics
- Target RRP: $2.79
- Retailer margin: ~50% (sell-in = RRP minus 50%)
- Distributor margin (if used): 15–25%
- Trade spend: 20–30% of revenue to activate retail
- Key KPIs: units per store per week (USPW), dollars per store per week
- Gross margin target: 50%+ to survive retail economics
